## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys added 8,000 employees net during the quarter. The company had guided to approximately 20,000 freshers for FY26 and had hired more than 12,000 freshers in the first half. These freshers were being onboarded at the time of the press conference.

## Europe and Project Maximus

### Salil Parekh

Europe has performed extremely well over the past two years. Infosys invested in multiple European countries, where companies increasingly opened up to transformation and cost-efficiency services. The company expects the U.S. market to remain strong and plans to continue investing and making acquisitions in the U.S., Europe, and other global markets.

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

Project Maximus continues to deliver. In the prior year, Infosys expanded its margin by 50 basis points despite compensation-related headwinds, higher variable pay, acquisition impacts, and the ramp-up of multiple mega deals signed in the preceding year.

In the current quarter, operating margin expanded by 20 basis points sequentially. Project Maximus contributed 30 basis points, with pricing providing a tailwind that was offset by higher subcontracting costs and lower onsite utilization.

## Large-deal pipeline, client contribution, hiring, and margins

### Beena Parmar

The questions covered the reduction in contribution from the top five and top ten clients, the outlook for campus and off-campus hiring, the sources of the deal pipeline by industry and geography, and the margin levers after wage increases and fresher onboarding.

### Salil Parekh

The large-deal pipeline remains strong. Clients are focused on cost optimization, consolidation, automation, and the use of AI to improve efficiency. Infosys is also seeing interest in AI transformation for growth activities.

Financial Services and Manufacturing are showing good traction. The Retail pipeline is also strong, although conversion remains to be seen. Overall sentiment is favorable for large deals, with an emphasis on cost efficiency, automation, and related activities.

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

Changes in client contribution are marginal and reflect projects ramping up or down.

Expected second-half margin headwinds include seasonally lower working days and furloughs. Margin tailwinds include multiple Project Maximus tracks, including pricing, lean and automation, and optimization of large portfolios and programs. As newly hired freshers are onboarded to projects, the employee pyramid is expected to provide an additional benefit.

## Acquisition contribution and guidance

### Beena Parmar

The question concerned the contribution of acquisitions to the quarter and to the company's guidance.

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

Acquisitions contributed 20 basis points in the quarter, described in the context of the 2.2% result. Two months of the acquisition contribution had already been included in the prior quarter, and the acquisition contribution was already incorporated into guidance. The Versent project and the joint venture in Australia will be reflected when the transaction closes.

The acquisition contribution is already part of the run rate, with no additional contribution being built into guidance for the third or fourth quarter.

## AI revenue disclosure and modernization

### Beena Parmar

The question concerned whether Infosys would disclose AI contribution to revenue in FY26.

### Salil Parekh

Infosys is currently focused on externally sharing what it is doing in AI. The company tracks AI-related developments internally and will share further information externally when appropriate.

### Jas Bardia

The questions concerned whether Infosys would maintain its asset-light approach to embedding AI in software services or invest heavily in AI infrastructure, and whether AI tools are increasing business from legacy-modernization deals.

### Salil Parekh

AI is creating a significant opportunity in modernization. Without AI tools, modernization programs required a longer time horizon. AI tools can shorten that time horizon and improve the client's return on investment. As the tools mature, Infosys expects them to be deployed increasingly in modernization programs.

Infosys is comfortable with its current AI strategy.

## U.K. NHS transformation deal and nearshore strategy

### Veena Mani

The questions concerned the impact of stricter H-1B norms, the growth and scale of Infosys's nearshore centers, the sector and AI component of a recently announced mega deal, and the proportion of premium-skilled freshers among the 12,000 hired in the first half.

### Salil Parekh

The mega deal was with the U.K. National Health Service and was announced shortly after the quarter ended. It is a complete transformation program supported by multiple new technologies, including AI. Infosys is working with multiple partners to deliver the program.

Nearshore operations have been a major success. Infosys began its geographic localization strategy several years ago across Europe, Australia, and the U.S. The strategy includes hiring local employees and building nearshore centers in and around the U.S., including Canada and Mexico, as well as in Latin America and Europe. The company expects the nearshore model to scale further amid changes to H-1B requirements.
