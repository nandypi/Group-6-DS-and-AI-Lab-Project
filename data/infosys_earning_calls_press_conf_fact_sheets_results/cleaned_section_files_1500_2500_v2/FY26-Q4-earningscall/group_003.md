## Yogesh Aggarwal

Yogesh Aggarwal asked about the assumptions at the lower and upper ends of guidance and why significant INR depreciation had not changed the margin outlook after Project Maximus.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

At the lower end of guidance, Infosys assumes greater deterioration in the environment. At the upper end, it assumes an improved environment, consistent with the approach used in the prior year.

The benefits from the rupee and Project Maximus have largely been reinvested in the business. Sales and marketing costs increased by 40 basis points on a full-year basis, and additional investments included AI talent and AI partnerships. These investments were absorbed in the financial-year margin.

### Productivity pass-through

Yogesh Aggarwal asked why productivity improvements should affect margins.

Jayesh Sanghrajka said the market is competitive and competitive intensity has increased. Productivity benefits are therefore largely passed back to clients.

## Ankur Rudra

Ankur Rudra asked whether the narrower 200-basis-point guidance band reflected better visibility than in prior years. He also asked about the approximately 2.25% organic growth midpoint compared with 2.4% organic growth in Fiscal '26, including the effects of demand conditions, structural AI deflation, and the ramp-down of a large account.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

In the prior year, Infosys provided a three-point guidance range because the environment changed close to the time guidance was issued, particularly following tariff changes. At that time, visibility into the effect on client behavior was limited.

The environment has remained broadly similar for the last few quarters, and Infosys has greater clarity about client behavior than it had in the prior year. Management noted that guidance could change if the environment or client behavior changes.

## Salil Parekh

The main positive factors in the guidance include:

- Growth in AI services, where Infosys is seeing strong traction.
- A program to work with large companies where Infosys currently has a smaller footprint, with the objective of expanding those relationships.
- Large deals, with a 55% net-new mix, which are expected to contribute significantly during the financial year.
- A favorable demand environment and a strong large-deal pipeline.

The main compression factors include productivity benefits from AI in existing portfolios, which clients are seeking, as well as technical factors related to Manufacturing, Europe, and the on-site mix.

At Investor Day, Infosys described AI as having both a growth component and a compression component, alongside other growth drivers. Management said the current guidance reflects the balance between these factors and the operating environment.

### Conditions for organic growth acceleration

Ankur Rudra asked what would need to happen for organic growth to accelerate at the midpoint.

Salil Parekh said a favorable resolution to the Middle East situation could support growth. Underlying economic trends are also described as favorable in Infosys's larger markets, which could create a more stable macro environment. Strong AI traction and partnerships could provide additional support if these factors improve.

Management said it had not seen a major change requiring a three-point guidance range and currently expected growth rather than compression.

## Bryan Bergin

Bryan Bergin asked whether advances in AI models had changed the level of productivity compression in current contracts compared with one or two quarters earlier. He also asked about the share of business directly exposed to productivity pass-throughs versus business that was more insulated.

## Salil Parekh

AI models and technology continue to advance rapidly. Infosys has not seen a material change over the most recent one or two quarters attributable solely to technology developments, although there have been changes over the last two years.

Management said competitive intensity is high, and competitors occasionally demonstrate capabilities that appear outside the range of what current models are expected to deliver. Infosys has not separately disclosed the proportion of services exposed to productivity pass-throughs, but has disclosed service-line data that may help users estimate the exposure.

### Hiring, headcount, and subcontractors

Bryan Bergin asked about fresher hiring, the possibility of lower total headcount at the end of FY27, and expected subcontractor intensity.

Salil Parekh said Infosys plans to recruit approximately 20,000 college graduates. Some hiring will occur at a particular point in time and the rest throughout the year, providing flexibility if conditions change.

The company currently sees strong demand for higher-level lateral hires, and expects lateral recruitment to continue. Management does not currently have a plan for total headcount to be lower at the end of the year, although the outcome will depend on the demand environment. Hiring plans are being developed based on Q1, Q2, and the remaining periods of the year.

Jayesh Sanghrajka said subcontractors as a percentage of revenue have declined over the last few years. Infosys uses subcontractors to meet immediate demand when it lacks the required employee capacity or skill sets, and subsequently backfills those needs through employee hiring.

The company does not expect subcontractor usage to change significantly from current levels in the near term. Over the medium term, subcontractor usage may decline from the current level.

## Gaurav Rateria

Gaurav Rateria asked whether the deflationary impact from productivity savings in existing business was assumed to be higher at the midpoint of guidance than in the prior year. He noted that macro headwinds related to tariffs appeared lower than in the prior year and that AI services had become a larger and faster-growing business, while organic growth at the midpoint did not appear higher.
