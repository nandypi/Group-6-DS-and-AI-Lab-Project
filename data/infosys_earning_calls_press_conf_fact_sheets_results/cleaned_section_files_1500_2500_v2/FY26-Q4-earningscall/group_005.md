## Vibhor Singhal

### AI-related revenue compression and productivity

Vibhor Singhal asked whether Infosys had reached the trough of the AI-related revenue deflation cycle, or whether further compression was expected. He compared the cycle with the previous digital cycle, where revenue compression eventually reached a trough before declining as digital revenue grew.

Salil Parekh said the compression has multiple dimensions. Productivity assumptions are being built into some client deals, including multi-year arrangements, and the impact does not occur entirely in the first year. Actual compression depends on the mix of first-year, second-year, and later-year deals.

Management does not currently have a view to share on where the company is on the overall compression path. However, it has a clear view of what foundation models and other tools can support, and uses that understanding when structuring forward-looking three-year and five-year deals. Different models and tools are more relevant for different parts of the AI work being performed for clients.

### Recent deal productivity assumptions

Vibhor Singhal asked whether clients had returned to deals signed six or twelve months earlier to request additional productivity benefits.

Jayesh Sanghrajka said Infosys had not seen cases where a client returned to a deal signed a few months earlier and requested additional productivity benefits to be added. The productivity assumptions discussed by management apply when a new deal comes up for bid.

### Currency effects and quarterly margins

Vibhor Singhal asked whether the temporary quarterly margin benefit from Indian rupee depreciation had diminished because of industry maturity, or whether the benefit was being invested back into the business or passed on to clients.

Jayesh Sanghrajka said the rupee benefit is often offset by cross-currency headwinds. As the proportion of revenue from non-US markets increases, these offsets become larger. The US previously represented more than 70%-75% of the business, but that proportion has declined significantly, increasing the effect of other currencies.

For the quarter, the margin impact included:

- Approximately 50 basis points of headwind from amortization of acquisition-related intangible assets.
- A 30-basis-point gain in the previous quarter, which created an unfavorable sequential comparison.
- A 20-basis-point headwind from employee-related costs.
- A 40-basis-point benefit from currency.
- A 30-basis-point benefit from Project Maximus.

## Abhishek Pathak

### Deals not won and competitive pricing

Abhishek Pathak asked whether Infosys was losing deals to leaner, AI-native companies offering very low prices, or whether traditional vendors were engaging in irrational pricing. He also asked whether the industry would need newer, leaner pricing models over the next two to three years.

Salil Parekh said unusual competitor pricing occurs occasionally but is not widely prevalent. Such pricing has appeared for different reasons over time and may currently be associated with clients' expectations around AI productivity.

Infosys reported 96 large deals worth close to $15 billion during the last financial year. Management described the result as broad-based and robust, with a strong pipeline. In some cases, competitor productivity assumptions appeared outside the range that Infosys considers achievable based on its understanding of foundation models. Management does not view this as an industry-wide trend.

## Keith Bachman

### Pricing dynamics and AI cost uncertainty

Keith Bachman asked whether pricing competition had increased over the previous two years and whether the uncertainty around AI deployment cost curves created additional pricing risk.

Salil Parekh said competitive intensity had increased. He noted that Infosys had grown during the last financial year while some other companies reported negative revenue. Jayesh Sanghrajka added that overall realization was better than in prior periods, reflecting improved execution and a portfolio that management considered less risky.

Despite softer volumes during the year, most of Infosys's growth came from realization. Management attributed this to the value created for clients through AI and to Project Maximus initiatives, including lean automation and value-based selling.

Infosys maintained its annual margins despite market competitiveness. It reinvested approximately 40-50 basis points in sales and marketing, AI talent, AI capabilities, and other AI-related initiatives. These investments were absorbed while keeping margins constant.
