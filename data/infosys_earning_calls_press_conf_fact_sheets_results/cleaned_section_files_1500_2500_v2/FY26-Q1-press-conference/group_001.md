# Infosys Limited Q1 FY26 Financial Results Press Conference Call

July 23, 2025

## Salil Parekh

Infosys had a strong start to FY26. Revenue grew 2.6% sequentially and 3.8% year-on-year in constant-currency terms. Growth was broad-based, with the five largest industry groups and the largest geographies growing year-on-year in constant currency.

Large deals totaled $3.8 billion. Management attributed growth primarily to Infosys' enterprise AI capabilities, including AI agents, and continued client selections for consolidation work.

Infosys had built 300 AI agents across business operations and IT areas, and these agents had been deployed with clients. Horizontal and vertical agents were being used to support faster decision-making, improved customer experience, and greater operational efficiency.

Based on Q1 performance and the current outlook, FY26 constant-currency growth guidance was revised from 0%-3% to 1%-3%. Operating-margin guidance remained unchanged at 20%-22%.

### Guidance, inorganic growth, macroeconomic conditions, and hiring

In response to a question about the guidance revision, Salil Parekh said the company had increased the lower end of its guidance because Q1 performance was strong and the global economic environment appeared more stable, although conditions remained unsettled. Management also expects continued traction from consolidation work and enterprise AI, while Europe and the United States continue to undergo economic changes.

Jayesh Sanghrajka said inorganic growth contributed approximately 40 basis points to Q1 growth within the reported 2.6% sequential constant-currency growth. He expected a similar contribution from inorganic growth for the full year.

Excluding acquisitions, Infosys' Q1 constant-currency growth was above 2%, or approximately 2.2%.

Management said economic changes were affecting some activity in logistics, consumer products, and manufacturing. At the same time, demand for AI-agent work and consolidation opportunities provided growth benefits.

Infosys' headcount remained constant during the quarter, while utilization reached 85%. The company planned to continue hiring in line with the hiring plan announced at the beginning of FY26.

### Regional growth, utilization, and wage increases

Jayesh Sanghrajka said Europe had remained strong for several quarters, supported by investments made in the region several years earlier. The investment was producing results across sectors. Europe was growing faster than the Americas, although the United States remained Infosys' largest geography.

Despite flat headcount, Infosys delivered 2.6% sequential growth. Management attributed the growth to:

- RPP, or revenue per person, and pricing increases
- A seasonality benefit
- Approximately 40 basis points from acquisitions
- Volume increases managed through higher utilization

Because utilization was already at a peak, further volume growth would require additional hiring.

The first part of the wage increase became effective in January, and the second part was rolled out effective April. The impact was already reflected in the quarter's margin. Wage increases and higher variable pay together had a 100-basis-point impact on the quarter. Management said the timing of the next wage increase had not yet been decided.

### AI productivity, pricing, and revenue quality

Salil Parekh said Infosys was seeing productivity benefits from AI, as well as from previously implemented automation and lean initiatives. Productivity benefits are generally shared between clients and Infosys.

At a macro level, Infosys' revenue productivity and pricing were improving. Management attributed this to work that was creating greater business impact in addition to productivity improvements, allowing some of the benefits to be shared with clients. On balance, management said these factors were improving the quality of revenue.
