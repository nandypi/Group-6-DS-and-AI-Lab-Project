## Beena Parmar

What kind of productivity gains is Infosys passing on to clients?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys is not discussing the amounts passed on to clients. In client AI programs involving disparate systems, the productivity benefit is typically between 5% and 15%. Internal productivity gains are somewhat higher in certain areas. For example, Finacle has a uniform code base that enables better productivity.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

The 20-basis-point decline in operating margin included the following factors:

- 100 basis points of headwind from compensation-related factors, including the April salary hike and higher variable pay for employees.
- 30 basis points of headwind from currency.
- 20 basis points of headwind from other factors.
- 70 basis points of benefit from pricing, seasonality, productivity, and Project Maximus.
- 40 basis points of benefit from acquisition-related impairment recorded in the previous quarter, which was a one-off.
- 20 basis points of benefit from lower third-party costs.

The 2.6% growth was achieved despite third-party revenue and costs being 60 basis points lower.

## Beena Parmar

Have client conversations improved, and is tariff-related uncertainty or caution over?

## Salil Parekh

Clients are focused on enterprise AI, particularly demonstrated use cases and agent-based applications. The impact of economic changes is visible in industries including logistics, consumer packaged goods, and manufacturing.

Clients are also focused on the cost and efficiency of their own operations, including those not directly affected by economic changes. Infosys is seeing benefits from AI, and its large deals are benefiting from clients making consolidation decisions. Some open macroeconomic issues remain.

## Reshab Shaw

Is the decline in Financial Services' revenue share from approximately 28% two fiscal years ago to 27% a new normal, a change in composition, or a loss of market share?

Why has the number of active clients declined over the last two fiscal years despite revenue growth? Is this related to pricing or a shift from pyramid-based to IP-led businesses?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys is seeing strong traction in Financial Services. Among its 20 largest clients, it is the AI partner of choice for approximately half. Infosys is benefiting from consolidation decisions and is at the forefront of regulatory change and major technology and operations transformations.

The company has broad geographic and capability coverage in Financial Services and is gaining traction.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Active clients include a long tail of clients that may begin with small projects, later become larger projects, fall out, and return after several months or quarters. Active clients remain a metric to track, but management considers the growth and mining of sizable clients, particularly $50 million and $100 million clients, more important.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys has a growth-focused plan to recruit employees during the current financial year, both from colleges and laterally. Its insurance platform and Financial Services banking platform, Finacle, are areas with increasing IP content.

Some BPM work is outcome-driven without being IP-based. This differs from the rest of the company, where work is more headcount-based. Management does not see a major shift away from the current model and remains on track with its recruitment plan.

## Jas Bardia

Has AI caused revenue cannibalization or employee reshuffling? Given the lack of mega deals and the industry slowdown, has Infosys run out of steam because of macroeconomic uncertainty, client-specific issues, or AI?

## Salil Parekh

Large deals are performing well. The $3.8 billion large-deal total includes multiple deals, including one mega deal, focused on AI, transformation, and consolidation.

The economic environment remains a factor. Infosys is seeing strong activity in AI, digital transformation, cloud, and enterprise applications. Enterprise AI is contributing to productivity, while also enabling deeper analytics, assessments, and business optimization aimed at growth. These activities may create new revenue streams, and management expects the company's approach to continue supporting growth over the coming years.

## Veena Mani

Are reports of freshers being released from the Mysore campus accurate, and were they from the 2022 batch? What does this indicate about the quality of recent college graduates?

How does Infosys's hybrid interview process affect the selection of lateral hires and freshers? Is it intended to address misuse of virtual interviews?

Is Infosys considering a stricter bench policy? How does the capability quotient mentioned in the ESG report affect employee appraisals, salary hikes, internal job postings, and project opportunities? Are there onboarding delays for lateral hires?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys has a rigorous process for hiring college graduates. New hires undergo focused foundational training at the campus and must meet internal assessment standards. Each employee receives three attempts to meet those standards; employees who do not meet them after the third attempt do not continue with the company.

This process has been used for approximately 20 years to maintain the quality of service delivered to clients.

Infosys provides employees on the bench with training, learning projects, and other opportunities. Employees are deployed to client projects when they are ready. This process has been followed for a long time.
