## Kumar Rakesh

My second question was more near term. Over the last two quarters, large deal TCV has picked up, with reported large deal TCV above $3 billion. Management has also discussed vendor consolidation as a driver of deal signings. How comfortable are you with the margins of these large deals as they ramp up and begin contributing to revenue?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys does not disclose margins separately for large deals. However, it has a disciplined approach to taking on these deals. Chief Delivery Officer Dinesh and Satish are actively involved at the beginning of many large deals to understand the issues and maintain the margin profile at the start and throughout the program.

Management is comfortable with the large deals and does not see an unusual margin impact from them.

## Bryan Bergin

I wanted to ask about deal activity and average sizes. What are you seeing in smaller deal activity, and has it changed from the prior quarter? Large deal TCV has been healthy for two quarters; how are ACV levels in newly booked work trending?

## Salil Parekh

There has been no real change in smaller deal activity; it remains similar to recent quarters. The vast majority of large deals focuses on cost reduction, vendor consolidation, AI-enabled productivity, and lean automation.

Management sees no change in the ACV structure of large deals compared with the prior quarter, although it does not disclose ACV numbers separately.

## Bryan Bergin

How might the delivery mix change beyond fiscal 2026 as Infosys navigates visa changes? Subcontractor usage and offshore mix both increased during the quarter. Will these trends continue, and are there potential ceilings for offshore mix and subcontractor usage?

## Salil Parekh

Subcontracting is not viewed as a long-term lever for changing the delivery mix. Usage may move up or down during the next few phases depending on requirements.

Infosys has built localization across geographies outside India, particularly in the United States. This includes local technology capabilities, local recruiting, nearshore centers in locations such as Canada, Mexico, and other parts of South America, and offshore delivery.

The future delivery model will use a combination of local, nearshore, and offshore capabilities to maintain consistent client delivery. Management does not have a view on where the offshore ratio will end, but early client discussions indicate that demand for offshore delivery will increase over time.

## Jonathan Lee

Can you explain the step-down in utilization despite the increase in subcontractor usage? What skill or pyramid gaps are Infosys filling through subcontractors?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Subcontractor usage was approximately 11% a couple of quarters ago and has declined to 8.5%, reflecting a consistent effort to reduce it over the long term.

Quarter-to-quarter changes depend on client demand, available skills, and skill location. Subcontractors are typically used to bridge skill gaps across client projects. Management does not currently expect subcontractor usage to increase significantly from current levels.

## Jonathan Lee

How do forward-deployed engineers compare with the teams of forward-deployed engineers being scaled by software companies? Are these talent pools competing, and do forward-deployed engineers have higher costs than traditional engineers?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys has used forward-deployed engineers across its AI landscape for some time. Management did not comment on their cost or specific structure. They are different groups within the company, similar to other groups such as power programmers, and have different costs within the delivery structure. Infosys uses the appropriate cost level depending on the market in which the engineers operate.

## Satish H. C.

AI is creating a new services or software stack for clients, including new approaches to running businesses and delivering services. Because this is a different paradigm, the work involves co-creation with clients.

As clients move from proofs of concept to enterprise-scale adoption, Infosys expects greater need for forward-deployed engineers to work closely with clients and help develop this new software stack.

## Vibhor Singhal

Has the recent H-1B visa fee increase created greater short-term uncertainty, delayed deals, or raised concerns among clients? Over the longer term, could it lead to greater nearshore and offshore delivery as companies seek to avoid higher H-1B visa fees?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys has not seen any short-term change from the recent H-1B visa development.

Over the medium to long term, the delivery model will change through greater use of localization in markets outside India, including the United States. Infosys expects more work to be performed through local employees in technology hubs and centers, as well as through nearshore and offshore delivery. Management has not provided specific percentages for these changes.

## Vibhor Singhal

Would clients accept a greater shift toward nearshore and offshore delivery? Some onsite presence may be required by clients, regulators, or Infosys's own operating requirements. How easy would it be to move the relevant work to nearshore and offshore locations?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys will work jointly with each client to define the appropriate delivery model. Management believes that the company can maintain client delivery through different levers in the model. The mix will vary by client: some clients may rely more on one lever, while others may use a different combination.
