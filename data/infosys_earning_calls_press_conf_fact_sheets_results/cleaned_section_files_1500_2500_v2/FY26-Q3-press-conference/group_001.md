## Infosys Limited Press Conference Call

January 14, 2026

## Salil Parekh

Infosys reported strong Q3 performance:

- Revenue grew 0.6% sequentially and 1.7% year on year in constant currency.
- Large deals totaled $4.8 billion across 26 deals, with 57% net new.
- Adjusted operating margin was 21.2%.
- Free cash flow was $915 million.

One significant large deal was a $1.6 billion contract with the U.K. National Health Service. The deal expands Infosys' healthcare work, including the use of AI to streamline operations and improve patient care for U.K. citizens.

Infosys expanded its Topaz AI capability with Topaz Fabric, an agent services suite intended to help clients manage and implement AI agents across the enterprise. Infosys reported strong AI adoption across its client base:

- AI work with more than 90% of its 200 largest clients.
- 4,600 AI projects underway.
- More than 28 million lines of code generated by Infosys teams using AI.
- More than 500 AI agents built.
- Continued scaling of the forward-deployed engineer team.

Infosys identified six emerging AI-led value pools:

- AI engineering services
- Data for AI
- Agents for operations
- AI software development and legacy modernization
- AI deployed in physical devices
- AI trust and risk services

Management said AI adoption could create incremental opportunities while productivity benefits could compress some legacy areas. Infosys plans to provide a more comprehensive view of its AI approach at an investor day later in the quarter.

FY26 revenue growth guidance was revised to 3%-3.5% in constant currency. FY26 operating margin guidance remained unchanged at 20%-22%.

## Ritu Singh

Ritu Singh asked about the increase of 13,246 in headcount over the previous two quarters, the relationship between headcount growth and improving demand, the contribution of large deals such as the NHS contract and the pending Versent acquisition to the revised guidance, improvement in weaker sectors such as Retail, the sequential margin movement, the benefit from rupee depreciation, and Infosys' approach to reporting quantitative AI benefits.

## Salil Parekh

The headcount increase reflected management's confidence in market conditions and demand. The revised FY26 growth guidance was supported by large deals won in previous quarters and strong execution in Q3.

Financial services, Energy, Utilities, Resources, and Services showed a favorable outlook. Infosys said the way deals were developing and its position as an AI partner for its largest clients also supported the outlook for the next financial year. The guidance revision applied to the financial year ending in March.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys had previously stated that it planned to hire 20,000 freshers during the year and had onboarded approximately 18,000. Many of the new hires were still under training. Headcount growth therefore also reflected investment in building future capacity. Utilization including trainees had declined.

Jayesh Sanghrajka said operating margin expanded by 20 basis points sequentially and reached 21% for the nine-month period, the midpoint of the full-year guidance. The quarter's margin movement included:

- 40 basis points from currency.
- 50 basis points from Project Maximus, mainly through value-based selling, lean practices, and automation across multiple projects.
- Offsets from furloughs and fewer working days.
- Higher variable-pay accrual compared with the previous quarter.
- Offsets from certain one-off items.

Over the nine-month period, sales and marketing investment increased by 50 basis points year on year, and almost 1% lower utilization was absorbed in the margin. Management said the margin remained stable while Infosys invested in sales and marketing and built future capacity.

## Ritu Singh

Ritu Singh asked about the outlook for the next financial year, given the planned completion of fresher hiring and lower attrition during the quarter.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys would provide its outlook with the next year's guidance in April.

## Ritu Singh

Ritu Singh asked about the planned wage hikes and their potential margin impact.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys had completed one wage cycle, conducted in two parts in January and April. The next wage decision had not yet been made.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys was conducting AI work with more than 90% of its 200 largest clients. Its AI work included client-specific AI services and reshaping existing services through AI. For example, Infosys was using agents across several service lines to support growth or productivity.

## Mansee Dave

Mansee Dave asked about demand visibility, technology spending, and AI adoption. She asked how clients were viewing calendar-year 2026 technology spending, particularly discretionary spending versus transformation-led programs, the pace of enterprise AI adoption, and the evolution of pricing models.
