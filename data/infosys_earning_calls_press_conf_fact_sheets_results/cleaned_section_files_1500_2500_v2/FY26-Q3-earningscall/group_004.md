## Jonathan Lee

Jonathan Lee asked about the visibility and certainty of the implied fiscal Q4 revenue growth compared with prior years.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Guidance considers signed deals, deals ramping up, calendar days, working days, and other usual quarterly factors. The lower end of the guidance range includes a higher amount of uncertainty, while the higher end assumes a better macroeconomic environment.

### Subcontractor usage and utilization

Subcontractor usage depends on skill gaps, geography, and the tenure of requirements. During the quarter, some large deals and other ramping deals required Infosys to use subcontractors to fulfill staffing requirements, resulting in higher subcontractor usage.

## Vibhor Singhal

### Manufacturing and Hi-Tech demand

Vibhor Singhal asked about traction in Manufacturing amid global automotive headwinds, including slower electric-vehicle rollouts and competition. He also asked about the decline in Hi-Tech revenue, the effect of furlough seasonality, and the potential growth drivers for a business that had remained at an approximately $370 million-$380 million run rate.

## Salil Parekh

### Manufacturing

Manufacturing remains weak in automotive and in parts of the industrial sector across Europe. Infosys is seeing strength in some Manufacturing areas in the U.S. and Europe that supply capabilities for data-center build-outs.

The automotive business remains weak, although Infosys is focused on expanding its base and has successfully worked with multiple automotive companies. Growth beyond automotive may come from broader Manufacturing companies supplying capabilities related to AI and data-center activity rather than providing AI or data-center services directly.

### Hi-Tech

Hi-Tech has a mixed portfolio, with some groups of companies performing strongly and others requiring more attention. The industry continues to face cost pressure, and clients are pursuing productivity benefits. Some clients are also evaluating future sources of growth.

Companies supplying servers and related infrastructure are performing relatively well because of the AI boom. Other companies are evaluating future growth opportunities, with Infosys supporting that work. The industry continues to face constraints.

In the medium term or longer term, Hi-Tech remains an industry in which Infosys is comfortable operating. AI will affect different value pools across the industry, but Infosys must provide the services clients need in ways that help manage their cost pressures. Management did not provide a specific timeline for a recovery or acceleration.

## Vibhor Singhal

### Labor-law impact

Vibhor Singhal asked whether the exceptional item recorded for the labor-law impact represented the full one-time impact and what recurring impact the changed labor laws would have on margins.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Based on the regulation known at the time, the applicable impact was recorded in the quarter. Unless the regulation changes, Infosys does not expect further one-time impacts. The recurring impact is approximately 15 basis points on an ongoing basis.

## Keith Bachman

### Revenue per employee and productivity

Keith Bachman asked how revenue per employee could change over the next several years as AI and other technology waves affect Infosys's operating model.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

As with other technology waves, companies that are ahead of the curve can achieve higher productivity and retain some of the resulting premium. Companies that lag may experience pricing declines.

The difference is that the premium is increasingly measured through productivity. Delivering better productivity can support better pricing, while failing to deliver productivity can result in pricing pressure. Pricing models are also changing, including models based on agents and underlying platforms. It remains too early to determine how these models will affect linear or non-linear revenue.

### Client contracts and potential attrition

Keith Bachman asked about press reports concerning Daimler and whether Q4 guidance reflected contract attrition or whether investors should consider major contract attrition beyond the current period.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys's current contracts are valid through the end of December 2026. Management does not currently see any contracts within that period expiring. Infosys does not provide client-specific or project-specific details beyond that period.

## Gaurav Rateria

### AI productivity and large-deal renewals

Gaurav Rateria asked about the renewal cycle for large deals, the productivity benefits expected from AI tools in legacy services, and whether productivity-related leakage had been higher than usual.

## Salil Parekh

Large-deal renewals, as well as deals won from other providers through consolidation, generally include significant client expectations for AI-related productivity benefits. Infosys models the productivity it is currently seeing and builds a path toward those benefits because large deals typically run for three to five years. Client expectations over a five-year period can differ materially from current expectations, so the company uses current observations as the basis for estimating the benefits it can provide.

Infosys has been winning large deals at a strong pace. Across the first three quarters, it had booked more large deals than during the full previous year. Q3 deal TCV was $4.8 billion. Net-new deals included NHS. In addition to AI-related productivity benefits, Infosys is benefiting from consolidation and winning business across the market, which management believes is supporting market-share gains.
