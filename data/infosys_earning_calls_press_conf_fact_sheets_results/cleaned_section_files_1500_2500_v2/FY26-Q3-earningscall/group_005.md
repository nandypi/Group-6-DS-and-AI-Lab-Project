## Gaurav Rateria

### Question

Is visibility for CY26 better than last year because the prior year included a significant fourth-quarter decline, while the current guidance indicates a better fourth-quarter outlook? Does the stronger exit rate provide a tailwind entering FY27 compared with the entry into FY26?

## Salil Parekh

The full-year guidance range provides good traction for Q4, supported by large deals signed and the performance seen in Q3. Discretionary spending is returning in financial services. Energy, Utilities, and Financial Services are expected to look better in the next financial year than in the current financial year. Infosys also sees good growth opportunities in six AI-services value pools. Taken together, these factors provide a good view of the next year.

## Sandeep Shah

### Question

Are the average sizes of AI-led deals increasing materially year over year across sectors? Are clients becoming more comfortable with modernizing legacy applications, and could this lead to better discretionary spending on modernization services in CY26?

## Salil Parekh

AI is involved in two types of engagements: deals focused exclusively on AI and deals in which AI is pervasive across the services offered. Because AI is increasingly used across all services, it is difficult to assess deal sizes on an overall basis. Standalone AI deals are also progressing well.

Infosys is seeing legacy-application modernization across the board because AI can make the process more efficient and improve clients' returns. Whether this leads to additional discretionary spending depends on industry conditions. The trend is visible in financial services, while developments in other industries will vary.

### Question

Has the number of deals below $50 million started increasing, and could this become a continuing trend? Also, could third-party costs as a percentage of revenue remain stable or decline beyond FY26? Could the ₹165 crore profit on property, plant and equipment become a headwind in Q4?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys does not externally disclose data for deals below $50 million because it shares only large-deal information. Overall wins are looking good, and work with smaller accounts is also showing good growth.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

At the beginning of the year, Infosys expected third-party costs to be lower than the previous year. This was based on deals signed in the prior year that were ramping up and on deals in the pipeline. The outlook for the following year depends on this combination, and the company did not yet have all the details needed to provide next-year guidance.

One-off items can create either headwinds or tailwinds. They may occur in revenue or cost line items. The ₹165 crore property, plant and equipment profit was more than offset by an increase in variable pay.

## Jamie Friedman

### Question

Is the improvement in discretionary spending and financial services expected to expand into other verticals, including Energy and Resources? How is Infosys thinking about the progression from AI to agentification and the productization of its AI initiatives?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys is seeing an improvement in financial services. In Energy and Utilities, the company expects the next financial year to show growth over the current financial year, supported by its deals and its AI work with 15 of its 25 largest clients. Infosys is monitoring other industries for signs that discretionary spending is returning. Management also cited economic and U.S. GDP-growth indicators as factors that could give clients the ability to spend on technology.

Agents are already a significant focus. Infosys has built Topaz Fabric, a set of purpose-built agents that work with interfaces from multiple native AI companies and support horizontal and vertical client functions. This suite is intended to be Infosys' agent suite.

Infosys will also integrate, implement, and expand agents developed by other companies. Its knowledge of clients' technology landscapes and industry domains supports this work.

Infosys has built four small language models. Its Finacle product suite will become more AI-oriented, but the company is not currently planning large-scale foundational-model development. It plans to focus on small language models and other AI capabilities, including AI wrappers and orchestration modules that allow clients to switch between foundation models, switch between agents, and select agents. These capabilities form part of the Topaz product platform.

## Dipesh Mehta

### Question

What is the potential growth opportunity in the six AI-services value pools, where is Infosys positioned today, and how could that position evolve over the next three to five years? What early indication can management provide on the CY26 budget?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys is beginning work in many of the six AI-services value pools. At its Investor Day, the company plans to provide more detail on its work across industries and value pools, demonstrate work being conducted in its AI labs, and discuss the scale of the opportunity and its potential growth dynamics.

Infosys expects to discuss the CY26 budget during the April call, after completing the financial year. The company is currently working on the budget.

## Salil Parekh - Closing Comments

Infosys reported a strong quarter, good large deals, and an increase in guidance. Nine-month operating margin was 21%, after absorbing a 50-basis-point increase in sales costs and lower utilization as the company builds capacity for the future.

Management also highlighted a significant shift toward AI, including the six AI-services value pools and partnerships. Discretionary spending is beginning to return in financial services. Energy, Utilities, and Financial Services are expected to look better next year than this year. Management expressed confidence entering Q4 following the increase in guidance.
