## Salil Parekh

Regarding the acquisition that has already closed, the revenue estimate was known when the acquisition was announced. Its inclusion in annual estimates depends on the closing date, which could take a week, a month, or two months. Infosys is not providing guidance that assumes the acquisition will be included, but will incorporate it once the acquisition closes.

The full-year calculation discussed is approximately correct. Infosys expects growth during the year even without acquisitions and expects additional growth when acquisitions are included. AI services and large deals are expected to contribute to growth. Although there is some compression, the company is managing it and has a clear growth forecast for the year.

## Salil Parekh

The Board is actively involved in overseeing Infosys's AI work. Nandan Nilekani is also closely involved, providing vision and ideas related to AI and the Topaz Fabric initiative.

Infosys expects to continue making acquisitions and has a good pipeline. The company is targeting areas such as healthcare, where it sees opportunities to expand in an attractive market.

## Jas Bardia

What types of contracts is Infosys seeing, and how have they evolved over the past three to four years from fixed-price contracts toward outcome-based contracts? What mechanisms does the Board use to oversee Infosys's AI activities?

## Salil Parekh

The Board is highly involved in Infosys's AI work, although there is no single specific oversight mechanism. Regarding contracting, Infosys continues to use the types of contracts it has used in the past. There are now discussions about using more outcome-based arrangements because AI is transformative.

Infosys has developed models and templates with its delivery and sales leadership for structuring outcome-based contracts. Active discussions are underway, but the transition is still at an early stage. Many contracts continue to follow the historical contracting model.
