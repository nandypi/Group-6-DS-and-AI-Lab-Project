# FY26-Q4 Press Conference: Demand, Margins, AI, Acquisitions, and Workforce

## Economic Environment and Growth Guidance

Management said the economic environment had changed because of the situation with the Iran war, but observed possible paths toward stabilization. Based on discussions with clients and market participants, management saw resilience in the major economies where Infosys operates, continued investment, strong AI growth, and a strategic opportunity in AI services.

Growth guidance was based on conditions visible at the time of the conference, with management noting that the outlook could change as the year progressed.

## Margin Program and Guidance

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys expanded margins by 50 basis points in the first year of its margin program. In the current year, it maintained a 21% margin while absorbing several headwinds and investing in the business.

Sales and marketing costs increased by 40 basis points. Infosys also invested in AI-related capabilities, AI partnerships, and talent. Sanghrajka said the company was confident of delivering a 20%-22% margin in the year ahead and intended to improve margins over the medium to long term.

Expected headwinds included market competitiveness and acquisition-related costs. Acquisition-related costs were expected to affect margins by another 60-70 basis points.

## Industry Demand, AI Services, and Acquisitions

### Chandra R. Srikanth, Moneycontrol

Srikanth asked about management's confidence in Financial Services and Energy and Utilities, the status of normal and AI-related business, AI revenue, potential acquisitions of AI startups, the impact of Mythos, wage hikes, and the decline in employee headcount.

### Salil Parekh

Infosys said that, across several industry groups, it was already the AI partner of choice for many of its largest clients. In addition to Financial Services and Energy and Utilities, management cited Manufacturing and Telecommunications.

The pipeline included large programs involving:

- Building AI agents
- Legacy modernization
- Technology services combined with operations, including technology-and-operations deals

Management said these opportunities indicated that client demand among its existing client base had not changed sharply.

Infosys had completed two acquisitions and continued to evaluate additional opportunities. The acquisition process considered strategic fit, cultural fit, value fit, and integration requirements. Management said the acquisition pipeline could result in additional transactions or no transactions for several quarters.

The healthcare acquisition was intended to expand Infosys's presence in a market where it already had a good business. The insurance acquisition involved a company working with the Guidewire platform, an area Infosys wanted to expand. Management said acquisitions would continue.

## AI Revenue Disclosure

### Chandra R. Srikanth

Srikanth asked for an update on AI revenue, noting that Infosys had previously disclosed AI-related revenue as 5% of revenue.

### Salil Parekh

Parekh said Infosys was not sharing the current AI revenue figure, but confirmed that it was growing. He said the earlier disclosure at the AI Investor Day was intended to communicate that AI revenue was material and growing well. He declined to confirm whether AI revenue had reached 10% or 50% of Infosys revenue.

## Mythos and Cybersecurity

### Salil Parekh

Parekh said Mythos appeared to expose more vulnerabilities than previously thought possible, while noting that other models were also exposing vulnerabilities. He said Infosys was exploring whether this could create opportunities to help clients identify and protect against such vulnerabilities.

He characterized the discussions as very early and said Infosys was assessing whether it could build a capability to help clients protect their systems more quickly and effectively.

## Employee Headcount and Wage Hikes

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys's sequential headcount declined by 8,000 employees, while year-over-year headcount increased by 5,000 employees. Sanghrajka said quarterly seasonality should be considered when interpreting the sequential decline.

Headcount was described as a function of employee numbers, utilization, and business volumes. Softer quarterly volumes affected the demand-and-supply equation that determines net hiring, including the addition of freshers. Sanghrajka said the sequential decline should not be viewed as a continuing pattern and that full-year headcount growth was the more relevant measure.

Infosys had not yet decided the quantum or timing of wage hikes. Management said it would provide an update after making a decision.

## AI Productivity, Deal Pipeline, and Growth Areas

### Shilpa Phadnis, Times of India

Phadnis asked what portion of the deal pipeline could be affected by AI-related deflation and how Infosys would offset productivity pressure from clients.

### Salil Parekh

Parekh identified three growth areas:

1. AI services, which were growing well.
2. Large deals and the net-new portion of large deals, which would contribute to revenue expansion in the following year.
3. Expanding within clients where Infosys currently had a smaller presence but strong existing relationships.

Infosys had invested in AI and in programs targeting large companies where it had a smaller presence. Management said clients were seeking AI productivity improvements and that Infosys was participating in those efforts.

Through the Topaz Fabric, Infosys had developed an understanding of what AI-enabled productivity improvements were possible currently and what might become possible in the future. Technology-and-operations deals could apply these capabilities immediately.

Management said that, in many pipeline deals, Infosys's credibility and client trust were leading to adjacent opportunities and expansion of scope. While some work involved productivity-related compression, the broader outcome could include scope expansion or consolidation.

Management said like-for-like growth in the prior year had exceeded 3.1% after considering additional factors. The company expected to grow according to its forecast for the following year because the growth opportunities were larger than the areas of compression.
