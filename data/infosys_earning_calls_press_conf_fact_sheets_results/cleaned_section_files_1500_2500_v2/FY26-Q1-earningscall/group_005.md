## Sandeep Shah

Sandeep Shah asked whether Infosys could continue winning a higher share of large vendor-consolidation deals and whether this could support healthier deal TCV in subsequent quarters.

### Salil Parekh

Salil Parekh said Infosys does not provide a quarterly forecast for future large-deal values. The large-deal pipeline was in a good position and included mega deals. Infosys was benefiting from vendor consolidation, AI, and enterprise AI opportunities.

Sandeep Shah asked what could lead clients to resume discretionary spending, apart from an improving macroeconomic environment.

Parekh said clients remained comfortable working with Infosys on enterprise AI, cloud, data analytics, enterprise applications, and consolidation programs. Clients continued to focus substantially on cost and efficiency. Management did not provide a view on when client spending patterns would change in other areas.

Sandeep Shah asked whether Infosys could still improve EBIT margin in FY26 despite first-quarter margin being below the FY25 margin of 21.1%.

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

Jayesh Sanghrajka said only one-fourth of FY26 had elapsed and that the quarter included the impact of a compensation increase, which created a significant headwind. Margin tailwinds expected later in the year included Project Maximus and value-based selling. A reduction in third-party costs was also expected to support margins.

Mega deals and other deals ramping up could create headwinds because transition activities incur costs before generating revenue. Management's aspiration remained to improve the margin from its current level.

## Vibhor Singhal

Vibhor Singhal asked whether the unchanged outlook reflected tariff-related uncertainty and whether finalized trade agreements could lead clients to restart discretionary spending.

### Salil Parekh

Salil Parekh said clients across industries showed strong interest in using enterprise AI technology for productivity and new ways of doing business. This required greater attention to cloud, data infrastructure, and enterprise applications deployed in cloud environments.

Clients also remained focused on cost and efficiency. Infosys saw benefits from consolidation programs and believed it was well positioned through its capabilities in consolidation, enterprise AI, cloud, and related areas.

Management was assessing the year's activity based on current conditions and expected to update its view each quarter as conditions changed.

Vibhor Singhal asked whether the current AI cycle could resemble the digital and cloud adoption cycle of 2015-16, including an initial impact on existing services followed by renewed growth.

Parekh said each major technology shift affected enterprise decision-making differently, making direct comparisons difficult. Enterprise AI needed to be distinctive and work effectively with the broader technology ecosystem to have a significant impact.

Infosys was seeing substantial client interest in enterprise AI. Clients required foundational capabilities in areas such as cloud and data, where Infosys had capabilities, in addition to enterprise AI services. Parekh said Infosys was prepared for the continued development of the AI cycle.

The enterprise technology landscape was larger and more complex than it had been a decade earlier, which meant more areas could require change. Management did not provide a specific view on the timing or scale of the current AI cycle.

## Apurva Prasad

Apurva Prasad asked whether the outlook for the remainder of FY26 primarily reflected macroeconomic and client technology-spending uncertainty or the structural effect of AI-related productivity benefits.

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

Sanghrajka said the outlook primarily reflected macroeconomic uncertainty. The operating environment had not improved from the beginning of the year. Tariff-related and geopolitical uncertainties continued, and client behavior had not changed.

Many clients remained in a wait-and-watch mode regarding discretionary spending. The environment had not changed materially during the seasonally strong period of the business.

Apurva Prasad asked whether AI-related productivity effects varied by geography or industry.

### Salil Parekh

Parekh said Infosys was seeing good AI adoption across multiple areas rather than in one specific geography or industry. In financial services, half of Infosys's largest clients had made Infosys their AI strategic partner, which he described as a positional advantage for the company.
