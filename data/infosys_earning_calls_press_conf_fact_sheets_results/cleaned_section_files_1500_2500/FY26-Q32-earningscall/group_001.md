## Infosys Limited Q2 FY26 Earnings Conference Call

October 16, 2025

## Salil Parekh

Infosys delivered a strong Q2 FY26 performance, with increased market share gains. Revenue grew 2.2% sequentially and 2.9% year-on-year in constant-currency terms. Four of the five large industry verticals and three of the four geographies grew year-on-year in constant-currency terms.

Operating margin expanded by 20 basis points sequentially. Free cash flow was $1.1 billion. Large-deal TCV was $3.1 billion, with 67% net new. Infosys also announced a $1.6 billion mega deal after the quarter ended and before the results announcement.

Infosys added 8,000 employees during the quarter. Client discussions continued to show strong focus on deploying AI for enterprise growth and cost efficiency. The company is scaling its team of forward-deployed engineers.

Examples of client work included:

- Partnering with an apparel company using generative AI and AI Ops technologies to modernize core operations, simplify IT, and increase value from data.
- Infusing advanced intelligence across a telecom client’s operations to accelerate innovation and improve digital customer experiences.

Infosys announced a joint venture involving Versent in Australia.

Following the Q2 performance, Infosys revised its FY26 revenue growth guidance to 2%-3% in constant-currency terms. Operating margin guidance remained unchanged at 20%-22%.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys reported another quarter of broad-based performance despite an uncertain environment. Growth momentum continued for the second consecutive quarter, accompanied by higher margins and continued investments in technology, people, and the sales engine.

Key Q2 and H1 FY26 results:

1. Quarterly revenue crossed $5 billion in Q2 FY26, while H1 revenue crossed $10 billion.
2. Q2 revenue grew 2.2% sequentially in constant-currency terms, including a 20-basis-point contribution from acquisitions.
3. Q1 revenue had grown 2.6% sequentially; H1 revenue growth was therefore 3.3%.
4. Volumes remained soft, with most revenue growth driven by increased realization.
5. Financial Services and Manufacturing each grew more than 5% year-on-year in constant-currency terms in both Q2 and H1.
6. Europe grew more than 5% year-on-year in constant-currency terms.
7. H1 gross margin was 30.8%, flat year-on-year after absorbing compensation headwinds and reflecting progress under Project Maximus.
8. Q2 operating margin expanded by 20 basis points sequentially to 21.0%. H1 operating margin was 20.9%, compared with 21.1% in H1 FY25.
9. Sales and marketing costs increased 12.8% year-on-year in H1 as Infosys continued investing in growth.
10. Utilization excluding trainees remained stable at 85%, within the company’s comfort range.
11. Onsite mix decreased by 40 basis points during Q2 and by 60 basis points during H1.
12. Infosys hired more than 12,000 freshers during the first six months of FY26. Total headcount reached 332,000, an increase of more than 8,000 during Q2. Attrition was 14.3%.
13. DSO decreased by 2 days year-on-year to 71 days. DSO including net unbilled revenue decreased by 5 days to 87 days.
14. Q2 free cash flow was $1.1 billion, equal to 131% of net profit and above 100% for the sixth consecutive quarter, supported by tax refunds. H1 free-cash-flow conversion was 120%.
15. Q2 large-deal TCV was $3.1 billion, with 67% net new. H1 deal wins were $6.9 billion, with net-new mix above 60%. These figures excluded the mega deal announced during the week with NHS.
16. Q2 EPS increased 13% year-on-year in rupee terms to Rs. 17.6.

### Q2 FY26 operating margin movement

Q2 operating margin was 21.0%, an increase of 20 basis points sequentially.

- Tailwinds:
  - 60 basis points from currency movement.
  - 30 basis points from Project Maximus, including increased revenue per person from value-based selling and lean automation.
- Offsets:
  - 70 basis points from higher post-sale customer support and other expenses.
  - The Project Maximus benefit was partly offset by higher subcontractor costs and lower onsite utilization.

Consolidated cash and investments were $6.2 billion at the end of the quarter. Yield on the cash balance was 6.98%, and return on equity was 29.1%.

### Capital allocation and visa dependence

In line with its capital allocation policy, Infosys announced an Rs. 18,000 crore buyback through the tender route at Rs. 1,800 per share. The buyback was expected to be completed in Q3, subject to shareholder approval.

The Board approved an interim dividend of Rs. 23 per share, 9.5% higher than the FY25 interim dividend.

Infosys has taken steps to reduce dependence on U.S. work visas, particularly H-1Bs. These steps include reducing onsite mix, increasing near-shoring and local hiring, expanding university partnerships, and creating local delivery hubs. The company has several delivery centers across the United States that use local talent and focus on artificial intelligence, machine learning, cloud computing, big data, and user experience design.

### Large deals

Infosys signed 23 large deals during Q2:

- Financial Services: 6
- Manufacturing: 4
- Communications: 4
- Retail: 4
- EURS: 3
- Hi-Tech: 1
- Other: 1

By region, 14 deals were signed in the Americas, 7 in Europe, 1 in the rest of the world, and 1 in India.

## Coming to verticals:

### Financial Services

Clients were actively planning modernization and AI-driven initiatives focused on cost efficiency, improved customer experience, and business transformation. Momentum was strong in mortgages, capital markets, commercial banking, and wealth management.

Macroeconomic uncertainty and volatility continued to affect spending, although mortgage activity showed some acceleration following recent interest-rate reductions. The pipeline and signing environment remained strong, reflected in six large deals during the quarter.

Banks have spent significantly on AI infrastructure. Many initiatives are progressing from proofs of concept to full-scale projects, with notable traction in Agentic AI.

### Manufacturing

Trade and macroeconomic uncertainties continued to pressure discretionary spending, particularly in the automotive sector. Infosys supported clients with digital initiatives, application rationalization, and infrastructure-footprint rationalization.

The company continued using AI and automation to increase productivity and offset pricing deflation. In aerospace, opportunities included helping clients address supply-chain bottlenecks through technology and products.

More than 90% of Q2 large-deal TCV in Manufacturing was net new, which management said should support future growth.

### EURS

Clients in EURS maintained a strong focus on cost reduction, operational efficiency, and cash preservation, creating opportunities for vendor consolidation.

Large-scale generative AI deployments remained limited in Resources, but Agentic AI adoption was increasing in technology operations to reduce costs. The rapid construction of data centers was also creating opportunities with utility companies in renewable integration, grid modernization, and AI-driven optimization.

Year-on-year growth was affected by significantly higher third-party revenues in Q2 FY25.

### Retail

Retail clients remained cautious because of tariff-related uncertainty. Across geographies, clients focused on AI, cloud, estate modernization, de-risking, and cost reduction.

There was increasing urgency to improve operating-model productivity and offset inflationary pressures. Deal pipelines remained strong, but decision cycles were elongated.

Infosys continued leveraging its Topaz and AI Next platform capabilities in digital marketing, predictive analytics, and real-time insights to improve customer and employee experiences.

### Communications

Communications continued to face growth headwinds and high operating-expense pressure. Discretionary spending remained subdued, with investment prioritized toward AI, automation, and consumer experience.

Global capability centers were becoming key buying centers, creating opportunities for IT companies to support their transition. Lower interest rates provided cautious optimism, while geopolitical tensions and tariff risks added uncertainty.

### Hi-Tech

Hi-Tech clients remained focused on cost reduction, leading to budget cuts and program closures. Opportunities were emerging in areas such as semiconductors, with a strong focus on generative AI.

### H2 FY26 outlook

H1 performance reflected resilience in the business model and execution capabilities. For H2, Infosys expected seasonal factors to affect growth, including fewer working days, furloughs, and the onset of the new calendar year.

FY26 revenue growth guidance was revised to 2%-3% in constant-currency terms. This guidance did not include revenue from the joint venture with Telstra, which Infosys expected to close later in the year.

Operating margin guidance remained at 20%-22%.
