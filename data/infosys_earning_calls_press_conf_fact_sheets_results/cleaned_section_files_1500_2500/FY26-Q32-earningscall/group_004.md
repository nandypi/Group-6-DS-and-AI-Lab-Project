## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Before COVID, Infosys was close to 30% onsite and 70% offshore. The delivery mix historically changed by approximately 20-30 basis points each year. After COVID, the mix changed by 3%-4% or more in a year. When delivery constraints arise, Infosys and its clients work together to reach a workable model.

Increasing nearshoring and offshoring could mathematically support higher margins, but the outcome depends on the extent of offshoring, nearshoring, and local hiring. Management described this as a balanced approach.

The 70-basis-point impact from higher post-sale customer support was a quarter-on-quarter margin-walk impact. The prior quarter had benefited from a lower-than-normal level; the current quarter was at a normalized level.

Volumes were softer, while a significant portion of growth came from RPP expansion. This reflected higher working days and calendar days during the quarter, as well as Project Maximus, which uses various levers to drive effective pricing increases.

The Versent JV had not yet closed because it was pending regulatory approvals. Management expected closure during the year but did not know the exact timeline. The transaction was not included in guidance. Versent's revenue in the previous year was approximately Australian $210 million. Infosys does not disclose acquisition margins.

Furloughs were not expected to be significantly different from the previous year. Small deals remained similar to the previous year, while the overall pipeline continued to be strong.

Infosys's guidance is based on various internal models and is intended to reduce information asymmetry between the company and investors. The lower end of guidance includes an elevated level of uncertainty, while the upper end assumes a stable environment.

The second half is seasonally softer because of fewer working days, fewer calendar days, and a higher impact from furloughs. The first half was stronger than that of many peers, which also affects the comparison between the second half and the first half.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys is already seeing projects where AI agents work alongside people on the project. This will become part of the evolving delivery model. Visa-related changes and AI-enabled delivery are separate trends that will develop together over time and influence delivery ratios.

Infosys sees two types of AI opportunities:

- Growth opportunities, such as using AI in sales and marketing.
- Productivity and efficiency opportunities across client processes and activities.

Given the current economic environment and focus on cost reduction, much of the initial AI-related work is focused on efficiency. Modernization discussions can represent growth opportunities when clients are adopting capabilities they did not previously have. Management did not provide a view on whether the deflationary impact of AI or the volume-growth opportunity would be larger, but said the company sees significant growth opportunities from AI.

Many clients remain focused on cost control. Demand includes consolidation and non-AI automation aimed at reducing costs, and these areas are contributing to large deals. As AI approaches demonstrate business-growth benefits, demand for those opportunities may increase where the economic environment supports them.

It is difficult to predict next year's IT services growth. Management's historical experience is that improving macroeconomic conditions generally support stronger technology spending, but it did not provide a specific outlook.
