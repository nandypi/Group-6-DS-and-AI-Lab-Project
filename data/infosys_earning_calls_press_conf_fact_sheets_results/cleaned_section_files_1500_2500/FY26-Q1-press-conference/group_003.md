## Salil Parekh

Infosys has a flexible approach to where employees work. The number of employees on campus has increased every quarter for the last several quarters, while the company continues to maintain flexibility at the overall employee level.

On ESG metrics covering digital versus capabilities, these measures are published for ESG reporting and are not used as internal metrics for IJPs. Infosys has not stopped or deferred lateral hiring.

## Avik Das

Avik Das asked about the factors supporting Manufacturing growth, the outlook for North America and Financial Services, and potential margin pressure despite unchanged margin guidance.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys sees very good traction across all Financial Services businesses and geographies. North America, the company's largest market, remains in a strong position across industries despite changes in the economic environment.

Outside Logistics and Consumer Products, Infosys sees good activity in Energy, Utilities, and parts of Telecommunications. The company also sees strong traction for enterprise AI and consolidation wins in large deals originating from North America.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Some parts of Manufacturing, particularly automotive, industrial, and Europe, remain soft. Manufacturing performance benefited from consolidation and large deals won previously, but client-spending concerns continue in the identified areas.

The quarter's 20-basis-point margin decline reflected the following factors:

- 100 basis points from compensation and variable pay
- 30 basis points from currency
- 20 basis points from sales investments
- Benefits from Project Maximus
- 70 basis points from pricing increases and seasonality
- A 40-basis-point one-off benefit in the prior quarter
- 20 basis points from lower third-party costs

Project Maximus continues to run. Infosys delivered 50 basis points of margin expansion in the prior year despite multiple headwinds and expects Project Maximus to continue contributing. This may be offset by lower growth, with current guidance of 1% to 3%, as well as fixed costs in growth areas.

Other margin factors include the full-year impact of compensation changes, the ramp-up of large deals, and transition costs incurred before associated revenue begins. Infosys continues to aspire to increase margins from current levels.

## Sanjana B.

Sanjana B. asked whether geopolitical uncertainty had changed project ramp-ups or ramp-downs, whether third-party costs and revenues would be lower in FY26 than FY25, and whether AI and automation would affect hiring.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys has not seen changes in specific clients or projects due to shifts in the economic outlook in the United States and Europe. The main effects are broader: increased emphasis on cost and efficiency, industry-level pressure in Logistics, Consumer Products, and parts of Manufacturing such as automotive, and increased consolidation discussions.

Infosys has a recruitment plan for FY26 covering both college and lateral hiring. AI is producing productivity benefits and increasing client work, particularly in enterprise AI. Cloud transitions and data and analytics foundations are also accelerating because they are fundamental to implementing enterprise AI for clients. These programs, together with consolidation and cost-efficiency work, are supporting growth activity, and Infosys plans to continue hiring during the year.

## Ayanti Bera

Ayanti Bera asked why Infosys increased the lower end of its revenue growth guidance.

## Infosys

The previous guidance assumed heightened uncertainty at the lower end and a steady to marginally improving environment at the upper end. After one quarter of strong performance and strong deal wins, Infosys increased the lower end of its revenue growth guidance from 0% to 1%.

The upper end remains unchanged because of possible uncertainty related to tariffs and the broader macroeconomic environment. The quarter's performance and deal wins provide greater confidence in the lower end of the range.
