## Salil Parekh

Infosys sees a good demand outlook, supported by a healthy large-deals pipeline. The company is seeing strong AI-related activity in Financial Services and Energy, Resources & Utilities, with an outlook extending beyond the current financial year into the next financial year.

In Financial Services, Infosys is seeing discretionary spending and good traction across the market. Other industries and segments have not yet shown the same level of improvement.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

New technologies are leading to the development of new pricing models. Infosys is testing models including outcome-based pricing and pricing specific to AI agents. The industry is still evaluating how these pricing models will evolve.

## Shristi Achar

The questions concerned the decline in operating margins, the sequential decline in revenue contribution from the top five and top ten clients, and developments relating to H-1B visas.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Reported margins were affected by labor-code charges. Excluding the labor-code impact, adjusted margins expanded by 20 basis points sequentially. Full-year adjusted margins remained at 21%, similar to the previous year.

The company absorbed the impact of approximately 50 basis points of investment in sales and marketing and the impact of lower utilization associated with building capacity for future demand.

The sequential change in client contribution is affected by seasonality. Q3 typically includes furloughs, which can have a larger impact on larger clients when they occur. Infosys does not see a significant change in year-on-year client metrics.

## Salil Parekh

No Infosys employee has been apprehended by any U.S. authority. A few months earlier, one Infosys employee was denied entry into the United States and sent back to India.

## Chandra Srikanth

The questions concerned whether Infosys was contesting the employee's denial of entry and whether the company expected increased M&A activity following recent large acquisitions in the sector.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys has recently completed acquisitions in cyber, consulting, and energy services and will continue to consider similar opportunities. The company has a healthy pipeline of potential companies and ongoing discussions, supported by its balance sheet.

M&A evaluations include new geographies and service areas where Infosys can expand or deepen its capabilities.

## Chandra Srikanth

According to the fact sheet, Infosys incurred ₹1,289 crores related to labor codes. Has the full impact been absorbed, or will it be staggered?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

All labor-code costs accrued through the end of the quarter have been recorded in the books. The impact covers multiple areas, including gratuity and other wage-related items.

The labor code is expected to have an ongoing annual impact of approximately 15 basis points.

## Haripriya Suresh

The questions concerned future H-1B applications, U.S. hiring and deployment, Retail demand, and the CEO succession plan.

## Salil Parekh

The majority of Infosys employees in the United States do not require visas. The company is continuing its deployment and delivery model using a mix of employees working in the United States and India. There has been no change to this approach, and future requirements will be evaluated as they arise.

In Retail, some areas are showing positive trends, while some clients remain subject to cost containment. Infosys is working to convert its growing Retail pipeline into revenue growth.

No comment was provided on the CEO succession plan.

## Avik Das

The questions concerned growth within Financial Services, the constant-currency decline in North America, and the long-term margin implications of smaller or fewer large deals as AI-led deals increase.

## Salil Parekh

Financial Services is showing traction across most subverticals, including retail banks, mid-market banks, payments, and mortgages. Demand is strong overall, although strength varies by subvertical.

Large Financial Services clients are adopting AI across a broad range of activities. Infosys recently announced a partnership with Cognition and is working with Cognition jointly at financial-services companies.

The North America performance reflects a mix of industries and client engagements. Energy, Utilities, and Financial Services remain strong, while some other verticals are recovering but have not yet fully improved.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys signed large deals with a total contract value of $4.8 billion during the quarter. On a nine-month basis, large-deal signings were higher than in the prior year.

AI is increasing productivity expectations, while clients are also structuring deals around cost optimization and cost takeout. These elements are often bundled within the same deals.

Large deals typically carry slightly lower margins than the company average. At the portfolio level, this can be offset by new work that is undertaken at better margins. Infosys has not observed a change in this trend.
