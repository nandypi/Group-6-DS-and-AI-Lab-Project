## Sanjana B.

Sanjana B. asked about the recovery and growth in Manufacturing and Europe, client technology budgets and discretionary spending for calendar year 2026, the rationale for raising guidance despite seasonality and uncertainty, and the Cognition AI partnership.

**Salil Parekh:** Europe has been performing well for several quarters, while Manufacturing has seen strong activity across the board. Parts of the Manufacturing client base are benefiting significantly from AI growth, including companies involved in power solutions, engine capacity, and generating capacity. Infosys is also seeing traction in Manufacturing engineering services.

On discretionary spending, Financial Services has generated a good set of deals, and Infosys expects better outcomes in the next financial year than in the current financial year, supported by AI traction in the industry. Energy and Utilities is also seeing a good set of deals across the vertical. Infosys is not seeing deterioration in other industries.

The broader macro environment includes expectations of possible interest-rate cuts, particularly in the U.S. Infosys is also expanding programs with smaller sets of clients, which are growing well. Management expects these factors to support growth into the next year. AI opportunities across six areas are expected to support growth over several years.

Regarding Cognition, Parekh said the partnership is not intended to fill a specific portfolio gap. Cognition has built an agent for software development. Infosys is working with clients and Cognition to build agent capacity and enable the agents to operate in client environments. Infosys contributes detailed knowledge of clients' technology landscapes and industry constraints and opportunities, which is combined with Cognition's software agent capabilities.

## Jas Bardia

Jas Bardia asked where AI software engineers would be used, how they could affect delivery and billing, and what the implications could be for hiring from FY27 onward.

**Salil Parekh:** Over time, AI software engineers and agents are expected to be used across essentially every industry and client, depending on the client's technology landscape and objectives. In areas such as legacy modernization, combining software agents with Infosys' expertise and client knowledge can materially improve project economics and enable projects that previously did not take place.

AI agents will sometimes change how existing work is performed, but management also expects them to create incremental demand for work that was previously not undertaken. Infosys therefore expects to continue hiring. The company had completed 18,000 campus hires at that point in the year and planned to complete 20,000 campus hires. Parekh said Infosys expects to continue hiring in a similar range for the following year because these represent new areas of demand. Infosys employees and software agents will work together to improve client economics.

On billing, the value created will drive billing. Some work will continue to use traditional billing approaches, while billing models may evolve as the AI market develops. Parekh said there was no immediate change to billing.

## Poulomi Chatterjee

Poulomi Chatterjee asked about Infosys' approach to AI-related acquisitions and partnerships and about recruiting and compensating specialized AI talent.

**Salil Parekh:** There are currently relatively few AI services companies that Infosys considers acquisition candidates. Infosys is partnering with companies that build AI agents, models, foundational tools, and other AI capabilities. The company will consider acquisitions as larger AI services companies emerge. Infosys is evaluating some acquisition opportunities, including opportunities involving businesses with capabilities beyond AI services.

Infosys has launched a structure for highly skilled software engineers working in AI. These specialized engineers will have different and substantially higher compensation levels. Parekh said the AI workforce will include different types of employees working jointly with AI agents and with different levels of training. Infosys intends to remain competitive in recruiting specialized AI talent.

## Uma Kannan

Uma Kannan asked about progress on the AI-first GCC model, the potential for additional AI-native partnerships, and whether Infosys planned to change its office-hours or hybrid-work policy.

**Salil Parekh:** Infosys has launched an AI-specific GCC approach and has significant client activity in this area. The company is already working with some clients on AI-specific capability building for GCCs, with several additional opportunities in the pipeline. This activity is in addition to Infosys' regular GCC work.

Infosys expects to continue forming partnerships with smaller companies that have capabilities in foundation models, coding, agent development, and customer service. These are areas of strong client interest, and Infosys expects to make further strategic partnership announcements.

Infosys is not changing its current approach to hybrid work. The company will remain flexible in how employees interact with Infosys and its clients.

## Padmini Dhruvaraj

Padmini Dhruvaraj asked whether the labor code could affect profit margins and appraisals and whether a proposed U.S. credit-card interest-rate cap could affect Infosys.

**Salil Parekh:** The proposed U.S. credit-card interest-rate cap is an issue for the U.S. banking system to implement. Infosys supports large-bank clients as they respond to regulatory changes and will continue providing assistance where required. There will be no change to Infosys' appraisal approach.

**Jayesh Sanghrajka:** The labor-code impact through the end of December had already been recognized in the financial statements. This was a one-time impact related to the regulatory change and employees' years of service. There will also be an ongoing impact from the changed wage code, recognized as implementation proceeds. The ongoing impact is approximately 15 basis points annually.
