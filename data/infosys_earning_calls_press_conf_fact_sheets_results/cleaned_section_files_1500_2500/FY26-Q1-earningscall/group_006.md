## Ashwin Mehta

Hi. Thanks for the opportunity. Two questions. First, regarding depreciation and amortization declining to almost 50 bps, what was the driver? Second, SG&A increased by almost 90 bps this quarter. Was this mainly due to sales additions, or were there one-off events that caused a material increase?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

On depreciation and amortization, last quarter included a one-off amortization of intangibles related to one of our acquisitions, which affected the quarter-on-quarter comparison by 40 basis points. The absence of that charge this quarter accounts for the 40 bps delta. The balance is partly due to the currency impact.

The SG&A increase reflects multiple factors:

- Compensation increases made in Q1.
- Variable pay.
- Hiring in sales and marketing to improve the growth trajectory, which was included as a 20 bps sales investment in the margin walk.
- Brand-building investments and events during the quarter.

## Abhishek Pathak

Regarding the inorganic contribution, is the 40 bps impact entirely from the acquisitions consolidated this quarter? If there is residual impact from in-tech, the full-year inorganic contribution would be slightly higher.

There was also commentary that discretionary spending is being funded entirely by savings generated by AI. Is this likely to become a structural trend, with clients expecting providers to continue self-funding discretionary initiatives through these gains? Or is there likely to be a more structural demand recovery over the next 12 to 18 months, requiring clients to invest significantly in their data and technology stacks for modernization?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

The 40 basis points I referred to is sequential. The 2.6% figure includes 40 basis points attributable to acquisitions made in this quarter: MRE and The Missing Link in Australia.

In-tech was included for approximately 9 out of the 12 months of the previous year. Therefore, its full-year impact is not significant. Including 3.5 months of in-tech and 11 months of MRE and The Missing Link results in a similar impact on a full-year basis.

## Keith Bachman

Headcount, including software professionals, was relatively flat quarter-on-quarter. How does management expect headcount to trend through the year?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Utilization increased by 30 basis points this quarter, which helped. Part of the growth also came from pricing increases, including the effects of seasonality in the business.

Going forward, volume growth, given that the company is operating at peak headcount, will require additional capacity through subcontractors or Infosys employees.

## Keith Bachman

Could Infosys break the cycle of growing headcount faster than effort, with AI providing additional leverage? How might the delivery model change over the next year, particularly the balance between an FTE-based model and success-based or fixed-price contracts? Could AI capabilities enable or cause a change in the delivery model?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

The delivery model is unlikely to change over the next couple of quarters. Over a longer period, AI may contribute to the emergence of new pricing models, including outcome-based, pod-based, or studio-based pricing models. The overall model is not expected to change within the next year; the change is expected to occur gradually.

## Salil Parekh

It has been a strong quarter, with strong growth, large deals, and a focus on enterprise AI consolidation, as well as cloud and data work. Management views this as differentiated performance that is helping position Infosys in a leadership area and expects a good remainder of financial year 2026.
