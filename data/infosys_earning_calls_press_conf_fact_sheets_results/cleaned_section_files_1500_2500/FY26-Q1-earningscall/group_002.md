Leading to a 20 basis point drop in operating margin sequentially.

ETR for the quarter was 28.9%. Management expects the effective ETR rate for FY26 to be in the range of 29% to 30%.

While Q1 was steady, the business environment remains uncertain due to the lack of resolution on tariffs and the geopolitical situation. Clients continue to be cautious in discretionary spending decisions, resulting in delayed decision-making.

Near-term visibility remains good, and management expects a stronger H1 compared with H2 because of normal seasonality.

Coming to verticals:

Financial Services saw good momentum in the quarter in the U.S., with capital markets, commercial banking, and wealth management presenting significant transformation opportunities. Agentic AI is playing a pivotal role in areas such as KYC, onboarding, and portfolio management. Infosys is now the preferred AI partner for 10 of its top 20 Financial Services clients, with many initiatives moving from proof of concept to production, especially in Agentic AI.

Infosys is partnering with GCCs on both setup and growth-led deals. The pipeline remains strong for vendor consolidation, cost optimization, and simplification, although clients remain cautious about decision-making in a volatile environment.

The Manufacturing segment continues to face challenges in automotive, industrial, and Europe, with decision-making delays and soft discretionary spending. Clients are re-evaluating their supply chains because of tariff uncertainty, while Infosys is helping them leverage technology across the end-to-end lifecycle from design to manufacturing to sales.

The pipeline remains healthy, with a focus on cost takeout opportunities. Infosys won a large Q1 deal to help a client set up a GCC. In automotive, Infosys is helping clients rationalize their footprints; in industrial, it is helping clients with cost optimization.

EURS vertical outlook remains mixed because of economic uncertainties. The pipeline for large and mega deals remains strong. Investments in Industry Cloud, Energy Transition, and AI-driven operational efficiency are supporting growth and differentiation in large deals.

In Energy, high cost pressures caused by oil price volatility are prompting clients to consolidate vendors for savings. In Utilities, advances in renewable energy, smart grid technology, and sustainability regulations are reshaping the market. In Services, clients remain cautious about spending across both capex and opex.

In Retail, uncertainty around tariffs has led to muted spending in large geographies, supply chain impacts, and procurement disruptions. Budgets remain tight and decision cycles have lengthened. Clients have slowed discretionary spending, although the pipeline remains strong.

Clients continue to engage Infosys as a trusted partner for AI-first, outsourcing, and transformation deals across IT and BPM services. Increased interest in AI is resulting in budget reallocation, with discretionary spending expected to be self-funded through AI-led productivity benefits. Sector deals continue to use Topaz and AI Next platform capabilities.

Communications is facing growth challenges and increased opex pressures amid a volatile macroeconomic and political landscape. Clients are focusing on cost takeouts and vendor consolidation. There is a strong focus on AI and customization to monetize 5G use cases, although ROI concerns are delaying newer investments.

OEMs are targeting profitable growth and exploring multiple levers, including tighter and reduced IT budgets and the use of AI and automation. Infosys growth is being led by the ramp-up of previously won large deals.

Clients in Hi-Tech remain cautious because of macroeconomic headwinds and geopolitical tensions, resulting in cost pressures and budget cuts. Discretionary programs are paused because of significant investments in Gen AI GPUs and AI.

Driven by Q1 performance and its assessment of the remainder of the year, management revised FY26 revenue guidance to 1% to 3% in constant currency terms. This continues to assume a reduction in third-party revenues versus FY25 based on existing deals and new deals in the pipeline. FY26 operating margin guidance is 20% to 22%.

Management will continue to monitor the economic environment and its impact on client budgets and reassess guidance as the year progresses.
