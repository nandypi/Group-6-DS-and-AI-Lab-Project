## Abhishek Kumar

Infosys is among the companies expecting H2 to be weaker than H1. Is this view driven by seasonality, or by large deals in sectors such as Communication reaching a steady state and providing less visibility for second-half ramp-ups?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Seasonality also depends on H1 performance. If H1 is in line with expectations, usual seasonality applies; if H1 faces higher pressure, expectations for H2 improve. Infosys had a strong Q1 compared with other market results, supporting the expectation of usual seasonality.

## Bryan Bergin

North America was up slightly while Europe was strong. Does Infosys have visibility to improved growth in North America?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

North America remains Infosys's largest geography and an important part of the business. Opportunities are visible in pockets, particularly Financial Services. Manufacturing, Retail, and some other geographic pockets remain challenging.

Of the large deals signed during the quarter, 20 came from North America, 6 from Europe, and 2 from the rest of the world. Infosys sees opportunities in North America from large deals, cost takeout, and vendor consolidation.

### Smaller deals and pipeline

Infosys does not regularly comment on small deals. It previously highlighted a quarter with heightened small-deal activity because it was relevant from an investor perspective. The overall pipeline remains strong, including the large-deal pipeline.

Large deal TCV was $3.8 billion, up 44% sequentially, with 55% net-new business.

## Jonathan Lee

Does the midpoint of the outlook assume a slight deterioration in the demand environment?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys builds multiple models producing multiple ends of the guidance range; the midpoint is an outcome rather than a specific modeled scenario. The lower end includes higher uncertainty from current conditions, while the upper end assumes a stable environment. Multiple models produce the points between the two ends.

## Jonathan Lee

What changes did Infosys see in client demand from April through June, and did those trends continue into July?

## Salil Parekh

There is substantial client interest in AI, including agents, enterprise AI platforms, and small language models. This interest is translating into project work within larger programs.

Infosys is also seeing interest in vendor consolidation, cost, and efficiency. Other areas of demand include:

- Foundations of enterprise AI, including cloud, data, and analytics.
- Newer SaaS data platforms.
- Enterprise applications, particularly migrations to newer generations of SaaS platforms at enterprise scale.

The economic environment is creating constraints in logistics, consumer products, and parts of manufacturing and automotive. Overall, demand is mixed.

## Surendra Goyal

Was the slight lowering of the upper end of organic guidance due to a more conservative view of the environment, or to observed business factors such as slower ramps or declining discretionary spending?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

At the beginning of Q1, Infosys said the upper end of guidance assumed a slightly improving environment. After one quarter and with better visibility into Q2, the company does not see the environment changing significantly. The upper end of the guidance now assumes a steady environment.

Because H1 is stronger than H2 for Infosys, once the stronger part of the period has passed, an uncertain environment limits the ability to raise the upper end of guidance.

The underlying data points include client decision-making behavior and discretionary spending across accounts. These inputs are incorporated into the company's ground-up models.

## Rishi Jhunjhunwala

The wage-hike impact over the past two quarters was nearly 240 basis points. What was the rationale for the wage hikes, and should investors expect any further action in FY26?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

The wage hike was phased in two stages. A large part of the organization, up to middle-level employees, received the hike in January; the remaining employees received it effective April 1.

The 100-basis-point impact called out for the quarter reflects both wage hikes and higher variable pay. Infosys has not separately disclosed the contribution of each. The wage-hike percentages are broadly similar to those implemented in earlier years.

The timing of the next wage hike has not been decided. Factors considered include the market scenario, inflation, and peer practices.

## Rishi Jhunjhunwala

Do vendor-consolidation and GCC deals require different upfront investments on the P&L or balance sheet compared with large deals completed several years ago?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys continues to expect free cash flow conversion of 100% or more of net profit. The company has had five strong quarters of cash generation and expects that trend to continue for the rest of the year.

These deals are not expected to materially affect the balance sheet or cash flow. Infosys expects them to have the regular contours of its other deals and does not view them as significantly different from that perspective.
