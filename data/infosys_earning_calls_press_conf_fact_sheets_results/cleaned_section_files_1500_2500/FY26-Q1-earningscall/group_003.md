## Ankur Rudra

### FY26 Revenue-Growth Guidance and Macroeconomic Conditions

Ankur Rudra asked why the strong Q1 year-over-year constant-currency growth trajectory did not lead to a higher FY26 guidance midpoint or upper end.

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

At the beginning of FY26, the lower end of guidance reflected heightened uncertainty, while the upper end assumed a steady to improving environment. Although Q1 was strong, the underlying environment has not materially changed, and Q2 has not shown significant improvement.

Tariffs remain elevated, geopolitical conditions have not changed materially, and Q1 and Q2 together represent the strongest seasonal part of the year. The lower end of current guidance assumes continuing or elevated uncertainty, while the upper end assumes a steady environment based on conditions currently visible.

### Enterprise AI, Pricing, Productivity, and Vendor Consolidation

Ankur Rudra asked whether enterprise AI engagements create pricing or margin trade-offs, whether AI productivity benefits are incorporated into renewals, and whether AI-driven vendor consolidation could increase vendor replaceability.

### Salil Parekh

Enterprise AI engagements include both productivity benefits and new revenue opportunities. Productivity gains are typically shared between clients and Infosys. Clients may request these initiatives, or Infosys may introduce them to improve efficiency.

Enterprise AI work can also create opportunities to support clients in additional enterprise AI and other service areas.

Regarding vendor consolidation, Infosys has benefited in large deals where clients were dissatisfied with vendors' AI solutions, delivery quality, or the capabilities of smaller providers. Infosys believes its AI innovation, delivery track record, and scale across multiple services support its position with large clients. Vendor consolidation is not viewed as inherently making competition easier or more difficult.

## Kumar Rakesh

### Guidance Assumptions and Third-Party Costs

Kumar Rakesh asked whether the revision to the upper end of organic-growth guidance reflected changes in macroeconomic assumptions rather than changes in deal ramp-up, third-party costs, or operational issues.

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

The lower third-party-cost assumption had already been identified at the beginning of the year and had not changed. The upper end of guidance had assumed a steady to marginally improving environment, but the environment had not improved in Q1 and approximately one month of Q2 had passed.

Tariff and geopolitical challenges continue. Clients remain in a wait-and-watch mode regarding discretionary spending, deal-signing cycles remain elongated, and these factors are reflected in the current guidance.

Q1 third-party costs were flattish year over year. Therefore, extrapolating Q1 year-over-year growth to the full year would create a headwind from the third-party-cost comparison. Based on signed deals and the pipeline, Infosys expects FY26 third-party costs to be lower than FY25 third-party costs. No quarterly third-party-cost guidance was provided.

### Q1 Pricing and Productivity Benefit

Kumar Rakesh asked about the approximately 70-basis-point pricing and productivity benefit in Q1 and whether third-party costs would decline further from Q1 levels.

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

The 70-basis-point benefit comprised:

- Benefits from Project Maximus, including value-based selling.
- A seasonal benefit from higher working-calendar days and some furlough flush-back.

Infosys had previously reported a 3.5% pricing benefit for the full prior year, including the capture of some low-hanging opportunities. Project Maximus continues to contribute to pricing benefits.

### Europe Growth

Kumar Rakesh asked what has driven Europe's outperformance over the last four or five quarters and whether the growth is sustainable.

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

Europe's growth has been supported by investments made after Infosys identified the region as an opportunity several years ago. These investments helped Infosys win very large and mega deals in Europe.

Growth has also benefited from consolidation deals and the gradual opening of Europe to outsourcing. Infosys continues to see significant opportunity in Europe, with large deals in both the current business and the pipeline. Infosys did not provide guidance on whether Europe would continue growing faster than the company overall.

## Abhishek Kumar

### Vendor Consolidation and Competitive Dynamics

Abhishek Kumar asked whether vendor consolidation had shifted from smaller post-COVID vendors toward larger peers, potentially making competition for existing and new work more challenging and putting pressure on margins.

### Salil Parekh

Vendor consolidation has been ongoing for more than the last two or three years, and clients continue to consider a range of provider options. Infosys is benefiting from the type of work it brings to clients, its enterprise AI investments over the last couple of years, and consistent delivery across its other offerings.

Infosys also brings Automation and Lean capabilities. These factors have helped it win consolidation opportunities involving large, midsized, and small competitors.

Pricing discussions in consolidation engagements continue to focus on productivity. Clients expect productivity improvements over time, and Infosys evaluates the benefits it can provide through Automation, Lean, and enterprise AI.
