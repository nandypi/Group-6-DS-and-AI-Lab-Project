## Shilpa Phadnis

Shilpa Phadnis asked whether the wind-down of the Daimler account reflected a capability gap, how Infosys planned to backfill the associated revenue, and whether the impact was included in the current financial-year guidance.

## Salil Parekh

Salil Parekh declined to comment on a specific client and referred to manufacturing commentary for guidance.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Manufacturing is facing a challenging environment, particularly in the European automotive sector. A headwind from a particular client is expected to wind down toward the end of the year and is already included in the guidance. Jayesh Sanghrajka described the guidance as “two points generally” and said it was not expanded guidance.

## Padmini Dhruvaraj

Padmini Dhruvaraj asked how financial-services clients are adopting AI agents, whether tighter regulation creates implementation challenges, whether AI is compressing the traditional IT-services model, and which revenue streams may be cannibalized.

## Salil Parekh

Financial-services clients are moving quickly to adopt AI. Examples include:

- AI agents for bank KYC and AML processes, which are generating additional revenue for Infosys.
- AI use in credit-related work.
- AI-supported development of new capabilities.
- Use of foundation models in bank legacy modernization.

Infosys has partnered with foundation-model companies and other AI leaders, which management said provides an advantage in the financial-services market. Despite some productivity-related compression and the migration of certain activities to AI, Infosys is seeing accelerating growth in financial services.

AI-related compression is occurring in services where foundation models and related tools are highly efficient, including some technology-services and BPM work. This compression is being offset by growth in other services.

## Shristi Achar

Shristi Achar asked whether the sequential contraction in fourth-quarter TCV reflected reduced discretionary spending or AI-led deflation, and requested an update on regulatory delays affecting the Versent acquisition.

## Salil Parekh

Salil Parekh said Infosys recorded $3.2 billion in large deals during the quarter. Large deals, defined as deals with a value above $50 million, are inherently variable, but management considered the quarterly result strong. Full-year large-deal value was $14.9 billion, higher than the prior year.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

The previous quarter included a mega deal with a UK client, which increased the reported large-deal value. Excluding that deal, the quarterly figures were comparable because mega deals are lumpy and do not occur every quarter.

For the year, Infosys delivered approximately $15 billion of large deals, with a 55% net-new mix and 28% year-on-year growth.

The delay in closing the Versent acquisition relates to regulatory approvals. Infosys has received questions or queries from regulators and responded to them, but the process can be unpredictable.

## Shristi Achar

Shristi Achar also asked about the direct impact of geopolitical conflicts on client decision-making and potential delays.

## Salil Parekh

Clients are assessing how geopolitical conditions develop. If conditions stabilize and underlying economic conditions in Western economies remain resilient, management expects technology spending could receive support.

## Sai Ishwar

Sai Ishwar asked about the decline in North America's revenue contribution from 57% to approximately 55%, Europe's growth, and opportunities across the U.S. and other major geographies.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Multiple large and mega-deal wins in Europe have contributed to faster European growth. Examples include a large manufacturing deal, the NHS deal, and a Liberty Global deal.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys is performing strongly in selected U.S. industries and is also doing well in newer geographies such as Japan. Within Europe, the Nordic markets are growing well. Performance varies by market, investment levels, and local economic conditions.

## Avik Das

Avik Das asked whether Infosys was seeing a heightened trend of mid-market global capability centers carving out businesses or operations and outsourcing them to service providers. He also asked about the timing of wage-hike announcements, the possibility of stabilizing wage cycles, and potential effects on employee morale.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys has a robust GCC business and works with global companies both outside India and in India. Management said it had seen some examples of GCCs carving out operations, but did not identify this as a broad trend.

Infosys supports GCC clients across activities including:

- Starting and scaling GCCs.
- Build-operate-transfer models.
- AI skilling.
- Building GCCs designed for AI.

Management said AI-driven GCCs are receiving increased attention from clients and represent a particularly active area.
