## Jayesh Sanghrajka

When Infosys decides on wage increases, it considers company performance, expectations for the next few quarters, industry practice, expected industry growth, actions taken by peers, inflation in the respective markets, and employee morale.

## Rishi Basu

The next question is from Sanjana from *The Hindu BusinessLine*.

## Sanjana B.

How is Infosys recalibrating its strategy to navigate the continuing uncertainty? Will it take on large transformation programs such as legacy modernization that may initially dilute margins? Have visa costs become a headwind, and are they offsetting the benefits of cross-currency movements from rupee depreciation?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys's AI approach is resonating with clients. The six areas include AI strategy and engineering, legacy modernization, process work, and trust-related work. Infosys is seeing client activity in these areas while partnering with foundation-model companies, technology and cloud platforms, and tools built on foundation models.

Management expects a significant AI transformation over the next several years. Infosys is also focused on ensuring that clients receive available productivity benefits while retaining a portion of those benefits to support margin resilience. The AI strategy is intended to support growth, while productivity improvements are intended to support margins. Execution has started, and the early steps are progressing in the intended direction.

Infosys will take on large transformation programs when their economic profile is manageable. The company manages a portfolio of activities and seeks to align its commitments with its internal objectives. Infosys is undertaking large programs, including legacy modernization.

Some legacy modernization programs become self-funded. In one program with a transport company, Infosys is helping move from older technology to a microservices architecture. The estimated cost is 60% lower than it would have been before the AI approach, and the timeline is also substantially shorter. Infosys is executing the program with a partner and has similar programs in its pipeline.

### Visa costs

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Visa costs have remained stable. Infosys has not increased its visa costs, and it has not filed H-1B visas under the new regime.

## Rishi Basu

The next question is from Uma Kannan from the *Deccan Herald*.

## Uma Kannan

As AI becomes part of every project, are clients asking Infosys to reduce expenses, including employee costs? Is Infosys finding it difficult to retain existing business, and are there project ramp-downs?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys distinguishes between AI-first services, which represent new work in the six defined AI service areas, and AI-augmented services, where AI has been infused into existing work. Every client discussion now includes AI.

Some AI-related work is growth-oriented. For a consumer-products company, Infosys built an application that customers can use while purchasing. The application increased customer engagement and sales, rather than serving as a cost-reduction program.

For cost-oriented work, clients continue to seek productivity improvements. Infosys uses its tools and Topaz Fabric to create those improvements. Management described the overall effect as a balance between growth and compression, with growth currently exceeding compression. Infosys reported 3.1% growth in the prior year, with growth higher after accounting for the relevant variations, and its forecast also includes growth.

## Uma Kannan

Infosys has partnered with AI companies, including OpenAI and Anthropic. Are these partnerships helping clients, and are projects moving from pilots to production?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys has multiple projects already in production, including large-scale products. The transport and consumer-products examples are large programs rather than pilots. Infosys also described approximately 12 client projects at its AI Day, which were large projects.

Infosys announced its OpenAI partnership the previous day and its Anthropic partnership several weeks earlier. These partnerships provide access to foundation-model technology that Infosys can use with clients across its six AI service areas. Infosys has shown examples of clients and partnership progress using its Hexagon framework, with the objective of creating revenue and additional activity for Infosys.

## Rishi Basu

The next question is from Poulomi Chatterjee from *The Financial Express*.

## Poulomi Chatterjee

What caused the quarterly decline in headcount? As Infosys develops new AI service lines, how is it hiring freshers, including the mix of coding, consulting, and newer skills? How has pricing evolved so Infosys can capture value from AI-led services?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Headcount depends on volume growth, utilization, and the resulting number of people Infosys needs to hire laterally or through other channels. During the quarter, volumes were softer and utilization was lower, resulting in lower headcount.

On a full-year basis, Infosys added 5,000 employees. The pricing environment has remained stable. Most of the company's growth during the year was pricing-led because volumes were softer, which is consistent with AI revenue being generated at better pricing.

## Salil Parekh

The second half of the year, including the fourth quarter, is seasonally somewhat lower than the first half in terms of activity.

Infosys is hiring people with different skill sets and has developed a model for skilling employees at different levels. Starting compensation varies based on the skills employees bring, including skills more closely aligned with AI.

Infosys is also developing a forward-deployed engineering team to work directly with clients on business and technology applications for AI solutions. More than 90% of employees are now being skilled on different AI platforms.
