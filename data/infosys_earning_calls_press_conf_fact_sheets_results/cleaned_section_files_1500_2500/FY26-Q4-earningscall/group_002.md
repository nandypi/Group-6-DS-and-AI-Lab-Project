# FY26 Q4 Financial Performance, Large Deals, Verticals, and FY27 Guidance

Q4 yield on cash and investments was 6.2%, compared with 6.7% for FY26. Return on equity was 31.6%. Consolidated cash and investments were $4.5 billion after returning more than $4 billion to shareholders in FY26.

Infosys signed 19 large deals during the quarter with total contract value (TCV) of $3.2 billion:

- Financial Services: 5 deals
- Manufacturing: 5 deals
- Retail: 4 deals
- Life Sciences: 2 deals
- Communications: 2 deals
- EURS: 1 deal

By region, 11 deals were signed in Europe, 5 in the Americas, and 3 in the rest of the world.

For FY26, Infosys signed 96 large deals with TCV of $15 billion, of which 55% was net new. The year included 3 mega deals.

The Q4 tax rate was lower because prior-year tax provisions were reversed following favorable tax orders. The expected effective tax rate for FY27 is 29% to 30%.

In line with the capital allocation policy, the board proposed a final dividend of ₹25 per share. If approved by shareholders, total FY26 dividend would be ₹48 per share, an increase of 11.6% over the prior year.

## Verticals

### Financial Services

Financial Services grew 4.4% in FY26, above the company average. Growth was led by ramp-ups of large-deal wins and continued momentum in AI-led transformation, legacy modernization, and vendor consolidation.

Market sentiment remained positive, with continued consumer spending across US banking, capital markets, and Europe. US budgets for CY26 are expected to grow.

Infosys signed a large GCC deal for a regional US bank, described as an industry-first, large AI-first GCC deal. Infosys is the strategic AI partner for 18 of its top 20 clients in this vertical.

Significant large-deal closures and new account openings in FY26, together with a strong large-deal pipeline, are expected to support growth acceleration in FY27.

### Manufacturing

Manufacturing clients remain cautious amid softer demand, particularly in automotive and parts of Europe. Tariff uncertainty and the ongoing Middle East conflict are delaying decision-making in some areas.

Discretionary spending remains constrained, while clients are prioritizing cost optimization and operational resilience. The large-deal pipeline includes infrastructure outsourcing, application management services (AMS), and S/4HANA rollouts.

Near-term and FY27 growth will be affected by lower revenue from one large client.

### EURS

Demand across the EURS segment remains constructive, supported by a strong large-deal pipeline. Clients continue to prioritize cost reduction and operational efficiency, driving vendor consolidation.

Energy is seeing increased outsourcing and healthy deal momentum. Utilities demand is structurally higher, driven by grid constraints, renewable integration, and increasing electricity needs from data centers.

Of FY26 large-deal TCV in EURS, 80% was net new, which is expected to support growth acceleration in FY27.

### Retail

Retail clients continue to face uncertainty from supply chain disruptions, geopolitical conflict, and shifting trade policy. Consumer demand remains muted, and budgets are tightly controlled, with discretionary spending under pressure.

Clients expect AI-led productivity savings to support greater output within similar budgets. Demand is expected to increase for AI-assisted legacy modernization.

Topaz Fabric and AI Next platforms are helping clients move from ideation to deployable solutions with controls for privacy, ethics, and governance.

### Communications

FY26 Communications growth was led by large-deal ramp-ups. The overall environment remains cautious amid macroeconomic uncertainty and client margin pressures.

Budgets are flat to negative, affecting discretionary spending. Non-discretionary spending is selective and increasingly AI-led. Clients are shifting from generative AI to agentic AI while consolidating IT and BPM to reduce costs.

Infosys is seeing increased AI deal activity in IT operations, software replacement, and mainframe migration.

## FY27 Outlook and Guidance

Enterprise budgets are expected to remain measured and selective as companies address macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainty, higher interest rates, rapid technology shifts, and high competitive intensity.

FY27 revenue growth guidance is 1.5% to 3.5% in constant-currency terms. The guidance:

- Includes the contribution from Stratus, which closed earlier in the week.
- Excludes the Versent joint venture and Optimum Healthcare acquisitions, which had not yet closed.
- Includes a 0.75% to 1% reduction due to lower revenue from one large European manufacturing client. The reduction reflects lower client spending amid a challenging macroeconomic environment and the decision not to pursue certain deals that did not meet return expectations.
- Includes a further 0.75% to 1% reduction from a lower onsite mix.
- Assumes third-party costs will remain at FY26 levels.

FY27 operating margin guidance is 20% to 22%. The guidance assumes headwinds from wage increases, productivity pass-throughs, and AI investment, offset by initiatives under Project Maximus.

After closure, Optimum Healthcare, Stratus, and Versent are expected to affect operating margin by approximately 0.7% on a full-year annualized basis.
