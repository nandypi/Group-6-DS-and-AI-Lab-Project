## Keith Bachman

How may AI efficiency gains on the supply side change the volume required to achieve the same revenue growth over the next 12 months?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys is seeing some deflation from existing services, partly offset by new AI-driven services. Volumes remained flattish over the last year. Going forward, volumes are expected to remain flat or marginally positive, as reflected in the lower end of the guidance. The upper end assumes a better macroeconomic environment and better volumes.

## Apurva Prasad

What factors are affecting the onsite delivery mix, including AI compression, AI embedded in services, and contract structures?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

The change in onsite mix reflects multiple factors:

- The broader operating environment.
- Visa situations in some countries.
- Infosys's initiative to deliver more work from offshore.
- Lower discretionary spending, which generally requires a higher onsite presence.

## Apurva Prasad

Should the FY27 onsite exit mix be similar to FY26, and should third-party costs remain similar next year?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Third-party costs are expected to remain in a similar range. The FY27 exit onsite mix is difficult to project. The FY26 exit itself resulted in approximately 40-50 basis points of lower onsite mix, and management expects that trend to continue to some extent.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys reported FY26 growth of 3.1%, a 21% margin, and strong large deals totaling close to $15 billion. The company has provided growth guidance for the coming year, with growth drivers partly offset by compression and some one-off technical factors. On a like-for-like basis, the growth is higher after adjusting for those factors.

The AI services approach and strategy are resonating well with clients. All six areas are represented in the pipeline, supported by partnerships with AI foundation-model companies and other AI tool companies.
