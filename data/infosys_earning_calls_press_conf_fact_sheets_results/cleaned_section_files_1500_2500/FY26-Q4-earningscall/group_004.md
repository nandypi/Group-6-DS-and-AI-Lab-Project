## Salil Parekh

Salil Parekh said Infosys was seeing very strong activity in AI services. Tariff-related uncertainty became better understood during the previous year, while the start of the war had a constraining effect on the macroeconomic environment. Although economic indicators were being forecast more positively and there was a general expectation that the war would be resolved, the improvement had not yet translated into the broader business environment.

He said the company was seeing stronger activity than in the previous year, including in European manufacturing and in the onsite mix. Revenue compression was present, but management was also seeing growth in other areas.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Jayesh Sanghrajka said Infosys had begun the previous year with guidance of 0% to 3% and had tightened or improved the guidance as visibility improved during the year. The current guidance reflected the information available at the time and was intended to reduce asymmetry.

Infosys had stayed away from a European manufacturing deal that did not meet its return expectations. The client was also experiencing a ramp-down because of challenging macroeconomic conditions in the sector. The guidance included this impact and the expected change in onsite mix.

The exit trajectory already indicated a 40 to 50 basis-point impact from the onsite mix for the future year. Based on these factors, Infosys announced revenue-growth guidance of 1.5% to 3.5%. Management said it would reconsider the guidance if visibility improved during the year.

## Gaurav Rateria

Gaurav Rateria asked whether new AI services had higher revenue productivity and better gross margins than the core business, and when wage increases would begin during the financial year.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Jayesh Sanghrajka said AI projects generally had better pricing, which was reflected in better margins. However, they also had higher costs because AI talent was currently premium-priced. The premium depended on how far Infosys was ahead of market benchmarks: being at the benchmark level would not generate a premium, while being ahead of the curve could.

He referred to approximately $15 billion of deals won and said this reflected Infosys' market positioning. Management's confidence was also supported by the AI capabilities presented during AI Day and by client feedback.

Infosys had not decided the timing of wage increases. The decision would consider attrition levels, the timing of the previous wage increases, market conditions, and inflation.

## Sumeet Jain

Sumeet Jain asked whether the launch of newer Anthropic models had increased client demand for productivity and what types of productivity-related client conversations Infosys was having.

## Salil Parekh

Salil Parekh said the underlying need for productivity remained similar, although higher competitive intensity was generating additional demand. In a small number of cases, productivity expectations were so far outside acceptable bounds that Infosys did not pursue the opportunity.

For the vast majority of clients, Infosys had not seen a major change in the previous two months. Productivity expectations had changed over multiple quarters and years, but management had not observed a sudden step change during the last two months.

Sumeet Jain also asked whether AI-related productivity in new deals was being passed on to clients at levels similar to the existing business, whether the base business was experiencing greater deflation than expected, and whether improving AI models could accelerate the trend.

Salil Parekh said Infosys was not providing a specific breakdown of growth compression across its portfolio. The company was focused on maintaining growth through AI services and other growth areas while managing compression in other parts of the business.

## Sumeet Jain

Sumeet Jain asked for quantification of the effects of the European manufacturing client ramp-down, increased competition, and the shift toward a higher offshore mix on the year's guidance.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Jayesh Sanghrajka said the European client was expected to have an approximately 75-basis-point to 1-percentage-point impact. This reflected both the decision not to pursue a deal that did not meet return expectations and the client's ramp-down during the year because of challenging macroeconomic conditions.

The reduction in onsite mix was expected to have an approximately 70-basis-point impact on revenue growth. Of this, 40 to 50 basis points were already visible in the exit trajectory, and management expected further improvement in the onsite mix going forward.

## Jonathan Lee

Jonathan Lee asked why the percentage of net-new deals was at its lowest level in recent years and what Infosys expected for net-new deals during the year.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Jayesh Sanghrajka said the analysis should consider the combination of renewals and net-new deals. During the full year, Infosys signed $15 billion of deals, of which 96 were deals and approximately 55% were net new. He characterized this as strong performance and said it represented approximately 28% year-on-year growth.

Jonathan Lee also asked about quarterly performance versus internal expectations, including when deflationary pressure or lower revenue realization began to appear.

Jayesh Sanghrajka said Infosys did not provide visibility into internal goals or plans for net-new large deals or performance against those plans. He characterized quarterly large-deal signings of $3.2 billion as strong. Infosys had seen slower decision-making in some areas during March, but management did not believe this had materially affected large-deal signings.
