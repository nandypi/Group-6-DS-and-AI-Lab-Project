## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys does not split freshers into higher-skill and regular-skill categories. Every fresher undergoes training based on business requirements and the individual's skill set.

## Rukmini Rao

Rukmini Rao asked about:

- Board-level scenario planning and contingency plans under extreme uncertainty.
- The company's internal investigation related to the disclosed DOJ investigation into H-1B visas.
- Liability and indemnity arrangements in AI partnerships with hyperscalers.

## Salil Parekh

The Board evaluates different scenarios and uncertainties from an overall perspective rather than only from a quarterly business perspective. The risk committee regularly evaluates these scenarios. The Board includes members with experience assessing global situations.

Legal questions concerning liability in new AI partnerships are not yet fully clarified. Infosys seeks to take responsibility for matters within its control. The allocation of responsibility beyond that depends on discussions with clients and partners. Parekh said the company had no comment on the investigation at that stage.

## Sanjana B

Sanjana B asked about recent acquisitions, the contribution of inorganic moves to growth, public-sector opportunities across geographies, the recent buyback announcement, and the outlook for the second half of FY26.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys has long focused on the public sector and is increasing that focus across markets. The company has performed well in Australia's public sector, has a smaller U.S. public-sector business, and is expanding further in the United Kingdom. Infosys also has experience in India through work with the Income Tax Department and GSTN, which it can leverage globally.

Acquisitions are not the primary driver of growth. Infosys first focuses on organic growth and considers acquisitions when they add capability in an industry, service line, skill area, or geography. Recent focus areas have included energy and consulting, cybersecurity, and digital, AI, and cloud. Organic work with clients remains the primary focus.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys follows a capital return policy under which it returns 85% of free cash flow over a five-year period. The policy includes regular dividends and other forms of capital return.

In the prior year, strong cash flow supported by tax refunds created additional capacity to return capital to shareholders. After considering various options, Infosys announced a buyback of ₹18,000 crores at ₹1,800 per share, subject to shareholder approval. The postal ballot had been issued.

## Ritu Singh

Ritu Singh asked whether Infosys would continue sending employees to the United States on H-1B visas, participate in the next visa cycle, or significantly reduce its already limited use of the visas.

## Salil Parekh

The majority of Infosys employees in the United States do not require Infosys immigration support. Future decisions will be guided by client needs, scaling requirements, nearshore delivery, and local hiring and localization efforts. Infosys has been working with clients to maintain service-delivery and business continuity.

When asked whether the cost of sending employees to the United States on H-1B visas remained viable, Parekh referred to his earlier response and provided no additional comment.

## Rukmini Rao

Rukmini Rao asked why Infosys had incorporated a subsidiary in Egypt and whether higher subcontracting costs were related to the addition of specialized AI talent.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

The Egypt subsidiary was established to meet the requirements of a specific project for a client based in Egypt, including the need to send employees there.

Subcontracting costs vary with demand, available talent, project requirements, and location. Some subcontracting may involve newer skills such as AI, while other requirements may involve regular skills.

## Veena Mani

Veena Mani asked about the increase in voluntary attrition compared with the prior year and whether Infosys had directly recruited employees for its Hubli campus, in addition to providing incentives for employees to move there.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys moves some senior employees to new campuses to begin meeting demand requirements and then builds the broader team below them. The company provides incentives for certain employees to move to these campuses and has also hired, and will continue hiring, people locally in Hubli. Infosys does not disclose hiring numbers by campus.

The company conducted the relevant cycles in January and April. These are generally annual cycles, and no decision had been made about the current year at that point.
