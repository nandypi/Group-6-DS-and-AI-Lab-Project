## Infosys

The impact of higher variable pay was partly offset by one-off benefits during the quarter.

Consolidated cash and investments were $3.9 billion at the end of the quarter, after returning $3 billion to shareholders through dividends and buybacks. Yield on the cash balance was 6.19%, and return on equity was 32.8%.

Infosys signed 26 large deals during the quarter, including two mega deals:

- Financial Services: 10
- Retail: 4
- Life Sciences: 3
- Manufacturing: 3
- Communications: 2
- EURS: 2
- Hi-tech: 2

Regionally, 16 deals were signed in the Americas, 9 in Europe, and 1 in the Rest of World.

### Financial Services

Financial Services grew approximately 5% over the first nine months, led by large-deal wins and an uptick in discretionary spending across banking, payments, mortgages, and asset and wealth management.

There is elevated interest in AI-led transformation, platform modernization, and vendor consolidation. Spending is shifting from compliance toward business growth. Significant core transformation across Financial Services sub-verticals is creating a long-term strategic pipeline and opportunities for IP and platform partnerships.

Infosys is a preferred AI partner for 15 of its top 25 banking clients. Increased discretionary spending and recent deal wins position the vertical for better growth in FY27.

### Manufacturing

Manufacturing is affected by tariff uncertainties, which are preventing clients from committing to long-term investments. Discretionary spending is under pressure, and decision-making is slow. Industrial and Aerospace are performing well, while the Auto sector remains challenged.

Clients are prioritizing cost discipline, consolidation, and efficiency. Infosys is supporting these priorities through digital rationalization and AI-led productivity initiatives. The overall pipeline remains healthy, with continued focus on cost takeouts, infrastructure consolidation, and ERP modernization.

### EURS

EURS companies are increasingly allocating budgets to AI infrastructure, data readiness, cloud, and software platforms. Demand is also increasing for setting up global capability centers across sectors, with many clients seeking systems integrators to complement their GCC strategies.

Infosys is a preferred AI partner for 15 of its top 25 clients. Discretionary demand is increasing in Utilities and Energy, which is expected to support growth acceleration in FY27.

The Utilities sector is seeing increased demand driven by significant investments in infrastructure and AI data centers. In Energy, discretionary demand is focused on decarbonization and low-carbon solutions, alongside cost optimization and consolidation related to enterprise AI adoption.

### Retail and CPG

Retail and CPG clients continue to face uncertainty from tariff negotiations and evolving geopolitical conditions. Clients are prioritizing cost takeouts and AI-led productivity deals, while discretionary spending remains soft except in SAP, DNA, testing, and AI-augmented services.

Infosys is using Topaz and its AI Next platform to deliver measurable business value, with guardrails around privacy and governance and a focus on augmenting customer and employee experiences.

### Communications

The Communications sector continues to be affected by geopolitical uncertainty. Deal wins in prior quarters are supporting year-on-year growth acceleration.

Telecommunications companies are prioritizing AI automation and productivity improvements through transformation, while traditional IT spending remains under pressure. Clients are increasingly seeking systems-integrator partnerships to scale and innovate within complex ecosystems.

There is a shift toward outcome-based engagement models rather than traditional effort-based pricing. Infosys said its execution in the seasonally weak quarter reflected its ability to navigate the uncertain environment.

### Guidance

Strong year-to-date performance and robust deal wins enabled Infosys to revise its FY26 revenue-growth guidance upward to 3%-3.5%.

The guidance does not include revenue from the Telstra joint venture, pending regulatory approvals. FY26 operating-margin guidance remains 20%-22%.
