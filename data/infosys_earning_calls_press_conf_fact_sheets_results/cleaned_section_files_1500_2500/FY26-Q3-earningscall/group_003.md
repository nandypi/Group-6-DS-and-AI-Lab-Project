## Ankur Rudra

Ankur Rudra asked why the implied Q4 outlook was not stronger, whether FY27 growth acceleration applied to the overall business or only certain segments, and whether short-cycle projects or discretionary spending were expanding beyond Financial Services and Energy.

### Salil Parekh

Management said momentum and deal activity supported the increase in guidance. Energy, Utilities, Resources and Services and Financial Services were showing good FY27 growth compared with FY26, supported by large and other deals and AI partnerships involving 15 of the company's largest 25 clients. Management was not providing an outlook for the overall business at this stage.

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

Q4 guidance also reflects a lower working-day and calendar-day impact, which is a seasonal headwind.

### Margins and AI pricing

Ankur Rudra asked about the approximately 20-basis-point margin decline after adjusting for the property-sale gain, pricing pressure, future margin puts and takes, and whether AI projects were a margin headwind or tailwind.

Jayesh Sanghrajka said the property-sale benefit was largely offset by higher variable pay recorded in the quarter. He said pricing remained accretive and had consistently increased due to factors including AI and Project Maximus. New-generation pricing under Project Maximus includes pricing for new AI projects, and management did not see AI projects creating a pricing headwind.

## Bryan Bergin

Bryan Bergin asked whether North America's year-on-year contraction of 1% was related to particular industries and how the region might progress in the coming quarters.

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

Management said it had expected third-party costs, and therefore third-party revenue, to be lower than the prior year based on the deal pipeline and projects under execution. The impact was across segments, although its magnitude could vary by segment.

Bryan Bergin also asked about enterprise conversations concerning calendar 2026 budgets compared with the same period a year earlier.

### Salil Parekh

Management reported increased discretionary activity in Financial Services, continued momentum in Energy, Utilities and Financial Services, and greater AI activity than at the same time in the prior year. It was identifying six AI value-pool areas where faster growth could be generated and was seeing traction in those areas. Partnerships already announced, as well as additional company partnerships under development, were contributing to momentum.

## Ashwin Mehta

Ashwin Mehta asked what drove healthcare growth of nearly $44 million in incremental revenue and whether the NHS deal contributed during the quarter.

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

Healthcare benefited from the NHS deal signed by Infosys. Management does not provide deal-by-deal revenue estimates, but confirmed that healthcare benefited from the NHS deal.

Ashwin Mehta also asked about wage-hike decisions and whether they were expected in Q4.

### Wage hikes

Jayesh Sanghrajka said wage-hike decisions consider the timing of the previous increase, market conditions, inflation, and other factors. The company had already rolled out wage hikes in two stages in the current calendar year: one effective in January and another effective in April. The next cycle had not yet been decided.

## Abhishek Pathak

Abhishek Pathak asked whether clients were moving from treating AI strategy as optional to treating it as necessary, whether foundational AI work was gaining traction across industries, and how the Cognition partnership involving the Devin software agent was priced and structured.

### Salil Parekh

Management said interest in AI work was increasing across industries. In each quarter so far, particularly Q3, the AI pipeline, number of projects, and penetration of the company's 200 largest clients had increased.

AI activity was strongest in Financial Services but was also visible in Telecommunications, Pharma and Healthcare, Energy and Utilities, and to a lesser extent Life Sciences.

Regarding Cognition, Infosys and the Devin software agent were already undertaking joint client work. The combination could make activities such as legacy modernization more addressable by pairing the software agent with Infosys's knowledge of client landscapes and industries. Management described this as one of six AI value pools. It said the combination could enable new work that previously would not have been undertaken.
