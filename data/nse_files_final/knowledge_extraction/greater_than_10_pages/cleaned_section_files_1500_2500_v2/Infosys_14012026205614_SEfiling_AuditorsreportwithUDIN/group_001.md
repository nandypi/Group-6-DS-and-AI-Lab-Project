Infosys Limited submitted the auditors' reports on its financial statements for the quarter and nine months ended December 31, 2025, following the Board Meeting held on January 14, 2026.

## INDEPENDENT AUDITOR'S REPORT ON AUDIT OF QUARTERLY AND NINE MONTHS ENDED CONSOLIDATED FINANCIAL RESULTS

### Opinion

The auditors audited the accompanying Statement of Consolidated Financial Results of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries, collectively referred to as the Group, for the quarter and nine months ended December 31, 2025.

In the auditors' opinion, the Statement:

- Includes the financial results of the subsidiaries listed in the Annexure.
- Is presented in accordance with Regulation 33 of the SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015, as amended.
- Is prepared in accordance with Indian Accounting Standard 34, “Interim Financial Reporting,” the relevant rules issued under the standard, and other accounting principles generally accepted in India.

### Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under Section 143(10) of the applicable Act. The auditors stated that they were independent of the Group in accordance with the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India and that the audit evidence obtained was sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their opinion.

### Management's and Board of Directors' Responsibilities for the Statement

The Statement, including the Consolidated Financial Results, was the responsibility of the Company's Board of Directors and was approved by them for issuance. It was compiled from the related audited interim condensed consolidated financial statements for the three months and nine months ended December 31, 2025.

The respective Boards of Directors of the companies included in the Group were responsible for:

- Preparing the Statement in accordance with Ind AS 34, Section 133 of the Act, relevant rules, other accounting principles generally accepted in India, and Regulation 33 of the Listing Regulations.
- Maintaining adequate accounting records.
- Safeguarding Group assets and preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls that operated effectively to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting records.
- Assessing each entity's ability to continue as a going concern.
- Disclosing applicable going-concern matters and using the going-concern basis of accounting unless liquidation or cessation of operations was intended or there was no realistic alternative.
- Overseeing the Group's financial reporting process.

### Auditor's Responsibilities for Audit of the Consolidated Financial Results for the Quarter and Nine Months Ended December 31, 2025

The auditors' objectives were to obtain reasonable assurance that the Consolidated Financial Results were free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report containing their opinion.

The audit responsibilities included:

- Identifying and assessing risks of material misstatement, including the higher fraud risk associated with collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or management override of internal control.
- Obtaining an understanding of relevant internal controls to design appropriate audit procedures.
- Evaluating the appropriateness of accounting policies and the reasonableness of accounting estimates made by the Board of Directors.
- Evaluating the appropriateness and reasonableness of disclosures required under Regulation 33 of the LODR Regulations.
- Assessing the appropriateness of the going-concern basis and whether material uncertainty existed that could cast significant doubt on the Group's ability to continue as a going concern.
- Evaluating the overall presentation, structure, content, and disclosures in the Statement.
- Performing procedures in accordance with the SEBI circular issued under Regulation 33(8) of the LODR Regulations, to the extent applicable.
- Obtaining sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the financial information of entities within the Group.

Materiality was described as the magnitude of misstatements that, individually or in aggregate, could influence the economic decisions of a reasonably knowledgeable user. The auditors considered quantitative materiality and qualitative factors when planning the audit, evaluating results, and assessing identified misstatements.

The auditors communicated with those charged with governance regarding the planned scope and timing of the audit, significant audit findings, and significant deficiencies in internal control. They also communicated their compliance with relevant ethical requirements regarding independence and disclosed relationships or other matters that could reasonably bear on their independence.
