## 2.18 Revenue from operations

## Accounting policy

The Group derives revenues primarily from IT services, including:

- Software development and related services
- Cloud and infrastructure services
- Maintenance
- Consulting and package implementation
- Licensing of software products and platforms across the Group’s core and digital offerings
- Business process management services

Customer contracts are entered into on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe basis.

Revenue is recognized when:

- The contract has been approved in writing by the parties.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- The contract is legally enforceable.
- Control of the promised products or services has transferred to the customer.
- The consideration expected to be received can be measured appropriately.

When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies the distinct performance obligations in each contract and allocates the transaction price to those obligations based on their relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged when an item is sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. When that evidence is unavailable, the Group generally estimates standalone selling price using the expected cost-plus-margin approach.

Contracts may include variable consideration such as rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue recognized will not occur when the uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue recognition by contract type is as follows:

- Revenue from time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized using a percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and the Group’s costs to fulfill the contract are uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.
- Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts whose performance obligations are satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Progress toward completion is measured using the ratio of costs or efforts incurred to date to estimated total costs or efforts, because inputs have a direct relationship with productivity.
- Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are monitored throughout the contract. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates are revised.
- Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable, based on estimated costs or efforts to complete the contract.

Customer billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development and related services and maintenance arrangements, the Group generally treats software development and related services as distinct performance obligations. These obligations are satisfied as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements that may be subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. Where hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated between them based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period based on the measure of progress specified in the contract.

Revenue from licenses is recognized as follows:

- Licenses providing a right to use are recognized when the licenses are made available to the customer.
- Licenses providing a right to access are recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally contain three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated between them based on their relative standalone selling prices. When the standalone selling price of implementation is unavailable, it is estimated using the expected cost-plus-margin approach.

When the license requires substantial customization as part of implementation, the license and implementation fees are treated as a single performance obligation, and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services related to software products is recognized as the performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

In certain integrated services arrangements, contracts include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when the Group is the principal. The Group assesses whether it obtains control before transfer to the customer, considering factors including:

- Responsibility for fulfilling the promise to provide the specified goods or services
- Inventory risk
- Pricing discretion
- Other relevant factors

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price, or both, of a contract that is approved by the parties. A modification is accounted for:

- As a separate contract when it adds distinct performance obligations priced at their standalone selling prices.
- As termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract when it adds distinct performance obligations that are not priced at their standalone selling prices.
- As part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis when it does not add a distinct performance obligation.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them.

Eligible, non-recurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as an asset when they:

- Relate directly to the contract.
- Generate or enhance resources that will be used to satisfy future performance obligations.
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to expenses over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored for impairment. Impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss.

## Products and platforms

The Group derives revenue from products and platforms including:

- Finacle, a core banking solution
- Edge Suite of products
- Panaya platform
- Stater digital platform
- Infosys McCamish insurance platform
