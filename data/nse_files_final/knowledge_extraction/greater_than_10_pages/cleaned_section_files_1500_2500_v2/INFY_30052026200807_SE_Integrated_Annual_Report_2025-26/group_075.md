## Information Other than the Financial Statements and Auditor's Report Thereon

The Company's Board of Directors is responsible for the other information, comprising the Management Discussion and Analysis, Board's Report and annexures, Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report, Corporate Governance, and Shareholder's Information. It does not include the Consolidated Financial Statements, Standalone Financial Statements, or the auditor's report thereon.

The auditor's opinion on the Standalone Financial Statements does not cover the other information. As part of the audit, the auditor read the other information and considered whether it was materially inconsistent with the Standalone Financial Statements or the auditor's knowledge obtained during the audit. The auditor had nothing to report regarding material misstatement in the other information.

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Standalone Financial Statements

Under Section 134(5) of the Act, the Company's Board of Directors is responsible for preparing Standalone Financial Statements that give a true and fair view of the Company's financial position, financial performance including other comprehensive income, changes in equity, and cash flows, in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles in India, including Ind AS specified under Section 133 of the Act.

These responsibilities include:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records to safeguard assets and prevent and detect fraud and other irregularities.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls that operate effectively to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting records and the fair presentation of the Standalone Financial Statements.
- Assessing the Company's ability to continue as a going concern, making required disclosures, and using the going concern basis of accounting unless the Board intends to liquidate the Company, cease operations, or has no realistic alternative.
- Overseeing the Company's financial reporting process.

## Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit of the Standalone Financial Statements

The auditor's objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance that the Standalone Financial Statements are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report containing the opinion.

The audit included:

- Identifying and assessing risks of material misstatement, designing responsive audit procedures, and obtaining sufficient and appropriate audit evidence.
- Obtaining an understanding of internal financial controls relevant to the audit. Under Section 143(3)(i) of the Act, the auditor also reported on the adequacy and operating effectiveness of the Company's internal financial controls with reference to the Standalone Financial Statements.
- Evaluating the appropriateness of accounting policies, the reasonableness of accounting estimates, and related disclosures.
- Assessing the appropriateness of management's use of the going concern basis of accounting and whether any material uncertainty existed that could cast significant doubt on the Company's ability to continue as a going concern.
- Evaluating the overall presentation, structure, content, and disclosures of the Standalone Financial Statements.

Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but does not guarantee that an audit will detect every material misstatement. The risk of failing to detect a material misstatement arising from fraud is higher than for one arising from error because fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or override of internal controls.

Materiality is the magnitude of misstatements that, individually or in aggregate, could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of a reasonably knowledgeable user. The auditor considers both quantitative materiality and qualitative factors when planning and evaluating the audit.

The auditor communicated with those charged with governance regarding the planned scope and timing of the audit, significant audit findings, and significant deficiencies in internal financial controls. The auditor also communicated compliance with relevant ethical and independence requirements.

## Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements

1. As required by Section 143(3) of the Act, based on the audit, the auditor reported that:

   - The information and explanations necessary for the audit were sought and obtained.
   - Proper books of account required by law were maintained.
   - The Balance Sheet, Statement of Profit and Loss including Other Comprehensive Income, Statement of Changes in Equity, and Statement of Cash Flows agreed with the books of account.
   - The Standalone Financial Statements complied with the Ind AS specified under Section 133 of the Act.
   - Based on written representations received from the directors as of March 31, 2026 and taken on record by the Board, none of the directors was disqualified as of March 31, 2026 under Section 164(2) of the Act.
   - The auditor's separate report in Annexure A expressed an unmodified opinion on the adequacy and operating effectiveness of the Company's internal financial controls with reference to the Standalone Financial Statements.
   - Remuneration paid to the Company's directors during the year complied with Section 197 of the Act.
   - The Company disclosed the impact of pending litigation on its financial position in the Standalone Financial Statements. Refer to Note 2.23.
   - The Company made provisions required under applicable law or accounting standards for material foreseeable losses. Refer to Note 2.16. The Company did not have any long-term derivative contracts.
   - There was no delay in transferring amounts required to be transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund.
   - Management represented that no funds had been advanced, loaned, or invested by the Company, including through foreign entities, with the understanding that intermediaries would lend or invest in ultimate beneficiaries or provide guarantees, securities, or similar support on the Company's behalf.
   - Management represented that the Company had not received funds from any person or entity, including foreign entities, with the understanding that the Company would lend or invest in ultimate beneficiaries or provide guarantees, securities, or similar support on behalf of funding parties.
   - Based on the audit procedures performed, nothing came to the auditor's attention indicating that these management representations contained a material misstatement.
   - As stated in Note 2.12.3:
     - The final dividend proposed in the previous year and declared and paid during the year complied with Section 123 of the Act, as applicable.
     - The interim dividend declared and paid during the year and up to the date of the auditor's report complied with Section 123 of the Act.
     - The Board proposed a final dividend for the year, subject to approval by members at the ensuing Annual General Meeting. The proposed dividend complied with Section 123 of the Act, as applicable.
   - The accounting software used to maintain the books of account for the financial year ended March 31, 2026 had an audit trail/edit-log facility, which operated throughout the year for all relevant transactions. The auditor did not identify any instance of tampering, and the audit trail was preserved in accordance with statutory record-retention requirements.

2. As required by the Companies (Auditor's Report) Order, 2020, issued by the Central Government under Section 143(11) of the Act, Annexure B contains the auditor's statement on the matters specified in paragraphs 3 and 4 of the Order.
