## 2.18 Revenue from operations

## Accounting policy

The Company derives revenues primarily from IT services, including:

- Software development and related services
- Cloud and infrastructure services
- Maintenance
- Consulting and package implementation
- Licensing of software products and platforms across the Company's core and digital offerings

These are collectively referred to as “software related services.” Customer contracts may be based on time and material, unit of work, fixed price, or a fixed timeframe.

Revenue is recognized when:

- The contract has been approved in writing by the parties.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- The contract is legally enforceable.
- Control of the promised products or services has transferred to the customer.
- The amount of revenue reflects the consideration the Company expects to receive.

When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Company identifies distinct performance obligations in each contract and allocates the transaction price to them based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged when an item is sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. If that evidence is unavailable, the Company generally estimates standalone selling price using expected cost plus an appropriate margin based on similar services.

Contracts may include variable consideration, such as rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue when the uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue recognition by contract type is as follows:

- Revenue from time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services consist of an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized using a percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.
- Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Progress toward completion is measured using costs or efforts incurred to date compared with estimated total costs or efforts.
- Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are monitored throughout the contract. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which they arise.
- Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when the losses become probable.

Billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as “unearned revenues.”

For software development and related services and maintenance services, software development and related services generally constitute distinct performance obligations. Revenue is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices, using the expected cost-plus-margin approach when standalone selling prices cannot be determined. Software development and related services are recognized as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. Where hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period based on the contractual promise and related measure of progress.

Revenue from software licenses is recognized as follows:

- Licenses providing the customer with a “right-to-use” are recognized when the license is made available to the customer.
- Licenses providing the customer with a “right-to-access” are recognized over the access period.

Software product arrangements generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When the license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. If the standalone selling price of implementation cannot be determined, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used.

When the license is substantially customized as part of the implementation service, the license and implementation fee is treated as a single performance obligation and recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services arising from the sale of software products is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

For integrated services arrangements that include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue from third-party products or services is recorded:

- Net of costs when the Company acts as an agent.
- Gross when the Company acts as principal.

The Company assesses whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other indicators of control.

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price, or both, of a contract that is approved by the parties. A modification that adds distinct performance obligations is accounted for:

- As a separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price.
- As termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract when the additional services are not priced at their standalone selling price.

A modification that does not add a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Company expects to recover them. Eligible non-recurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as an asset when they:

- Relate directly to the contract.
- Generate or enhance resources that will be used to satisfy future performance obligations.
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized contract costs are amortized to expenses over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored for impairment, and impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover their carrying amount.

The Company presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Statement of Profit and Loss.

## Products & platforms

The Company derives revenue from the sale of products and platforms, including Infosys Applied AI, which applies next-generation artificial intelligence and machine learning.

The percentage of revenue from fixed-price contracts for each of the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025 was 58%.

## Trade receivables and Contract Balances

The timing of revenue recognition, billing, and cash collection results in trade receivables, unbilled revenue, and unearned revenue on the Company's Balance Sheet. Amounts are billed as work progresses under agreed contractual terms, either periodically, such as monthly or quarterly, or upon achievement of contractual milestones.

Trade receivables are unconditional rights to consideration. Unbilled revenue from time-and-material contracts and fixed-price maintenance contracts is classified as a financial asset when the right to consideration is unconditional and payment is due only after the passage of time.

For other fixed-price contracts, invoicing is based on contractual milestones. Because revenue recognition may occur before invoicing, unbilled revenue for these contracts is classified as a non-financial contract asset when the right to consideration depends on completion of contractual milestones.

Invoicing in excess of earnings is classified as unearned revenue. Trade receivables and unbilled revenue are presented net of impairment in the Balance Sheet.
