The applicable Indian corporate statutory tax rate for the year ended March 31, 2026, was 25.17%; the rate for the year ended March 31, 2025, was also 25.17%.

Income tax expense for the year ended March 31, 2026, includes a net reversal of prior-period provisions of ₹877 crore. Income tax expense for the year ended March 31, 2025, includes net provisions of ₹132 crore. These amounts primarily relate to the adjudication of disputed matters following tax-return filing and completion of assessments across various jurisdictions.

During the year ended March 31, 2026, the Company received orders under Sections 250 and 254 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, from the Income Tax Authorities in India for assessment year 2013–14 and assessment years 2017–18 to 2021–22. The orders confirmed the Company’s position on the tax treatment of certain matters. Consequently, the Company recognized pre-tax interest income of ₹381 crore and reversed income tax provisions aggregating ₹869 crore, with a corresponding credit to the Statement of Profit and Loss. Following resolution of the disputes, contingent liabilities were reduced by ₹86 crore.

During the year ended March 31, 2025, the Company received orders under Section 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, from the Income Tax Authorities in India for assessment years 2016–17 and 2019–20. The orders confirmed the Company’s position on the tax treatment of certain matters. Consequently, the Company recognized pre-tax interest income of ₹327 crore and reversed income tax provisions aggregating ₹183 crore, with a corresponding credit to the Statement of Profit and Loss. Following resolution of the disputes, contingent liabilities were reduced by ₹1,068 crore.

Foreign tax expense primarily relates to income taxes payable overseas, principally in the United States. In India, the Group benefited in prior years from tax incentives for software and services exports by units registered under the Special Economic Zones (SEZs) Act, 2005. Eligible SEZ units that commenced providing services on or after April 1, 2005, were entitled to:

- A deduction of 100% of profits or gains from exports of services for the first five years from the financial year in which the unit commenced providing services.
- A deduction of 50% of such profits or gains for the following five years.
- A further deduction of up to 50% for an additional five years, subject to creating and using a Special Economic Zone Re-investment Reserve to acquire new plant and machinery for business purposes under the Income Tax Act, 1961.

Deferred income tax for the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025, substantially relates to the origination and reversal of temporary differences.

Infosys is subject to a 15% Branch Profit Tax in the United States to the extent that its US branch’s net profit during the year exceeds the increase in the US branch’s net assets during the year, calculated in accordance with the Internal Revenue Code.

As at March 31, 2026, Infosys’ US branch net assets amounted to approximately ₹7,736 crore. The Company recognized a deferred tax liability for Branch Profit Tax of ₹207 crore, net of credits, because it estimates that the branch profits are expected to be distributed in the foreseeable future.

Deferred income tax liabilities were not recognized on temporary differences associated with investments in subsidiaries and branches amounting to ₹19,270 crore as at March 31, 2026, and ₹16,593 crore as at March 31, 2025. The Company can control the timing of reversal of these temporary differences and considers it probable that they will not reverse in the foreseeable future. The Group majorly intends to repatriate earnings from subsidiaries and branches only to the extent that they can be distributed in a tax-free manner.

Deferred income tax assets were not recognized on accumulated losses of ₹4,868 crore as at March 31, 2026, and ₹4,597 crore as at March 31, 2025, because it is probable that future taxable profit will not be available against which the unused tax losses can be utilized in the foreseeable future.
