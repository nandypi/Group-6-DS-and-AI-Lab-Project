## Information Other than the Financial Statements and Auditor's Report Thereon

The Company's Board of Directors is responsible for preparing the other information, comprising:

- Management Discussion and Analysis
- Board's Report, including its annexures
- Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report
- Corporate Governance and Shareholder's Information

The other information does not include the consolidated financial statements, standalone financial statements, or the auditor's report thereon.

The auditor's opinion on the consolidated financial statements does not cover the other information. The auditor's responsibility is to read the other information and consider whether it is materially inconsistent with the consolidated financial statements, the auditor's knowledge obtained during the audit, or otherwise materially misstated.

The auditor reported that there was nothing to report in this regard.

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Consolidated Financial Statements

The Company's Board of Directors is responsible under Section 134(5) of the Act for the preparation and presentation of consolidated financial statements that give a true and fair view of:

- The consolidated financial position
- Consolidated financial performance, including other comprehensive income
- Consolidated changes in equity
- Consolidated cash flows of the Group

The financial statements are prepared in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in India, including Ind AS specified under Section 133 of the Act.

The respective Boards of Directors or Trustees of entities included in the Group are responsible for:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records under the Act
- Safeguarding the Group's assets
- Preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining effective internal financial controls
- Ensuring the accuracy and completeness of accounting records used to prepare and present the consolidated financial statements

Management is also responsible for assessing the ability of the respective entities to continue as a going concern, making applicable going-concern disclosures, and using the going-concern basis of accounting unless the respective entity intends to liquidate, cease operations, or has no realistic alternative.

The respective Boards of Directors or Trustees are responsible for overseeing the Group's financial reporting process.

## Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit of the Consolidated Financial Statements

The auditor's objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance that the consolidated financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report containing the auditor's opinion.

Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but does not guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing will always detect a material misstatement. Misstatements may arise from fraud or error and are considered material if they could reasonably influence the economic decisions of users of the consolidated financial statements.

As part of the audit, the auditor exercises professional judgment and maintains professional scepticism. The auditor also:

- Identifies and assesses risks of material misstatement, designs and performs responsive audit procedures, and obtains sufficient and appropriate audit evidence. The risk of failing to detect a fraud-related misstatement is higher because fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or management override of internal control.
- Obtains an understanding of relevant internal financial controls to design appropriate audit procedures. Under Section 143(3)(i) of the Act, the auditor also reports on whether the Company and its Indian-incorporated subsidiary companies have adequate internal financial controls relating to the consolidated financial statements and whether those controls operated effectively.
- Evaluates the appropriateness of accounting policies, the reasonableness of accounting estimates, and related management disclosures.
- Evaluates management's use of the going-concern basis of accounting and whether audit evidence indicates a material uncertainty related to events or conditions that could cast significant doubt on the Group's ability to continue as a going concern. If such uncertainty exists, the auditor draws attention to the relevant disclosures or modifies the opinion if the disclosures are inadequate. The assessment is based on evidence obtained up to the date of the auditor's report; future events or conditions may cause the Group to cease to continue as a going concern.
- Evaluates the overall presentation, structure, content, and disclosures of the consolidated financial statements and whether they fairly represent the underlying transactions and events.
- Obtains sufficient and appropriate audit evidence regarding the financial information of entities within the Group to express an opinion on the consolidated financial statements.

Materiality is the magnitude of a misstatement, individually or in aggregate, that could influence the economic decisions of a reasonably knowledgeable user of the consolidated financial statements. The auditor considers quantitative materiality and qualitative factors when planning the audit, evaluating audit results, and assessing the effect of identified misstatements.

The auditor communicates with those charged with governance regarding the planned scope and timing of the audit, significant audit findings, and significant deficiencies in internal financial controls identified during the audit.

The auditor also provides those charged with governance with a statement confirming compliance with relevant ethical requirements regarding independence and communicates relationships and other matters that may reasonably affect independence, together with related safeguards where applicable.

The auditor determines which matters communicated with those charged with governance were of most significance in the audit of the current period and therefore constitute key audit matters. Such matters are described in the auditor's report unless law or regulation precludes disclosure or, in extremely rare circumstances, the adverse consequences of disclosure would reasonably be expected to outweigh the public interest benefits.

## Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements

1. As required by Section 143(3) of the Act, based on the audit, the auditor reported that:

   - The auditor sought and obtained all information and explanations considered necessary for the audit of the consolidated financial statements.
   - Proper books of account required by law for preparing the consolidated financial statements were maintained by the Group, including relevant records examined by the auditor.
   - The Consolidated Balance Sheet, Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss including Other Comprehensive Income, Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity, and Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows covered by the report agreed with the relevant books of account.
   - The consolidated financial statements complied with the Ind AS specified under Section 133 of the Act.
   - Based on written representations received from the directors as of March 31, 2026 and taken on record by the Board, together with reports of the statutory auditors of Indian-incorporated subsidiary companies, none of the directors of the Indian-incorporated Group companies was disqualified as of March 31, 2026 under Section 164(2) of the Act.
   - The auditor's separate Report in Annexure A, based on the auditors' reports of the Company and its Indian-incorporated subsidiary companies, expressed an unmodified opinion on the adequacy and operating effectiveness of internal financial controls relating to the consolidated financial statements.
   - Remuneration paid by the Company to its directors during the year complied with Section 197 of the Act.
   - The auditor reported the following matters under Rule 11 of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014, as amended:
     - The consolidated financial statements disclosed the impact of pending litigations on the Group's consolidated financial position. Refer to Note 2.24 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.
     - The Group made provisions required under applicable law or accounting standards for material foreseeable losses. Refer to Note 2.16 to the Consolidated Financial Statements. The Group did not have any long-term derivative contracts.
     - There was no delay in transferring amounts required to be transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund by the Company and its Indian-incorporated subsidiary companies.
     - Management of the Company and its Indian-incorporated subsidiaries represented that, to the best of their knowledge and belief, no funds had been advanced, loaned, or invested from borrowed funds, share premium, or other sources to persons or entities outside the Group, including foreign entities, with the understanding that such intermediaries would directly or indirectly lend or invest in identified ultimate beneficiaries or provide guarantees or securities on their behalf.
     - Management of the Company and its Indian-incorporated subsidiaries represented that, to the best of their knowledge and belief, no funds had been received from any person or entity, including a foreign entity, with the understanding that the Company or its subsidiaries would directly or indirectly lend or invest in identified ultimate beneficiaries or provide guarantees or securities on their behalf.
     - Based on audit procedures considered reasonable and appropriate, nothing came to the auditor's attention indicating that the above representations contained any material misstatement.
     - As stated in Note 2.12 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.
