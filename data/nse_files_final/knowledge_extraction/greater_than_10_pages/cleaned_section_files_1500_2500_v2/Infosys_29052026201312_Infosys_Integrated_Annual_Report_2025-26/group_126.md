## Proposed acquisitions

1. On August 13, 2025, Infosys Singapore Pte. Ltd., a wholly-owned subsidiary of Infosys Limited, entered into a definitive agreement to acquire 75% of the equity share capital in Telstra Purple Pty Ltd, including some of its subsidiaries, together known as Versent Group. Versent Group is an Australian digital transformation solutions provider. The consideration, including earn-outs and deferred consideration, is up to AUD 233 million (approximately ` 1,335 crore), excluding retention bonuses and management incentives, and is subject to regulatory approvals and customary closing adjustments.

2. On March 25, 2026, Infosys Nova Holdings LLC, a wholly-owned subsidiary of Infosys Limited, entered into a definitive agreement to acquire 100% of the equity share capital of Optimum Achieve Holdings Inc., a healthcare digital transformation and consulting firm headquartered in the United States, along with its subsidiaries, including Optimum Healthcare IT, LLC. The consideration, including earn-outs, is up to $465 million (approximately ` 4,410 crore), excluding management incentives and retention bonuses, and is subject to customary closing adjustments.

## Update on acquisition completed after the end of the reporting period

On March 25, 2026, Infosys Nova Holdings LLC entered into a definitive agreement to acquire 100% of the partnership interests of Stratus Global LLC, an insurance technology partner serving property and casualty insurers and managing general agents, headquartered in the United States. The consideration, including earn-outs, is up to $95 million (approximately ` 901 crore), excluding management incentives and retention bonuses, and is subject to customary closing adjustments.

In April 2026, Infosys Nova Holdings LLC completed its acquisition of Stratus Global LLC. Because of the recent timing of the acquisition and pending valuation of identifiable net assets acquired and liabilities assumed, the initial accounting for the business combination was incomplete when the financial statements were authorized for issuance. Accordingly, all required business combination disclosures had not been made.

## 2.2 Property, plant and equipment

## Accounting policy

Property, plant and equipment are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment, if any. Costs directly attributable to acquisition are capitalized until assets are ready for their intended use.

The Group depreciates property, plant and equipment over their estimated useful lives using the straight-line method. Estimated useful lives are as follows:

| Asset | Estimated useful life |
|---|---:|
| Buildings (1) | 22–25 years |
| Plant and machinery (1)(2) | 5 years |
| Office equipment | 5 years |
| Computer equipment (1) | 3–5 years |
| Furniture and fixtures (1) | 5 years |
| Vehicles (1) | 5 years |
| Leasehold improvements | Lower of the asset’s useful life or the lease term |

(1) Based on technical evaluation, Management believes these useful lives best represent the period over which the assets are expected to be used. Accordingly, the useful lives for these assets differ from those prescribed under Part C of Schedule II of the Companies Act, 2013.

(2) Includes a solar plant with a useful life of 25 years.

Depreciation methods, useful lives, and residual values are reviewed periodically, including at each financial year end. Useful lives are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events that may affect asset lives, such as changes in technology.

Advances paid toward the acquisition of property, plant and equipment that are outstanding at each Balance Sheet date are classified as capital advances under other non-current assets. The cost of assets not ready for use by that date is disclosed under “Capital work-in-progress.”

Subsequent expenditure is capitalized only when future economic benefits are probable and the cost can be measured reliably. The cost and related accumulated depreciation are removed from the financial statements upon sale or retirement of an asset.

## Impairment

Property, plant and equipment are evaluated for recoverability whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. The recoverable amount, being the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value-in-use, is determined on an individual asset basis unless the asset does not generate cash flows that are largely independent of those from other assets. In such cases, recoverable amount is determined for the Cash Generating Unit to which the asset belongs.

If an asset is impaired, the impairment recognized in the Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss equals the amount by which its carrying value exceeds its estimated recoverable amount. An impairment loss is reversed if there has been a change in the estimates used to determine recoverable amount. The carrying amount is increased to the revised recoverable amount, subject to the amount that would have been determined, net of accumulated depreciation, had no impairment loss been recognized in prior years.
