## 2.20.1 Impact of labour codes

On November 21, 2025, the Government of India notified provisions of the Code on Wages, 2019; the Industrial Relations Code, 2020; the Code on Social Security, 2020; and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020. These Labour Codes consolidate 29 existing labour laws into a unified framework governing employee benefits during and after employment.

The changes include a uniform definition of wages and enhanced leave benefits. The Group assessed the financial implications, resulting in a ₹1,289 crore increase in gratuity liability, defined benefit plan liability arising from past service cost, and compensated absences. Because the legislation's impact is non-recurring, the Group presented the incremental amount as “Impact of Labour Codes” under “Exceptional Item” in the Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss for the year ended March 31, 2026.

The Group continues to monitor developments relating to the Labour Codes and will evaluate any resulting impact on the measurement of employee benefit liabilities.

## 2.21 Leases

## Accounting policy

### The Group as a lessee

The Group’s primary lease asset classes are land, buildings, vehicles, and computers. At contract inception, the Group assesses whether a contract contains a lease. A contract is, or contains, a lease if it conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration.

The Group assesses whether:

1. The contract involves the use of an identified asset.
2. The Group has substantially all the economic benefits from use of the asset during the lease period.
3. The Group has the right to direct the use of the asset.

At lease commencement, the Group recognizes a right-of-use (ROU) asset and corresponding lease liability for lease arrangements in which it is a lessee, except for short-term leases with a term of 12 months or less and low-value leases. Payments for short-term and low-value leases are recognized as operating expenses on a straight-line basis over the lease term.

The lease term is the non-cancellable period adjusted for options to extend or terminate the lease when exercise of those options is reasonably certain. The Group assesses the expected lease term on a lease-by-lease basis. Factors considered include significant leasehold improvements, termination costs, the importance and location of the underlying asset, and the availability of suitable alternatives. The lease term is reassessed in future periods to reflect current economic circumstances.

ROU assets and lease liabilities include extension or termination options when exercise is reasonably certain. ROU assets are initially recognized at cost, comprising the initial lease liability adjusted for lease payments made at or before commencement, initial direct costs, and lease incentives. They are subsequently measured at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment losses.

ROU assets are depreciated on a straight-line basis from the commencement date over the shorter of the lease term and the useful life of the underlying asset. They are evaluated for recoverability when events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. The recoverable amount is the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value-in-use. It is determined for an individual asset unless the asset does not generate largely independent cash flows, in which case it is determined for the relevant cash-generating unit.

Lease liabilities are initially measured at amortized cost at the present value of future lease payments. Payments are discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease or, when that rate is not readily determinable, the incremental borrowing rates in the country of domicile of the leases. Lease liabilities are remeasured with a corresponding adjustment to the ROU asset when the Group changes its assessment of whether it will exercise an extension or termination option.

Lease liabilities and ROU assets are presented separately in the Balance Sheet, and lease payments are classified as financing cash flows.

### The Group as a lessor

Leases for which the Group is a lessor are classified as finance or operating leases. A lease is classified as a finance lease when its terms transfer substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to the lessee. All other leases are classified as operating leases.

As an intermediate lessor, the Group accounts separately for its interests in the head lease and sublease. The sublease is classified as a finance or operating lease by reference to the ROU asset arising from the head lease.

For finance leases, finance income is recognized over the lease term based on a pattern reflecting a constant periodic rate of return on the lessor’s net investment. For operating leases, rental income is recognized on a straight-line basis over the relevant lease term.
