## 2.18 Revenue from operations

## Accounting policy

The Group derives revenues primarily from IT services comprising software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting and package implementation, and licensing of software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings. These are collectively referred to as “software-related services.” The Group also derives revenue from business process management services.

Contracts with customers are based on time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe arrangements.

Revenue is recognized when a customer contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable. Revenue is recognized when control of promised products or services, referred to as performance obligations, transfers to the customer, at an amount reflecting the consideration the Group expects to receive. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies the distinct performance obligations in each contract and allocates the transaction price to each obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. When that evidence is unavailable, the Group generally estimates standalone selling price using expected cost plus an appropriate margin.

Contracts may include variable consideration, including rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue recognized will not occur when the uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue from time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably:

- On a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
- Using a percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and the Group’s costs to fulfill the contract are uneven because the services are generally discrete and non-repetitive.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts whose performance obligations are satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Efforts or costs expended are used to measure progress because of the direct relationship between inputs and productivity. Progress is measured as:

> Costs or efforts incurred to date, representing work performed, divided by estimated total costs or efforts.

Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are monitored continuously over the contract term. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates change or are revised. Revenues and estimated total costs or efforts are subject to revision as the contract progresses. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

Customer billing schedules include periodic performance-based billing and/or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development, related services, and maintenance services, the arrangements generally treat software development and related services as distinct performance obligations. Revenue is allocated to each obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. Software development and related services performance obligations are satisfied as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, such as leasing guidance. These contracts are accounted for under the applicable specific guidance. When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is considered a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period based on the measure of progress specified in the contract.

Revenue from licenses is recognized as follows:

- Licenses providing a “right to use” are recognized when the licenses are made available to the customer.
- Licenses providing a “right to access” are recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When the license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When the standalone selling price for implementation is unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used.

When a license requires substantial customization as part of the implementation service, the license and implementation fees are treated as a single performance obligation, and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services related to software product sales is recognized as the performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the period in which the services are rendered.

Some integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Group first evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other relevant factors.

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price, or both, of a contract that is approved by the parties. A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for:

- As a separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price.
- As termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract when the additional services are not priced at their standalone selling price.

When a modification does not result in a distinct performance obligation, it is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract, meaning costs that would not have been incurred if the contract had not been obtained, are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them.

Certain eligible non-recurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as an asset when they:

1. Relate directly to the contract.
2. Generate or enhance resources that the Group will use to satisfy performance obligations in the future.
3. Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to expenses over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored regularly for impairment. Impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount of the capitalized costs.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss.

## Products and platforms

The Group derives revenue from products and platforms including:

- Finacle, a core banking solution
- Edge Suite of products
- Panaya platform
- Stater digital platform
- Infosys McCamish, an insurance platform
