## Independent Auditor's Report

To the Members of Infosys Limited

## Report on the Audit of the Standalone Financial Statements

## Opinion

The auditors audited the standalone financial statements of Infosys Limited, comprising:

- Balance Sheet as at March 31, 2026
- Statement of Profit and Loss, including Other Comprehensive Income, for the year ended March 31, 2026
- Statement of Changes in Equity
- Statement of Cash Flows
- Notes to the financial statements, including material accounting policies and explanatory information

In the auditors’ opinion, the standalone financial statements provide the information required by the Companies Act, 2013 and give a true and fair view, in conformity with the Indian Accounting Standards prescribed under Section 133 of the Act (`Ind AS`) and other generally accepted accounting principles in India, of:

- The state of affairs of the Company as at March 31, 2026
- Its profit and other comprehensive income for the year ended on that date
- Changes in equity
- Cash flows for the year ended on that date

## Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under Section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013. The auditors stated that they were independent of the Company in accordance with the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India and had fulfilled their other ethical responsibilities.

The auditors considered the audit evidence obtained to be sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their opinion.

## Key Audit Matters

Key audit matters were those matters that, in the auditors’ professional judgment, were of most significance in the audit of the standalone financial statements for the current period. These matters were addressed in the context of the standalone financial statements as a whole, and the auditors did not provide separate opinions on them.

### 1. Revenue recognition

Infosys’ customer contracts include multiple products and services. Revenue is derived from:

- Software development and related services
- Maintenance
- Consulting and package implementation
- Licensing of software products and platforms across core and digital offerings
- Business process management services

The Company assesses the services promised in each contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying distinct performance obligations, deliverables, and whether customers can benefit independently from those deliverables involves significant judgment.

For integrated service arrangements involving subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue from third-party products or services is recorded:

- Net of costs when Infosys acts as an agent between the customer and vendor
- Gross when Infosys acts as the principal in the transaction

The Company evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include:

- Responsibility for fulfilling the promise to provide the specified goods or services
- Inventory risk
- Pricing discretion
- Other factors relevant to determining whether the Company controls the products or services

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using either:

1. A straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period; or
2. The percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of customer benefits and the Company’s costs to fulfill the contract are uneven because the services are generally discrete and non-repetitive.

The method used to recognize maintenance revenue depends on the contractual promises and the nature of the deliverables.

Revenue recognition was identified as a key audit matter because it involves management judgment regarding:

- Identification of distinct performance obligations
- Whether the Company is acting as a principal or an agent
- Whether fixed-price maintenance revenue should be recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method

Refer to Notes 1.4 and 2.18 to the standalone financial statements.

#### Auditor’s response

The principal audit procedures included:

- Testing the effectiveness of controls relating to the identification of distinct performance obligations, principal-versus-agent assessments, and the method used to recognize fixed-price maintenance revenue.
- Selecting a sample of customer contracts.
- Reading contract documents, including master service agreements and other documents forming part of the agreements.
- Identifying significant contractual terms and deliverables.
- Assessing management’s conclusions regarding performance obligations, principal-versus-agent determinations, and the revenue recognition method.

### 2. Revenue recognition — Fixed-price contracts using the percentage-of-completion method

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using either:

1. A straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period; or
2. The percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and costs is uneven because the services are generally discrete and non-repetitive.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts, where performance obligations are satisfied over time, is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.

Under this method, the Company determines actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs required to complete the contract. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because there is a direct relationship between input and productivity.

Estimating total efforts or costs involves significant judgment and is reassessed throughout the contract period based on the latest available information. Provisions for estimated losses, if any, on uncompleted contracts are recorded in the financial statements.

The estimate of total efforts or costs required to complete fixed-price contracts was identified as a key audit matter because it has high inherent uncertainty and requires consideration of:

- Contract progress
- Efforts or costs incurred to date
- Estimated efforts or costs required to complete the remaining performance obligations

The matter required a high degree of auditor judgment and a higher extent of audit effort to evaluate the reasonableness of revenue recognized on fixed-price contracts.

Refer to Notes 1.4 and 2.18 to the standalone financial statements.

#### Auditor’s response

The principal audit procedures included:

- Testing controls over recording efforts or costs incurred and estimating efforts or costs required to complete remaining contract performance obligations.
- Testing access and application controls over time-recording, allocation, and budgeting systems to prevent unauthorized changes to recorded efforts.
- Selecting a sample of fixed-price contracts measured using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Evaluating management’s ability to estimate progress by comparing actual efforts or costs incurred with prior-year estimates and budgeted costs for fulfilled performance obligations.
- Comparing efforts or costs incurred with the Company’s estimates to identify significant variations and assessing whether those variations were appropriately considered in estimating remaining costs or efforts.
- Testing the consistency of estimates with delivery milestones, customer acceptances, and customer sign-offs to identify possible delays requiring changes to estimated costs or efforts needed to complete the remaining performance obligations.
