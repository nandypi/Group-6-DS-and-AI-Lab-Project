# Risk management report

The risk-related information in this section may not be exhaustive. It includes forward-looking statements and is subject to uncertainties that could cause actual results, financial condition, prospects, or operating performance to differ materially. Risks not currently known or not considered material may also adversely affect the Company.

## Enterprise Risk Management

Infosys’ Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) function supports the achievement of the Company’s strategic objectives by identifying, analyzing, assessing, mitigating, monitoring, and governing risks and potential threats.

The ERM framework supports strategic decision-making, resource allocation, and risk prioritization through qualitative and quantitative assessments based on the Company’s defined risk appetite. Risk impact and likelihood are assessed using a four-point scale. Risks are categorized and mitigated in order of criticality: critical, high, medium, and low. Low impact is the default risk appetite for all risks. Preventive, detective, and corrective controls, together with governance mechanisms, are used to keep residual risk exposures within acceptable limits.

The framework also identifies potential opportunities during risk assessments for further evaluation and action by the business. It covers strategic and strategy execution risks, operational risks, and legal and compliance risks, each of which may have internal or external dimensions. Risk indicators are used to identify risks proactively, taking account of their cumulative impact.

Infosys has adopted an integrated ERM framework implemented across the organization by the Risk Management Office. The framework incorporates practices based on COSO and ISO 31000 and is tailored to the Company’s business requirements.

## Risk Categories

| Risk category | Description |
|---|---|
| Strategy and strategy execution | Risks arising from strategic choices and from the successful execution of strategy, including risks related to the industry, competitiveness, target markets, market offerings, business model, and talent base. |
| Operational | Risks affecting policies, procedures, people, and systems that may impact service delivery or operations or compromise core values or business practices. Examples include internal-process inefficiencies, human rights, fraud, business disruptions caused by natural calamities, climate change events, human conflicts, system failures, and cybersecurity attacks. |
| Legal and compliance | Risks arising from threats to financial, organizational, or reputational standing resulting from litigation, non-conformance with laws, regulatory or geopolitical developments, codes of conduct, and contractual compliance. |

## Emerging Risks

The ERM framework includes a process to identify, assess, plan for, and monitor risks emerging from the internal and external environment. The process includes horizon scanning and SWOT analysis covering political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental aspects.

Emerging risks are monitored continuously and discussed periodically in risk councils and with the Risk Management Committee (RMC) of the Board.

## Integrated Enterprise Risk Management Framework

Risk management at Infosys encompasses the first, second, and third lines of defense. It is embedded in core business processes, day-to-day decision-making, development processes, and project execution. Risk management responsibilities are included in the roles of senior management, middle management, and employees who engage with clients.

The first line of defense evaluates risks and opportunities before key business decisions. It works with corporate ERM through risk councils and sub-risk councils established across organizational units.

The second line of defense includes functions such as the Information Security Group (ISG), Data Privacy Office (DPO), Quality, Delivery Risk Management (DRM), Business Continuity, Legal, and Compliance. These functions work with ERM to identify, assess, report, and govern risks. Risks are generally mitigated by the first line and tracked and governed by the second line. They are reported to executive leadership through councils including the Operational Risk Council (ORC) and the Legal & Compliance Risk Council (LCRC). Key risks are presented to the RMC of the Board quarterly.

The third line of defense consists of internal audit, which periodically reviews risks and controls to assess the effectiveness of the risk management process and reports independently. ERM inputs on key risks are considered when preparing the audit plan.

Infosys maintains a single hierarchical risk repository from L1 to L5, covering strategic to tactical risks. These risks are governed through the integrated ERM framework.

The Company periodically conducts an independent external assessment of ERM process maturity. The maturity assessment was conducted in March 2024.

## Risk Culture and Preparedness

Infosys uses the integrated ERM framework as a decision-support system for executive leadership. A multi-layered governance structure provides forums for risk discussions at different levels.

The Company conducts regular risk-awareness and training programs for employees and contractors. Lex, its in-house learning platform, provides risk-management courses accessible worldwide. Periodic tabletop exercises are conducted in key areas to assess preparedness for high-impact, low-probability risks.
