## Credit risk

Credit risk refers to the risk of default by a counterparty resulting in a financial loss. The maximum exposure to credit risk at the reporting date is primarily from the following:

- Trade receivables: ` 35,234 crore as at March 31, 2026 and ` 31,158 crore as at March 31, 2025.
- Unbilled revenues: ` 17,221 crore as at March 31, 2026 and ` 15,083 crore as at March 31, 2025.

Trade receivables and unbilled revenues are typically unsecured and arise primarily from customers located in the United States of America and Europe. The Group manages credit risk through credit approvals, credit limits, and continuous monitoring of customer creditworthiness. It uses the expected credit loss model and a provision matrix to calculate allowances for trade receivables and unbilled revenues, considering available credit reports and related information.

The Group’s exposure is influenced mainly by individual customer characteristics and concentration among its largest customers. Exposure is diversified, and no single customer contributes more than 10% of outstanding trade receivables and unbilled revenues.

Credit risk on cash and cash equivalents is limited because the Group generally invests in deposits with banks that have high ratings assigned by international and domestic credit rating agencies. Ratings are monitored periodically using the latest available ratings as at the date of approval of the Consolidated financial statements.

The Group’s investments primarily include mutual fund units, target maturity fund units, tax-free bonds, certificates of deposit, commercial paper, treasury bills, government securities, non-convertible debentures, and quoted bonds issued by government and quasi-government organizations.

The Group assesses counterparty risk using multiple criteria, including Tier I Capital, Capital Adequacy Ratio, credit rating, profitability, non-performing asset levels, and deposit base. These risks are monitored regularly under the Group’s risk management program.

## Liquidity risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that the Group will not be able to settle or meet its obligations on time.

The Group’s principal sources of liquidity are cash and cash equivalents and cash flow generated from operations. The Group has no outstanding borrowings and believes that its working capital is sufficient to meet current requirements.

## 2.12 Equity

## Ordinary shares

Ordinary shares are classified as equity share capital. Incremental costs directly attributable to issuing new ordinary shares, share options, and buybacks are recognized as a deduction from equity, net of related tax effects.

## Treasury shares

When an entity within the Group purchases the Company’s ordinary shares, the consideration paid, including directly attributable incremental costs, is presented as a deduction from total equity until the shares are cancelled, sold, or reissued.

When treasury shares are subsequently sold or reissued, the amount received is recognized as an increase in equity, and the resulting surplus or deficit is transferred to or from securities premium.
