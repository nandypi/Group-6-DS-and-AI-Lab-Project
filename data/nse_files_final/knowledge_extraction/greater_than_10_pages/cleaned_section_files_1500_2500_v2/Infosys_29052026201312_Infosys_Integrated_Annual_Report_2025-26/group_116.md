## Independent Auditor's Report

To The Members of Infosys Limited

## Report on the Audit of the Consolidated Financial Statements

## Opinion

We have audited the accompanying consolidated financial statements of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries (together, the “Group”), comprising:

- The Consolidated Balance Sheet as at March 31, 2026.
- The Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss, including Other Comprehensive Income, for the year ended March 31, 2026.
- The Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity.
- The Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows.
- Notes to the financial statements, including a summary of material accounting policies and other explanatory information.

In our opinion, based on the information and explanations provided, the consolidated financial statements:

- Give the information required by the Companies Act, 2013.
- Give a true and fair view of the consolidated state of affairs of the Group as at March 31, 2026.
- Present the Group’s consolidated profit, consolidated other comprehensive income, consolidated changes in equity, and consolidated cash flows for the year ended on that date.
- Conform with the Indian Accounting Standards prescribed under Section 133 of the Act and other accounting principles generally accepted in India.

## Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under Section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013.

The auditors were independent of the Group in accordance with the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India and fulfilled their other ethical responsibilities under the Act, related rules, and the ICAI Code of Ethics.

The auditors believe that the audit evidence obtained was sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for the audit opinion.

## Key Audit Matters

Key audit matters were matters that, in the auditors’ professional judgment, were of most significance in the audit of the consolidated financial statements for the current period. These matters were addressed in the context of the audit of the consolidated financial statements as a whole, and no separate opinion was provided on them.

### 1. Revenue Recognition

The Group’s customer contracts include multiple products and services. Revenue is derived from:

- Software development and related IT services.
- Maintenance.
- Consulting and package implementation.
- Licensing of software products and platforms across the Group’s core and digital offerings.
- Business process management services.

The Group assesses the services promised in each contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying distinct performance obligations, deliverables, and whether customers can benefit independently from those deliverables involves significant judgment.

For certain integrated services arrangements, contracts include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded:

- Net of costs when the Group acts as an agent between the customer and the vendor.
- Gross when the Group is the principal in the transaction.

The Group first evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before they are transferred to the customer. Factors considered include whether the Group is primarily responsible for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other relevant factors.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using either:

1. The straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
2. The percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits from the services and the Group’s costs to fulfil the contract is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.

The choice of method requires judgment based on the promises in the contract and the nature of the deliverables.

Revenue recognition was identified as a key audit matter because it involves management judgment in:

1. Identifying distinct performance obligations.
2. Determining whether the Group acts as a principal or an agent.
3. Determining whether fixed-price maintenance revenue should be recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method.

Refer to Notes 1.5 and 2.18 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.

#### Auditor’s Response

The principal audit procedures included:

- Testing the effectiveness of controls relating to:
  - Identification of distinct performance obligations.
  - Determination of whether the Group acts as a principal or an agent.
  - Determination of whether fixed-price maintenance revenue for certain contracts is recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Selecting a sample of customer contracts and:
  - Obtaining and reading contract documents, including master service agreements and other documents forming part of the agreements.
  - Identifying significant terms and deliverables.
  - Assessing management’s conclusions regarding the identification of distinct performance obligations, the principal-versus-agent assessment, and the revenue recognition method for fixed-price maintenance contracts.

### 2. Revenue Recognition — Fixed-Price Contracts Using the Percentage-of-Completion Method

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using either:

1. The straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
2. The percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits from services and the Group’s costs to fulfil the contract is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts, where performance obligations are satisfied over time, is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.

Under this method, the Group determines actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs required to complete the contract. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because there is a direct relationship between input and productivity.

Estimating total efforts or costs involves significant judgment and is assessed throughout the contract period using the latest available information. Provisions for estimated losses, if any, on uncompleted contracts are recorded in the period in which they are identified.

The estimate of total efforts or costs required to complete fixed-price contracts was identified as a key audit matter because it involves significant judgment and has high inherent uncertainty. The assessment considers:

- Contract progress.
- Efforts or costs incurred to date.
- Estimated efforts or costs required to complete the remaining performance obligations over the contract term.

This required a high degree of auditor judgment and a higher extent of audit effort to evaluate the reasonableness of revenue recognized on fixed-price contracts.

Refer to Notes 1.5 and 2.18 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.

#### Auditor’s Response

The principal audit procedures included:

- Testing the effectiveness of controls relating to:
  - Recording efforts or costs incurred.
  - Estimating efforts or costs required to complete the remaining contract performance obligations.
  - Access and application controls for time recording, allocation, and budgeting systems that prevent unauthorized changes to recorded efforts incurred.
- Selecting a sample of fixed-price customer contracts measured using the percentage-of-completion method and:
  - Evaluating management’s ability to reasonably estimate progress by comparing actual efforts or costs incurred with prior-year estimates of efforts or costs budgeted for performance obligations that had been fulfilled.
  - Comparing efforts or costs incurred with the Group’s estimates of efforts or costs incurred to date to identify significant variations and assess whether those variations were appropriately considered in estimating remaining costs or efforts to complete the contract.
  - Testing estimates for consistency with delivery milestones, customer acceptances, and customer sign-offs to identify possible delays in achieving milestones that could require changes to estimated costs or efforts needed to complete the remaining performance obligations.
