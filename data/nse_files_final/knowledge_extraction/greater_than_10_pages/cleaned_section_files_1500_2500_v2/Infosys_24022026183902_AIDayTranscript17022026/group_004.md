## Satish H.C.

Every major technology shift—including PCs, the internet, cloud, and digital—has rewired enterprise work and workflows. Infosys describes AI as the next enterprise rewrite.

Enterprise environments are complex because of their scale, internal and external fragmentation, heterogeneous systems, divergent operating models, regulatory requirements, and technology debt. Infosys argues that enterprise AI involves more than localized efficiency or user productivity. It requires reimagining systems, processes, and embedded ways of working, while changing legacy organizational structures.

A typical enterprise technology stack includes:

- **Systems of record:** Deterministic, programmatic capabilities that codify structured processes and enforce policies, governance, accountability, and compliance.
- **Systems of intelligence:** Capabilities that support engagement, collaboration, and transactions, generally requiring human interaction with data and workflows.
- **Non-deterministic or non-programmed flows:** Unique and unstructured activities requiring experience, judgment, and novel problem-solving. Infosys identifies this as an under-served layer that is suited to AI-led transformation.

Infosys states that enterprises require more than AI and data layers. AI is a major increase in raw capability but is not system-complete, requiring a multilayer transformation approach and purposeful orchestration.

Under the AI-enabled enterprise stack:

- Human and machine intelligence will be shared across layers.
- Systems of record will be accelerated to improve business and operational processes.
- Systems of intelligence will integrate structured and unstructured data into user journeys, business processes, transactions, and decision-making.
- Approximately 60% of the effort in an AI project is estimated to involve this integration, requiring deep industry and enterprise knowledge.
- Encoding intelligence and AI into workflows will increase automation and autonomy.
- A new layer, called **systems of cognitive work**, will support these AI-enabled flows.
- Humans will increasingly move from acting directly on data to governing and overseeing flows and decisions.

Infosys says its AI approach is intended to address technology debt and enterprise complexity, support enterprise-grade AI adoption, and expand its addressable market.

### Monetization and delivery approach

Infosys describes its AI playbook as operating at the intersection of intelligence, engineering, and domain expertise. It states that AI capabilities are increasingly commoditized because they are broadly available, while sustainable enterprise value depends on integrating AI into specialized workflows and preserving unique organizational knowledge and context.

Each enterprise has different data, processes, risks, and complexity, so AI will not unlock value uniformly across companies. Infosys says its client intimacy and understanding of client context support value realization, culture, and organizational change.

Infosys has developed an engineering approach to codify enterprise context and accelerate AI scaling. This approach is also intended to allow enterprises to retain and protect their unique context within their own environments rather than diffuse it into AI models.

The company cites its engineering expertise, frameworks, intellectual property, and patents, together with **Topaz Fabric** and specialized full-stack and FDE talent, as elements supporting AI readiness and adoption.

For a financial services client, Infosys found that the client already had a strong enterprise AI platform but faced an adoption gap. Infosys applied its **agent control framework** to improve the quality of generated code and address “AI slop,” which had contributed to poor organizational adoption. Infosys is working with the client to strengthen its enterprise AI platform and accelerate adoption.

Infosys has begun a talent transformation program to build an ambidextrous workforce combining engineering depth with the ability to reimagine work and workflows from first principles. The company also describes opportunities from a domain stack based on more than 25 years of industry-focused experience. Its objective is to integrate AI tools and plug-ins deeply into business flows and enable new outcomes through the agentic economy.

### Services stack and client example

AI is described as integral to Infosys’s delivery of every service. The services stack is powered by Topaz Fabric and is intended to productize and reimagine work and workflows through a human-plus-agent model.

Infosys reports momentum in several deal archetypes:

- Legacy modernization with reduced risk, greater cost predictability, and accelerated timelines.
- Large deals integrating operations, technology, and transformation.
- Organization transformation covering the enterprise stack and people.
- The AI-first DCC approach, which Infosys states it pioneered in the market.

Infosys also describes an end-to-end delivery model covering strategy, actionable roadmaps, execution, and outcomes. Its capabilities include both “above the line” business value and “below the line” efficiency.

A consumer packaged goods client wanted to grow revenue to approximately **$7 billion** and engaged Infosys to develop an AI operating model. The work included:

- An actionable roadmap.
- An executive AI value office.
- Risk management, governance, and assurance.
- A unified data foundation using Infosys intellectual property.
- An enterprise Agentic AI platform with guardrails.
- Rapid innovation and AI diffusion across business functions.

The client had **10 Agentic AI products** across functions including R&D, sales, and marketing.

Reported outcomes included:

- R&D agents for product formulation providing line of sight to **$50 million** in revenue.
- **$25 million** in cost savings from operational-work optimization.
- **40%** business productivity improvement in functions including procurement and marketing.

## Dinesh Rao

Over the prior **6 months to 1 year**, Infosys’s priority was to help customers move from AI experimentation to scalable, industry-level AI adoption.

Infosys states that AI performance depends on orchestration across systems of record, understanding complex business processes, and navigating deeply embedded legacy systems. In response to the complexity of client environments, it codified its services into **six integrated strategic pillars**. The stated intent is to guide customers through the full journey of scaling AI adoption.

### Pillar 1: AI strategy and engineering

Infosys describes the first pillar as AI strategy and engineering. The approach includes:

- Creating an AI blueprint.
- Establishing an AI transformation office.
- Identifying the business units and processes where AI value can be unlocked.
- Mapping business processes to expected value through Infosys’s value realization framework.
- Designing a technology-centric AI platform with suitable models.
- Establishing governance.
- Diffusing a standardized AI platform across multiple organizational layers.
- Selecting AI infrastructure purposefully and continuously monitoring AI operations.

Infosys states that AI deployment should begin with a top-down organizational strategy rather than only with software development.

Infosys recently worked with the CEO and Board of **Danske Bank** on its transition toward being AI-first and digital-first. The work included:

- Establishing an AI office with the Board and CEO.
- Developing a strategy through engineering.
- Supporting innovation labs.
- Identifying core processes for AI application, including KYC, fraud detection, and credit.

### Pillar 2: Data

Infosys describes data as foundational to AI adoption. Organizations hold multiple pools of both structured and unstructured data, including video and speech. Unstructured and multimodal data may account for approximately **60% to 80%** of organizational data.

Infosys states that projects spend most of their time on data, and that data can either accelerate the AI journey or reduce the speed of adoption.
