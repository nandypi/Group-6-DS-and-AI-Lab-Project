# AI Deployment Gap and Enterprise Transformation

## AI Deployment Gap

The section describes a gap between advancing AI technology and actual enterprise deployment. Professor Clayton Christensen referred to this dynamic as “technology overshoot,” where technology advances beyond customer needs. A related concept, “model overhang,” describes improving model capabilities without a corresponding increase in enterprise deployment.

AI technology is expected to continue improving because of substantial investment and competition, but enterprise adoption may not increase at the same pace. Addressing this deployment gap requires helping enterprises implement AI effectively.

## Talent Transformation

AI adoption is expected to change the nature of work rather than eliminate the need for talent. Roles may shift from quality assurance testing and development toward positions such as:

- AI engineers
- Forward deployment engineers
- AI leads
- Forensic analysts
- Data analysts

The key workforce challenge is reskilling employees for new forms of work. Hiring, training, and deployment practices are expected to change, while demand for people continues in different roles.

## Brownfield Modernization

AI productivity discussions often focus on greenfield development, where new code can be generated quickly. Enterprise environments are more complex because companies have substantial investments in existing systems, technical debt, data silos, undocumented dependencies, and incomplete documentation.

Modernizing brownfield systems is considerably more difficult than developing new systems. Productivity in large enterprises therefore differs from productivity in individual tasks or greenfield projects.

## AI Implementation Controls

AI can generate large volumes of output, including low-quality or unnecessary content. Without appropriate controls, organizations may create additional AI-generated legacy systems and face future cleanup requirements.

To ensure that AI investments produce genuine performance and productivity improvements, organizations need:

- Usage guidelines
- Quality gates
- Explainability
- Measures focused on actual productivity rather than AI tool usage

## Requirements for Enterprise AI Deployment

The section identifies several capabilities that remain important for successful AI transformation:

1. **First-principles thinking:** Employees should understand underlying concepts before relying on tools or black-box systems.
2. **Enterprise context:** Each company has a different legacy, system estate, business structure, and history of acquisitions or decentralized technology decisions. Capturing implicit knowledge and making it explicit is essential.
3. **Agnostic design:** Systems should avoid dependence on a particular tool that may become obsolete.
4. **Technical debt reduction:** Enterprises need to address existing technical debt and prepare their systems for transformation.
5. **Change management:** AI transformation affects organizations, business sectors, and employees, requiring strong leadership and effective change management.
6. **Cross-firm collaboration:** Organizations need to make knowledge held by different individuals explicit across the customer journey.
7. **AI engineering:** AI implementation requires an engineering approach as part of organizational change and transition.
8. **Productivity focus:** The objective is productivity generated through AI tools, rather than tool adoption alone.

## Opportunity and Execution Risk

The section characterizes the primary risk in capturing the AI opportunity as execution risk rather than opportunity risk. Key assessment questions include whether a firm has a plan to:

- Transform its talent
- Design services and products for AI-enabled markets
- Communicate effectively with customers
- Improve front-end client conversations
- Execute with speed and scale
- Adopt new mindsets and operating practices
