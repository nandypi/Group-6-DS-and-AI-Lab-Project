## Rishi Basu

Thank you, Ritu. The next question is from Mansee Dave from ET Now.

## Mansee Dave

Good afternoon, Salil and Jayesh. This is Mansee Dave from ET Now, ET Now Swadesh. My question is on demand visibility, tech spending and AI adoption. Looking at the constant-currency growth scenarios and commentary around fewer billing days and deal timing, how are clients thinking about calendar year 2026 tech spending, especially discretionary versus transformation-led programs? At the same time, the pace of enterprise AI adoption and the technology-spending outlook are key monitorables. How does the scenario look, and how are pricing models evolving?

## Salil Parekh

On demand, we see a good outlook and have had strong large deals. Our large-deals pipeline remains healthy. In Financial Services and Energy, Resources, Utilities and Services, we are seeing progress in our AI work and in the way deals have shaped up. We see a good outlook beyond this financial year into the next financial year.

In Financial Services specifically, we see discretionary spending and good traction across the market. We still want to see the other industries and segments begin to show similar traction, but these two areas are showing it today.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

As newer technologies evolve, new pricing models also emerge. We are seeing multiple pricing models develop, including models led by Infosys, such as outcome-based pricing and pricing specific to agents. It is still early to identify the specific models that will ultimately evolve, and companies are currently testing new pricing models.

## Rishi Basu

Thank you. The next question is from Shristi Achar from The Economic Times.

## Shristi Achar

I wanted to ask about the sharp decline in operating margins and whether the impact extends beyond the labor-code charges taken by the company.

There has also been a sequential decline in revenue contribution from the top five and top ten clients. What is driving this, and what should be expected over the next couple of quarters?

I also wanted to ask about the H-1B situation and reports of employees being deported.

## Salil Parekh

You want to start on the labor code?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Reported margins were impacted by the labor code. However, adjusted margins expanded by 20 basis points sequentially excluding the labor-code impact. On a full-year basis, adjusted margins remained at 21%, similar to the previous year's margin.

This was after absorbing investments in sales and marketing, which affected margins by 50 basis points, and the impact of lower utilization resulting from building capacity for the future. After absorbing both impacts, we maintained margins.

## Rishi Basu

Client contribution.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Sequential client contribution is not the best metric because of seasonality. Every Q3 typically includes furloughs, which can affect specific clients. The larger the client, the larger the impact of furloughs when they occur in that account. Year over year, we do not see a significant change in client metrics.

## Salil Parekh

No Infosys employee has been apprehended by any U.S. authority. A few months ago, one employee was denied entry into the U.S. and sent back to India.

## Rishi Basu

Thank you. The next question is from Chandra Srikanth from Moneycontrol.

## Chandra Srikanth

Regarding the employee who was denied entry and sent back, are you contesting that in any form?

One major trend this quarter has been acquisition activity, including Coforge's acquisition of Encora for $2.35 billion and TCS's acquisition of Coastal Cloud for $700 million. Can we expect more M&A activity? Are there attractive assets, and what is Infosys's M&A strategy?

## Salil Parekh

Over the last few quarters, we have made acquisitions in cyber, consulting and energy services, and we will continue with that approach. We have a good pipeline of potential companies under consideration and in discussions, along with strong balance-sheet support.

We are also looking at new geographies and expanding in service areas where we can go deeper. This approach will continue.

## Chandra Srikanth

On the ICE, are there any other details that you can share?

## Salil Parekh

That is what I have to share.

## Chandra Srikanth

According to the fact sheet, Infosys incurred ₹1,289 crores on account of labor codes. Has the full impact been absorbed, or will it be staggered?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Whatever was required to be accrued through the end of the quarter has been accrued in the books. The labor code affects multiple areas, including gratuity and other wage-related aspects. There will be an ongoing impact of approximately 15 basis points annually.

## Rishi Basu

Thanks, Chandra. The next question is from Haripriya Suresh from Reuters News.

## Haripriya Suresh

On H-1B, will Infosys make new applications, or will it primarily hire in the U.S. and use existing employees? Is there specific softness in Retail because of current conditions in the U.S., and when might recovery occur?

Also, Salil, your five-year CEO term ends in March 2027. What is the succession plan, and has it started?

The majority of our U.S. employees do not require a visa. We are continuing deployments and delivery using a mix of work in the U.S. and work in India, with no change to that approach. We are continuing the existing process and will examine future requirements as they arise.

In Retail, some areas are positive, while some clients are under cost containment within the subvertical. We are waiting for and working to convert the growing Retail pipeline into Retail growth.

On my own situation, no comment.

## Salil Parekh

No comment from my side.

## Rishi Basu

Thank you, Haripriya. The next question is from Avik Das from The Business Standard.

## Avik Das

I wanted more detail on the Financial Services commentary. Financial Services and BFSI appear to be improving in North America. Which sectors or subsegments are growing?

North America declined on a constant-currency basis. Was this client-specific or sector-specific, perhaps due to Retail?

There is also a view that new large deals may become smaller or less frequent as AI-led deals take center stage. How might margins develop across the industry and for Infosys over the long term?

## Salil Parekh

In Financial Services, we see good traction across most subverticals. We are seeing good traction with retail banks, mid-market banks, payments and mortgages. Overall, the demand environment is strong, although some areas are stronger than others.

We are also seeing good AI adoption across the spectrum among our large Financial Services clients. We recently announced a partnership with Cognition and are working jointly with them at some Financial Services companies.

There is no single specific factor behind North America's performance. It is a mix of different industries and plays. Energy and Utilities and Financial Services remain strong, while some other verticals are recovering over time but have not yet recovered fully.
