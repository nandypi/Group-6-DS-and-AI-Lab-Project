## Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing (“SAs”) specified under Section 143(10) of the Act. The auditor’s responsibilities under those standards are further described in the auditor’s responsibilities section.

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (“ICAI”), together with the ethical requirements relevant to the audit of the consolidated financial results for the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026. The auditor believes that the audit evidence obtained is sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for the audit opinion.

## Management's and Board of Directors'Responsibilities for the Statement

Management is responsible for the preparation and presentation of the related audited interim condensed consolidated financial statements for the three months and year ended March 31, 2026. This includes preparing and presenting the Statement to give a true and fair view of the consolidated net profit, consolidated other comprehensive income, and other financial information of the Group in accordance with the recognition and measurement principles laid down in Ind AS 34 under Section 133 of the Act, relevant rules issued thereunder, other accounting principles generally accepted in India, and Regulation 33 of the Listing Regulations.

The respective boards of directors of the companies included in the Group are responsible for:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records in accordance with the Act.
- Safeguarding the Group’s assets and preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting records relevant to the preparation of the Statement and its freedom from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error.
- Assessing the ability of the respective entities to continue as going concerns, unless the respective board intends to liquidate the entity, cease operations, or has no realistic alternative but to do so.
- Overseeing the Group’s financial reporting process.

## Auditor’s Responsibilities

The auditor’s objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the consolidated financial results for the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026, as a whole, are free from material misstatement. Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but is not a guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with SAs will always detect a material misstatement when it exists.

Misstatements can arise from fraud or error and are considered material if, individually or in aggregate, they could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of users taken on the basis of the consolidated financial results.

As part of an audit conducted in accordance with SAs, the auditor exercises professional judgment and maintains professional skepticism throughout the audit. The auditor also:

- Identifies and assesses the risks of material misstatement of the Statement, whether due to fraud or error; designs and performs audit procedures responsive to those risks; and obtains sufficient and appropriate audit evidence to provide a basis for the audit opinion. The risk of not detecting a material misstatement resulting from fraud is higher than for one resulting from error because fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or the override of internal control.
- Obtains an understanding of internal control relevant to the audit to design appropriate audit procedures, but not to express an opinion on the effectiveness of those controls.
- Evaluates the appropriateness of accounting policies and the reasonableness of accounting estimates made by the Board of Directors.
- Evaluates the appropriateness and reasonableness of disclosures made by the Board of Directors under the requirements of Regulation 33 of the LODR Regulations.
- Concludes on the appropriateness of the Board of Directors’ use of the going-concern basis of accounting and, based on the audit evidence obtained, whether a material uncertainty exists related to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the Group’s ability to continue as a going concern.
- Considers the related disclosures in the Statement and modifies the audit opinion if those disclosures are inadequate.

The auditor’s conclusions are based on audit evidence obtained up to the date of the auditor’s report. However, future events or conditions may cause the Group to cease to continue as a going concern.
