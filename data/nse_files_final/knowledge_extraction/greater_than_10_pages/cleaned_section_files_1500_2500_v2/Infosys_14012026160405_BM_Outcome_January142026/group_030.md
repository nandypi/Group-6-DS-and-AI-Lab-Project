## 2.9 Goodwill and Intangible assets

## 2.9.1 Goodwill

## Accounting Policy

Goodwill represents purchase consideration in excess of the Group's interest in the net fair value of identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities of the acquired entity. When the net fair value of the identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities acquired exceeds the purchase consideration, the fair value of net assets acquired is reassessed and the bargain purchase gain is recognized immediately in net profit in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.

Goodwill is measured at cost less accumulated impairment losses.

## Impairment

Goodwill is tested for impairment annually and whenever there is an indication that the recoverable amount of a cash-generating unit (CGU) is less than its carrying amount. Goodwill is allocated to the CGU or groups of CGUs that benefit from acquisition synergies and represent the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes.

A CGU is the smallest identifiable group of assets that generates cash inflows largely independent of the cash inflows from other assets or groups of assets. Impairment occurs when the carrying amount of a CGU, including goodwill, exceeds its estimated recoverable amount.

The recoverable amount of a CGU is the higher of its fair value less costs to sell and its value-in-use. Value-in-use is the present value of future cash flows expected to be derived from the CGU. Key assumptions in cash-flow projections include current economic conditions, estimated long-term growth rates, weighted average cost of capital, and estimated operating margins.

## 2.9.2 Intangible assets

## Accounting Policy

Intangible assets are stated at cost less accumulated amortization and impairment. They are amortized over their estimated useful lives on a straight-line basis from the date they are available for use.

The estimated useful life of an identifiable intangible asset is based on factors including obsolescence, demand, competition, industry stability, known technological advances, and the maintenance expenditures required to obtain expected future cash flows. Amortization methods and useful lives are reviewed periodically, including at each financial year-end.

Research costs are expensed as incurred. Software product development costs are expensed as incurred unless the project's technical and commercial feasibility is demonstrated, future economic benefits are probable, the Group intends and is able to complete and use or sell the software, and the costs can be measured reliably. Capitalized costs may include materials, direct labor, and directly attributable overhead costs required to prepare the asset for its intended use.

## Impairment

Intangible assets are evaluated for recoverability whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. The recoverable amount, being the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value-in-use, is determined on an individual asset basis unless the asset does not generate cash flows largely independent of those from other assets. In such cases, recoverability is determined for the CGU to which the asset belongs.

If an asset is impaired, the impairment recognized in net profit in the Statement of Comprehensive Income equals the amount by which its carrying value exceeds its estimated recoverable amount. An impairment loss is reversed in net profit if there has been a change in the estimates used to determine the recoverable amount. The asset's carrying amount is increased to its revised recoverable amount, provided that it does not exceed the carrying amount that would have been determined, net of accumulated amortization, had no impairment loss been recognized in prior years.

## 2.10 Business combinations

## Accounting policy

Business combinations are accounted for using the acquisition method under IFRS 3 (Revised), *Business Combinations*.

The purchase price is measured at the fair value of assets transferred, equity instruments issued, and liabilities incurred or assumed at the date control is transferred. It also includes the fair value of contingent consideration. Identifiable assets acquired and liabilities and contingent liabilities assumed are initially measured at fair value on the acquisition date.

Contingent consideration is remeasured at fair value at each reporting date, with changes recognized in the interim condensed Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income.

Non-controlling interests are initially measured either at fair value or at the non-controlling interests' proportionate share of the acquiree's identifiable net assets. The measurement basis is selected separately for each acquisition. After acquisition, the carrying amount of non-controlling interests includes the initial amount recognized plus the non-controlling interests' share of subsequent changes in the equity of subsidiaries.

Business combinations between entities under common control are outside the scope of IFRS 3 (Revised), *Business Combinations*, and are accounted for at the carrying value of assets acquired and liabilities assumed.

Payments related to options issued by the Group over non-controlling interests in subsidiaries are accounted for as financial liabilities and initially recognized at the estimated present value of gross obligations. These options are subsequently measured at fair value to reflect the amount payable when the option becomes exercisable. If an option expires unexercised, the liability is derecognized.
