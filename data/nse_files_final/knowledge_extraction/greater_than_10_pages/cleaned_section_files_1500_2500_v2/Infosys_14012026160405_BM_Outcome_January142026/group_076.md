## 2.17 REVENUE FROM OPERATIONS

## Accounting Policy

The Company derives revenue primarily from IT services, including software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting, package implementation, and licensing of software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings. Contracts with customers may be on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe basis.

Revenue from customer contracts is recognized when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their respective obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable. Revenue is recognized upon transfer of control of promised products or services, referred to as performance obligations, in an amount reflecting the consideration the Company has received or expects to receive. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Company identifies the distinct performance obligations in a contract and allocates the transaction price to each obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged when an item is sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. Where that evidence is unavailable, the Company primarily uses an expected-cost-plus-margin approach.

Contracts may include variable consideration, including rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue when the uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue recognition by contract type is as follows:

- Revenue from time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized using a percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of customer benefits and the Company's costs to fulfill the contract is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.
- Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts whose performance obligations are satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.
- For percentage-of-completion contracts, efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because they have a direct relationship with productivity. Progress is measured as costs or efforts incurred to date divided by estimated total costs or efforts.
- Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are monitored throughout the contract term. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates are revised.
- Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable, based on estimated costs or efforts to complete the contract.

Billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development, related services, and maintenance arrangements, software development and related services generally constitute distinct performance obligations. The transaction price is allocated to each performance obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. Software development and related services are recognized as the services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. These contracts are accounted for under the applicable specific guidance. When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. If standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected-cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period using a measure of progress based on the contractual promise.

Revenue from licenses is recognized as follows:

- Licenses providing the customer with a right to use are recognized when the license is made available to the customer.
- Licenses providing the customer with a right to access are recognized over the access period.

Software product arrangements generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When the license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. If the standalone selling price for implementation is unavailable, the expected-cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the license requires substantial customization as part of implementation, the license and implementation fee are treated as a single performance obligation, and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services related to software products is recognized as the performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Company acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Company first evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other indicators of control.

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price, or both, of a contract that is approved by the parties. A modification that adds distinct performance obligations is accounted for as:

- A separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price; or
- A termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract when the additional services are not priced at their standalone selling price.

A modification that does not add a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract, meaning costs that would not have been incurred if the contract had not been obtained, are recognized as an asset when the Company expects to recover them.

Eligible nonrecurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, that do not represent a separate performance obligation are recognized as an asset when they:

- Relate directly to the contract;
- Generate or enhance Company resources that will be used to satisfy future performance obligations; and
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to expenses over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services to the customer. Capitalized costs are regularly monitored for impairment. Impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount of the capitalized costs.

The Company presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Statement of Profit and Loss.

## Trade receivables and Contract Balances

The timing of revenue recognition, billings, and cash collections results in receivables, unbilled revenue, and unearned revenue on the Company's Balance Sheet. Amounts are billed as work progresses under agreed contractual terms, either periodically, such as monthly or quarterly, or upon achievement of contractual milestones.

Trade receivables are unconditional rights to consideration. Unbilled revenue from time-and-material contracts and fixed-price maintenance contracts is classified as a financial asset when the right to consideration is unconditional and payment is due only after the passage of time.

Invoicing for other fixed-price contracts is based on contractual milestones. Because the timing of revenue recognition differs from the timing of invoicing, unbilled revenue for these contracts is classified as a non-financial contract asset when the right to consideration depends on completion of contractual milestones.

Invoicing in excess of earnings is classified as unearned revenue.

Trade receivables and unbilled revenue are presented net of impairment on the Balance Sheet.
