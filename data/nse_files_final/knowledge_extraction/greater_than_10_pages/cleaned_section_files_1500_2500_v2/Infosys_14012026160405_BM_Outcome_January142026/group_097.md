# 2.16 REVENUE FROM OPERATIONS

## Accounting policy

The Group derives revenues primarily from IT services comprising software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting and package implementation, licensing of software products and platforms across the Group's core and digital offerings, and business process management services. Contracts with customers are entered into on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe basis.

Revenue is recognized when:

- The contract has been approved in writing by the parties.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- The contract is legally enforceable.
- Control of the promised products or services has transferred to the customer.
- The amount of revenue can be measured based on the consideration the Group has received or expects to receive.

When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies the distinct performance obligations in each contract and allocates the transaction price to them based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. When that evidence is unavailable, the Group generally estimates standalone selling price using expected cost plus an appropriate margin.

Contracts may include variable consideration, including rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue when the uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue recognition by contract type is as follows:

- Revenue from time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized using a percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of customer benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.
- Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts whose performance obligations are satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Progress toward completion is measured using costs or efforts incurred to date compared with estimated total costs or efforts, because there is a direct relationship between input and productivity.
- Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are monitored throughout the contract. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which they arise.
- Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recognized in the period when the losses become probable.

Customer billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development and related services and maintenance services, the arrangements generally contain distinct performance obligations. Software development and related services are recognized as the services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements that may be subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. Where hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices are unavailable, the Group uses the expected cost-plus-margin approach. Where the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period based on the promise in the contract.

Revenue from licenses is recognized as follows:

- Licenses providing the customer with a right to use are recognized when the licenses are made available to the customer.
- Licenses providing the customer with a right to access are recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on their relative standalone selling prices. When the standalone selling price for implementation cannot be determined, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. If the license is substantially customized as part of implementation, the license and implementation are treated as a single performance obligation and the arrangement fee is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed.

Revenue from client training, support, and other services arising from the sale of software products is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

Some integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Group assesses whether it controls the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer, considering factors such as responsibility for fulfillment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other relevant factors.

A contract modification is a change in the scope, price, or both of a contract approved by the parties. A modification that adds distinct performance obligations is accounted for:

- As a separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price.
- As termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract when the additional services are not priced at their standalone selling price.

A modification that does not add a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them.

Eligible nonrecurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as an asset when they:

- Relate directly to the contract.
- Generate or enhance resources that will be used to satisfy future performance obligations.
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to expenses over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored regularly for impairment. Impairment losses are recognized when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount of the capitalized costs.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss.
