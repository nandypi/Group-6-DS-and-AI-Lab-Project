## 2.16 Revenue from Operations

### Accounting Policy

The Group derives revenues primarily from IT services, including:

- Software development and related services
- Cloud and infrastructure services
- Maintenance
- Consulting and package implementation
- Licensing of software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings
- Business process management services

Customer contracts may be based on time and materials, units of work, fixed prices, or fixed time frames.

Revenue is recognized when:

- The contract has been approved in writing by the parties.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- The contract is legally enforceable.
- Control of the promised products or services has transferred to the customer.
- The transaction price can be determined and collectability is reasonably assured.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price based on relative standalone selling prices. Where standalone selling prices are unavailable, the Group generally estimates them using expected cost plus an appropriate margin.

Variable consideration, including rebates, volume discounts, and penalties, is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant revenue reversal.

Revenue recognition by contract type is as follows:

- Time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts: recognized as services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance contracts: recognized ratably, either on a straight-line basis or using a percentage-of-completion method, depending on the pattern of services and benefits.
- Other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts satisfied over time: recognized using the percentage-of-completion method based on costs or efforts incurred relative to estimated total costs or efforts.
- Licenses providing a right to use: recognized when the license is made available to the customer.
- Licenses providing a right to access: recognized over the access period.
- Annual Technical Services: recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

Estimates of transaction prices, total costs, and efforts are monitored throughout the contract term. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which they arise. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when the losses become probable.

Billing arrangements may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue recognized in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development and related services, performance obligations are generally satisfied as services are rendered because customers obtain control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. Where hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. Where the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period based on the contractual promise and related measure of progress.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include a license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services. When the license and implementation are distinct, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. If the license is substantially customized as part of implementation, the license and implementation are treated as a single performance obligation and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Revenue from client training, support, and other services is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied.

Where contracts include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when the Group acts as principal. The assessment considers whether the Group controls the specified goods or services, is primarily responsible for fulfilling the promise, bears inventory risk, and has pricing discretion.

A contract modification that adds distinct performance obligations is accounted for as a separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling prices. Otherwise, it is accounted for as a termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract. Modifications that do not add distinct performance obligations are accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them. Eligible nonrecurring setup, transition, or transformation costs are also recognized as assets when they:

- Relate directly to the contract.
- Generate or enhance resources used to satisfy future performance obligations.
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to cost of sales over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored for impairment, with impairment losses recorded when projected remaining operating cash flows are insufficient to recover their carrying amounts.

The Group presents revenues net of indirect taxes in its interim condensed Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income.
