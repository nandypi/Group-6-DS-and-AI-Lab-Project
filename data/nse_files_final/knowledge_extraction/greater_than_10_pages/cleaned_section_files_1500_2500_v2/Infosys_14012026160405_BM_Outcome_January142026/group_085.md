## 1.3 Basis of consolidation

Infosys consolidates entities that it owns or controls. The interim condensed consolidated financial statements comprise the financial statements of the Company, its controlled trusts, and its subsidiaries.

Control exists when the parent has power over an entity, is exposed or has rights to variable returns from its involvement with the entity, and has the ability to affect those returns through its power over the entity. Power is demonstrated through existing rights that give the ability to direct relevant activities that significantly affect the entity’s returns. Subsidiaries are consolidated from the date control commences until the date control ceases.

The financial statements of Group companies are consolidated on a line-by-line basis. Intra-group balances and transactions, including unrealized gains and losses, are eliminated upon consolidation. The financial statements are prepared using uniform accounting policies across the Group.

Non-controlling interests represent the portion of the net profit or loss and net assets of subsidiaries that are not directly or indirectly owned or controlled by the Company.

## 1.4 Use of estimates and judgments

Preparation of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements in conformity with Ind AS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies; reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses; and disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities.

Critical accounting estimates and judgments involving complex or subjective assumptions are disclosed in Note 1.5. These estimates and judgments may change from period to period, and actual results may differ from estimates. Changes are reflected in the period in which they are made, with material effects disclosed in the notes to the interim condensed consolidated financial statements.

### a. Revenue recognition

The Group’s customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is recognized and measured when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to those obligations. Determining the deliverables, whether the customer can benefit independently from them, and the allocation of transaction price involves significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and fulfilment costs is uneven because services are discrete rather than repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method.

For other fixed-price contracts, the Group uses the percentage-of-completion method. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs, because input and productivity have a direct relationship. Estimated total efforts and costs are reassessed throughout the contract period.

In certain integrated services arrangements involving subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Group assesses whether it controls the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer, considering responsibility for fulfilment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs required to complete the contract.

### b. Income taxes

The Group’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. The Company also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is required to determine income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred tax assets, management considers scheduled reversals of deferred tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies. The amount of deferred tax assets considered realizable could be reduced if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. See Note 2.15.

### c. Business combinations and intangible assets

Business combinations are accounted for under Ind AS 103, Business Combinations. Identifiable intangible assets and contingent consideration are measured at fair value to determine the net fair value of the acquiree’s identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities. External valuation experts conduct these valuations.

Determining the value of contingent consideration, option arrangements, and intangible assets requires estimates based on information available at the acquisition date and management’s reasonable expectations and assumptions. See Notes 2.1 and 2.3.

### d. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Group’s asset base. Periodic depreciation is determined using estimates of an asset’s expected useful life and residual value.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. See Note 2.2.

### e. Impairment of Goodwill

Goodwill is tested for impairment annually and whenever there is an indication that the recoverable amount of a cash-generating unit (CGU) is less than its carrying amount.

For impairment testing, goodwill is allocated to the CGU or group of CGUs that benefit from acquisition synergies and represent the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes.

The recoverable amount of CGUs is determined as the higher of value in use and fair value less costs to sell. Key assumptions in cash-flow projections include estimated long-term growth rates, weighted average cost of capital, and estimated operating margins. See Note 2.3.

## 2. Notes to the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements

## 2.1 BUSINESS COMBINATIONS

### Accounting policy

Business combinations are accounted for using the acquisition method under Ind AS 103, Business Combinations.

The purchase price is measured at the fair value of assets transferred, equity instruments issued, and liabilities incurred or assumed at the acquisition date, when control transfers to the Group. It also includes the fair value of contingent consideration.

Identifiable assets acquired and liabilities and contingent liabilities assumed are initially measured at fair value on the acquisition date. Contingent consideration is remeasured at fair value at each reporting date, with changes recognized in the interim condensed Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss.

Non-controlling interests are initially measured either at fair value or at the proportionate share of the acquiree’s identifiable net assets. The measurement basis is selected separately for each acquisition. Subsequently, the carrying amount includes the initial amount plus the non-controlling interests’ share of subsequent changes in subsidiary equity.

Business combinations between entities under common control are accounted for at the carrying values of the assets acquired and liabilities assumed in the Group’s consolidated financial statements.

Options issued by the Group over non-controlling interests in subsidiaries are accounted for as financial liabilities and initially recognized at the estimated present value of gross obligations. They are subsequently measured at fair value to reflect the amount payable when the option becomes exercisable. If an option expires unexercised, the liability is derecognized.

### Acquisition

During the nine months ended December 31, 2025, the Group completed two business combinations by acquiring 100% of the partnership or voting interests:

1. **MRE Consulting Ltd.** was acquired on April 30, 2025. The energy and business consulting services company is headquartered in Texas, U.S. The acquisition is expected to bring newer capabilities in trading and risk management, particularly in the energy sector.

2. **The Missing Link Security Pty. Ltd., The Missing Link Security Limited, and The Missing Link Automation Pty. Ltd.**, collectively known as **The Missing Link**, were acquired on April 30, 2025. The cybersecurity service provider is headquartered in Australia. The acquisition is expected to strengthen the Group’s cybersecurity capabilities and its presence in the Australian market.
