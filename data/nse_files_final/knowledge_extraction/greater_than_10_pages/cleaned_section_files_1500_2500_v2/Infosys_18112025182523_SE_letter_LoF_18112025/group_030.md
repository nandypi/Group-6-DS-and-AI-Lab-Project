## Classification of Income

Under the Income-tax Act, 1961, as amended by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024, consideration paid by a company to shareholders on a share buyback is treated as dividend with effect from October 1, 2024. The consideration is taxable in the hands of shareholders as dividend.

Section 46A of the Income-tax Act was amended with effect from October 1, 2024. For a share buyback, the consideration received by the shareholder is deemed to be nil for computing capital gains. Consequently, a capital loss arises for the shareholder equal to the cost of acquisition of the shares bought back.

The Company is required to deduct tax at source (TDS) or withhold tax (WHT) from the buyback consideration when payment is made. If shareholders do not submit the required documents, tax will be deducted or withheld at the standard prescribed rates under the Income-tax Act.

Shareholder residential status will be determined using information available with NSDL, CDSL, or the Registrar and Share Transfer Agent, KFin Technologies Limited, as of the buyback record date.

## Tax Deduction at Source / Withholding of Taxes

For resident shareholders, tax will be deducted at source under Section 194 of the Income-tax Act:

| Shareholder category | TDS rate |
|---|---:|
| Members having a valid Permanent Account Number (PAN) | 10% or the rate notified by the Government of India |
| Members not having a PAN or having an invalid PAN | 20% or the rate notified by the Government of India |

Under Section 139AA, individuals who have been allotted a PAN and are eligible to obtain Aadhaar must link PAN with Aadhaar. If PAN is not linked with Aadhaar, it may be treated as invalid or inoperative, and tax may be deducted at the higher rate under Section 206AA, currently 20%.

No tax will be deducted from resident individual shareholders if the aggregate of dividends and buyback consideration treated as dividend received during financial year 2025-2026 does not exceed ₹10,000.

No tax will be deducted where an individual shareholder submits Form 15G or Form 15H, subject to the conditions of the Income-tax Act. Form 15G applies to resident individual shareholders, while Form 15H applies to resident individual shareholders aged 60 years or more. PAN is mandatory for shareholders submitting Form 15G or Form 15H.

Resident shareholders may also submit a declaration or other document identifying the applicable Income-tax Act provision to claim lower or nil TDS. They may submit a Lower Tax Deduction Certificate issued by the Income Tax Department under Section 197 or another applicable section.

For non-resident shareholders, tax is required to be withheld from buyback consideration treated as dividend under Section 195, Section 196D, or another applicable section of the Income-tax Act. The current withholding tax rate is 20%, plus applicable surcharge and cess, or the rate notified by the Government of India.

Non-resident shareholders may submit a Lower Tax Deduction Certificate issued by the Income Tax Department under Section 195 or another applicable section to obtain a lower WHT rate.

Under Section 90 of the Income-tax Act, non-resident shareholders may claim benefits under an applicable Double Tax Avoidance Agreement (DTAA), read with the Multilateral Instrument (MLI), if the relevant treaty provides beneficial treatment for buyback consideration treated as dividend and all applicable conditions are satisfied. The documents required include:

- A copy of the PAN card allotted by the Indian income-tax authorities, duly attested by the shareholder, or the details prescribed under Rule 37BC of the Income-tax Rules, 1962 if the shareholder does not have a PAN.
- A Tax Residency Certificate for financial year 2025-2026 issued by the tax authorities of the country of tax residence, duly attested by the shareholder or authorised signatory.
- Electronic Form 10F, as required under Notification No. 03/2022 dated July 16, 2022, issued by the Central Board of Direct Taxes.
- A self-declaration that the shareholder has no permanent establishment in India under the applicable tax treaty.
- A self-declaration confirming beneficial ownership of the equity shares.
- A self-declaration confirming fulfilment of all conditions for claiming benefits under the applicable DTAA and MLI.
- Any other documents prescribed under the Income-tax Act for lower withholding, where applicable, duly attested by the shareholder.

The availability of DTAA benefits depends on the documents submitted.

The required documents had to be submitted to Infosys by November 26, 2025, at 5:00 PM IST. The Company also enabled a shareholder portal for electronic submission of the documents. No communications or documents would be accepted after the last date for tendering the buyback offer, November 26, 2025.

Shareholders with PAN can check their tax credit in Form 26AS through the Income Tax Department e-filing account or the “View Your Tax Credit” facility on the TDS Centralised Processing Cell website.

## Taxation for American Depositary Shares ('ADS') holders

Withdrawal of the equity shares underlying ADSs, re-deposit of equity shares not tendered or not accepted in the buyback against the creation of ADSs, and sale in the buyback of withdrawn equity shares may have tax implications in India, the United States, and other jurisdictions. The implications may differ from, and may be more onerous than, those applicable to sales of ADSs on the NYSE.

The tax treatment may depend on factors including the holding period, residential status, holder classification, and nature of income.

## a. India

### Withdrawal and Re-Deposit of Equity Shares by ADS Holders

Because there is limited precedent, it is unclear whether re-depositing equity shares against the creation of ADSs is subject to Indian taxation. The Company cannot assure ADS holders that tax will not be imposed or that an Indian bank, broker, or nominee will not withhold tax in connection with the re-deposit of the equity shares.

ADS holders should consider the potential tax implications in India, their country of residence, and any other jurisdiction in which they may be subject to tax before requesting withdrawal and re-deposit of equity shares.

## b. United States

### i. Withdrawal and Re-Deposit of Equity Shares by ADS Holders

For U.S. federal income-tax purposes, ADS holders generally are treated as the owners of the equity shares underlying their ADSs. Accordingly, withdrawal of equity shares underlying ADSs and re-deposit of equity shares that are not tendered or are tendered but not accepted in the buyback generally will not be subject to U.S. federal income tax.

Further information on the U.S. federal income-tax consequences relevant to U.S. holders of ADSs or equity shares is provided in the Company’s Form 20-F.

### ii. Tendering Equity Shares in the Buyback

Information on the tax treatment of tendering equity shares in the buyback was provided in a Form 6-K furnished by the Company to the SEC on or about the date of the Postal Ballot Notice and in the Letter of Offer.

U.S. holders should consult their own tax advisers regarding the U.S. federal, state, and local tax consequences of withdrawing and re-depositing equity shares, participating in the buyback, the treatment of dividends, eligibility for reduced tax rates in the year of the buyback, and the availability of foreign tax credits for any Indian tax.

## Securities Transaction Tax

Because the buyback is undertaken through the settlement mechanism of the Indian Stock Exchanges, the transaction is subject to Securities Transaction Tax (STT). The STT rate currently applicable to the purchase or sale of shares on a stock exchange is 0.10% of the transaction value.
