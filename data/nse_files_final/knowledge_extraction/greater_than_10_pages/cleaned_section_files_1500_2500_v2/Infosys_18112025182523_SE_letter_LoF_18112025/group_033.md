Passive Foreign Investment Company (PFIC)

A non-U.S. corporation will be classified as a PFIC for U.S. federal income tax purposes if either:

- 75% or more of its gross income for the taxable year is passive income; or
- On average for the taxable year by value, 50% or more of its assets produce or are held for the production of passive income.

The Company does not believe that it satisfied either PFIC test for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025, and does not expect to satisfy either test for the fiscal year ending March 31, 2026. This determination is made annually and depends on factors including the Company’s market capitalization. Accordingly, no assurance can be given that the Company was not a PFIC for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025, or that it will not be a PFIC for the current or future fiscal years.

If the Company were a PFIC for any taxable year, U.S. holders could be required to pay an interest charge together with tax calculated at ordinary income rates on “excess distributions” and on gains from the sale or other disposition of Equity Shares, unless a QEF election or mark-to-market election applies. Individual U.S. holders would also not be eligible for reduced dividend tax rates if the Company were a PFIC for the fiscal year of the dividend payment or the preceding taxable year.

If the Company is a PFIC and the Equity Shares or ADSs remain “marketable,” a U.S. holder may be able to avoid the excess distribution rules by making a timely mark-to-market election. Securities are considered marketable if they remain regularly traded on a national securities exchange, such as the New York Stock Exchange, or on a foreign securities exchange regulated or supervised by the relevant governmental authority.

A mark-to-market election applies for the taxable year in which it is made and for subsequent taxable years unless the securities cease to be regularly traded on a qualified exchange or the IRS consents to revocation. No assurance is given that the Equity Shares or ADSs will qualify, or continue to qualify, as regularly traded securities. The election cannot be made for lower-tier PFICs that the Company may own, including subsidiaries that are treated as PFIC equity interests for U.S. federal income tax purposes.

If the Company is a PFIC, a U.S. holder might also avoid the excess distribution rules by making a timely qualified electing fund (QEF) election and being taxed currently on the holder’s pro rata share of the Company’s income and gain. The Company has not provided and does not plan to provide the information necessary for a QEF election; therefore, this election would not have been available to U.S. holders.

Certain information reporting obligations on IRS Form 8621 may apply to U.S. holders if the Company is determined to be a PFIC, including in the year of a sale or disposition.

## Backup Withholding Tax and Information Reporting

Dividends paid, or proceeds from the sale of Equity Shares, to or by a U.S. holder may be subject to U.S. information reporting. Backup withholding, currently at a rate of 24%, may apply unless the holder is an exempt recipient, provides a U.S. taxpayer identification number, certifies that the holder is not subject to backup withholding, and otherwise complies with applicable requirements.

Amounts withheld under the backup withholding rules may be claimed as a refund or credit against the holder’s U.S. federal income tax when the required information is furnished to the IRS.

The tax summary is not a complete analysis of all tax consequences relating to participation in the buyback. U.S. holders should consult their own tax advisors regarding the application of U.S. federal income tax laws and any additional tax consequences under U.S. state, local, or non-U.S. laws, including estate, gift, and inheritance laws.

## 25. DECLARATION BY THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS

Declaration as required under clause (ix) and clause (x) of Schedule I under the Buyback Regulations:

- **25.1** The Board of Directors confirmed that there were no defaults subsisting in:
  - repayment of deposits;
  - redemption of debentures or payment of interest thereon;
  - payment of dividends; or
  - repayment of term loans or payment of interest thereon to any financial institution or banking company.

- **25.2** The Board confirmed that it had made a full enquiry into the affairs and prospects of the Company and formed the following opinions:
  - **25.2.1** Immediately following the Board meeting held on September 11, 2025, and the members’ resolution approving the buyback passed on November 4, 2025, there would be no grounds on which the Company could be found unable to pay its debts.
  - **25.2.2** Having regard to the Company’s prospects for the year immediately following September 11, 2025, and the year immediately following November 4, 2025, the Board’s intended management of the business, and the financial resources expected to be available, the Company would be able to meet its liabilities as they fell due and would not become insolvent within one year from either date.
  - **25.2.3** In forming its opinion, the Board took into account liabilities, including prospective and contingent liabilities, as if the Company were being wound up under the Companies Act, 2013 and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, as amended.

This declaration was made and issued pursuant to the resolution passed at the Board meeting held on September 11, 2025.
