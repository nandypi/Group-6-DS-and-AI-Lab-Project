An exchange of Equity Shares for cash generally will be a substantially disproportionate redemption with respect to a U.S. holder if:

1. The percentage of voting stock owned by the U.S. holder immediately after the exchange is less than 80% of the percentage owned immediately before the exchange; and
2. Immediately after the exchange, the U.S. holder owns less than 50% of the total combined voting power of all classes of stock entitled to vote.

In applying the Section 302 tests, each U.S. holder must take into account Equity Shares and ADSs constructively owned under applicable attribution rules. These rules may attribute to a U.S. holder Equity Shares and ADSs owned by certain family members and related entities, as well as Equity Shares and ADSs that the U.S. holder has the right to acquire by exercising an option. Certain family attribution may be waived in some circumstances. U.S. holders should consult their own tax advisors regarding these rules.

## Sale or Exchange

Subject to the passive foreign investment company (PFIC) rules, if a U.S. holder is treated as recognizing gain or loss for U.S. federal income tax purposes from the disposition of Equity Shares for cash, the gain or loss will equal the difference between:

- The U.S. dollar value of the amount realized; and
- The U.S. holder’s tax basis in the Equity Shares, determined in U.S. dollars.

Gain or loss generally will be long-term capital gain or loss for Equity Shares held for more than 12 months at the time of sale or other disposition. Any gain recognized generally will be income from sources within the United States for foreign tax credit limitation purposes. Long-term capital gains of non-corporate U.S. holders are generally taxed at preferential rates.

Capital gains realized by a U.S. holder upon sale of Equity Shares may be subject to tax in India, including withholding tax. Due to limitations on foreign tax credits, a U.S. holder may not be able to use such taxes as a credit against its U.S. federal income tax liability.

## Distribution

If a U.S. holder does not satisfy the Section 302 tests for recognizing gain or loss on an exchange of Equity Shares for cash, the U.S. holder will be treated as having received a distribution from the Company.

The gross amount of the distribution generally will be treated as dividend income to the extent it is made from the Company’s current or accumulated earnings and profits, as determined under U.S. federal income tax principles. These dividends will not be eligible for the dividends-received deduction generally allowed to corporate U.S. holders.

To the extent the Buyback exceeds the Company’s current and accumulated earnings and profits, the excess will be treated first as a tax-free return of the U.S. holder’s tax basis in the Equity Shares and thereafter as capital gain.

The Company does not intend to calculate its earnings and profits according to U.S. tax accounting principles. Accordingly, if the Buyback is treated as a distribution on Equity Shares, the distribution generally will be taxed as a dividend for U.S. tax purposes.

Subject to certain conditions and limitations, including the PFIC rules, dividends paid to non-corporate U.S. holders may be eligible for a reduced tax rate if the Company is a qualified foreign corporation for U.S. federal income tax purposes. A qualified foreign corporation includes a foreign corporation if:

1. Its shares are readily tradable on an established securities market in the United States; or
2. It is eligible for benefits under a comprehensive income tax treaty with the United States, including the applicable treaty.

The Company’s ADSs are listed on NYSE. The Company believes, but cannot assure, that the ADSs will be readily tradable on an established securities market in the United States and that the Company will be a qualified foreign corporation with respect to dividends paid on the ADSs.

Based on existing guidance, it is unclear whether a dividend on an Equity Share will be treated as a qualified dividend because the Equity Shares are not themselves listed on a U.S. exchange. However, the Company may be eligible for treaty benefits. A corporation is not a qualified foreign corporation if it is a PFIC in the current or prior taxable year.

## Proceeds Paid in Indian Rupees

If proceeds are paid in Indian Rupees, the amount included in a U.S. holder’s income will be the U.S. dollar value of the Indian Rupee payments, determined at the spot exchange rate between Indian Rupees and U.S. dollars on the date the proceeds are included in the U.S. holder’s income, regardless of whether the payment is actually converted into U.S. dollars.

Generally, any gain or loss resulting from currency exchange fluctuations between the date the dividend is paid and the date the payment is converted into U.S. dollars will be treated as U.S.-source ordinary income or loss.
