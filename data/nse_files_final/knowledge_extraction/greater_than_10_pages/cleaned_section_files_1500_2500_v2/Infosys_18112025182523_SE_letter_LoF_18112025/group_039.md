# Foreign Investment Scheme

The Letter of Offer dated November 18, 2025 provided for Infosys Limited to buy back up to 10,00,00,000 fully paid-up equity shares with a face value of ₹5 each.

- **Buyback offer price:** ₹1,800 per equity share, payable in cash.
- **Maximum aggregate buyback amount:** ₹18,000 crore.

Shareholders tendering shares confirmed that they had read and understood the Letter of Offer and authorized Infosys to buy back the offered shares and instruct the registrar to extinguish the shares.

Tendered shares were required to be free from liens, equitable interests, charges, and encumbrances. Shareholders also confirmed that no restraint, injunction, or other order restricted their right to tender the shares and that they were legally entitled to do so.

Infosys was not obliged to accept shares where the loss of share certificates had been notified to the company. If no physical shares were accepted, the company was authorized to return the share certificates. If only part of the physical shares was accepted, the company could issue a Letter of Confirmation for the unaccepted shares.

The buyback consideration would be paid after applicable income-tax deductions and verification of the validity of the documents. Payment would be made to the first-named eligible shareholder. Shareholders undertook to return any consideration wrongfully received.

Shareholders were responsible for determining and paying applicable taxes on gains arising from the buyback and for filing tax returns in consultation with their custodians, authorized dealers, or tax advisors. They also undertook to indemnify Infosys against any tax demand raised on the company in connection with gains arising to them from the buyback and to provide relevant taxability details, tax returns, and evidence of tax payment when requested.

For non-resident shareholders, the tender was subject to the Companies Act, 2013, the SEBI (Buyback of Securities) Regulations, 2018, and applicable foreign exchange regulations. Non-resident shareholders undertook to execute further documents and provide assurances required to give effect to the tender. The form was deemed to constitute consent for filings under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, and related rules.
