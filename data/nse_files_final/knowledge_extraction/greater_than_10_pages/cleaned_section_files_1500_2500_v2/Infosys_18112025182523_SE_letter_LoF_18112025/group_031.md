## Surcharge:

In case of individuals, HUF, AOP, and BOI, surcharge at 15% is leviable where total income exceeds ₹10 million (approximately US$0.11 million), and at 10% where total income exceeds ₹5 million (approximately US$0.06 million) but does not exceed ₹10 million (approximately US$0.11 million).

In case of foreign companies, surcharge at 5% is leviable where total income exceeds ₹100 million (approximately US$1.14 million), and at 2% where total income exceeds ₹10 million (approximately US$0.11 million) but does not exceed ₹100 million (approximately US$1.14 million).

The U.S. dollar amounts are based on the exchange rate of ₹88.06/USD as of September 19, 2025.

## Cess:

Health and Education Cess at 4% is leviable in all cases.

## Taxation for American Depositary Share Holders

As described in more detail in the Postal Ballot Notice, a holder of ADSs may participate in the buyback by withdrawing the ADSs from the depositary facility, becoming a holder of Equity Shares as of the Record Date, and then participating in the buyback.

There can be no assurance that Equity Shares offered by an ADS holder in the buyback will be accepted. ADS holders should consult their legal, financial, and tax advisors before participating, including regarding regulatory approvals and tax issues.

### Income-tax implications on withdrawal and re-deposit of Equity Shares

- There are arguments that conversion of ADSs into Equity Shares should not be subject to capital gains tax, but this position is uncertain because the law is not clear.
- Shares not accepted by the Company for buyback could be reconverted into ADSs. Two possible views exist on whether this reconversion constitutes a taxable transfer and attracts capital gains tax. Arguments support both views, although the view that reconversion is not a taxable transfer may be considered the better view.

## Certain Material U.S. Federal Income Tax Consequences

The following is a summary of certain material U.S. federal income tax consequences relevant to participation in the buyback by U.S. holders. It is for general information only.

For this discussion, “U.S. holders” include:

- Individuals who are citizens or residents of the United States.
- Corporations or other entities treated as corporations for U.S. federal income tax purposes that are created under U.S. law or the law of a U.S. political subdivision.
- Estates whose income is subject to U.S. federal income taxation regardless of source.
- Trusts having a valid election to be treated as U.S. persons under Section 7701(a)(30) of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended, or trusts for which a U.S. court exercises primary supervision and a U.S. person controls all substantial decisions.

This summary is limited to U.S. holders who:

- Hold Equity Shares or ADSs as capital assets, generally property held for investment.
- Are not residents in India for purposes of the Convention between the Governments of the United States and India for the Avoidance of Double Taxation and the Prevention of Fiscal Evasion with respect to Taxes on Income.

If a partnership or pass-through entity holds Equity Shares or ADSs, the tax treatment of its partner or owner will generally depend on that person’s status and the activities of the entity. Partners and owners should consult their own tax advisors.

The summary does not address:

- U.S. state, local, or non-U.S. tax laws.
- The potential Medicare contribution tax on net investment income.
- U.S. non-income tax laws.
- Special tax rules applicable to banks, insurance companies, regulated investment companies, pension plans, real estate investment trusts, financial institutions, securities or currency dealers, tax-exempt entities, persons liable for alternative minimum tax, or persons holding Equity Shares or ADSs in straddles, hedging transactions, or conversion transactions.
- Holders using a functional currency other than the U.S. dollar.
- Holders owning 10% or more, by voting power or value, of the Company’s Equity Shares.
- Controlled foreign corporations, foreign-controlled foreign corporations, passive foreign investment companies, or corporations accumulating earnings to avoid U.S. federal income tax.
- Persons subject to special tax accounting rules because income from Equity Shares or ADSs is recognized in an applicable financial statement.
- Persons receiving Equity Shares or ADSs as compensation or through the exercise of an option.
- Persons deemed to sell Equity Shares or ADSs under the constructive sale provisions of the Code.
- Certain U.S. expatriates and former U.S. citizens or long-term residents.

This summary is based on the Internal Revenue Code as in effect on the date of the document, U.S. Treasury Regulations in effect or proposed as of that date, and relevant judicial and administrative interpretations available on or before that date. It also assumes that the obligations under the Deposit Agreement and related agreements will be performed according to their terms. These provisions and interpretations may change, potentially retroactively, and could affect the tax consequences described.

Each prospective U.S. holder considering participation in the buyback should consult its own tax advisor regarding U.S. federal, state, local, and non-U.S. tax consequences.

### Ownership of ADSs

For U.S. federal income tax purposes, U.S. holders generally will be treated as the owners of the Equity Shares represented by their ADSs. Accordingly, converting ADSs into Equity Shares to participate in the buyback, and converting Equity Shares that are not tendered or are tendered but not accepted into ADSs, generally will not be subject to U.S. federal income tax.

### Tax Treatment of Buyback

An exchange of Equity Shares for cash by a U.S. holder pursuant to the buyback will be a taxable transaction for U.S. federal income tax purposes. Depending on the U.S. holder’s circumstances, the exchange may be treated either as:

- Gain or loss from the disposition of the Equity Shares; or
- A distribution from the Company.

Under Section 302 of the Code, a tendering U.S. holder will recognize gain or loss on the exchange of Equity Shares for cash if the exchange:

- Results in a “substantially disproportionate” redemption with respect to that U.S. holder; or
- Is “not essentially equivalent to a dividend” with respect to that U.S. holder.
