## What is the purpose of the Buyback?

The Buyback is being undertaken after considering the Company’s medium-term strategic and operational cash needs and to return surplus funds to shareholders in line with its Capital Allocation Policy. The reasons for the Buyback are set forth in Section 8.

## What are the significant conditions to the Offer?

Under the Buyback Regulations, the Company cannot withdraw the Buyback Offer once the public announcement has been made.

## Following the Buyback, will the Company continue as a public company?

Yes. Completion of the Buyback will not cause the Equity Shares to be delisted from BSE or NSE.

## How does an Eligible Shareholder tender his/her Equity Shares?

Eligible Shareholders must place orders through their Seller Members (stock brokers) on the separate Buyback acquisition window provided on BSE and NSE. They must also submit the documents required with their Tender Forms. Seller Members can enter orders for both dematerialized and physical Equity Shares. See Sections 22 and 23 and the Tender Form for details.

## Can an Eligible Shareholder tender some, but not all, of his Equity Shares?

Yes. An Eligible Shareholder may tender some or all of their Equity Shares. The maximum number that may be tendered is the number held on the Record Date. See Section 23 and the Tender Form.

## Once an Eligible Shareholder has tendered Equity Shares in the Buyback, can they withdraw their tender?

Yes. Orders may be modified or cancelled during the Tendering Period, which runs from November 20, 2025 to November 26, 2025. See Section 23.

## How does an Eligible Shareholder withdraw Equity Shares they have previously tendered?

An Eligible Shareholder may cancel or modify an order by contacting the Seller Member through whom the initial bid was placed. The request may be made through the shareholder’s registered email address or telephone number, or by visiting the Seller Member’s branch and submitting a written request.

## Do the Promoters, Directors or Key Managerial Personnel of the Company intend to tender their Equity Shares in the Buyback?

The Promoter and Promoter Group and persons in control of the Company may participate in the Buyback. The Promoter and Promoter Group expressed their intention not to participate in letters dated September 14, 2025, September 16, 2025, September 17, 2025, September 18, 2025, and September 19, 2025.

The Directors and Key Managerial Personnel are eligible to participate on the same terms as other Eligible Shareholders. See Section 11.

## If an Eligible Shareholder decides not to tender, how will the Buyback affect their Equity Shares?

Shareholders who do not tender their Equity Shares will have a resultant increase in their shareholding after closure of the Buyback. See Section 23.

## When will the Company pay for the Equity Shares tendered by an Eligible Shareholder and accepted in the Buyback?

For Equity Shares accepted in the Offer, the Company will pay the purchase price in cash promptly after the Offer expires and no later than five working days from closure of the Offer Period. See Section 23.

## Will the Eligible Shareholder have to pay brokerage commissions or other costs and expenses if they tender their Equity Shares?

The Buyback consideration received from the Seller Member for accepted Equity Shares may be net of tax deducted at source, costs, charges, and expenses, including brokerage. The Company will not bear costs, charges, or expenses incurred solely by selling Eligible Shareholders. See Section 23.

## What are the income tax consequences if an Eligible Shareholder tenders their Equity Shares?

### Indian tax consequences

The Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 shifted the tax liability for buybacks to shareholders, and the Company is not required to pay tax on distributed income.

Under Section 2(22)(f) of the Income-tax Act, the entire amount paid by a domestic company for buying back its shares is treated as dividend income in the hands of resident and non-resident shareholders and is taxable under the head “Income from Other Sources.”

Section 46A of the Income-tax Act, amended with effect from October 1, 2024, provides that consideration received by a shareholder for a buyback is deemed to be nil for computing capital gains. Consequently, a capital loss arises for the shareholder equal to the cost of acquisition of the shares bought back.

The Company is required to deduct tax at source (TDS) or withholding tax (WHT) when paying the total Buyback consideration to shareholders, which will be treated as dividend under the Income-tax Act. See Section 24 for the detailed taxation note.

### U.S. federal tax consequences

For a U.S. holder, as defined in Section 24, exchanging Equity Shares for cash pursuant to the Buyback will generally be a taxable transaction for U.S. federal income-tax purposes. Subject to the passive foreign investment company (PFIC) rules and the holder’s circumstances, the holder will generally be treated as either recognizing gain or loss from disposing of the Equity Shares or receiving a distribution from the Company.

Gain or loss will generally be long-term capital gain or loss for Equity Shares held for more than 12 months. A distribution will generally be taxed as a dividend to the extent it is made from the Company’s current or accumulated earnings and profits, as determined under U.S. federal income-tax principles. See Section 24.

### Withdrawal and re-deposit of Equity Shares

Whether withdrawal of Equity Shares underlying ADSs is subject to Indian capital-gains tax is not free from doubt. The tax treatment of re-depositing Equity Shares for the creation of ADSs, where those shares are not accepted for the Buyback, is also unclear.

The Indian, United States, and other tax consequences of participating in the Buyback are complex. Eligible Shareholders should consult their own tax advisors. See Section 24.

## What should an Eligible Shareholder do if they have lost their share certificate and wish to participate in the Buyback?

An Eligible Shareholder holding physical shares who has lost a share certificate must obtain a duplicate share certificate before tendering the Equity Shares. The shares can then be tendered in the same manner as other physical Equity Shares. See Section 23 and the Tender Form.

## Who can a shareholder talk to in case of any questions relating to the Buyback?

For clarifications or investor grievances, shareholders may contact the Registrar to the Buyback from Monday to Friday, between 10:00 a.m. and 5:00 p.m. Indian Standard Time, on working days. Contact details are set out in Section 30.
