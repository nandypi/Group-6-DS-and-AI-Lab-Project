## Intense competition in the market for technology services could affect our win rates and pricing, which could reduce our market share and decrease our revenues and profits.

Our revenues and profits depend partly on continued demand from existing and new clients and our ability to meet that demand competitively and cost-effectively. The technology services market is highly competitive. Competitors include:

- Large global consulting firms
- India-based technology services firms
- Software and solution providers
- Niche service providers
- In-house IT departments and Global Capability Centers (GCCs) of large corporations

Recent divestitures and acquisitions have increased consolidation in the industry. Competitors may have significant resources or more competitive offerings in emerging areas such as generative AI, agentic AI, digital design, cloud-based solutions and cybersecurity. Some competitors have also added offshore capabilities and may provide offshore and onsite services more efficiently.

Competition may arise in countries where we currently operate and in countries where we expect to expand, including from firms operating in Eastern Europe, China and the Philippines. Many competitors have:

- Greater financial, technical and marketing resources
- Higher revenues
- More extensive client relationships and technology partnerships
- Greater brand recognition
- Greater ability to hire key technology and management talent
- Potentially higher productivity from AI investments and lower client total cost of ownership (TCO)

These factors may create significant pricing pressure. We may need to reduce prices to retain existing clients or win new business. Our ability to compete also depends on factors outside our control, including competitors’ prices for comparable services and their responsiveness to client needs. Losing clients, failing to attract new clients or making pricing concessions because of commoditization or increased competition could adversely affect our business, financial condition and results of operations.

We have invested in building capabilities and training employees in emerging technologies, while many large competitors have made similar investments. Recent technological advances have reduced entry barriers, allowing new competitors to enter and disrupt the software services market. Failure to compete effectively and defend market share could negatively affect revenues and financial condition.

Clients often expect efficiency gains, volume discounts or lower rates as the scale of their business with us increases. This may limit our ability to pass cost increases to clients. Existing and new clients are also increasingly using third-party consultants with broad market knowledge to negotiate contractual terms. Macroeconomic uncertainties may further restrict our ability to increase prices and may lead clients to renegotiate existing contracts.

Clients may increase their in-house IT capabilities or establish or expand their own GCCs in India, Eastern Europe, Latin America or Southeast Asia. These centers may create opportunities for partnerships but may also erode part of our addressable market and increase pricing pressure.

If we cannot transform existing services using AI or scale new AI-first service offerings at appropriate price points, our win rates, market share, revenues and profits could decline. Increasing client demand for outcome-based pricing, under which fees are linked to specific business objectives or outcomes, may challenge traditional pricing models. Failure to deliver expected outcomes could adversely affect revenues and profitability.

## Our engagements with clients are typically singular in nature and do not necessarily provide for subsequent engagements.

Clients generally retain us on a short-term, engagement-by-engagement basis for specific projects rather than on a recurring basis under long-term contracts. A substantial majority of revenues are generated from repeat business, defined as revenues from a client that also contributed to revenues during the prior fiscal year. However, engagements are typically singular projects, requiring us to continually secure new engagements as current engagements are completed or terminated while expanding business with existing clients.

In some cases, we may transition clients from traditional services to newer offerings, including AI-enabled services, to maintain or expand client relationships and remain competitive. These transitions may reduce revenues from certain traditional services, involve pricing concessions or temporarily affect profitability.

Expanding the business may require a significant increase in the sales and marketing group, which would increase expenses without necessarily producing a substantial increase in business. Failure to generate a substantial number of new project engagements continually could adversely affect the business and results of operations.

Macroeconomic uncertainties, tariff changes, trade restrictions and geopolitical conflicts may cause clients to reassess spending and decide not to extend or renew business. With AI adoption, client buying centers have shifted or are shifting from IT to business functions. Business leaders, including the Chief Human Resource Officer, Chief Marketing Officer and Chief Financial Officer, are increasingly involved in project-award decisions. Failure to establish and maintain relationships with these decision-makers could adversely affect our ability to win projects.

## II. Risks related to the investments we make for our growth

## Our business will suffer if we fail to anticipate and develop new services and enhance existing services in order to keep pace with rapid changes in technology and in the industries on which we focus.

The technology services market is characterized by rapid technological change, evolving industry standards, changing client preferences and frequent introductions of new products and services. Increased adoption of AI and other emerging technologies may reduce demand for certain existing services, accelerate pricing and productivity pressures, and increase demand for new AI-enabled offerings.

In some cases, we may need to transition existing client engagements to more automated or AI-enabled services. This may reduce revenues from certain traditional services or adversely affect margins. Maintaining our competitive position, market share and revenues depends on our ability to:

- Anticipate and respond to evolving technologies and client demands
- Develop and commercialize new offerings
- Execute go-to-market strategies effectively
- Enhance delivery capabilities

Failure to adapt to technological changes, develop competitive offerings or bring new solutions to market in a timely and cost-effective manner could adversely affect the business, revenues and profitability. Developing and deploying new technologies and services may require significant upfront investments. We may be unable to recover some or all of these investments if offerings are unsuccessful, fail to achieve market acceptance or become obsolete because of competing technologies or services.

Evolving regulatory frameworks in the technology sector may affect new service offerings and require additional compliance and operational costs.

Macroeconomic conditions, tariff changes, trade restrictions, geopolitical uncertainties and economic downturns may lead us to reduce expenditures on ideating, incubating, developing and marketing new service offerings, which could adversely affect long-term growth prospects.

## Our investments in or use of AI technologies may not be successful and may present business, financial, legal, and reputational risks.

The proliferation of AI technologies is significantly affecting the industry. Our ability to develop, integrate and deploy AI-enabled solutions will affect our competitive position, financial performance and long-term growth prospects. We have incurred and expect to continue incurring significant costs for AI capabilities, including technology, infrastructure, talent, compliance and risk management.

Failure to successfully develop, commercialize or scale AI-enabled solutions could adversely affect revenues, competitive position and long-term growth prospects. Increased adoption of AI and automation may:

- Reduce demand for traditional technology services
- Change client purchasing behavior
- Compress pricing and margins
- Reduce demand for labor-based delivery models
- Increase expectations for productivity improvements and outcome-based pricing

Clients may also develop in-house AI capabilities or rely on lower-cost competitors, open-source technologies or AI platform providers, further reducing demand for our services.

Our AI offerings and internal use of AI may create operational, legal, regulatory and reputational risks. AI technologies may generate inaccurate, flawed, biased or unreliable outputs. AI system failures or perceived failures could lead to reputational harm, client disputes, regulatory scrutiny or legal liability. AI use may also increase risks related to:

- Intellectual property infringement
- Confidentiality
- Cybersecurity
- Privacy and data protection
- Misuse of sensitive information

Employees may independently use unauthorized AI tools or large language model applications to process client or proprietary data outside approved systems and information security controls. This could expose sensitive information to third-party platforms and potentially result in violations of data protection laws or breaches of contractual obligations. An incident involving exposure of client data may require notification to affected clients and regulators and could adversely affect reputation and business.

Certain AI-enabled offerings rely on third-party AI providers, cloud providers and technology partners. Risks include:

- Outages and performance limitations
- Licensing restrictions
- Changes in pricing or contractual terms
- Regulatory developments
- Insufficient indemnification protections
- Providers offering competing services

Clients may seek broader contractual protections, warranties, indemnities or liability commitments for AI-related services, increasing our legal and financial exposure.

AI-related laws and regulations, including the EU AI Act and emerging regulations in other jurisdictions, are evolving rapidly and may be inconsistent across jurisdictions. Compliance may increase costs, restrict the development or deployment of AI-enabled offerings, and expose us to investigations, litigation, fines, penalties, remediation costs or reputational harm in cases of actual or alleged non-compliance.
