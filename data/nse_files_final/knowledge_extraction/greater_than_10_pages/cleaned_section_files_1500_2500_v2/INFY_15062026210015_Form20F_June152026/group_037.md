## Risk Management Procedures

Infosys manages market risk through treasury operations. The objectives and policies of treasury operations are approved by senior management and the Audit Committee. Treasury activities include managing cash resources, implementing hedging strategies for foreign currency exposures, developing borrowing strategies, if any, and ensuring compliance with market risk limits and policies.

## Components of Market Risk

### Exchange rate risk

Infosys’ exposure to market risk arises primarily from exchange rate risk. Although its functional currency is the Indian rupee, it generates a major portion of revenue in foreign currencies, particularly the U.S. dollar, euro, Australian dollar, and United Kingdom pound sterling. A significant portion of expenses is incurred in Indian rupees and U.S. dollars.

## Liquidity and capital resources

The exchange rate between the Indian rupee and the U.S. dollar has changed substantially in recent years and may fluctuate substantially in the future. Results of operations may be adversely affected if the Indian rupee appreciates against the U.S. dollar.

| Currency | Fiscal 2026 revenue | Fiscal 2025 revenue |
|---|---:|---:|
| U.S. dollar | 62.9% | 64.2% |
| Euro | 17.4% | 16.8% |
| Australian dollar | 4.6% | 4.6% |
| United Kingdom pound sterling | 4.5% | 3.8% |

Exchange rate risk primarily arises from foreign currency revenues, receivables, and payables.

Infosys uses derivative financial instruments, including foreign exchange forward and option contracts, to mitigate the risk of changes in exchange rates on foreign currency exposures. Counterparties for these contracts are generally banks. Refer to Note 2.3 in Item 18 of this Annual Report for details of outstanding foreign exchange forward and option contracts.

The forward and option contracts typically mature within 12 months, must be settled on the maturity date, and may be cancelled subject to receipt or payment of gains or losses arising from the difference between the contract exchange rate and the market exchange rate on the cancellation date. Infosys uses these derivative instruments only as a hedging mechanism and not for speculative purposes.

Infosys may not purchase adequate instruments to insulate itself from foreign exchange currency risks, and such instruments may not perform adequately as a hedging mechanism. Changes in Reserve Bank of India policies may limit its ability to adequately hedge foreign currency exposures. Infosys may adopt more active hedging policies in the future and has done so in the past.

## Size and composition of the Board

The Securities Exchange Board of India (SEBI) Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements Regulations, 2015, as amended, mandate that, for a company with a non-executive Chairman who is a Promoter, at least half of the Board of Directors should be independent directors. The Board of Directors of the top 1,000 listed companies, effective April 1, 2020, must also have at least one independent woman director.

As of June 15, 2026, the Board comprised ten members:

- One non-executive and non-independent Chairman
- One Chief Executive Officer and Managing Director
- Eight Independent Directors

Independent directors constituted 80% of the Board, exceeding the requirements of the Indian Companies Act, 2013, the Listing Regulations, and Rule 303A.02 of the NYSE Listed Company Manual. Three of the ten directors were independent women, representing 30% of the Board. The Board periodically evaluates the need for changes to its size and composition.

## Definition of Independent Directors

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013 and the Listing Regulations, an independent director is a person who is not a promoter, employee, or key managerial personnel of the company or its subsidiaries. The person must also not have a material pecuniary relationship exceeding 10% of total income, or such other amount as may be prescribed, with the company or its holding, subsidiary, or associate company, or their promoters or directors, during the two immediately preceding financial years or the current financial year, apart from receiving remuneration as an independent director.

Infosys applies these definitions in addition to the definitions of an independent director under the NYSE Listed Company Manual, the Sarbanes-Oxley Act, and U.S. securities laws, due to its NYSE listing.

Based on disclosures received from all independent directors and the Board’s assessment, the independent directors fulfilled the conditions specified in the Indian Companies Act, 2013, the Listing Regulations, and the NYSE Listed Company Manual and were independent of management.

## Key Board qualifications, expertise and attributes

The qualifications, skills, and attributes considered when nominating candidates to the Board include:

- **Financial:** Leadership of a financial firm or management of an enterprise’s finance function, including proficiency in complex financial management, capital allocation, and financial reporting, or experience supervising a principal financial officer, principal accounting officer, controller, public accountant, auditor, or similar role.
- **Diversity:** Gender, ethnic, geographic, cultural, or other perspectives that broaden the Board’s understanding of clients, partners, employees, governments, and other stakeholders worldwide.
- **Global Business:** Experience driving business success in international markets and understanding diverse business environments, economic conditions, cultures, regulatory frameworks, and global market opportunities.
- **Leadership:** Extensive leadership experience in a significant enterprise, including organizational understanding, process management, strategic planning, risk management, talent development, succession planning, change management, and long-term growth.
- **Information Technology:** Significant technology experience, including anticipating technological trends, generating disruptive innovation, and extending or creating business models.
- **Mergers and Acquisitions:** Experience leading growth through acquisitions and other business combinations, assessing build-or-buy decisions, evaluating strategic and cultural fit, valuing transactions, and assessing operational integration plans.
- **Board Service and Governance:** Public company board experience and understanding of board and management accountability, shareholder interests, and governance practices.
- **Sales and Marketing:** Experience developing strategies to grow sales and market share, build brand awareness and equity, and enhance enterprise reputation.
- **Sustainability, and Environment, Social and Governance (ESG):** Experience leading organizational sustainability and ESG initiatives and integrating them into company strategy.
- **Risk Management:** Experience identifying and evaluating significant risks to business strategy and assessing management actions to mitigate strategic, legal, compliance, and operational risks.
- **Cybersecurity:** Experience assessing and managing cybersecurity risks and implementing cybersecurity policies, procedures, and strategies.
