## Foreign Portfolio Investment

A single FPI, including its investor group, may purchase equity shares of an Indian company only below 10% of the company’s paid-up equity capital on a fully diluted basis.

An investment by a person resident outside India in a listed Indian company is categorized as foreign portfolio investment if it is:

- Less than 10% of the listed company’s post-issue paid-up equity share capital on a fully diluted basis; or
- Less than 10% of the paid-up value of each series of equity instruments of the listed company.

Foreign portfolio investment and investment by an entity registered with SEBI as an FPI are distinct categories. All investments by a SEBI-registered FPI are necessarily categorized as foreign portfolio investments. However, investments by entities not registered with SEBI as FPIs may also qualify as foreign portfolio investments if they remain within the prescribed limits.

## Offshore Derivative Instruments

Subject to applicable Indian laws, rules, regulations, guidelines, approvals, and Regulation 21 of the FPI Regulations, an FPI may issue, subscribe to, or otherwise deal in offshore derivative instruments, directly or indirectly, only when:

1. The instruments are issued by persons registered as Category I FPIs.
2. The instruments are issued only to persons eligible for registration as Category I FPIs.
3. The instruments are issued after compliance with know-your-client requirements.
4. Other conditions prescribed by SEBI from time to time are satisfied.

An FPI issuing offshore derivative instruments must ensure that any transfer is made only to persons meeting these requirements. Prior consent of the issuing FPI is required for a transfer, except where the transferee has been pre-approved by the FPI.

Category II FPIs, and persons that would be categorized as Category II FPIs, cannot deal in offshore derivative instruments.

## FPI Transactions and Permitted Exceptions

Under the Non-Debt Rules, an FPI may purchase equity instruments of a listed Indian company through a recognized stock exchange in India, a public offer, or a private placement, subject to applicable individual and aggregate limits and conditions.

FPI securities transactions must generally be conducted through SEBI-registered stockbrokers. Exceptions include:

- Transactions in Government securities and other securities under RBI jurisdiction, conducted as specified by the RBI.
- Sales in response to an acquirer’s offer under the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011.
- Sales in response to an offer by a promoter or acquirer under the erstwhile SEBI (Delisting of Equity Shares) Regulations, 2009.
- Sales under the SEBI (Buy-back of Securities) Regulations, 2018.
- Divestment in response to offers by Indian companies under the applicable guidelines for issuing American Depository Receipts or Global Depository Receipts in overseas markets.
- Bids for, or acquisitions of, securities offered for disinvestment by the Central or a State Government.
- Transactions under an agreement with a merchant banker for market making or subscribing to an unsubscribed portion of an issue under Chapter IX of the SEBI (ICDR) Regulations, 2018.
- Transactions in corporate bonds.
- Transactions on the electronic book provider platform of recognized stock exchanges.
- Transactions to receive and hold unlisted securities under Regulation 20(2) of the FPI Regulations.
- Transactions in unlisted securities received through involuntary corporate actions, including mergers or demergers approved under the Indian Companies Act, 2013 and applicable SEBI guidelines.
- Transactions in unlisted securities received through implementation of a resolution plan approved under the Indian Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, or under Government of India, RBI, or other applicable regulatory guidelines for debt resolution. Such unlisted holdings are treated as FDI.
- Transfers of rights entitlements.
- Purchases of illiquid, suspended, or delisted securities.
- Transactions with other registered FPIs that have a multi-investment-manager structure, the same beneficial owner, and a common Permanent Account Number.
- Any other transaction specified by SEBI.

## Investment Limits and Breaches

The total holding of each FPI or investor group must be below:

- 10% of an Indian company’s total paid-up equity capital on a fully diluted basis; or
- 10% of the paid-up value of each series of debentures, preference shares, or share warrants issued by an Indian company.

An FPI that breaches the prescribed 10% limit may divest its holdings within five trading days from the settlement date of the trades causing the breach.

If the FPI does not divest:

- The entire investment in the company by the FPI and its investor group is treated as FDI.
- The FPI and its investor group may not make further portfolio investments in the company.

Divestment and reclassification of FPI investment as FDI remain subject to conditions specified by SEBI and the RBI.

## Aggregate FPI Investment Limits

Effective April 1, 2020, the aggregate limit for FPI investment is the sectoral cap applicable to the Indian company under the FDI route.

Before March 31, 2020, a company could establish a lower aggregate limit of 24%, 49%, or 74%, with approval from its board of directors and shareholders through a special resolution. A company that had reduced its limit could subsequently increase it to 49%, 74%, the applicable sectoral cap, or the statutory ceiling, as applicable, with the same approvals. Once increased to a higher threshold, the limit could not be reduced to a lower threshold.

For an Indian company in a sector where FDI is prohibited, the aggregate limit is 24%.

Aggregate foreign portfolio investment of up to 49% of paid-up capital on a fully diluted basis, or the applicable sectoral or statutory cap, whichever is lower, is not subject to Government approval or sectoral conditions if the investment does not result in:

- Transfer of ownership and control of the resident Indian company from resident Indian citizens; or
- Transfer of ownership or control to persons resident outside India.

Other investments by persons resident outside India are subject to Government approval and sectoral conditions under the Non-Debt Rules.

## Income Tax Act Treatment

Section 9 of the Income Tax Act was amended by the Finance Act, 2017 to clarify that Explanation 5 does not apply to an asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in an FII referred to in Section 115AD(a), for assessment years beginning on or after April 1, 2012, but before April 1, 2015. The amendment applies retrospectively from April 1, 2012, beginning with assessment year 2012–13.

Section 9 was further amended to clarify that Explanation 5 does not apply to an asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in a Category I or Category II FPI under the SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2014. This amendment applies retrospectively from April 1, 2015, beginning with assessment year 2015–16.

The Finance Act 2020, dated March 27, 2020, modified the proviso to Explanation 5 with effect from April 1, 2020, applying from assessment year 2020–21 onward. Explanation 5 does not apply to assets or capital assets held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in:

- An FII referred to in Section 115AD(a), for assessment years beginning on or after April 1, 2012, but before April 1, 2015.
- A Category I or Category II FPI under the SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2014, before those regulations were repealed.
- A Category I foreign portfolio investor under the SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2019.

## Provisions on Changes in Capital

Our authorized capital can be altered by an ordinary resolution of the shareholders in a general meeting.

The additional issue of shares is subject to the pre-emptive rights of the shareholders.

The company may also:

- Increase its share capital.
- Consolidate its share capital into shares of a larger face value than its existing shares.
- Subdivide its shares by reducing their par value.

These actions require an ordinary resolution of the shareholders in a general meeting.
