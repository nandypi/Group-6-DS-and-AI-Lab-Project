The deposit agreement disclaims liability for:

- Failure to carry out voting instructions, the manner in which a vote is cast, the effect of a vote, failure to determine whether a distribution or action is lawful or practicable, or failure to exercise voting rights.
- Failure to provide timely notices, the content of information submitted for distribution, or inaccuracies in translations.
- Investment risks associated with deposited securities or the creditworthiness of third parties.
- Tax consequences arising from ownership of ADSs, equity shares, or deposited securities.
- Acts or omissions of a successor depositary.

Each party to the deposit agreement, including ADS holders, beneficial owners, and holders of interests in ADRs, irrevocably waives, to the fullest extent permitted by applicable law, any right to a jury trial in lawsuits or proceedings against the depositary or Infosys relating to the shares, ADSs, or deposit agreement.

Infosys and the depositary agree to indemnify each other under certain circumstances.

## Requirements for Depositary Actions

Before the depositary issues, delivers, or registers a transfer of an ADS; splits, subdivides, or combines ADSs; makes a distribution on an ADS; or permits withdrawal of equity shares, the depositary or Infosys may require:

- Payment of stock transfer or other taxes, governmental charges, third-party transfer or registration fees, and applicable depositary fees, expenses, and charges.
- Satisfactory proof of identity, signature genuineness, and other matters contemplated by the deposit agreement.
- Compliance with:
  - Infosys’s memorandum and articles of association and applicable Board resolutions and regulations.
  - Laws and governmental regulations relating to ADRs, ADSs, or the withdrawal or delivery of deposited securities.
  - Reasonable regulations and procedures established by the depositary consistent with the deposit agreement and applicable laws, including presentation of transfer documents.

The depositary may generally refuse to issue or deliver ADSs or register ADS transfers when its register or Infosys’s transfer books are closed. It may also do so whenever the depositary or Infosys determines that refusal is necessary or advisable.

## Your Right to Receive the Shares Underlying Your ADSs

ADS holders may cancel their ADSs and withdraw the underlying equity shares at any time, except:

- During temporary delays when the depositary or Infosys has closed its transfer books.
- When the transfer of equity shares is blocked to permit voting at a shareholders’ meeting.
- While Infosys is paying a dividend on its equity shares.
- When the ADS holder owes fees, taxes, or similar charges.
- When withdrawals must be prohibited to comply with applicable laws or governmental regulations.
- In other circumstances contemplated by Section I.A.(l) of the General Instructions to Form F-6, as amended from time to time.
- For any other reason when the depositary or Infosys determines in good faith that prohibiting withdrawals is necessary or advisable.

The depositary shall not knowingly accept for deposit equity shares or other deposited securities required to be registered under the Securities Act unless a registration statement is effective for those securities.

The right of withdrawal may not be limited by any other provision of the deposit agreement.

## Direct Registration System

The deposit agreement provides that the Direct Registration System (DRS) and Profile Modification System (Profile) apply to uncertificated ADSs after their acceptance into DRS by DTC.

DRS is administered by DTC and allows the depositary to register ownership of uncertificated ADSs. Ownership is evidenced by periodic statements issued by the depositary to eligible ADS holders.

Profile is a required feature of DRS. It allows a DTC participant acting on behalf of an ADS holder to direct the depositary to transfer ADSs to DTC or its nominee and deliver them to the DTC account of that participant without prior authorization from the ADS holder.

## OVERVIEW OF EXECUTIVE LEADERSHIP COMPENSATION

## 1. Executive Leadership Compensation at Infosys

Infosys’s Executive Leadership Compensation follows a Total Rewards model comprising:

- Base/Fixed Pay.
- Performance Bonus.
- Stock Incentives.

A significant portion of rewards is provided through stock incentives to align executive interests with those of shareholders.

Executive Leadership, also called Executive Officers, comprises the Chief Executive Officer and Managing Director, Chief Financial Officer, Chief Legal Officer and Chief Compliance Officer, and Chief Human Resources Officer.

## 2. Nomination and Remuneration Committee of the Board

The Nomination and Remuneration Committee of the Board (NRC) acts on behalf of the Board on matters relating to the compensation of Directors and other Executive Officers.

The NRC recommends changes to the compensation structure within limits approved by shareholders, as applicable. Shareholders approve the overall compensation structure for all whole-time directors.

## 3. Key Aspects of Executive Compensation Policy

### a. Key Objectives:

Infosys’s executive pay programs aim to:

- Attract and retain executive talent.
- Create a performance-oriented culture by rewarding financial, strategic, and operational performance.
- Support sustainable, long-term shareholder value creation through performance-oriented stock-based incentives.

### b. Primary Pay Components of the Executive Officers' Compensation

The three primary components of executive Total Rewards are Base/Fixed Pay, Performance Bonus, and Stock Incentives.

- **Base/Fixed Pay:** Fixed cash compensation paid periodically.
- **Performance Bonus:** Cash compensation linked to performance against defined Key Performance Indicators. Infosys operates a non-contractual, discretionary Performance Bonus Plan, subject to eligibility.
- **Stock Incentives:** A substantial component of executive Total Rewards, determined by the desired pay mix for the relevant level.

Stock incentives may be granted as part of Total Rewards or in addition to Total Rewards, as determined by the CEO and NRC. They are governed by the 2015 Incentive Compensation Plan, the Expanded Stock Ownership Program 2019, or other plans approved by shareholders.

Equity grants generally vest or are granted based on Company and/or individual performance parameters, although some grants may be time-based. The NRC may approve special or one-time stock-based incentives, including time-based retention grants and awards or vesting linked to extraordinary performance goals. One-time equity grants may also be issued to new executive hires to meet specific compensation expectations and may include special vesting conditions.

### c. Executive Compensation Fitment and Benchmarking

Executive compensation is generally targeted toward the market median of total compensation among the selected peer group, adjusted for internal equity, consistency, and overall affordability.

For executives, stock incentives and performance bonuses together constitute approximately 60% to 90% of total rewards.

### d. Compensation Practices

Current compensation practices for Executive Officers include:

- Using appropriate peer groups to establish total-rewards benchmarks for executive roles.
- Aligning executive pay with shareholder value through performance-based stock incentives.
- Retaining executives through multi-year vesting of stock incentives.
