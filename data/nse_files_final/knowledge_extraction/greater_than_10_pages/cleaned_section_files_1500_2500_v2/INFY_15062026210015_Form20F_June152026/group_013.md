## Our reputation, access to capital and longer-term financial stability could be at risk if we are unable to meet our stated goals under our ESG 2030 vision.

Infosys is an early signatory to the United Nations Global Compact. In 2020, after becoming carbon neutral, it published its ESG Vision and Ambitions 2030, outlining a roadmap for responsible and sustainable business. Annual ESG publications report performance against these ambitions and are mapped to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals.

In 2025, at the midpoint of its ESG Vision 2030 journey, Infosys refreshed its ESG ambitions to address changing stakeholder priorities and reinforce its commitment to responsible business. ESG performance and leadership are monitored internally and by external agencies, including global ESG assessment providers.

Failure to meet ESG goals or unfavorable assessments by external agencies could adversely affect Infosys' reputation, access to capital, longer-term financial stability, brand reputation, and the price of its shares. Conversely, proposed or enacted “anti-ESG” legislation, regulation, or investment policies could expose Infosys to additional legal, financial, or reputational risks based on its ESG ambitions and disclosures.

## Negative media coverage and public scrutiny may divert the time and attention of our board and management and adversely affect our reputation and the prices of our equity shares and ADSs.

Infosys' business practices, policies, and actions are subject to media coverage and public scrutiny, including negative or inaccurate posts and comments. Negative media coverage concerning the business, Board, or senior management could harm its reputation regardless of the factual basis of the allegations.

Responding to media allegations could divert the time and attention of the Board and senior management from the business and disrupt operations. Infosys may be unable to respond publicly to certain comments because of obligations to employees, clients, and other stakeholders. Unfavorable publicity could reduce investor confidence and directly or indirectly cause the price of its equity shares and ADSs to decline.

If employees, acting independently, engage in conduct perceived to be against the interests of local communities or in violation of local regulations, mainstream or social media attention and regulatory scrutiny could negatively affect Infosys' reputation. Regulatory requirements requiring management to respond to rumors within specified periods could also strain Board and management resources and divert attention from business-critical strategic work.

## VII. Risks related to legislation and regulatory compliance

Political uncertainty arising from geopolitical tensions, tariff and trade restrictions, and potentially more restrictive immigration legislation and regulation could adversely affect Infosys' ability to service clients in relevant geographies.

Current administrations in key operating geographies have indicated potentially more restrictive immigration laws or interpretations. Changes could include higher wage requirements, stricter application of immigration laws, and more complex or delayed processes. These changes could increase immigration sponsorship costs or prevent Infosys from executing its global delivery model effectively, affecting its results of operations and financial condition.

## New and changing regulatory compliance, corporate governance and public disclosure requirements add uncertainty to our compliance efforts and increase our costs of compliance.

Infosys is subject to laws, regulations, and industry standards in the countries where it operates. These govern areas including privacy, information security, labor and employment, immigration, data protection, AI, import and export practices, and marketing and communications. They are subject to change and evolving interpretations, particularly as Infosys introduces new solutions and services and expands into new jurisdictions.

Actual or perceived breaches could result in investigations, regulatory inquiries, litigation, fines, tax demands, injunctions, negative client sentiment, impairment of existing or planned solutions and services, or other adverse effects on the business.

Changes to accounting, corporate governance, and public disclosure requirements create uncertainty and may increase compliance costs. In India, the Companies Act, 2013; the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015, as amended (“Listing Regulations”); the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018, as amended (“SEBI ICDR Regulations”); the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011, as amended (“Takeover Code”); and the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015, as amended, have added complexity to Infosys' corporate compliance regime.

Infosys is also subject to social regulations, including modern slavery legislation in countries such as the United Kingdom and Australia and the UK Corporate Criminal Offence Act. Failure by Infosys or its suppliers to comply with applicable regulations, including those concerning human trafficking, could result in sanctions affecting its reputation and ability to provide services to clients.

In connection with the Annual Report on Form 20-F, management assessed internal controls over financial reporting and determined that they were effective as of March 31, 2026. Future deficiencies identified in these assessments, or an inability of the auditors to issue an unqualified opinion regarding internal controls over financial reporting, could harm Infosys' reputation and the price of its equity shares and ADSs.

Compliance with evolving laws, regulations, and standards has increased and may continue to increase general and administrative expenses and management time. Director and officer liability insurance may become more expensive or difficult to obtain. Board members and executive officers may face increased personal liability risks related to their duties and regulatory reporting obligations, potentially making it more difficult to attract and retain qualified directors and executives. Failure to comply with new or changed laws or regulations could harm the business and reputation.

## The intellectual property ('IP') laws of India may not give sufficient protection to software and the related IP rights to the same extent as those in the United States. We may be unsuccessful in protecting our IP rights. We may also be subject to third party claims of IP infringement.

Infosys relies on patent, copyright, trademark, and design laws; trade secrets; confidentiality procedures; and contractual provisions to protect its intellectual property. Indian law may not protect proprietary rights to the same extent as U.S. law. Competitors may independently develop similar technology or duplicate Infosys' products or services, and unauthorized parties may infringe or misappropriate its products, services, or proprietary information.

Misappropriation or duplication of intellectual property could disrupt operations, divert management and employee attention, reduce revenues, and increase expenses. Litigation to enforce Infosys' IP rights or determine the validity and scope of others' proprietary rights could be costly and time-consuming. As the number and scope of IP rights in the software industry increase, infringement claims may become more frequent. Defending claims, including claims without merit, could divert management resources and increase costs.

The growing use of AI introduces evolving IP risks, including ownership of AI-generated output; the legality of using third-party IP in training datasets; use of third-party IP as an AI knowledge base; unclear or inconsistent AI model license terms; and potential memorization and reproduction of training datasets by AI systems. Infosys will need to manage these risks through contractual arrangements with clients and partners.

Third parties, including companies with greater resources, have asserted and may assert patent, copyright, trademark, trade secret, and other IP claims against Infosys, its clients, or business partners. On August 23, 2024, Cognizant TriZetto Software Group, Inc. (“TriZetto”) filed a lawsuit against Infosys in the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas. The lawsuit alleges misappropriation of TriZetto trade secrets relating to its Facets and QNXT software products, breach of contract, and unfair competition. TriZetto seeks unspecified damages and, among other relief, an injunction preventing Infosys from using TriZetto's trade secrets.

If Infosys becomes liable for infringing or misappropriating third-party IP, it could be required to pay substantial damages, develop alternative technology, obtain a license, or stop selling applications or products containing the relevant technology. Infosys may be unable to develop alternative technology or obtain a license on commercially reasonable terms, or at all. An unfavorable outcome from litigation, other proceedings, or settlement could materially and adversely affect its business, results of operations, and financial position.

Non-practicing entities also continue to bring patent litigation in the software industry, seeking monetary damages and court-issued injunctions based on alleged infringement by product features. Such claims could increase operating costs and disrupt the business, particularly if plaintiffs succeed in blocking sales of products or services.
