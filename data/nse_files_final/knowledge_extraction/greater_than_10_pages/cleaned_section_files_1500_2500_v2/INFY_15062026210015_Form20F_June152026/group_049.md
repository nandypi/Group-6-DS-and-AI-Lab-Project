## Capital allocation policy

The Company’s capital allocation policy effective from fiscal 2020 was to return approximately 85% of free cash flow cumulatively over a five-year period through a combination of semi-annual dividends, share buybacks and/or special dividends, subject to applicable laws and requisite approvals.

Effective from fiscal 2025, the Company expects to continue returning approximately 85% of free cash flow cumulatively over a five-year period through a combination of semi-annual dividends, share buybacks and/or special dividends, subject to applicable laws and requisite approvals. Under this policy, the Company expects to progressively increase its annual dividend per share, excluding any special dividend.

*Free cash flow is defined as net cash provided by operating activities less capital expenditure, as reported in the consolidated statement of cash flows prepared under IFRS. Dividends and buybacks include applicable taxes.*

## Dividends

Under Indian law, a corporation pays dividends upon recommendation by the Board and approval by a majority of shareholders. Shareholders may decrease, but not increase, the amount of the dividend recommended by the Board.

Dividends may be paid out of an Indian company’s profits after providing for depreciation in the year in which the dividend is declared, out of undistributed profits of previous fiscal years, or out of both. An interim dividend requires only Board approval under Indian law.

Holders of ADSs are entitled to receive dividends payable on the equity shares represented by those ADSs. Cash dividends are paid to the Depositary in Indian rupees and are generally converted by the Depositary into U.S. dollars and distributed to ADS holders, net of Depositary fees, taxes, if any, and expenses. The Company has no current intention to discontinue dividend payments, but future dividends may not be declared or paid, and the amount may be decreased.

## Dividend Distribution Policy

Under Regulation 43A of the SEBI LODR, the top 500 listed companies must formulate a dividend distribution policy. Infosys adopted a policy establishing the parameters and circumstances the Board will consider when determining dividend distributions and whether to retain profits.

The dividend policy is filed as an exhibit to this Annual Report on Form 20-F and is available on the Company’s website: https://www.infosys.com/investors/corporate-governance/Pages/policies.aspx

## Item 9. The Offer and Listing

## PRICE HISTORY

Infosys equity shares are traded in India on the BSE and NSE. Infosys ADSs are traded on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) under the ticker symbol “INFY.” Each ADS represents one equity share.

Infosys ADSs began trading on NASDAQ on March 11, 1999. Following voluntary delisting from the NASDAQ Global Select Market on December 11, 2012, the ADSs began trading on the NYSE on December 12, 2012. Infosys listed its ADSs on the Euronext London and Paris markets on February 20, 2013.

Effective July 5, 2018, Infosys voluntarily delisted its ADSs from the Euronext Paris and Euronext London exchanges because the low average daily trading volume was not commensurate with the related administrative expenses. Infosys ADSs continue to be listed on the NYSE under the symbol “INFY.”

Deutsche Bank Trust Company Americas serves as depositary for the ADSs traded under the Deposit Agreement dated March 10, 1999, as amended and restated on June 28, 2017.

## Item 10. Additional Information

## MEMORANDUM AND ARTICLES OF ASSOCIATION

By a postal ballot concluded on March 31, 2017, the Company adopted Articles of Association aligned with the Indian Companies Act, 2013, as amended. By a postal ballot concluded on August 22, 2018, the Company altered clause V of its Memorandum of Association to reflect an increase in authorized capital for the issue of bonus shares.

The corporate legal framework governing the Company is the notified provisions of the Companies Act, 2013, as amended from time to time.

The Company’s Articles of Association provide that:

- The minimum number of directors is three.
- The maximum number of directors is 15, unless shareholders approve the appointment of more than 15 directors by special resolution.
- As of March 31, 2026, the Company had nine directors.
- One-third of the directors liable to retire by rotation under the Indian Companies Act, 2013, excluding independent directors, must retire from office every year.
- Directors whose terms of office have lasted the longest must retire by rotation.
- Retiring directors are eligible for reappointment.
- Executive directors are appointed by shareholders for a maximum period of five years and are eligible for reappointment.
- An independent director may hold office for a term of up to five consecutive years and is eligible for reappointment upon passage of a special resolution by shareholders.
- An independent director may serve only two consecutive terms and may be reappointed after three years of ceasing to be an independent director, provided the individual is not appointed in or associated with the Company in any capacity during that three-year period.
- Directors are not required to hold shares of the Company to serve on the Board.

The Company appoints or reappoints its Managing Director and CEO or whole-time directors in accordance with applicable law. Non-executive directors are subject to retirement by rotation at the annual general meeting as specified under applicable law. Directors, key managerial personnel and senior management retire in accordance with applicable legal provisions. Subject to required approvals, the Board may retain directors, key managerial personnel and senior management in the same position or remuneration, or otherwise, after they reach retirement age for the benefit of the Company.
