## Introduction of new forms of taxes on distribution of profits or changes to the basis of application of these taxes and/or changes to Buyback regulations could adversely affect the returns to our shareholders.

Under the Corporate Policy on Capital Allocation, effective from financial year 2025, Infosys expects to continue returning approximately 85% of free cash flow cumulatively over a five-year period through a combination of semi-annual dividends and/or share buybacks or special dividends, subject to applicable laws and requisite approvals.

Under this policy, Infosys expects to progressively increase its annual dividend per share, excluding special dividends. Free cash flow is defined as net cash provided by operating activities less capital expenditure, as reported in the consolidated statement of cash flows prepared under IFRS. Dividend and buyback amounts include applicable taxes. Dividends are declared and paid in Indian rupees.

The Finance Act, 2020 replaced the Dividend Distribution Tax with a system under which dividend income is taxed in the hands of shareholders at their applicable tax rates. Companies paying dividends must withhold tax at applicable rates under the Indian Income Tax Act and relevant tax treaties, including the Multilateral Instrument where applicable. Non-resident shareholders may need to provide tax forms, including a tax residency certificate.

An increase in the effective tax rate on dividends or the introduction of new taxes on profit distributions could reduce the after-tax dividend amount received by shareholders.

For Indian listed companies announcing a share buyback after July 5, 2019, section 115QA of the Income Tax Act required the company to pay additional income tax on the buyback, while shareholders received an exemption under section 10(34A). Under the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024, section 115QA no longer applies to buybacks undertaken on or after October 1, 2024, and the shareholder exemption under section 10(34A) ceased to apply.

For buybacks on or after October 1, 2024:

- Consideration received by shareholders is treated as dividend income under section 2(22)(f) and taxed as income from other sources.
- The income is subject to withholding tax at applicable rates under the Indian Income Tax Act and relevant tax treaties, including the Multilateral Instrument where applicable, subject to the required tax forms.
- For purposes of computing capital gains under the Indian Income Tax Act, consideration received for the bought-back shares is deemed to be nil under section 46A, resulting in a capital loss for shareholders.
- The capital loss may be eligible for set-off and carry-forward under section 74.

Effective April 1, 2026, buyback proceeds will be taxed as capital gains rather than dividend income. Promoters will be subject to additional income tax on capital gains arising from a buyback. Their total tax payable will consist of regular capital gains tax plus the prescribed additional tax.

From April 1, 2025, open-market buybacks through stock exchanges are no longer permitted. Infosys stated that open-market buybacks had been its preferred method because shares could be purchased at market prices, the method provided higher EPS accretion compared with tender-offer buybacks, and execution was easier. The phase-out of open-market buybacks may affect Infosys’ ability to conduct buybacks and provide higher EPS accretion for remaining shareholders.

Each investor or prospective investor should consult their own tax advisors regarding the Indian and local tax consequences of acquiring, owning, or disposing of Infosys equity shares or ADSs.

## A. HISTORY AND DEVELOPMENT OF THE COMPANY

Infosys was incorporated on July 2, 1981, in Pune, Maharashtra, India, as Infosys Consultants Private Limited under the Indian Companies Act, 1956. It changed its name to Infosys Technologies Private Limited in April 1992 and to Infosys Technologies Limited in June 1992, when it became a public limited company.

In June 2011, the company changed its name to Infosys Limited following approval from its Board, shareholders, and Indian regulatory authorities.

Infosys completed its initial public offering of equity shares in India in February 1993 and was listed on Indian stock exchanges in June 1993. It completed its initial public offering of American Depositary Shares (ADSs) in the United States in 1999. Sponsored secondary ADS offerings were completed in August 2003, June 2005, and November 2006. The 2005 and 2006 offerings also included Public Offerings Without Listing in Japan.

In 2008, Infosys was selected as an original component member of The Global Dow, a worldwide stock index of 150 blue-chip stocks. Following its voluntary delisting from the NASDAQ Global Select Market on December 11, 2012, Infosys ADSs began trading on the New York Stock Exchange on December 12, 2012, under the ticker symbol INFY. Infosys was inducted into the Dow Jones Sustainability Indices in fiscal 2018.
