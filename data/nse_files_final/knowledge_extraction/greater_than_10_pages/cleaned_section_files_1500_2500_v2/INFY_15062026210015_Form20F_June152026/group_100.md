The applicable Indian corporate statutory tax rates were 25.17% for fiscal 2026, 25.17% for fiscal 2025, and 34.94% for fiscal 2024.

Income tax expense included reversals, net of provisions, of $93 million in fiscal 2026 and $113 million in fiscal 2024, and provisions, net of reversals, of $16 million in fiscal 2025. These prior-period provisions and reversals primarily resulted from adjudication of disputed matters, tax-return filings, and completion of assessments across various jurisdictions.

During the year ended March 31, 2026, Infosys received orders under sections 250 and 254 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, for assessment year 2013-14 and assessment years 2017-18 to 2021-22. The orders confirmed the Company’s tax treatment of certain matters. Infosys recognized $41 million of pre-tax interest income and reversed $93 million of income tax provisions, with the reversal credited to the Statement of Profit and Loss. Contingent liabilities decreased by $9 million following resolution of the disputes.

During the year ended March 31, 2025, Infosys received orders under section 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, for assessment years 2016-17 and 2019-20. The orders confirmed the Company’s tax treatment of certain matters. Infosys recognized $38 million of pre-tax interest income and reversed $21 million of income tax provisions, with the reversal credited to the Statement of Profit and Loss. Contingent liabilities decreased by $125 million following resolution of the disputes.

During the year ended March 31, 2024, Infosys received orders under sections 250 and 254 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, for assessment years 2007-08 to 2015-16, 2017-18, and 2018-19. The orders confirmed the Company’s tax treatment of certain matters. Infosys recognized $232 million of pre-tax interest income along with the corresponding tax impact, and reversed $63 million of income tax provisions, with the reversal credited to the Statement of Profit and Loss. Contingent liabilities decreased by $196 million.

Foreign tax expense primarily relates to income taxes payable overseas, principally in the United States.

In India, Infosys previously benefited from income tax incentives for software and services exports from units registered under the Special Economic Zones (SEZs) Act, 2005. Eligible SEZ units that began providing services on or after April 1, 2005, could claim:

- A 100% deduction of profits or gains from exported services for the first five years.
- A 50% deduction for a further five years.
- Up to a 50% deduction for an additional five years, subject to creation and use of a Special Economic Zone Re-investment Reserve for acquiring new plant and machinery.

These incentives reduced fiscal 2024 income tax expense by $363 million compared with the estimated tax expense without the incentives. The per-share effect was $0.09 based on both basic and diluted weighted-average equity shares.

The Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019 introduced section 115BAA, under which a domestic company may elect a 22% corporate tax rate plus a 10% surcharge and 4% cess, without claiming certain deductions, including the deduction for SEZ units under section 10AA of the Income Tax Act. From fiscal 2025, Infosys recognized income tax expense under section 115BAA without claiming the section 10AA SEZ deduction.

Deferred income tax for fiscal 2026, 2025, and 2024 substantially related to the origination and reversal of temporary differences.

Infosys is subject to a 15% U.S. Branch Profit Tax (BPT) to the extent that its U.S. branch’s net profit exceeds the increase in the branch’s net assets during the year, calculated under the Internal Revenue Code. As of March 31, 2026, the U.S. branch’s net assets were approximately $816 million, and Infosys had a $22 million deferred tax liability for BPT, net of credits, because the Company expects the branch profits to be distributed in the foreseeable future.

Deferred income tax liabilities were not recognized on temporary differences associated with investments in subsidiaries and branches of $2,032 million as of March 31, 2026, and $1,941 million as of March 31, 2025. Infosys can control the timing of reversal, and it is probable that the temporary differences will not reverse in the foreseeable future. The Group primarily intends to repatriate earnings from subsidiaries and branches only when they can be distributed tax-free.

Deferred income tax assets were not recognized on accumulated losses of $513 million as of March 31, 2026, and $538 million as of March 31, 2025, because future taxable profit is not expected to be available against which the unused tax losses can be utilized in the foreseeable future.
