# Takeover Code and Change of Control

The acquisition of:

1. Shares or voting rights that entitle the acquirer and persons acting in concert with the acquirer to exercise:
   - 25% or more of the voting rights; or
   - More than 5% of the voting rights in a target company in a single financial year, where the acquirer and persons acting in concert already hold 25% or more of the voting rights; or
2. Direct or indirect control over the target company

triggers a requirement for the acquirer and persons acting in concert with the acquirer to make an open offer to acquire at least 26% of the total shares of the target company. The offer price is determined under the Takeover Code. The acquirer must make a public announcement on the date on which it agrees to acquire the shares, voting rights, or control.

If public shareholding in the target company falls below the limit specified in the Securities Contract (Regulation) Rules, 1957 (“SCRR”), read with the Listing Regulations, because of shares acquired pursuant to an open offer, the acquirer must take the steps permitted under SEBI Master Circular SEBI/HO/49/14/14(7)2025-CFD-POD2/I/3762/2026 dated January 30, 2026. These steps must facilitate compliance with the public shareholding threshold within the period prescribed in the SCRR.

Under amendments to the Takeover Code dated December 6, 2021, an acquirer may make an offer to delist the company if the intention to do so is declared both when making the public announcement of an open offer and when making the detailed public statement under the Takeover Code. For an indirect acquisition that is not deemed to be a direct acquisition under Regulation 5(2) of the Takeover Code, the intention to delist initially needs to be declared only in the detailed public statement.

If the target company is not delisted pursuant to the delisting offer, but the acquirer’s shareholding exceeds the maximum permissible non-public shareholding threshold, the acquirer may make a further attempt to delist the target company under the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Delisting of Equity Shares) Regulations, 2021 (“Delisting Regulations”) within 12 months after completion of the open offer. This is subject to the acquirer continuing to exceed the maximum permissible non-public shareholding threshold.

If the further delisting attempt fails as prescribed under the Takeover Code, the acquirer must ensure compliance with the target company’s minimum public shareholding requirement under the SCRR within 12 months after the end of that period.

If the acquirer’s shareholding exceeds the maximum permissible non-public shareholding pursuant to an open offer, the acquirer is not eligible to make a voluntary delisting offer under the Delisting Regulations unless 12 months have elapsed from the date of completion of the offer period.

Because Infosys is listed in India, the Takeover Code applies to Infosys and to any person acquiring its equity shares or voting rights, including through ADSs.

ADS holders may exercise voting rights in respect of the deposited equity shares represented by their ADSs. Accordingly, acquisition of ADSs is subject to the Takeover Code. An open offer of at least 26% of the Company’s shares to existing shareholders would be triggered if the shares underlying the ADSs held by an ADS holder and persons acting in concert with that holder represent 25% or more of the Company’s shares or voting rights.

Under the Listing Regulations, Infosys has entered into listing agreements with each Indian stock exchange on which its equity shares are listed. Infosys must report to the stock exchanges disclosures made to the Company under the Takeover Code.

The Articles of Association do not contain provisions that would delay, defer, or prevent a change in control of the Company and that would operate only in connection with a merger, acquisition, or corporate restructuring involving the Company.
