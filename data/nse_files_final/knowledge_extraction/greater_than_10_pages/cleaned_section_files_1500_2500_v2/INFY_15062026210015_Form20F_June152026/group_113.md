## Maintenance of Minimum Public Shareholding as a Condition for Continuous Listing

The Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957 (SCRR) were amended on June 4, 2010, making it mandatory for listed companies in India to maintain minimum public shareholding of 25%.

For this purpose, “public shareholding” means equity shares held by the public, including shares underlying depository receipts if:

1. The holder of the depository receipts has the right to issue voting instructions; and
2. The depository receipts are listed on an international exchange in accordance with the Depository Receipts Scheme.

Equity shares held by a trust established to implement employee benefit schemes under SEBI regulations are excluded from public shareholding.

Existing listed companies with public shareholding below 25% may meet the prescribed threshold through the following methods, subject to applicable conditions:

- Sale of shares held by promoters or the promoter group in the open market:
  - Promoters or the promoter group may sell up to 2% of the listed entity’s total paid-up equity share capital, subject to five times the entity’s average monthly trading volume, in each financial year until the applicable MPS compliance deadline.
  - Promoters or the promoter group may sell up to a maximum of 5% of the listed entity’s paid-up capital during a financial year, provided that public holding reaches 25% after the sale. The sale may occur in one or multiple tranches over a period not exceeding 12 months, and the shares sold may not exceed the entity’s trading volume during the preceding 12 months from the announcement date.
- Allotment of eligible securities through a qualified institutional placement under Chapter VI of the SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018.
- Issuance of shares to the public through a prospectus.
- Offer for sale of promoter or promoter-group shares to the public through a prospectus.
- Sale of promoter shares through the stock exchange mechanism, or secondary market, under SEBI circular no. SEBI/HO/MRD/MRD-PoD-3/P/CIR/2023/10 dated January 10, 2023.
- Rights issues to public shareholders, with promoter and promoter-group shareholders forgoing their entitlement to equity shares arising from the issue.
- Bonus issues to public shareholders, with promoter and promoter-group shareholders forgoing their entitlement to equity shares arising from the issue.
- Increase in public holding through the exercise of options and allotment of shares under an employee stock option scheme, subject to a maximum of 2% of the listed entity’s paid-up equity share capital.
- Transfer of promoter or promoter-group shares to an exchange-traded fund managed by a SEBI-registered mutual fund, subject to a maximum of 5% of the listed entity’s paid-up equity share capital.
- Any other method approved by SEBI on a case-by-case basis.

## Voting Rights of Deposited Equity Shares Represented by ADSs

Under Indian law, voting on equity shares is generally conducted by a show of hands unless a poll is demanded by members present in person or by proxy who hold at least 10% of the total shares entitled to vote on the resolution or shares with aggregate paid-up capital of at least ₹5,00,000.

Every company whose equity shares are listed on a recognized stock exchange, and every company with at least 1,000 members, must provide members with electronic voting facilities for resolutions proposed at general meetings. The Listing Regulations require listed companies to provide e-voting for all shareholders’ resolutions in accordance with the Indian Companies Act. A proxy, including a body corporate represented by an authorized representative, may vote on resolutions.

For the voting rights of ADS holders, see “Description of American Depositary Shares Voting Rights” below.

## Issue of Securities through the Depository Receipt Mechanism

The issue of securities through the depository receipt mechanism by Indian companies is governed by the Indian Companies Act, the Companies (Issue of Global Depository Receipts) Rules, 2014, the Depository Receipts Scheme, and SEBI circulars dated October 10, 2019, November 28, 2019, October 1, 2020, and December 18, 2020.

The Government of India approved the Depository Receipts Scheme on October 21, 2014, and it came into force on December 15, 2014. The Issue of Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds and Ordinary Shares (Through Depository Receipt Mechanism) Scheme, 1993 was repealed, except for provisions relating to foreign currency convertible bonds.

Under the Depository Receipts Scheme, an Indian company may issue or transfer permissible securities to a foreign depository for the issuance of depository receipts, provided the company has not been prohibited from accessing capital markets or dealing in securities and complies with prescribed conditions. SEBI’s October 10, 2019 circular restricts depository receipt issuance to companies incorporated in and listed on a recognized stock exchange in India.

“Permissible securities” are equity shares and debt securities in dematerialized form that rank pari passu with securities issued and listed on a recognized stock exchange.

The SEBI framework effective October 10, 2019 establishes eligibility requirements and issuer obligations for depository receipt issuances. Under the October 1, 2020 circular, listed companies must appoint an Indian depository as the “Designated Depository” to monitor depository receipt limits. The Depository Receipts (Amendment) Scheme, 2019, notified on October 7, 2019, expanded the definition of “permissible jurisdiction” to include the International Financial Services Centre in India.

In addition to not being prohibited from accessing capital markets or dealing in securities, the current framework requires issuers not to have been declared a willful defaulter or a fugitive economic offender.

A permissible holder, including a beneficial owner of depository receipts, generally excludes an Indian resident and an NRI. An NRI is not excluded as a permissible holder where depository receipts are issued pursuant to share-based employee benefit schemes implemented under the SEBI (Share Based Employee Benefits) Regulations, 2014, or pursuant to a bonus issue or rights issue.

A listed company issuing depository receipts must:

- File with Indian stock exchanges any public disclosure made to an international stock exchange within 24 hours of filing.
- File the offer document for an initial depository receipt issue with SEBI and the stock exchanges for their comments, if any.
- Comply with applicable laws, including minimum public shareholding requirements and foreign investment holding limits under FEMA.
- Price depository receipt issuances at no less than the price determined for the corresponding issue method available to domestic investors.

Under the revised framework, voting rights on the underlying shares must be exercised by depository receipt holders. Previously, voting rights could be governed contractually under the deposit agreement; where those rights were not exercisable by depository receipt holders, the underlying shares were not counted toward minimum public shareholding requirements.

## Description of American Depositary Shares (ADS)

Deutsche Bank Trust Company Americas is the depositary that registers and delivers the ADSs. Each ADS represents ownership of one equity share deposited with ICICI Bank Limited as custodian for the depositary. Each ADS may also represent ownership of securities, cash, or other property held by the depositary.

As of March 31, 2026, the depositary’s corporate trust office and principal executive office were located at Floor 17S, 1 Columbus Circle, New York, NY 10019, USA.

The Direct Registration System (DRS) is administered by The Depository Trust Company (DTC). Under the DRS, the depositary may register ownership of uncertificated ADSs, evidenced by periodic statements issued to entitled ADS holders.

ADS holders are not treated as shareholders and do not have shareholder rights. Shareholder rights are governed by the Indian Companies Act and the SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015. The depositary is the holder of the equity shares underlying the ADSs.

ADS holders have ADS holder rights under a deposit agreement among the company, the depositary, ADS holders, and the beneficial owners of ADSs. The deposit agreement sets out the rights and obligations of the ADS holders and the depositary and is governed by the laws of the State of New York.

The material provisions of the deposit agreement are qualified by the terms of the deposit agreement and the form of American Depositary Receipt filed as exhibits to the company’s Annual Report on Form 20-F filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.
