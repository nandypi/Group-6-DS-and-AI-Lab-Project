The technology services industry has experienced several major shifts. The current AI transition involves a foundational redesign of enterprise systems, architectures, and operating models. AI-native systems use probabilistic inference, continuous learning, and agent-based execution.

Infosys has a two-pronged AI strategy:

- **AI-first services:** Services designed with AI as the core execution layer, enabling new business models, products, and revenue streams by embedding intelligence into enterprise operations and decision-making.
- **AI-augmented services:** Existing offerings enhanced by integrating AI into established systems and workflows to improve efficiency, personalization, and client outcomes while extending the value of current investments.

These services are supported by platforms and intellectual property, ecosystem partnerships, talent, and the Infosys brand.

Infosys unveiled its AI-first value framework to help global enterprises scale AI using Infosys Topaz, its generative and agentic AI suite. The company cited market estimates of an incremental AI-first services opportunity of USD 300–400 billion by 2030.

The AI-first value framework is intended to help clients move beyond experimentation and scale AI within enterprise systems. It defines six interconnected value pools through which AI can deliver business impact across data, technology, and operations.

Infosys’ primary geographic markets are North America, Europe, the Rest of the World, and India. Its industry markets include:

- Financial services and insurance
- Retail, consumer packaged goods, and logistics
- Communication, telecom OEM, and media
- Energy, utilities, resources, and services
- Manufacturing
- Hi-tech
- Life sciences and healthcare

Infosys was founded in 1981 and is headquartered in Bengaluru, India. It completed its initial public offering of equity shares in India in 1993 and its initial public offering of ADSs in the United States in 1999. It completed sponsored secondary ADS offerings in the United States in August 2003, June 2005, and November 2006; Infosys did not receive proceeds from these offerings.

## ESG Vision and Ambitions

In October 2020, Infosys launched its ESG Vision 2030. In fiscal 2025, at the midpoint of its progress toward its 2030 goals, the company refreshed its ESG ambitions and launched the ESG Vision 2030 Refresh to address changing stakeholder priorities.

Infosys remained carbon neutral across its global operations for the seventh consecutive year. Its ESG Vision 2030 Refresh includes an ambition to become climate positive in 2030 through initiatives covering Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions. The objectives include:

- Reducing its carbon footprint and sequestering more carbon from the atmosphere than it emits
- Engaging clients on climate action through its solutions
- Maintaining 100% recycling of wastewater
- Sequestering more water than it consumes year over year
- Achieving zero waste to landfill

Its digital skilling programs aim to empower more than 18 million people and enable employment opportunities for more than 500,000 people. Other initiatives include Tech for Good, corporate social responsibility, employee volunteering, healthcare, and women’s empowerment.

Infosys remains focused on employee experience and recognition as an employer in its key operating regions. It also states that it will focus on stakeholder interests through an empowered, diverse, and inclusive board; leading data privacy standards; and information security practices.

## Industry structure and developments

Fiscal 2026 saw lower interest rates and stable economic sentiment, while policy changes and geopolitical uncertainties affected the technology services industry.

The year was characterized by subdued growth, with some economic green shoots supporting technology-led growth. AI-led transformation shifted discretionary spending dynamics and increased pressure on traditional IT services.

Global GDP growth remained flat at 3.3% in calendar year 2025. During the same period, IT services contracted by 10 basis points and grew at 4.6%. Infosys expects client spending and investments to continue moving toward AI in the near term.

Businesses used enterprise AI to reimagine cost structures, increase resilience and agility, personalize customer and employee experiences, and launch new products and services.
