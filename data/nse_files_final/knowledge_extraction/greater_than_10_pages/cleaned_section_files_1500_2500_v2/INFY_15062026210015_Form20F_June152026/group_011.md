## We may be the subject of litigation which, if adversely determined, could harm our business and impact reputation, growth, profitability, and results of operations.

In the normal course of business, we are, and may in the future be, subject to legal disputes or claims. An unfavorable outcome could require substantial damages or, in connection with intellectual property infringement claims, ongoing royalty payments or restrictions on selling certain products. Settlements could also result in significant costs. Litigation settlements or unfavorable outcomes could materially adversely affect our business, results of operations, reputation, financial position, or cash flows.

Cognizant TriZetto Software Group, Inc. (“TriZetto”) has filed a lawsuit against Infosys alleging misappropriation of TriZetto trade secrets relating to its Facets and QNXT software products, breach of contract, and unfair competition. The lawsuit seeks unspecified damages and, among other remedies, an injunction preventing Infosys from using TriZetto’s trade secrets.

Infosys filed counterclaims against TriZetto and its parent company, Cognizant Technology Services (collectively, “Cognizant”), alleging monopolization and restraint of trade in the markets for U.S. healthcare payor software products and related IT support, in violation of federal and state antitrust laws. Infosys seeks treble damages, attorneys’ fees, and an injunction to stop the alleged anticompetitive conduct. Discovery is ongoing, and the trial is scheduled to occur in the near future.

Economic downturns, changes in tariffs or trade restrictions, geopolitical conflicts, cybersecurity threats, data breaches, contractual suspensions, delayed payments, and similar incidents may result in additional litigation or disputes with stakeholders, additional costs, or reputational damage.

## Our insurance coverage may not be adequate to protect us against all potential losses to which we may be subject, which could adversely affect our business.

Infosys’s insurance policies generally cover loss of or damage to insured property and business interruption following property loss or damage. The policies cover property and assets worldwide, including leased property. Infosys also maintains coverage for damage caused by disclosure of employee- and client-related personally identifiable confidential information, system failures, errors, or unsatisfactory performance of services in the event of a third-party claim alleging damages or financial loss.

Coverage may not remain available on reasonable terms or in sufficient amounts to cover one or more large claims. Insurers may disclaim coverage for future claims; for example, certain insurers have indicated that they may disclaim coverage for business interruption resulting from geopolitical conflicts.

Large claims exceeding available coverage, changes in policy terms, premium increases, large deductibles, or co-insurance requirements could adversely affect results of operations. Infosys’s hybrid working model may increase the risk of claims involving errors and omissions and data breaches, potentially making existing coverage inadequate. Increased claims could also increase insurance premiums and adversely affect profitability.

Losses arising from events not covered by insurance, or claims that are not honored fully or in a timely manner, could adversely affect financial condition and results of operations.

## The markets in which we operate are subject to the risk of earthquakes, floods, tsunamis, storms, pandemics and other disasters.

Some regions in which Infosys operates are prone to earthquakes, floods, tsunamis, storms, pandemics, and other disasters. If business centers or telecommunications networks are affected, Infosys may incur costs to redeploy personnel and property, sustain damage to operations and properties, suffer financial losses, or be unable to complete client engagements on time or at all.

Disasters affecting locations where significant clients are based could cause client losses or business interruptions, impair Infosys’s ability to provide services, and limit clients’ ability to continue purchasing its products or services. Prolonged operational disruptions could also affect business continuity and employee productivity.

## The safety of our employees, assets and infrastructure may be affected by untoward incidents beyond our control, impacting business continuity or reputation.

The health and safety of employees, contractors, and people present in Infosys offices, as well as the security of its physical infrastructure, may be affected by violence, vandalism, disease pandemics, geopolitical conflicts, political demonstrations, civil unrest, random acts of violence, or public health crises. Such incidents could affect business continuity and reputation or expose Infosys to lawsuits from employees.

Infosys operates in 290 locations across 59 countries. Its global development centers are linked through a telecommunications network architecture using multiple service providers, satellite and optical links, and alternate routing. Despite infrastructure and business continuity plans intended to address communication network failures, operations and service delivery may be affected if these networks are disrupted by disasters.

Offshore and onsite operations may also be affected by disease, epidemics, war, conflicts, and local social instability, which could adversely affect revenues and profitability.

## Terrorist attacks or a war could adversely affect our business, results of operations and financial condition.

Terrorist attacks, violence, and war could directly affect Infosys or its clients. Events affecting the United States or Europe, including geopolitical conflicts in Eastern Europe and the Middle East, could significantly affect the business because a majority of revenues are derived from clients in those regions.

Terrorism, military coups, or warfare elsewhere could create geopolitical instability, affect clients, or impair project execution. Such events could destabilize India and other countries with major Infosys operations, make work visas and travel more difficult for technology professionals required to work in the United States or Europe, increase expenses, and negatively affect results of operations.

Attacks in India could disrupt service delivery, affect personnel and assets, and cause existing or potential clients to select other service providers.

South Asia has experienced civil unrest and hostilities among neighboring countries, including China and Pakistan. Military confrontations between India and Pakistan have occurred in Kashmir and along the India-Pakistan border, including as recently as May 2025. Continued conflict in South Asia, Eastern Europe, or the Middle East could affect employee and development-center safety, increase cybersecurity threats, and impair service to global clients if operations cannot be moved out of conflict zones promptly or if relocation increases operating costs.

Military activity or terrorist attacks could disrupt communications and travel, adversely affect the Indian economy, increase perceptions of risk associated with Indian companies, and affect the market for Indian securities, including Infosys equity shares and ADSs, as well as the market for Infosys services. Prolonged conflicts could also delay economic recovery from the pandemic.

## Climate change risks are increasingly manifesting in our business as strategic, physical and transitional (market and compliance) risks, which if not managed adequately, can affect our operations, reputation and profitability.

Organizations face increasing expectations to address ESG risks arising from climate change, environmental management, duty of care, workplace and safety conditions, human rights, anti-bribery and anticorruption practices, and compliance with applicable laws and regulations. This section discusses climate change-related risks.

Strategic climate change risks arise because aligning the business with evolving climate-related trends can affect business success. As a member of the global IT and digital supply chain ecosystem, Infosys may face strategic risks if its climate action goals are not aligned with global treaties such as the Paris Agreement on climate change.

Physical climate change risks include extreme weather events that may:

1. Damage buildings and other physical assets, disrupting business continuity.
2. Disrupt transportation networks, utilities, and other city infrastructure in operating locations.
3. Reduce employee morale.
4. Increase food prices or cause shortages, such as during droughts.
5. Cause disease and epidemics, such as after flooding.
6. Reduce productivity and increase operating costs.
7. Reduce the availability of essential natural resources, including water, affecting operations and employee livelihoods.
8. Disrupt power and water supplies to large operating campuses in major urban cities.
