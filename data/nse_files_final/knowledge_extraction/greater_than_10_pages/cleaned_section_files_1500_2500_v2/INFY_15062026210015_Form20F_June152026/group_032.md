The impact of the Labour Codes on reported operating profit was $143 million, or 0.7% of revenues.

(1) On November 21, 2025, the Government of India notified the Code on Wages, 2019, the Industrial Relations Code, 2020, the Code on Social Security, 2020, and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 (collectively, the “Labour Codes”). These laws consolidate 29 existing labour laws into a unified framework governing employee benefits during and after employment. The Labour Codes introduce changes including a uniform definition of wages and enhanced leave benefits. The Group assessed the financial implications, resulting in a $143 million increase in gratuity liability, defined benefit plan costs arising from past service cost relating to plan amendments, and compensated absences. This amount was recognized in the Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income for the year ended March 31, 2026.

Non-IFRS financial performance measures supplement financial information reported under IFRS and should not be considered in isolation or as substitutes for the relevant IFRS measures. They should be read in conjunction with the reported IFRS information.

## Functional currency, presentation currency and foreign exchange

The functional currency of Infosys, its Indian subsidiaries, and controlled trusts is the Indian rupee. The functional currency of all other subsidiaries is the respective local currency.

The consolidated financial statements in the Annual Report on Form 20-F are presented in U.S. dollars, rounded to the nearest million, to facilitate comparison with similar companies in other geographic locations. Assets and liabilities of foreign subsidiaries are translated using exchange rates at the balance sheet date. Revenues, expenses, and cash flow items are translated using average exchange rates for the respective periods. Resulting translation gains or losses are included in other comprehensive income and presented as currency translation reserves under other components of equity.

Indian law generally requires residents of India to repatriate foreign currency earnings to India. Section 8 of the Foreign Exchange Management Act requires an Indian company to take reasonable steps to realize and repatriate foreign currency earned outside India within the periods and in the manner specified by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). RBI guidelines require realized foreign currency to be repatriated to a foreign currency account, such as an Exchange Earners’ Foreign Currency account with an authorized dealer in India. Subject to approved uses and forward commitments, accruals during a calendar month must be converted into rupees by the last day of the succeeding calendar month.

Infosys generally collects foreign-currency earnings through dedicated foreign currency accounts in the local countries of operation. Maintaining these overseas accounts requires approval from an authorized dealer acting on behalf of the RBI. Failure to comply with RBI regulations could result in enforcement action.

Infosys generates substantially all revenues in foreign currencies, particularly the U.S. dollar, euro, United Kingdom pound sterling, and Australian dollar. A significant portion of expenses is incurred in U.S. dollars and Indian rupees. Foreign exchange gains and losses arise from movements in the Indian rupee against currencies used in operations and from foreign exchange forward and option contracts. Results may be adversely affected if the Indian rupee appreciates against the U.S. dollar.
