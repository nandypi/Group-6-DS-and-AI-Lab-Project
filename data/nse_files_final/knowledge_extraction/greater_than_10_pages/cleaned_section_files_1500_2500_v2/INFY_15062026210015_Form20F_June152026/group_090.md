The total purchase consideration of $385 million comprises the cash consideration paid to selling shareholders at the acquisition date.

The acquisition also includes retention bonuses and management incentives payable to acquiree employees over two to five years. These payments are subject to continuous employment with the Group and achievement of financial targets for the respective years. Bonuses and incentives are recognized in employee benefit expenses in the Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income over the service period.

The fair value of trade receivables acquired was $17 million at the acquisition date. As of March 31, 2026, the amounts had been fully collected.

Transaction costs incurred in connection with a business combination, including finder’s fees, legal fees, due diligence fees, and other professional and consulting fees, are expensed as incurred. ## 2.11 Revenue from operations

## Accounting policy

The Group derives revenue primarily from:

- IT services, including software development and related services
- Cloud and infrastructure services
- Maintenance
- Consulting and package implementation
- Licensing of software products and platforms across the Group’s core and digital offerings, collectively referred to as software-related services
- Business process management services

Customer contracts may be based on time and materials, units of work, fixed prices, or fixed timeframes.

Revenue from customer contracts is recognized when:

- The contract has been approved in writing by the parties.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- The contract is legally enforceable.

Revenue is recognized upon transfer of control of promised products or services, referred to as performance obligations, in an amount reflecting the consideration the Group has received or expects to receive. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations in each contract and allocates the transaction price to them based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. When that evidence is unavailable, the Group generally estimates standalone selling price using expected cost plus a margin based on similar services.

Contracts may include variable consideration, including rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue when the uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue recognition by contract type is as follows:

- Time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts: recognized as the related services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance contracts: recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services consist of an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When services are discrete and the pattern of benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method.
- Other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts satisfied over time: recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.

For percentage-of-completion accounting, efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because of the direct relationship between input and productivity. Progress is measured as the ratio of costs or efforts incurred to date, representing work performed, to estimated total costs or efforts. Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are monitored throughout the contract term. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates change or are revised. Revenues and estimated total costs or efforts are subject to revision as the contract progresses. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

Customer billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development, related services, and maintenance arrangements, software development and related services generally qualify as distinct performance obligations. Revenue is allocated to each performance obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices cannot be determined, the Group uses the expected cost-plus-margin approach. Performance obligations for software development and related services are satisfied as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure service contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, such as lease accounting. These contracts are accounted for under the applicable specific guidance. When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices are unavailable, the Group uses the expected cost-plus-margin approach. When the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period, with progress measured based on the promise in the contract.

Revenue from licenses is recognized as follows:

- Licenses providing a right to use: recognized when the license is made available to the customer.
- Licenses providing a right to access: recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When the license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated to each based on relative standalone selling prices. When the standalone selling price for implementation is unavailable, the Group uses the expected cost-plus-margin approach.

When a license requires substantial customization as part of the implementation service, the license and implementation arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services arising from the sale of software products is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

Certain integrated service arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent between the customer and vendor, and gross when the Group is the principal. The Group first assesses whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include whether the Group is primarily responsible for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors indicating whether the Group controls the goods or services.

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price, or both, of a contract that is approved by the parties. A modification that adds distinct performance obligations is accounted for as:

- A separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price.
- A termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract when the additional services are not priced at their standalone selling price.

When a modification does not create a distinct performance obligation, it is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract, meaning costs that would not have been incurred if the contract had not been obtained, are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them.

Certain eligible, nonrecurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as assets when they:

1. Relate directly to the contract.
2. Generate or enhance resources that the Group will use to satisfy performance obligations in the future.
3. Are expected to be recovered.
