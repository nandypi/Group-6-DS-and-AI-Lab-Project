# Company buyback tax treatment

An exchange of equity shares for cash generally will be a substantially disproportionate redemption for a U.S. holder if:

- Immediately after the exchange, the percentage of voting stock owned by the U.S. holder is less than 80% of the percentage owned immediately before the exchange; and
- After the exchange, the U.S. holder owns less than 50% of the total combined voting power of all classes of stock entitled to vote.

For purposes of the Section 302 tests, a U.S. holder must account for equity shares and ADSs constructively owned under applicable attribution rules. These rules may attribute shares and ADSs owned by certain family members and related entities, as well as shares and ADSs that the U.S. holder has the right to acquire by exercising an option. Family attribution may be waived in certain circumstances.

If the substantially disproportionate test is not satisfied, an exchange may still qualify under the “not essentially equivalent to a dividend” test if it results in a meaningful reduction in the U.S. holder’s equity interest, based on the holder’s particular facts and circumstances. IRS published rulings indicate that a relatively minor reduction may qualify where the U.S. holder’s relative equity interest is minimal and the holder does not exercise control over, or participate in, corporate management.

Because the Section 302 tests are applied separately to each stockholder, a buyback may be treated as a sale or exchange for some U.S. holders and as a distribution for others.

## Distribution

If a U.S. holder does not recognize gain or loss under the Section 302 tests, the holder will generally be treated as receiving a distribution from the Company. The gross distribution will generally be treated as dividend income to the extent of the Company’s current or accumulated earnings and profits, determined under U.S. federal income tax principles.

Each prospective participant in a company buyback should consult a tax advisor regarding the U.S. federal, state, local, and non-U.S. tax consequences of participating in the buyback.

# Sale or exchange of equity shares or ADSs

Subject to the passive foreign investment company rules, a U.S. holder generally recognizes gain or loss on the sale or exchange of equity shares or ADSs equal to the difference between the amount realized and the holder’s adjusted tax basis. The gain or loss generally is capital gain or loss and is long-term capital gain or loss if the shares or ADSs were held for more than one year.

For U.S. foreign tax credit purposes, such gain or loss generally is treated as U.S.-source passive category income or loss. The sale of equity shares or ADSs may be subject to taxes in India. Due to limitations on the use of foreign tax credits, a U.S. holder may not be able to use those taxes as a credit against the U.S. federal income tax liability resulting from the sale.

## Estate taxes

For U.S. federal estate tax purposes, the value of equity shares or ADSs held by an individual U.S. holder generally is included in the holder’s gross estate. An individual holder who pays non-U.S. estate tax on equity shares may be entitled to claim a credit for that tax against U.S. federal estate tax, subject to conditions and limitations.

## Additional Tax on Investment Income

U.S. holders who are individuals, estates, or trusts and whose income exceeds certain thresholds may be subject to a 3.8% tax on certain net investment income, including dividends and capital gains from the sale or other taxable disposition of equity shares or ADSs, subject to limitations and exceptions.

## Backup withholding tax and information reporting requirements

Dividends paid on, and proceeds from the sale of, equity shares or ADSs may be subject to U.S. information reporting. Backup withholding may apply unless the holder is an exempt recipient, provides a correct U.S. taxpayer identification number, certifies under penalty of perjury that the number is correct and that the holder is not subject to backup withholding, and otherwise satisfies applicable requirements.

Amounts withheld under the backup withholding rules may be allowed as a refund or credit against the holder’s U.S. federal income tax liability if the required information is timely provided to the IRS.

Certain U.S. holders must report information about investments in equity shares or ADSs that are not held through a custodial account with a U.S. financial institution on IRS Form 8938, attached to the holder’s annual income tax return. Failure to report required information may result in substantial penalties.

# Passive foreign investment company

A non-U.S. corporation generally will be classified as a passive foreign investment company for U.S. federal income tax purposes if either:

- At least 75% of its gross income for the taxable year is passive income; or
- On average for the taxable year, at least 50% of the value of its assets, measured at the end of each quarter, is attributable to assets that produce or are held for the production of passive income.

The Company does not believe that it satisfied either test for passive foreign investment company status for the year ended March 31, 2026. Because the determination is made annually, there is no assurance that the Company will not be considered a passive foreign investment company in future taxable years.

If the Company were a passive foreign investment company for any taxable year, U.S. holders:

- May be required to pay an interest charge and tax at ordinary income rates on “excess distributions” and on gains from the sale or other disposition of equity shares.
- Could avoid the excess distribution rules by making a qualified electing fund election and including their pro rata share of undistributed income in taxable income. The Company does not plan to provide the information necessary for U.S. holders to make that election.
- May avoid the excess distribution rules for marketable equity shares or ADSs by making a mark-to-market election. Under that election, the U.S. holder must mark the shares or ADSs to market each taxable year and recognize ordinary gain, and ordinary loss to the extent of prior ordinary gain, based on changes in market value.
- Will generally be subject to additional annual return requirements and may be required to file IRS Form 8621, unless an exemption applies.

The Company’s ADSs are traded on the NYSE and its equity shares are traded on Indian stock exchanges. A U.S. holder may therefore be able to make a mark-to-market election for the ADSs or equity shares, subject to the applicable rules and the holder’s circumstances.

If the Company is treated as a passive foreign investment company for any taxable year during which a U.S. holder owns ADSs or equity shares, it generally will continue to be treated as a passive foreign investment company for that holder in subsequent years while the holder continues to own the securities, unless the Company ceases to be a passive foreign investment company and the holder makes a deemed sale election.

The tax consequences described above are not a complete analysis of all consequences relating to ownership or disposition of equity shares or ADSs or participation in a company buyback. U.S. holders should consult their own tax advisors regarding the consequences applicable to their circumstances, including U.S. state, local, and non-U.S. tax laws and estate, gift, and inheritance taxes.
