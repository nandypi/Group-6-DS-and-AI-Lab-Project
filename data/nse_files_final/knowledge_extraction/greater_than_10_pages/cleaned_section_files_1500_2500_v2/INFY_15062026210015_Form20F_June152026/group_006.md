In addition, AI technologies may significantly alter workforce requirements and delivery models within the technology services industry. If we are unable to effectively adapt our workforce, delivery capabilities, and service offerings to evolving market expectations and technological changes, our business, revenues, profitability, and competitive position could be adversely affected.

## We may be unable to recoup investment costs incurred in developing our software products and platforms.

The development of our software products and platforms requires significant investments. The markets for these products and platforms are competitive, and current or newly developed offerings may not be commercially successful. Development costs may not be recouped, and delayed revenues may cause periodic fluctuations in results of operations because product and platform revenues typically occur after development costs are incurred.

## We may engage in acquisitions, strategic investments, strategic partnerships or alliances or other ventures that may or may not be successful.

We have previously, and may in the future, acquire or invest in complementary businesses, emerging technologies, services, or products, or enter into strategic partnerships and alliances to enhance our business.

We may be unable to identify suitable acquisition targets, strategic investments, or partnerships aligned with our overall strategy, including our AI strategy. Even if suitable targets are identified, transactions may not be completed on commercially acceptable terms. This could affect our competitiveness, growth prospects, and ability to evolve as an AI service provider at scale.

Future acquisitions could require us to:

- issue equity securities, diluting current shareholders’ percentage ownership;
- incur substantial debt;
- incur significant acquisition-related expenses;
- assume contingent liabilities; or
- expend significant cash.

These financing activities and expenditures could adversely affect our business, results of operations, financial condition, or the price of our equity shares and ADSs. Difficulties in the capital and credit markets may also prevent us from securing capital on acceptable terms, if at all, to complete acquisitions.

Expected acquisition synergies may not materialize because of inadequate business alignment or because acquisition assumptions do not hold due to internal or external factors. Benefits such as increased sales and earnings may also be delayed relative to the expenses incurred.

Acquired companies may be difficult to integrate, including their personnel, operations, products, services, solutions, technology, and software. Key personnel may leave. Integration difficulties could disrupt ongoing business, distract management and employees, and increase expenses.

We have made, and may continue to make, strategic investments in early-stage technology start-ups to gain experience in or exploit niche technologies. These investments may not generate expected returns if economic uncertainty, tariff changes, trade restrictions, or geopolitical conflicts adversely affect the start-ups’ demand, supply, or cash flow. Losses or a lack of profitability from these investments could adversely affect results of operations.

We have entered into alliances with many technology companies to enhance services offered to clients. Most alliances are non-exclusive, and partners may enter into agreements with competitors or compete against us. Failure to establish alliances with new partners, or reduced demand for partners’ services or products, could limit service enhancements and affect revenue growth.

Goodwill and acquired intangibles are subject to impairment review under International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) issued by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB). Impairment testing has resulted in charges for intangibles and may lead to additional charges in the future. Significant impairment charges could adversely affect results of operations.

## III.    Risks related to our cost structure

## Our expenses are difficult to predict and can vary significantly from period to period, which could cause fluctuations to our profitability.

A significant portion of total operating expenses, particularly personnel and facilities expenses, is fixed in advance for a given period. Unanticipated changes in project volume and timing, employee utilization, employee deployment locations, resource estimates, or economic conditions may therefore cause significant period-to-period fluctuations in results of operations.

Other factors that may affect profitability include:

- pricing pressure from AI-led compression;
- clients moving work to offshore-based global capability centers (GCCs);
- competition for digital capabilities;
- exchange-rate volatility involving the Indian rupee, U.S. dollar, and other currencies;
- wage pressures in India and other operating locations;
- regulatory changes affecting employee payouts, including overtime payments;
- tax increases or expiration of tax benefits;
- the size and timing of facilities expansion and related depreciation and amortization;
- economic downturns;
- changes in immigration laws, policies, or enforcement that restrict offshore outsourcing or visa availability;
- investments in localization; and
- wage increases caused by market pressures or immigration regulations.

If a greater proportion of services is delivered at client sites, operating costs may increase. Hiring in technology and investment hubs, as well as staffing for enabling functions and management teams, may also increase costs. Onsite operations cost more than offshore operations, and most bench capacity is therefore maintained offshore.

Economic uncertainty, tariff changes, or trade restrictions may cause clients to reduce their business with us. Profitability may be negatively affected if fixed or committed costs cannot be reduced in line with lower demand. Sudden changes in demand may also reduce short-term utilization and profits.

Large deals may have higher costs in their initial periods because of onsite efforts, transition costs, and other deal-specific expenses, creating volatility in margins. As part of large deals, we may acquire hardware, software, products, physical infrastructure, or intellectual property, and/or rebadge employees. Pricing and other cost-structure assumptions are often made when the deal is signed. Changes in these assumptions or in deal-execution conditions may affect profitability.

Competition for talent has affected and continues to affect wage costs. Wage increases may reduce our competitive advantage and negatively affect profits.

Competitive conditions and pricing pressures are leading us to commit to higher productivity improvements in client contracts. Clients have increased their demands for productivity commitments in connection with evolving AI technologies. Failure to achieve anticipated productivity improvements, including through automation, process optimization, technology use, service-delivery risk management, or client-requirement management, may affect profitability.

Operating-margin pressure may result from operating-expense increases that are not offset by pricing increases or from acquisitions with lower profitability. Unplanned expenditures supporting the hybrid working model, including increased information-security requirements, may also adversely affect profitability.

## Any inability to manage our growth could disrupt our business, reduce our profitability and adversely impact our ability to implement our growth strategy.

In recent years, we have expanded existing facilities and constructed new facilities globally. Total employee count increased from 259,619 on March 31, 2021, to 328,594 on March 31, 2026.

As of March 31, 2026, we operated in 290 locations across 59 countries and had more than 100 group companies. This scale creates challenges in maintaining uniform strategies, standards, policies, and procedures across the organization.

We have institutionalized processes, standard operating procedures, enterprise risk management, internal controls, information-security policies, data-privacy programs, and compliance programs. However, controls may have design or operating gaps or weaknesses because of the organization’s size. Control failures, including fraud, cybersecurity attacks, and privacy breaches, could negatively affect our reputation, financial condition, profitability, and stakeholder relationships.

Inadequate financial controls may increase the possibility of fraud, impair the accuracy of financial reporting, and negatively affect shareholder relationships. Continued growth also increases the challenges involved in:

- recruiting, training, and retaining sufficient skilled technical, marketing, and management personnel;
- maintaining and improving quality and process-execution standards;
- preserving culture, values, and the entrepreneurial environment;
- expanding the range of services offered to clients;
- developing and improving financial, operational, communications, and other internal systems;
- maintaining high levels of client satisfaction; and
- maintaining effective internal controls and training employees to mitigate unlawful or fraudulent conduct, contractual breaches, and other unacceptable business risks.
