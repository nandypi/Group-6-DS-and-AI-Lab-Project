## Improper Opportunities

When we receive information as part of our job, we must not trade on it for personal benefit, pass it to friends or family members, or indirectly compete with the Company. Information obtained through employment must not be used improperly, even after leaving the organization.

## Company Intellectual Property

The Company's intellectual property (IP) is a vital business asset. The IP portfolio includes:

- Copyrights
- Patents
- Trademarks
- Service marks
- Trade secrets
- Design rights
- Logos
- Brands
- Know-how

Employees must use the Company's IP in ways that protect these assets. To the extent permitted by law, rights to IP created using Company time and expense and within the scope of an employee's duties must be assigned to and remain the property of the Company. Employees should promptly disclose works, inventions, or developments so that legal protection can be obtained.

## Speaking to the Media

Only authorized representatives should respond to media inquiries. An inappropriate or inaccurate response may result in adverse publicity or affect the Company's legal position.

## Providing Information to the Media

When speaking about the Company in a public forum, including social media, employees must make clear when they are expressing personal views rather than representing the Company. Personal posts may still reflect on the Company or be mistaken for Company statements.

The Chief Executive Officer, Chief Financial Officer, and Investor Relations Department are the official Company spokespeople for financial matters. Press and financial analyst inquiries should be referred to the Corporate Communications team. The Company's Code on Fair Disclosures and Investor Relations identifies who may communicate Company information, and the Social Media Policy provides guidance on social media usage.

## Physical Access Control

Infosys has procedures for physical access control to protect the privacy of communications, secure Company communication equipment, and safeguard Company assets from theft, misuse, and destruction. Employees must comply with security policies at their locations and must not bypass security controls.

## Use of Company Assets

Infosys assets may not be used for individual profit or for unlawful, unauthorized, personal, or unethical purposes. Information technology, intellectual property, facilities, equipment, machines, software, and cash may be used for business purposes in accordance with applicable policies, including accurate expense reimbursement.

Computers, printers, copiers, and similar assets may be used for minor and incidental personal purposes only when the use is minimal, does not create significant incremental costs, does not interfere with work duties, and does not violate law or Company policy. Infosys resources may not be used for personal political activities.

Employees must protect hardware, software, data, facilities, and confidential client or Infosys information from destruction, theft, misuse, and unauthorized access. Required precautions may include using ID badges, passwords, security codes, computer cable locks, and physical security measures such as not leaving computers unattended in cars, including when working remotely.

Copyrighted materials must not be reproduced, distributed, or altered without permission from the copyright owner or an authorized agent. Business software must be properly licensed and used only according to its license. Use of unlicensed software may constitute copyright infringement and may result in disciplinary action.

### Expense Claims

Business-related expense claims must be authorized by the employee's manager before the expenses are incurred. Personal expenses will not be reimbursed by the Company. Supervisors, managers, and employees are responsible for complying with Infosys business-expense and reimbursement policies.

### Prior-Employer Confidentiality

An employee who may be developing technology for Infosys that is similar to technology developed for a prior employer should immediately inform their supervisor of a possible conflict. The employee must not disclose or use confidential technical information belonging to the prior employer and should contact the IP Cell when the scope of the obligations is unclear.

## Ethical Use of AI and Emerging Technologies

Infosys must develop and use AI responsibly, ethically, and securely, in compliance with applicable laws and regulations, the Infosys AI Policy, and relevant client or vendor contractual requirements.

Only approved AI tools may be used. Confidential, client-related, personal, or proprietary information must not be shared with public AI systems unless explicitly authorized. AI-generated content used in internal, client, or public deliverables must be transparently disclosed and reviewed for accuracy, bias, and policy compliance.

Employees must accurately represent both the benefits and potential harms of AI, respect intellectual property, and remain accountable for actions and decisions made with AI support.

## D.  COMMITTED TO OUR CUSTOMERS AND OUR SUPPLIERS

Employees who interact with current or potential customers or suppliers must act in a manner that represents the Company and its C-LIFE values. Employees are expected to protect the Company's reputation in their interactions.

## Fair Dealings

The Company must deal fairly with customers, suppliers, partners, service providers, competitors, and others with whom it interacts. Employees must not take unfair advantage through manipulation, concealment, abuse of privileged information, misrepresentation of facts, or other unfair dealing practices.

## Confidential Information of Clients and Third Parties

The Confidentiality and Nondisclosure Agreement signed when joining the Company establishes confidentiality obligations to Infosys and its clients. Employees may have access to significant amounts of non-public client information and must use, control, and protect confidential, private, and sensitive information against prohibited, arbitrary, or careless disclosure.

Unless the client has provided specific consent, preferably in writing, or there is a legal or professional right or duty to process or disclose the information, employees must not process or disclose confidential client information.

Confidential or proprietary information, including personal information about clients, Infosys, or other parties, must not be used for personal advantage or for the benefit of third parties. Infosys requires adequate technical and organizational measures to protect personal information. Employees, agents, consultants, suppliers, and contractors are responsible for using or processing personal information only when authorized by law or contract.

## Free and Fair Competition

Infosys supports free and fair markets and complies with laws regulating anti-competitive conduct and relationships with customers, competitors, distributors, and resellers.

### What do we need to know? What is regulated? How do we comply?

- Antitrust laws may address unfair pricing practices, including price discrimination and price fixing; secret rebates; questionable entry barriers through exclusive dealerships or distributorships; restrictions on carrying competing products; and other practices. Questionable practices encountered during work, including while bidding for services, should be reported through the Helpline.
- Employees must not enter into commercially motivated written or oral understandings, whether express or implied, that undermine fair market competition.
- Employees must not knowingly make false or misleading statements about competitors or the products and services of competitors, customers, or suppliers.
- Collusion among competitors is illegal. Communications with competitors should avoid prices, other terms and conditions of sale, customers, and suppliers. Employees must not enter into written or oral agreements or understandings with competitors on these subjects.

## Vendor Conflicts of Interest

A company owned by an employee's family member may compete for vendor work, but the employee related to the owner must not participate in selecting the vendor or managing the vendor if it is selected. The employee must not provide confidential Infosys information or another advantage to the family member's company. Managers should be consulted regarding actual or apparent conflicts of interest, particularly when family members are involved.
