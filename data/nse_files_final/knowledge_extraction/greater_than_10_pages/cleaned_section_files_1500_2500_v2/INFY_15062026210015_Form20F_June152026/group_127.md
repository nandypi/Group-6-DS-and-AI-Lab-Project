## ILLUSTRATIVE LIST OF LEGITIMATE PURPOSE

Sharing UPSI is considered to serve a legitimate purpose in the following cases:

- Under proceedings or pursuant to an order of courts or tribunals, including the National Company Law Tribunal, National Company Law Appellate Tribunal, quasi-judicial authorities, other appellate tribunals, and arbitration proceedings.
- For investigation, inquiry, internal or external review, or requests for information by statutory or governmental authorities or other administrative bodies recognized by law. Examples include the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, Income Tax Authority, SEBI, stock exchanges, the Reserve Bank of India, and sectoral regulatory bodies.
- In compliance with applicable laws, regulations, rules, and requirements, including company, securities, income tax, and banking laws.
- To fulfill contractual obligations under contracts, agreements, arrangements, settlements, understandings, or undertakings.
- When sharing information with intermediaries and fiduciaries such as auditors, merchant bankers, management consultants, partners, collaborators, advisers, or other consultants.
- To obtain legal, financial, or other professional advice, or for accounting, audit, litigation defense, or dispute resolution.
- For transactions that would entail an obligation to make an open offer under the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011, where the Board of Directors determines that sharing the information is in the best interests of the Company.
- For transactions that do not attract an open-offer obligation under the Takeover Regulations, where the Board of Directors determines that sharing the information is in the best interests of the Company.
- For acquisitions, mergers, amalgamations, or other corporate restructuring, including legal due diligence of target or merging companies and advice on commercial matters such as transaction structuring or valuation.
- For sharing financial information required to prepare the consolidated financial statements of the holding company.
- For sharing information with statutory, secretarial, internal, or cost auditors in the course of their duties, or when obtaining certificates, comfort, or confirmations from them, including for placing transactions before the Board.
- For activities undertaken by the Company in furtherance of the objects listed in its memorandum of association.

Information constituting UPSI must be made generally available at least two trading days before the proposed transaction is effected, in a form adequate and fair to cover all relevant and material facts.

## Infosys Code of Conduct

The Infosys Code of Conduct sets out the Company’s core values, shared responsibilities, global commitments, and promises. It provides guidance on ethical conduct, identifies situations requiring particular attention, and refers employees to additional resources and communication channels. Employees may be required to confirm in writing that they have reviewed, understood, and agreed to follow the Code.

The Code is available as:

- Code of Conduct and Ethics
- Code of Conduct and Ethics (Digital)

## A MESSAGE FROM THE CEO & MD

The CEO and Managing Director’s message states that integrity and transparency, fairness, leadership by example, and excellence are foundational to Infosys’s workplace culture and Code of Conduct. Employees are responsible for upholding the Company’s values and raising concerns about potential violations.

Concerns may be reported to the Office of Integrity and Compliance or through the helpline and incident tracker. The message states that reports will be reviewed while protecting employees against retaliation.

## VALUES: C-LIFE

The Infosys values are represented by the acronym C-LIFE:

| CLIENT VALUE | LEADERSHIP BY EXAMPLE | INTEGRITY & TRANSPARENCY | FAIRNESS | EXCELLENCE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| To surpass client expectations consistently. | To act on the conviction that ordinary people can be inspired and mentored to do extraordinary things. | To be ethical, sincere, and open in all transactions. | To be objective, empathetic, and caring in transactions. | To strive relentlessly, constantly learn, upskill, and improve employees, teams, and services. |

Infosys describes its employees as trustees of the Company’s legacy, resources, assets, and opportunities. Trusteeship includes meeting or exceeding stakeholder commitments, developing employees’ potential, and maintaining the Company’s reputation.

The trusteeship principle also includes using natural resources sustainably and improving the communities in which Infosys operates. The Company describes its CSR programs as supporting positive environmental, social, and economic value.
