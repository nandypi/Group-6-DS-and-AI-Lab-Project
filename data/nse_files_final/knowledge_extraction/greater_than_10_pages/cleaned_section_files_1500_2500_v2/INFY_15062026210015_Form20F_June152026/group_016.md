## VIII.    Risks related to the ADSs

## Historically, our ADSs have traded at a premium to the trading prices of our underlying equity shares. Currently, they do not do so, and they may not continue to do so in the future.

Historically, Infosys ADSs traded at a premium to the trading prices of the underlying equity shares on Indian stock exchanges. The company attributes this premium to the relatively small portion of its market capitalization represented by ADSs, restrictions under Indian law on converting equity shares into ADSs, and an apparent preference among some investors for dollar-denominated securities.

Infosys has completed three secondary ADS offerings, significantly increasing the number of outstanding ADSs. Restrictions under Indian law on issuing ADSs have also been relaxed over time. Consequently, the ADSs currently do not command a premium and may not trade at a premium in the future.

If a substantial amount of ADSs is converted into underlying equity shares in India, ADS liquidity on the New York Stock Exchange and the ADS price could be affected.

## Sales of our equity shares may adversely affect the prices of our equity shares and ADSs.

Sales of substantial amounts of equity shares, including sales by insiders in the public market, or the perception that such sales may occur, could adversely affect the market price of Infosys equity shares or ADSs and the company’s ability to raise capital through securities offerings.

Infosys may sponsor the sale of shares held by certain shareholders, as it has done in the past, or issue new shares. The company cannot predict the timing or effect of future sales or the availability of equity shares for future sale on prevailing equity share or ADS prices.

## The  price  of  our  ADSs  and  the  U.S.  dollar  value  of  any  dividends  we  declare  may  be  negatively  affected  by fluctuations in the U.S. dollar to Indian rupee exchange rate.

Fluctuations between the Indian rupee and the U.S. dollar affect the conversion by Deutsche Bank Trust Company Americas, the depositary for the ADSs, of cash dividends paid in Indian rupees on the equity shares represented by the ADSs.

## An investor in our ADSs may not be able to exercise pre-emptive rights for additional shares and may thereby suffer dilution of such investor's equity interest in us.

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013, an Indian company must offer equity shareholders pre-emptive rights to subscribe and pay for a proportionate number of shares to maintain their existing ownership percentages before issuing new equity shares, unless three-fourths of shareholders voting on the resolution waive those rights.

ADS holders may be unable to exercise pre-emptive rights for the equity shares underlying their ADSs unless a registration statement under the Securities Act of 1933, as amended, is effective or an exemption from registration is available. Infosys is not obligated to prepare or file such a registration statement. Any decision to do so would depend on the associated costs, potential liabilities, perceived benefits, and other relevant factors.

If such securities are issued, they may be issued to the depositary, which may sell them for the benefit of ADS holders. There is no assurance regarding the value the depositary would receive from such sales. If ADS holders cannot exercise pre-emptive rights, their proportional interests in Infosys would be reduced.

## ADS holders may be restricted in their ability to exercise voting rights.

The SEBI Listing Regulations and the Indian Companies Act, 2013 require an e-voting facility for shareholder resolutions in accordance with prescribed procedures. This may allow ADS holders to vote on resolutions regardless of their location or ability to attend shareholder meetings.

At Infosys’s request, the depositary will electronically mail ADS holders any shareholder-meeting notice received from the company, together with instructions for directing the depositary to exercise voting rights represented by the ADSs. If timely voting instructions are received, the depositary will endeavor to vote according to those instructions.

The depositary’s ability to carry out voting instructions may be limited by practical and legal limitations and the terms of the deposited securities. ADS holders may not receive voting materials in time to return instructions. Securities for which no voting instructions are received will not be voted. Other communications, notices, or offerings made only to equity shareholders may not be forwarded to ADS holders, limiting their participation in certain offerings, transactions, or votes.

## ADS holders may be restricted in their ability to participate in a buy-back of shares offered by us.

Under Indian law, a company may acquire its own equity shares without court or tribunal approval if it complies with the prescribed rules, regulations, and conditions of the Indian Companies Act, 2013. Public companies listed on recognized Indian stock exchanges must also comply with the SEBI (Buy-back of Securities) Regulations, 2018, as amended.

As a public company listed on two recognized stock exchanges in India, Infosys must comply with the applicable provisions of the Indian Companies Act, 2013 and the Buy-back Regulations.

For ADS holders to participate in a purchase of Infosys shares under the tender-offer route, Infosys must obtain exemptive relief from the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, and ADS holders must convert their ADSs into equity shares and tender those shares into the offer.

## It may be difficult for holders of our ADSs to enforce any judgment obtained in the United States against us.

Because Infosys is incorporated under Indian law and is primarily located outside the United States, ADS holders may have difficulty serving process on the company outside the United States. They may also be unable to enforce judgments obtained against Infosys in U.S. courts, including judgments based solely on U.S. federal securities laws.

The United States and India do not currently have a treaty providing for reciprocal recognition and enforcement of judgments, other than arbitration awards, in civil and commercial matters. Accordingly, a final U.S. federal or state court judgment for payment of money based on civil liability would not be directly executable by an Indian court.

The party in whose favor the U.S. judgment is rendered may bring a new suit in a competent Indian court based on that judgment. The suit must be brought within three years from the date of the U.S. judgment and would proceed like any other civil-liability suit in India.

An Indian court may be unlikely to award damages on the same basis as a foreign court and may not enforce a foreign judgment if it considers the damages inconsistent with Indian law. RBI approval under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, would separately be required to repatriate amounts outside India as damages, including amounts obtained through execution of a judgment.

## Holders of ADSs are subject to the Securities and Exchange Board of India's Takeover Code with respect to their acquisitions of ADSs or the underlying equity shares, and this may impose requirements on such holders with respect to disclosure and offers to purchase additional ADSs or equity shares.

The Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers (SEBI) Regulations, 2011, as amended from time to time, apply to publicly listed Indian companies. The Takeover Code therefore applies to Infosys and to any person acquiring its equity shares or voting rights, including through ADSs.

An acquirer, together with persons acting in concert, who acquires shares or voting rights representing 25% or more of the voting rights in or control over Infosys must make an open offer to acquire at least 26% of the company’s total shares. The offer price is determined under the Takeover Code, and a public announcement is required on the date the acquisition is agreed.

If completion of the open offer causes the acquirer and persons acting in concert to exceed the maximum permissible non-public shareholding, the acquirer must reduce the non-public shareholding using methods permitted by SEBI and comply with the public-shareholding threshold within the timeline prescribed under the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957.

An acquisition by an acquirer and persons acting in concert who already hold 25% or more of the shares or voting rights triggers an open-offer requirement if the additional shares or voting rights would enable them to exercise more than 5% of the voting rights.

When an acquisition causes aggregate shareholding to reach 5% or more, the acquirer must disclose the aggregate shareholding and voting rights to Infosys and the relevant stock exchanges within two working days.

An acquirer and persons acting in concert holding 5% or more must disclose changes in shareholding or voting rights exceeding 2% of the company’s total shares or voting rights compared with the last reported holding. The disclosure must be made within two working days of the acquisition, disposal, or sale, or receipt of intimation of allotment, to Infosys and the relevant stock exchanges. This disclosure is required even if the change causes the holding to fall below 5%.

The Takeover Code may impose conditions that discourage a potential acquirer and prevent an acquisition of Infosys that could otherwise benefit equity holders.
