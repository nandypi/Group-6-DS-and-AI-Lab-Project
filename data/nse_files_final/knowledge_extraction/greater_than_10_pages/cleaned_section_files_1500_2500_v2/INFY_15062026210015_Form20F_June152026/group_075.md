## 1.4 Use of estimates and judgments

The preparation of the consolidated financial statements in conformity with IFRS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies; reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses; and disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities.

Critical accounting estimates and judgments may change from period to period, and actual results may differ from those estimates. Changes are reflected in the consolidated financial statements in the period in which they are made. If material, their effects are disclosed in the notes.

## 1.5 Critical accounting estimates and judgements

### a. Revenue recognition

The Group’s customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is considered for recognition and measurement when:

- The contract has been approved in writing by the parties.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- The contract is legally enforceable.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations based on the deliverables and the customer’s ability to benefit independently from them. Identifying performance obligations and allocating the transaction price to them involves significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method. The method used depends on the contractual promises and nature of the deliverables.

For other fixed-price contracts, the Group uses the percentage-of-completion method. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs. Estimating total efforts or costs involves significant judgment and is reassessed throughout the contract period.

Some integrated service arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Group assesses whether it controls the specified goods or services before transfer by considering factors including:

- Responsibility for fulfilling the promise to provide the goods or services
- Inventory risk
- Pricing discretion
- Other control indicators

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs required to complete the contract.

### b. Income taxes

The Group’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. The Group also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is required when determining income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, management considers:

- Scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities
- Projected future taxable income
- Tax planning strategies
- Historical taxable income
- The periods in which temporary differences become deductible

Based on historical and projected taxable income, management believes the Group will realize the benefits of its deductible temporary differences. The amount of deferred income tax assets considered realizable could be reduced if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. Refer to Note 2.18.

### c. Business combinations and intangible assets

Business combinations are accounted for under IFRS 3 (Revised), *Business Combinations*. Identifiable intangible assets and contingent consideration are fair-valued to determine the net fair value of the acquiree’s identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities.

External valuation experts conduct these valuations. Estimates are required to determine the value of contingent consideration, option arrangements, and intangible assets. The measurements are based on information available at the acquisition date and on assumptions considered reasonable by management. Refer to Notes 2.9 and 2.10.

### d. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Group’s asset base. Periodic depreciation is determined using estimates of each asset’s expected useful life and residual value.

Useful lives and residual values are determined when assets are acquired and reviewed periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. Refer to Note 2.7.

### e. Impairment of Goodwill

Goodwill is tested for impairment annually and whenever there is an indication that the recoverable amount of a cash-generating unit (CGU) is less than its carrying amount.

Goodwill is allocated to the CGU or group of CGUs that benefits from acquisition synergies and represents the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes.

The recoverable amount of CGUs is determined as the higher of value-in-use and fair value less costs to sell. Key assumptions in cash-flow projections include:

- Estimated long-term growth rates
- Weighted average cost of capital
- Estimated operating margins

Refer to Note 2.9.

## 1.6 Recent accounting pronouncements

### New and revised IFRS Standards in issue but not yet effective:

- IFRS 18, *Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements*
- Amendments to the classification and measurement of financial instruments
- Amendments to IFRS 9, *Financial Instruments*, and IFRS 7, *Financial Instruments: Disclosures*
- Amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7 relating to contracts referencing nature-dependent electricity

### IFRS 18 - Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements

On April 9, 2024, the IASB issued IFRS 18, *Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements*, which will replace IAS 1, *Presentation of Financial Statements*, from its effective date.

IFRS 18 introduces:

- Three categories for income and expenses: operating, investing, and financing
- Disclosure of newly defined management-defined performance measures
- Requirements for disclosure of subtotals of income and expenses
- New requirements for aggregation and disaggregation of financial information based on the identified roles of the primary financial statements and notes

Narrow-scope amendments to IAS 7, *Statement of Cash Flows*, change the starting point for determining operating cash flows under the indirect method from “profit or loss” to “operating profit or loss” and remove optionality around the classification of cash flows from dividends and interest. Consequential amendments have also been made to several other standards.

IFRS 18 is effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2027. Early adoption is permitted. The Group is evaluating the impact of IFRS 18 and other amendments on its consolidated financial statements.

### Amendments to IFRS 9 Financial Instruments and IFRS 7 Financial Instruments: Disclosures

On May 30, 2024, the IASB issued amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7 that:

- Clarify the classification of financial assets with environmental, social, and governance (ESG) and similar features
- Address derecognition of financial liabilities settled through electronic payment systems
- Add disclosure requirements for equity instruments designated at fair value through other comprehensive income
- Add disclosure requirements for financial instruments with contingent features
- Clarify the meaning of “non-recourse” where an entity’s ultimate right to receive cash flows is contractually limited to cash flows generated by specified assets

These amendments are effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026. Early adoption is permitted. The Group evaluated the amendments and concluded that they have no impact on its consolidated financial statements.

On December 18, 2024, the IASB issued further amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7 relating to:

- Factors considered when assessing the own-use requirements for contracts to buy and take delivery of nature-dependent renewable electricity
- Hedge accounting treatment for nature-dependent renewable electricity
- Related disclosures

These amendments are effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026. Early adoption is permitted. The Group evaluated the amendments and concluded that they have no impact on its consolidated financial statements.
