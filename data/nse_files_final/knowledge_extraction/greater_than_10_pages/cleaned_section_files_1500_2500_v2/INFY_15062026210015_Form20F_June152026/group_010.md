Cybersecurity breaches and other information security incidents may increase as Infosys scales, expands its cloud-based offerings and services and AI-powered enterprise transformation, processes increasingly large amounts of client data, and hosts or manages parts of clients' businesses. The risks may be greater in industries involving sensitive data, including financial services, energy, utilities, and healthcare.

Increased dependence on cloud vendors may create additional risks. A consequential, large-scale security failure at a cloud vendor, combined with difficulties in porting data from one vendor to another, could jeopardize Infosys' and its clients' business continuity.

Because Infosys operates across continents, alleged or actual non-compliance with cybersecurity and information security obligations in applicable jurisdictions could lead to regulatory investigations, claims, litigation, damages, fines, penalties, and other liabilities.

Cybersecurity breaches and other data security incidents have previously, and could in the future, adversely affect the business, operations, financial performance, and reputation. Critical or numerous systems could become unavailable or otherwise impaired, disrupting intended business delivery. Incidents could also result in unauthorized access to, or the loss, corruption, or theft of, intellectual property, personal data, or sensitive information.

If Infosys or a third-party service provider suffers a cybersecurity breach or other data security incident, or if such an incident is believed to have occurred, Infosys could face claims, litigation, regulatory investigations and inquiries, damages, fines, penalties, other liabilities, reputational harm, loss of business, and significant investigation, remediation, and response costs. Infosys also expects to incur increased costs to prevent future cybersecurity breaches and information security incidents. Failure to demonstrate strong corporate governance around cybersecurity could further affect its reputation, particularly amid increased ESG focus.

Infosys' cybersecurity insurance covers first-party losses arising from a cybersecurity incident, including forensic costs, the appointment of a crisis consultant, and data restoration. The insurance also covers business-interruption losses resulting from a system shutdown caused by a cyber incident. The coverage may not be adequate for all losses, and current or future coverage may not remain available on commercially reasonable terms or at all.

Privacy breaches and incidents attributed to Infosys under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) or other global data privacy regulations could affect its reputation and create financial liabilities. Risks may also arise if Infosys cannot report breaches to regulators and data subjects within stipulated timeframes, respond to data-subject requests on time, or address client claims for losses caused by privacy breaches involving its employees.

The GDPR and similar data privacy laws in other jurisdictions impose additional obligations, increase business risk exposure, and substantially increase potential penalties. Because the GDPR's enforcement history in Infosys' industry sector is limited, the application of certain obligations remains difficult to predict. Regulators may determine that Infosys' compliance measures are insufficient and impose fines or public censure, which could harm its business and reputation.

The number of data privacy incidents may increase with the scale of Infosys' operations and its move to a hybrid working model. More of its work may also become subject to privacy regulations as countries adopt new regulations or strengthen existing laws similar to the GDPR.

Infosys established its data privacy function more than a decade ago. The function operates as an independent business-enabling function, reports to top management, and uses the globally recognized Privacy Information Management System (PIMS) framework. Infosys assesses its liabilities as a processor and controller and implements controls where required to mitigate risks. It has policies and procedures for identifying and reporting privacy breaches to affected data subjects and/or regulators, as required, within stipulated timeframes. Despite these controls and some insurance coverage, Infosys remains exposed to data privacy breaches that could adversely affect its financial results and reputation.

AI-based tools and technologies have increasingly proliferated across the digital industry and Infosys' business, introducing new privacy threats that are still at an early stage of understanding for both the industry and regulators. Infosys may not be able to anticipate or adequately manage privacy threats arising from greater AI adoption.
