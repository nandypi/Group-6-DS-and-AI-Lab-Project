## Reddeppa Gundluru

The shareholder asked about Infosys’ two- to five-year vision, FY2025–FY2026 revenue growth and profitability outlook, initiatives in the BFSI and manufacturing verticals, digital transformation focus areas, portfolio diversification, artificial intelligence and emerging technologies, changes in other expenses, and the potential impact of geopolitical tensions on revenue.

## Nandan Nilekani

### Capital allocation and bonus issue

In response to questions about a bonus issue, the Company stated that it has a defined Capital Allocation Policy. Effective FY2025, Infosys expects to return approximately 85% of free cash flow cumulatively over a five-year period through a combination of semiannual dividends, share buybacks, and special dividends, subject to applicable laws and requisite approvals.

Under the policy, Infosys expects to progressively increase its annual dividend per share, excluding any special dividend. The Company had no announcement regarding a bonus issue at the time of the meeting.

### Dividend policy

For FY2025, the Board proposed a final dividend of ₹22 per share, in addition to the interim dividend of ₹21 per share paid in the second quarter. The total dividend for the year was ₹43 per share, an increase of 13.2% over the previous year.

Over the previous 20 years, Infosys’ dividend per share increased at a compound annual growth rate of 23%. The Company stated that annual payments may vary but that it remained committed to returning 85% of free cash flow to shareholders over FY2025–FY2029.

### Macroeconomic and geopolitical risks

Infosys stated that it remained confident in its positioning in cost takeout and discretionary spending despite macroeconomic challenges. Its cloud and AI offerings through Infosys Cobalt and Infosys Topaz were described as supporting potential market-share gains.

The Company cited a robust deal pipeline, continued capability investments, and differentiation in its offerings. It also referred to 58 leadership ratings in digital and nine in AI.

### AI and workforce transformation

Infosys described its approach as “AI-first,” involving a transformation across work, workplace, and workforce. The approach includes talent transformation, smart workplaces, and the development of smart and sustainable products.

The Company is investing in employee AI reskilling and upskilling. More than 275,000 employees had been trained in AI at different proficiency levels. More than 20,000 employees were using GitHub for coding. Infosys also stated that it was investing in developing additional AI builders and AI masters and working with partners to upskill employees.

### Global capability centers

Infosys stated that artificial intelligence and the expansion of global capability centers (GCCs) represented new growth opportunities rather than threats. The current GCC trend was described as being driven by “innovation arbitrage” rather than cost arbitrage.

Companies are establishing research centers and AI/ML centers as GCCs, and Infosys is helping several of them. The Company therefore described GCCs as critical clients rather than competitors.

## Salil Parekh

### Use of AI across projects

AI is being used across many Infosys projects, and clients are prioritizing strategic AI partnerships. The Company reported traction in generative AI applications including:

- Software engineering
- Process optimization
- Customer support
- Advisory services
- Sales and marketing

Infosys also reported business cases focused on improving efficiency and customer experience, particularly through the use of AI agents. A significant part of this work is supported by the Infosys Topaz capability.

### Growth outlook

Infosys maintained its previously issued growth guidance of 0% to 3%. The Company had identified challenges in the operating environment at the beginning of the year and stated that it would provide updates with its next quarterly results.

Infosys stated that its cloud, AI, cost-efficiency, and automation offerings positioned it to support clients on both growth and cost objectives. The Company reported a strong large-deals pipeline and continued investments in capability development and differentiation.

As clients consider consolidating their spending, Infosys stated that it expected to benefit from those discussions. The Company also highlighted its strong client relationships and partnerships.

### Recent acquisitions

Infosys stated that its two acquisitions were recent and that integration and growth initiatives were at an early stage. Plans had been developed for integrating the businesses, expanding their energy consulting and cybersecurity capabilities, and taking those capabilities to additional clients and geographies.

The Company referred specifically to growth opportunities in the US and Australian markets and stated that the capabilities could be extended to other clients and geographies.
