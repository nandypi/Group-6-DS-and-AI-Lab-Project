## Shareholder Questions

### Krishan Lal Chadha

The shareholder cited the following figures for 2025:

- Revenue: ₹1,62,990 crore
- Clients: 1,869
- Employees: 323,578

The shareholder questioned the company’s growth from 2021 to 2025, stating that it had grown by 60% while shareholders had not seen a comparable increase. The shareholder also referred to 174 active employees or clients, but the translated wording was unclear.

The shareholder cited CSR expenditure of ₹526 crore out of ₹539 crore and stated that spending had been concentrated in Karnataka, Bhubaneswar, and Odisha. The shareholder asked why CSR spending had not also been directed to Delhi and other central areas.

The shareholder compared net profit and EPS between 2020 and the previous year, citing a change from 0.62 to 0.76. The shareholder attributed the profitability improvement primarily to higher other income and lower expenses, as shown on page 58, rather than to an increase in core profitability.

The dividends discussed were:

- Previous year: ₹38, comprising ₹18 interim and ₹20 final
- Current year: ₹21 interim and ₹22 final

The shareholder requested a higher dividend or greater appreciation for shareholders.

The shareholder referred to page 43 and asked for more detail on the distribution of benefits to two lakh rural families, including a distinction showing how the beneficiaries received the benefits.

The shareholder referred to page 59 and asked why other reserves and surplus had declined during the financial year.

The shareholder referred to page 70 and stated that Infosys had approximately 109 subsidiaries, including one in Israel and two in Germany. The shareholder expressed concern about the performance of the German subsidiaries over the previous two years and asked what measures were being planned to prevent further losses from affecting the company’s profitability.

The shareholder stated that MD and CEO Salil Parekh received 382,071 equity shares as an incentive during the year. The shareholder estimated their value at more than ₹50 crore and referred to approximately ₹80 crore in salary and remuneration disclosed on page 83.

The shareholder referred to page 186 and stated that 103 complaints involving physical harassment and sexual harassment had remained pending in both the previous and current financial years. The shareholder asked why the complaints had not been resolved sooner and what initiatives had been taken to improve employee safety, prevent misconduct, and resolve the pending complaints.

### Kaushik Shahukar

The shareholder asked how Infosys was recalibrating its growth strategy to maintain deal flow and protect margins in FY26 amid a global slowdown in IT spending and rising geopolitical uncertainty.

The shareholder also requested consideration of a branch visit or CSR initiative visit for speaker shareholders and suggested that savings from the virtual AGM be used for shareholder welfare.

### Manjit Singh

The shareholder referred to Infosys’ establishment in 1993 and its subsequent progress. The shareholder asked whether the company was considering a bonus similar to those issued around 2018–2019.

Questions were raised about corporate governance and whether any related decision would be taken at a board meeting.

The shareholder referred to 39 investor complaints during the last quarter and asked how they had been resolved, why they had arisen, and what the complaints indicated about corporate-governance shortcomings.

The shareholder identified Infosys’ BSE code as 500209 and its trading symbol as INFY.

The shareholder asked what proportion of total business came from financial services, healthcare, and other services.

The shareholder also asked about employee benefits and questioned the decline in basic EPS, citing a decrease from 65.62 to 61.58.
