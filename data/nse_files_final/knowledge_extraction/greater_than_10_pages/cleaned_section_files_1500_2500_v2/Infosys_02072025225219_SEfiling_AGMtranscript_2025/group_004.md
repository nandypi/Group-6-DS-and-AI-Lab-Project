## Nandan Nilekani

The following resolutions were tabled at the 44th AGM:

1. Adoption of the financial statements of Infosys Limited, including the consolidated financial statements for the financial year ended March 31, 2025, together with the auditors’ reports.
2. Declaration of a final dividend of ₹22 per equity share for FY2025. An interim dividend of ₹21 per equity share had already been paid for FY2025.
3. Appointment of Salil Parekh as a director liable to retire by rotation.
4. Approval of material related-party transactions of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries with Stater N.V.
5. Approval of material related-party transactions of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries with Stater Nederland B.V.
6. Appointment of Makarand M. Joshi and Company, Company Secretaries, as Secretarial Auditor of the Company.

## Audio and video questions

### Dharav Vipul Jamadar

Shareholder questions included:

- Which sectors would be most and least affected by AI, machine learning, IoT, big data, and other technology disruptions?
- Whether Infosys could use its cash flows and reserves to acquire, merge with, or build technology companies developing and selling software and hardware products, thereby diversifying beyond consulting and services.
- How Infosys conducts currency hedging for currencies with derivatives and for currencies without derivatives or with illiquid markets.
- Whether Europe could generate the maximum deal wins in the coming period. The shareholder cited 5% growth in European deal wins compared with 2% in the United States, a partnership with Allied Irish Bank, and potential India–UK and EU free-trade agreements.
- What Infosys expected regarding government deals during the fiscal year and the potential margin improvement from those deals.

### H.S. Patel

The shareholder cited the following company information and raised related questions:

- Dividend of ₹43 per share, comprising the ₹22 final dividend and ₹21 interim dividend.
- AI, cloud, data, digital capabilities, and cost efficiency as key balance-sheet strengths.
- Operations across six continents, 59 offices in different countries, and 292 offices in India.
- Cash and cash equivalents of ₹47,500 crores, including questions about their planned utilization and average rate of return.
- Future expansion of AI, cloud, digital, and data capabilities.
- A decline in the stock price over the preceding six months and steps to restore investor confidence.
- Reported revenue of ₹1,62,990 crores, the factors behind the growth, its sustainability, and why profits had not increased at a similar rate.
- Global CSR spending of approximately ₹628 crores.
- Infosys’ position as a top employer for the fourth consecutive year.
- Women comprising approximately 39% of the workforce.
- The company’s 823 patents, including questions about the number of pending and granted patents.

### Jaydip Bakshi

The shareholder raised questions about:

- Preparation for geopolitical risks that could affect global delivery centers.
- Sustaining or improving operating margins during geopolitical turmoil.
- Differentiating Infosys’ AI offerings in a rapidly commoditizing market.
- Foreign-exchange earnings.
- Employee retention among nearly 3 lakh-plus professionals recruited for client service delivery.
- Employee engagement, productivity improvement, and preparing the workforce for future requirements.
- Acquiring startups in emerging technology areas.
- Key growth drivers and Infosys’ positioning relative to global peers over the next three to five years.
- The role and work of the Infosys Knowledge Institute in business research.
- Potential Government of India orders in the pipeline.
