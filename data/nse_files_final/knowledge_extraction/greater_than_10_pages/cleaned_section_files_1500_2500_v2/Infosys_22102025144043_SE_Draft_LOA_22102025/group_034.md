## Section 302 Tests

An exchange of Equity Shares for cash generally will be a substantially disproportionate redemption with respect to a U.S. holder if:

1. The percentage of voting stock owned by the U.S. holder immediately after the exchange is less than 80% of the percentage owned immediately before the exchange; and
2. Immediately after the exchange, the U.S. holder owns less than 50% of the total combined voting power of all classes of stock entitled to vote.

For purposes of the Section 302 tests, each U.S. holder must consider Equity Shares and ADSs constructively owned under applicable attribution rules. These rules may treat a U.S. holder as owning Equity Shares and ADSs held by certain family members and related entities, as well as shares and ADSs the holder has the right to acquire by exercising an option. Family attribution may be waived in certain circumstances. U.S. holders should consult their own tax advisors regarding the application of these rules.

If an exchange fails to satisfy the substantially disproportionate test, it may still satisfy the not essentially equivalent to a dividend test. The exchange satisfies this test if it results in a meaningful reduction of the U.S. holder's equity interest in the Company, based on the holder's particular facts and circumstances.

The Internal Revenue Service has indicated in published rulings that a relatively minor reduction may be treated as not essentially equivalent to a dividend where the U.S. holder's relative equity interest is minimal and the holder does not exercise control over or participate in managing corporate affairs.

Because the Section 302 tests are applied on a stockholder-by-stockholder basis, the Buyback may be treated as a sale or exchange for some U.S. holders and as a distribution for others.

## Sale or Exchange

Subject to the passive foreign investment company (PFIC) rules, if a U.S. holder recognizes gain or loss for U.S. federal income tax purposes from disposing of Equity Shares for cash, the gain or loss equals the difference between:

- The U.S. dollar value of the amount realized; and
- The U.S. holder's tax basis in the Equity Shares, determined in U.S. dollars.

Gain or loss generally will be long-term capital gain or loss for Equity Shares held for more than 12 months at the time of sale or other disposition. Any gain recognized generally will be income from sources within the United States for foreign tax credit limitation purposes. Long-term capital gains of non-corporate U.S. holders are generally taxed at preferential rates.

Capital gains realized by a U.S. holder on the sale of Equity Shares may be subject to tax in India, including withholding tax. Due to foreign tax credit limitations, a U.S. holder may not be able to use such taxes as a credit against its U.S. federal income tax liability.

## Distribution

If a U.S. holder is not treated under the Section 302 tests as recognizing gain or loss on an exchange of Equity Shares for cash, the holder will be treated as having received a distribution from the Company.

The gross amount of the distribution generally will be treated as dividend income to the extent it is made from the Company's current or accumulated earnings and profits, as determined under U.S. federal income tax principles. The dividends will not be eligible for the dividends-received deduction generally available to corporate U.S. holders.

To the extent the Buyback exceeds the Company's current and accumulated earnings and profits, as determined under U.S. federal income tax principles, the excess will be treated first as a tax-free return of the U.S. holder's tax basis in the Equity Shares and thereafter as capital gain.

The Company does not intend to calculate its earnings and profits according to U.S. tax accounting principles. Accordingly, if the Buyback is treated as a distribution on Equity Shares, the distribution generally will be taxed as a dividend for U.S. tax purposes.

## Qualified Foreign Corporation Considerations

Subject to conditions and limitations, including the PFIC rules, dividends paid to non-corporate U.S. holders, including individuals, may qualify for a reduced tax rate if the Company is deemed to be a qualified foreign corporation for U.S. federal income tax purposes.

A qualified foreign corporation includes a foreign corporation if:

1. Its shares are readily tradable on an established securities market in the United States; or
2. It is eligible for benefits under a comprehensive income tax treaty with the United States, including the applicable Treaty.

The Company's ADSs are listed on NYSE. The Company believes, but cannot assure, that the ADSs will be readily tradable on an established securities market in the United States and that the Company will be a qualified foreign corporation with respect to dividends paid on the ADSs.

Based on existing guidance, it is not clear whether a dividend on an Equity Share will be treated as a qualified dividend because the Equity Shares are not themselves listed on a U.S. exchange. However, the Company may be eligible for benefits under the Treaty.

A corporation is not a qualified foreign corporation if it is a PFIC in the current or prior taxable year.

## Currency Conversion

If proceeds are paid in Indian Rupees, the amount included in a U.S. holder's income will be the U.S. dollar value of the Indian Rupee payments, determined using the spot exchange rate between Indian Rupees and U.S. dollars on the date the proceeds are included in income. This applies regardless of whether the payment is actually converted into U.S. dollars.

Generally, any gain or loss resulting from currency exchange fluctuations between the date a dividend is paid and the date the payment is converted into U.S. dollars will be treated as U.S.-source ordinary income or loss.
