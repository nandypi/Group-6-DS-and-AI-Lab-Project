## Surcharge

For individuals, HUFs, AOPs, and BOIs:

- A 15% surcharge applies where total income exceeds ₹10 million (approximately US$0.11 million).
- A 10% surcharge applies where total income exceeds ₹5 million (approximately US$0.06 million) but does not exceed ₹10 million (approximately US$0.11 million).

For foreign companies:

- A 5% surcharge applies where total income exceeds ₹100 million (approximately US$1.14 million).
- A 2% surcharge applies where total income exceeds ₹10 million (approximately US$0.11 million) but does not exceed ₹100 million (approximately US$1.14 million).

The U.S. dollar amounts are based on an exchange rate of ₹88.06/USD as of September 19, 2025.

## Cess

Health and Education Cess at 4% is leviable in all cases.

## Taxation for American Depositary Share Holders

An ADS holder may participate in the buyback by withdrawing their ADSs from the depositary facility so that they become holders of Equity Shares as of the Record Date and then participating in the buyback.

There can be no assurance that Equity Shares offered by an ADS holder in the buyback will be accepted. ADS holders should consult their legal, financial, and tax advisors before participating, including regarding regulatory approvals and tax issues.

### Income-tax implications on withdrawal and re-deposit of Equity Shares

- There are arguments that conversion of ADSs into Equity Shares should not be subject to capital gains tax, but this position is not free from doubt because the law is not clear on this issue.
- Shares not accepted by the Company for the buyback may be reconverted into ADSs. Two possible views exist on whether this reconversion is a taxable transfer that would attract capital gains tax. Arguments support both views, although the view that reconversion is not a taxable transfer may be considered stronger.

The taxation disclosure summarizes applicable law and is not a complete analysis or listing of all potential tax consequences of disposing of Equity Shares. It is not binding on regulators, and regulators may take a position contrary to the views described. Holders should consult their own tax advisors regarding the tax provisions applicable to their circumstances.

## Certain Material U.S. Federal Income Tax Consequences

This section summarizes certain material U.S. federal income tax consequences that may be relevant to U.S. holders participating in the buyback of Equity Shares. It is intended for general information only.

For this discussion, “U.S. holders” include:

- Individuals who are citizens or residents of the United States.
- Corporations, or entities treated as corporations for U.S. federal income tax purposes, created under U.S. law or the law of a U.S. political subdivision.
- Estates whose income is subject to U.S. federal income taxation regardless of source.
- Trusts that have elected to be treated as U.S. persons under applicable U.S. Treasury Regulations or for which a U.S. court exercises primary supervision and a U.S. person controls all substantial decisions.

The summary is limited to U.S. holders who:

- Hold Equity Shares or ADSs as capital assets, generally property held for investment.
- Are not residents in India for purposes of the Convention between the United States and India for the Avoidance of Double Taxation and the Prevention of Fiscal Evasion with Respect to Taxes on Income.

If a partnership or pass-through entity holds Equity Shares or ADSs, the tax treatment of its partners or owners will generally depend on their status and the activities of the entity. Partners or owners should consult their own tax advisors.

The summary does not address:

- U.S. state, local, or non-U.S. tax laws.
- The potential Medicare contribution tax on net investment income.
- U.S. non-income tax laws.
- Special tax rules applicable to banks, insurance companies, regulated investment companies, pension plans, real estate investment trusts, financial institutions, securities or currency dealers, tax-exempt entities, persons liable for alternative minimum tax, or persons holding Equity Shares or ADSs in straddles, hedging transactions, or conversion transactions.
- Holders with a functional currency other than the U.S. dollar.
- Holders of 10% or more of the Company’s Equity Shares by voting power or value.
- Controlled foreign corporations, foreign-controlled foreign corporations, passive foreign investment companies, or corporations accumulating earnings to avoid U.S. federal income tax.
- Persons subject to special tax accounting rules because income from Equity Shares or ADSs is included in an applicable financial statement.
- Persons receiving Equity Shares or ADSs through the exercise of an option or as compensation.
- Persons deemed to sell Equity Shares or ADSs under the constructive sale provisions of the Internal Revenue Code.
- Certain U.S. expatriates and former citizens or long-term residents of the United States.

The summary is based on the Internal Revenue Code as in effect on the date of the document, U.S. Treasury Regulations in effect or proposed as of that date, and judicial and administrative interpretations available on or before that date. It also assumes that obligations under the Deposit Agreement and related agreements will be performed according to their terms. These provisions may change, including retroactively, which could affect the tax consequences described.

Each prospective U.S. holder participating in the buyback should consult their own tax advisor regarding U.S. federal, state, local, and non-U.S. tax consequences.

### Ownership of ADSs

For U.S. federal income tax purposes, U.S. holders generally will be treated as the owners of the Equity Shares represented by their ADSs. Accordingly, conversion of ADSs into Equity Shares to participate in the buyback, and conversion of Equity Shares that are not tendered or are tendered but not accepted into ADSs, generally will not be subject to U.S. federal income tax.

### Tax Treatment of Buyback

An exchange of Equity Shares for cash by a U.S. holder pursuant to the buyback will be a taxable transaction for U.S. federal income tax purposes. Depending on the holder’s circumstances, the holder may be treated as either:

- Recognizing gain or loss from the disposition of the Equity Shares; or
- Receiving a distribution from the Company.

Under Section 302 of the Internal Revenue Code, a tendering U.S. holder will recognize gain or loss on the exchange of Equity Shares for cash if the exchange:

- Results in a “substantially disproportionate” redemption with respect to the U.S. holder; or
- Is “not essentially equivalent to a dividend” with respect to the U.S. holder.
