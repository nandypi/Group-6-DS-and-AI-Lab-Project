## Passive Foreign Investment Company

A non-U.S. corporation will be classified as a Passive Foreign Investment Company (PFIC) for U.S. federal income tax purposes if either:

- At least 75% of its gross income for the taxable year is passive income; or
- On average during the taxable year, at least 50% of its assets by value produce or are held for the production of passive income.

The Company does not believe that it satisfied either PFIC test for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025, and does not expect to satisfy either test for the fiscal year ending March 31, 2026. PFIC status is determined annually and depends on various factors, including the Company's market capitalization. Accordingly, no assurance can be given that the Company was not a PFIC for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025, or that it will not be a PFIC in the current or future fiscal years.

If the Company were a PFIC for any taxable year, U.S. holders generally would be subject to an interest charge and tax at ordinary income rates on excess distributions and gains from the sale or other disposition of Equity Shares, unless an available election applies. Individual U.S. holders also would not be eligible for reduced dividend tax rates if the Company were a PFIC for the fiscal year of the dividend payment or the preceding taxable year.

If the Equity Shares or ADSs remain marketable, a U.S. holder may be able to avoid the excess distribution rules by making a timely mark-to-market election. The Equity Shares or ADSs will generally be considered marketable while regularly traded on a national securities exchange, such as the New York Stock Exchange, or on a regulated or supervised foreign securities exchange. The election applies for the taxable year in which it is made and subsequent taxable years unless the securities cease to be regularly traded on a qualified exchange or the IRS consents to revocation.

A mark-to-market election cannot be made for lower-tier PFICs that the Company may own. U.S. holders may therefore remain subject to PFIC rules for indirect interests in investments held by the Company that are treated as equity interests in a PFIC, including interests in subsidiaries.

A U.S. holder might also avoid the excess distribution rules by making a timely qualified electing fund (QEF) election and being taxed currently on the holder's pro rata share of the Company's income and gain. The Company has not provided and does not plan to provide the information necessary for a QEF election; therefore, the election would not have been available to U.S. holders.

Certain information reporting obligations on IRS Form 8621 may apply to U.S. holders if the Company is determined to be a PFIC, including in the year of a sale or disposition.

## Backup Withholding Tax and Information Reporting

Dividends paid to, or proceeds from the sale of Equity Shares received by, a U.S. holder may be subject to U.S. information reporting. Backup withholding, currently at a rate of 24%, may apply unless the holder is an exempt recipient, provides a U.S. taxpayer identification number, certifies that the holder is not subject to backup withholding, and otherwise complies with applicable requirements.

Amounts withheld under the backup withholding rules may be claimed as a refund or credit against the holder's U.S. federal income tax if the required information is furnished to the IRS.

## 25. DECLARATION BY THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS

Declaration as required under clause (ix) and clause (x) of Schedule I under the Buyback Regulations:

- **25.1** The Board of Directors confirms that there are no subsisting defaults in:
  - Repayment of deposits;
  - Redemption of debentures or payment of interest thereon;
  - Payment of dividends; or
  - Repayment of term loans or payment of interest thereon to any financial institution or banking company.

- **25.2** The Board of Directors confirms that it has made a full enquiry into the affairs and prospects of the Company and has formed the following opinions:
  - **25.2.1** Immediately following the Board meeting held on September 11, 2025, and the members' resolution approving the Buyback on November 4, 2025, there will be no grounds on which the Company can be found unable to pay its debts.
  - **25.2.2** Having regard to the Company's prospects for the year immediately following September 11, 2025 and the year immediately following November 4, 2025, the Board's intentions concerning management of the Company's business, and the financial resources expected to be available, the Company will be able to meet its liabilities as they fall due and will not become insolvent within one year from either date.
  - **25.2.3** In forming these opinions, the Board considered all liabilities, including prospective and contingent liabilities, as if the Company were being wound up under the Companies Act, 2013 and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, as amended.

The declaration was made and issued pursuant to the resolution passed at the Board meeting held on September 11, 2025.
