## How can an ADS holder participate in the Buyback?

As stated in the Postal Ballot Notice, ADS holders wishing to participate in the Buyback must withdraw the Equity Shares underlying their ADSs before the Record Date, i.e., [●], 2025, and become holders of Equity Shares on the Record Date. The Postal Ballot Notice stated that ADS holders had approximately 49 calendar days from its date to complete the required steps.

See paragraphs 22.26–22.30 of this Letter of Offer, “Participation by ADS Holders,” for additional details.

## What is the purpose of the Buyback?

The Company is undertaking the Buyback after considering its medium-term strategic and operational cash requirements and to return surplus funds to shareholders in accordance with its Capital Allocation Policy. The reasons are set forth in Section 8.

## What are the significant conditions to the Offer?

Under the Buyback Regulations, the Company cannot withdraw the Buyback Offer after the public announcement has been made.

## Following the Buyback, will the Company continue as a public company?

Yes. Completion of the Buyback will not result in the delisting of the Equity Shares from BSE or NSE.

## How does an Eligible Shareholder tender their Equity Shares?

Eligible Shareholders must place orders through their Seller Members, meaning stock brokers, on the separate acquisition window provided by BSE and NSE for the Buyback. They must also submit the required documents with their Tender Forms.

Seller Members may enter orders for both dematerialized and physical Equity Shares. See Sections 22 and 23 and the Tender Form for the required documents and tendering process.

## Can an Eligible Shareholder tender some, but not all, of their Equity Shares?

Yes. An Eligible Shareholder may tender some or all of their Equity Shares. The maximum number that may be tendered is the number of Equity Shares held on the Record Date.

See Section 23 and the Tender Form.

## Once an Eligible Shareholder has tendered Equity Shares in the Buyback, can they withdraw their tender?

Yes. Orders may be modified or cancelled during the Tendering Period, from [●] to [●]. See Section 23.

## How does an Eligible Shareholder withdraw Equity Shares they have previously tendered?

An Eligible Shareholder may contact the Seller Member through whom the initial bid was placed and submit a modification or cancellation request using their registered email address or telephone number. The shareholder may also visit the Seller Member’s branch and submit a written request.

## Do the Promoters, Directors or Key Managerial Personnel of the Company intend to tender their Equity Shares in the Buyback?

The Promoter and Promoter Group and persons in control of the Company may participate in the Buyback. However, the Promoter and Promoter Group have expressed their intention not to participate through letters dated September 14, 2025, September 16, 2025, September 17, 2025, September 18, 2025, and September 19, 2025.

The Directors and Key Managerial Personnel are eligible to participate in the Buyback on the same terms as other Eligible Shareholders. See Section 11.

## If an Eligible Shareholder decides not to tender, how will the Buyback affect their Equity Shares?

Shareholders who do not tender their Equity Shares will have a resultant increase in their shareholding after closure of the Buyback. See Section 23.

## When will the Company pay for the Equity Shares tendered by an Eligible Shareholder and accepted in the Buyback?

For Equity Shares accepted in the Offer, the Company will pay the purchase price in cash promptly after the Offer expires and no later than 5 working days from the closure of the Offer Period. See Section 23.

## Will the Eligible Shareholder have to pay brokerage commissions or other costs and expenses if they tender their Equity Shares?

The Buyback consideration received from the relevant Seller Member for accepted Equity Shares may be net of tax deducted at source, costs, charges, and expenses, including brokerage. The Company will not bear or pay additional costs, charges, or expenses incurred solely by selling Eligible Shareholders. See Section 23.

## What are the income tax consequences if an Eligible Shareholder tenders their Equity Shares?

### Indian tax treatment

The Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 shifted the tax liability for share buybacks to shareholders, and the Company is not required to pay tax on distributed income.

Under Section 2(22)(f) of the Income-tax Act, the entire amount paid by a domestic company for buying back its shares is treated as dividend income in the hands of resident and non-resident shareholders and is taxable under the head “Income from Other Sources.”

Section 46A of the Income-tax Act was amended with effect from October 1, 2024. For a share buyback, the consideration received by the shareholder is deemed to be nil for computing capital gains. Consequently, a capital loss arises for the shareholder equal to the cost of acquisition of the shares bought back.

The Company must deduct tax at source or withhold tax when paying the total Buyback consideration to shareholders, which is treated as dividend under the Income-tax Act. See Section 24 for the detailed taxation note.

### U.S. tax treatment

For a U.S. holder, as defined in Section 24 under “United States – Certain Material U.S. Federal Income Tax Consequences,” exchanging Equity Shares for cash in the Buyback will generally be a taxable transaction for U.S. federal income tax purposes.

Subject to the passive foreign investment company (PFIC) rules described in Section 24, the U.S. holder may be treated as recognizing gain or loss from disposing of the Equity Shares or as receiving a distribution from the Company. Gain or loss will generally be long-term capital gain or loss for Equity Shares held for more than 12 months at the time of disposition.

A distribution will generally be taxed as a dividend for U.S. federal income tax purposes to the extent it is made from the Company’s current or accumulated earnings and profits, as determined under U.S. federal income tax principles. See Section 24 for the detailed taxation note.

### Withdrawal and re-deposit of Equity Shares

Although there are arguments that withdrawing Equity Shares underlying ADSs should not be subject to Indian capital gains tax, this view is not free from doubt because the law is unclear. It is also unclear whether re-depositing Equity Shares to create ADSs, where those shares are not accepted for the Buyback, is subject to Indian taxation.

The Indian, United States, and other tax consequences of participating in the Buyback are complex. Each Eligible Shareholder should consult their own tax advisor. See Section 24.

## What should an Eligible Shareholder do if they have lost their share certificate and wish to participate in the Buyback?

An Eligible Shareholder holding physical shares who has lost their share certificate must obtain a duplicate share certificate before tendering the Equity Shares. The shares may then be tendered in the same manner as other physical Equity Shares holders. See Section 23 and the Tender Form.

## Who can a shareholder talk to in case of any questions relating to the Buyback?

For clarifications or investor grievances, shareholders may contact the Registrar to the Buyback during working days, Monday to Friday, from 10:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Indian Standard Time. Contact details are provided in Section 30.
