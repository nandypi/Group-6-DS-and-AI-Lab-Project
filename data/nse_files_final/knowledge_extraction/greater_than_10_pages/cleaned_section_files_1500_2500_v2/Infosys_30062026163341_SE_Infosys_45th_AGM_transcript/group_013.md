## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys reported FY26 growth of 3.1% in constant currency terms despite lower third-party revenues and higher offshoring. On an organic basis, management stated that Infosys had grown faster than its peers for more than seven years.

FY26 large deal wins totaled $15 billion, with 55% net new. Free cash flow was $3.7 billion, while operating margin remained stable despite investments in sales and marketing, AI, and talent.

Management described the overall demand environment as soft, with clients remaining cautious because of macroeconomic concerns. Growth was also affected by what management described as AI inflation. As clients move from siloed AI adoption to enterprise-wide adoption, Infosys expects spending to increase across six AI value pools and the addressable market to expand by $300 billion to $400 billion.

Current-year guidance is revenue growth of 1.5 to 3.5, excluding the Optimum acquisition completed during the quarter, and an operating margin of 20% to 22%. Infosys does not provide guidance for future years.

Infosys follows a capital allocation policy under which, from FY25, it expects to return approximately 85% of cumulative free cash flow over a five-year period through a combination of semi-annual dividends, share buybacks, and/or special dividends, subject to applicable laws and required approvals. The company expects to progressively increase dividend per share. The remaining cash is available for strategic acquisitions.

Management stated that Infosys maintains a disciplined focus on tuck-in acquisitions to fill capability or segment gaps and accelerate growth through synergies. Its balance sheet provides cash allocation capacity for acquisitions and allows the company to make acquisition decisions quickly.

Infosys disclosed AI revenue in Q3 at approximately 5.5% of revenue, equivalent to approximately $1 billion annualized, and stated that it was growing faster than the company average.

Management did not comment on the share price. It stated that the company is focused on its business, client value creation, delivery capabilities, client capabilities, AI capabilities, partnerships, and its trained employee base.

The recently concluded ₹18,000 crore share buyback resulted in EPS accretion for shareholders. Management stated that Infosys has announced multiple bonus issues, but there was no announcement regarding a new bonus issue at the time of the AGM.

Infosys stated that six AI-first services opportunity areas could represent approximately $300 billion to $400 billion of opportunities by 2030. The company is investing in sales and marketing, AI capabilities, and its partnership ecosystem.

Operating and free cash flow were approximately ₹1,400 crore lower in FY26, primarily because FY25 included higher income-tax refunds. The refunds related to orders received under Sections 250 and 254 of the Income Tax Act for certain assessment years, including years before FY25.

## A.G.S. Manikantha

The Ministry of Corporate Affairs and SEBI have permitted companies to hold AGMs through videoconference without the physical presence of members at a common venue. Infosys stated that it would evaluate holding a physical AGM in the future and keep shareholders informed.

Infosys ADRs are listed on the NYSE. Investors seeking to purchase the ADRs may contact authorized bankers, brokers, or depository participants and follow the applicable process for foreign investments.

## Nandan M. Nilekani

Management stated that the AI opportunity is large because of the range of new use cases enabled by AI. The main execution risks are reorienting service offerings toward AI-first and AI-augmented services, aligning sales and delivery teams, transforming talent, and developing new pricing models, including outcome-based pricing.

CSR proposals are evaluated by the Infosys Foundation based on alignment with its focus areas and potential impact. Proposals may be submitted through the CSR grant application on the Infosys Foundation portal.

Infosys stated that its water stewardship program includes 11 large lake rejuvenation projects. These projects created additional capacity of 4.2 billion litres, taking the total capacity of the lakes to more than 10 billion litres. The projects are intended to enhance water tables and improve community access to water. Future projects will be undertaken in areas where Infosys operates.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys crossed $20 billion in total revenue in FY26 and grew 3.1% in constant currency terms. Management stated that Infosys had been the fastest-growing company among its peers on an organic basis over the preceding seven years.

The communications and manufacturing verticals, and the European geography, grew significantly above the company average in FY26. Financial services and the combined services, utilities, energy, and resources grouping also grew above the company average in constant currency terms.

Management expects growth to accelerate in financial services and in the energy, utilities, resources, and services grouping during FY27.

Operating margins remained stable despite cross-currency effects, changes in the employee pyramid, and acquisitions that reduced the company’s weighted margin. Infosys stated that there would be no change to its capital allocation policy, under which 85% of free cash flow is returned to shareholders and the remaining 15% is available for strategic acquisitions.
