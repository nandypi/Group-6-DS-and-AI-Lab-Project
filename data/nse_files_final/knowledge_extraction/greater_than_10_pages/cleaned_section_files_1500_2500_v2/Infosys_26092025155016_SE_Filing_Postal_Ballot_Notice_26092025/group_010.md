## a. India

### Withdrawal and Re-Deposit of Equity Shares by ADS Holders

The tax treatment of withdrawing equity shares underlying ADSs is not fully clear under Indian law. The re-deposit of equity shares for the creation of ADSs is particularly uncertain because of limited precedent.

The Company cannot assure ADS holders that tax will not be imposed or that the Indian bank, broker, or nominee maintaining the ADS holder’s Indian Demat account (Subtype DR) and Brokerage Account will not withhold tax on the re-deposit of equity shares.

ADS holders should consider the potential tax consequences in India, their country of residence, and any other relevant jurisdiction before requesting withdrawal or re-deposit of equity shares.

## b. United States

### i. Withdrawal and Re-Deposit of Equity Shares by ADS Holders

For U.S. federal income tax purposes, ADS holders generally will be treated as the owners of the underlying equity shares. Accordingly, withdrawing equity shares underlying ADSs and re-depositing equity shares that are not tendered in the Buyback, or that are tendered but not accepted, generally will not be subject to U.S. federal income tax.

Further information on U.S. federal income tax consequences for holders of ADSs and/or equity shares is included in the Company’s Form 20-F.

### ii. Tendering Equity Shares in the Buyback

Tax treatment relevant to tendering equity shares in the Buyback will be provided in the Company’s Form 6-K and Letter of Offer. ADS holders should review those tax considerations before requesting withdrawal of the underlying equity shares for participation in the Buyback.

Each ADS holder should consult their own tax advisor regarding the federal, state, and local tax consequences of withdrawing and re-depositing equity shares and participating in the Buyback.

## v. Further Information

ADS holders should consult their legal, financial, and tax advisors before surrendering ADSs for cancellation, requesting withdrawal of the underlying equity shares, or participating in the Buyback. This includes advice regarding regulatory approvals and tax considerations in India, the United States, and other relevant jurisdictions.

## 14. Note on Taxation

Under the Income-tax Act, 1961, as amended by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024, the entire consideration paid by a company to shareholders for a buyback of shares is treated as dividend income. Section 2(22)(f) of the Income-tax Act includes payments made by a company for purchasing its own shares under Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013. The amendment applies to buybacks conducted on or after October 1, 2024.

Section 46A of the Income-tax Act was amended with effect from October 1, 2024. For a buyback, the consideration received by the shareholder is deemed to be nil for capital-gains purposes. Consequently, the shareholder incurs a capital loss equal to the cost of acquisition of the shares bought back.

The Company is required to deduct TDS or WHT when paying the buyback consideration treated as dividend. Shareholder residential status will be determined using information available with NSDL, CDSL, or the Registrar and Share Transfer Agent, KFin Technologies Limited, as of the Record Date for Buyback 2025.

For resident shareholders, tax will be deducted under Section 194 of the Income-tax Act as follows:

| Shareholder status | TDS rate |
|---|---:|
| Valid PAN | 10%* or the rate notified by the Government of India |
| No PAN or invalid PAN | 20% or the rate notified by the Government of India |

\* Under Section 139AA, individuals eligible to obtain Aadhaar must link PAN with Aadhaar. If PAN is not linked, it may be treated as invalid or inoperative, and tax may be deducted at the higher rate under Section 206AA, currently 20%.

No tax will be deducted from buyback consideration treated as dividend payable to a resident individual if the aggregate of all dividends and buyback proceeds treated as dividend received during financial year 2025–2026 does not exceed `10,000.

Resident individual shareholders may submit Form 15G, or Form 15H if aged 60 years or more, subject to the conditions of the Income-tax Act. PAN is mandatory when submitting these forms. If the forms become invalid during financial year 2025–2026, tax will be deducted from the total payments made during that financial year.

Resident shareholders may also submit documents under the Income-tax Act to claim lower or nil withholding. A Lower Tax Deduction Certificate issued by the Income Tax Department under Section 197 or another applicable provision may authorize deduction at a lower rate.

For non-resident shareholders, WHT will be deducted under Section 195, Section 196D, or another applicable provision at the rates in force for buyback consideration treated as dividend. The rate is 20%, plus applicable surcharge and cess, or the rate notified by the Government of India.

Non-resident shareholders may submit a Lower Tax Deduction Certificate issued by the Income Tax Department under Section 195 or another applicable provision. They may also claim benefits under an applicable Double Tax Avoidance Agreement under Section 90, subject to conditions including the non-application of the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, read with the Multilateral Instrument, and satisfaction of all treaty requirements.

To claim DTAA benefits, non-resident shareholders must provide:

- A duly attested copy of the PAN card issued by the Indian income-tax authorities, or the details prescribed under Rule 37BC of the Income-tax Rules, 1962, if they do not have a PAN card.
- A duly attested Tax Residency Certificate for financial year 2025–2026 obtained from the tax authorities of the shareholder’s country of tax residence.
- Electronic Form 10F, as required under Notification No. 03/2022 dated July 16, 2022, issued by the Central Board of Direct Taxes.
- A self-declaration confirming that the shareholder has no permanent establishment in India under the applicable tax treaty.
- A self-declaration confirming beneficial ownership.
- A self-declaration confirming that all conditions for claiming benefits under the applicable tax treaty and the Multilateral Instrument have been satisfied.
- Any other duly attested documents prescribed under the Income-tax Act for lower withholding, where applicable.
