Income tax expense for the three months ended September 30, 2025 and September 30, 2024 includes reversals, net of provisions, of ₹2 crore and provisions, net of reversals, of ₹83 crore, respectively. Income tax expense for the six months ended September 30, 2025 and September 30, 2024 includes provisions, net of reversals, of ₹114 crore and ₹143 crore, respectively. These prior-period provisions and reversals primarily relate to the adjudication of disputed matters following the filing of tax returns and completion of assessments across various jurisdictions.

Deferred income tax for the three and six months ended September 30, 2025 and September 30, 2024 substantially relates to the origination and reversal of temporary differences.

The Company's Advanced Pricing Arrangement (APA) with the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) for US branch income tax expired in March 2021. The Company has applied for renewal of the APA. US taxable income is currently based on the Company's best estimate determined using the expected value method.

## Accounting policy

The Group derives revenue primarily from IT services, including software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting and package implementation, licensing of software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings, and business process management services. Customer contracts may be on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe basis.

Revenue is recognized when:

- The contract has been approved in writing by the parties.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- The contract is legally enforceable.
- Control of the promised products or services has transferred to the customer.
- The transaction price can be determined and collection is not uncertain.

When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to each obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. If that evidence is unavailable, the Group generally estimates standalone selling price using expected cost plus an appropriate margin based on similar services.

Contracts may include variable consideration such as rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue when the related uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue recognition by contract type is as follows:

- Revenue from time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services consist of an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using the percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.
- Revenue from other fixed-price and fixed-timeframe contracts satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Progress toward completion is measured using the ratio of costs or efforts incurred to date to estimated total costs or efforts, because inputs have a direct relationship with productivity.
- Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are continuously monitored. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates change or are revised.
- Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable, based on estimated costs or efforts to complete the contract.

Customer billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as contract liabilities, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development and related services and maintenance services, the Group generally treats software development and related services as distinct performance obligations when the revenue recognition criteria are met. Revenue is allocated to each performance obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. For software development and related services, performance obligations are satisfied as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements that may be subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. These contracts are accounted for under the applicable specific guidance. When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. If standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period, with progress measured based on the promise in the contract.

Revenue from licenses is recognized as follows:

- Licenses providing the customer with a right to use are recognized when the licenses are made available to the customer.
- Licenses providing the customer with a right to access are recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. If the standalone selling price of implementation is unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used.

When a license must be substantially customized as part of implementation, the license and implementation arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services related to software product sales is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

Some integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as principal. The Group evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other indicators of control.

A contract modification is a change in the scope, price, or both, approved by the parties. A modification that adds distinct performance obligations is accounted for as:

- A separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price.
- A termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract when the additional services are not priced at their standalone selling price.

A modification that does not add a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them.

Certain eligible, nonrecurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as assets when they:

- Relate directly to the contract.
- Generate or enhance resources that will be used to satisfy future performance obligations.
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to expenses over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are regularly monitored for impairment. Impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount of the capitalized costs.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss.
