## Opinion

Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP audited the interim condensed consolidated financial statements of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries, comprising:

- The Condensed Consolidated Balance Sheet as at September 30, 2025
- The Condensed Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss, including Other Comprehensive Income, for the three months and six months ended September 30, 2025
- The Condensed Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity
- The Condensed Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows for the six months ended September 30, 2025
- The related notes, including material accounting policies and other explanatory information

In the auditor's opinion, the financial statements give a true and fair view of the Group's consolidated state of affairs as at September 30, 2025; its consolidated profit and total comprehensive income for the three months and six months ended on that date; and its consolidated changes in equity and cash flows for the six months ended on that date.

The financial statements were prepared in conformity with Indian Accounting Standard 34, *Interim Financial Reporting* (“Ind AS 34”), prescribed under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013, and other accounting principles generally accepted in India.

## Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under Section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013.

The auditor stated that it was independent of the Group in accordance with the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India and other applicable ethical requirements. The auditor also stated that it had fulfilled its other ethical responsibilities and obtained sufficient and appropriate audit evidence to support its opinion.

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements

The Boards of Directors of the entities included in the Group are responsible for:

- Preparing interim condensed consolidated financial statements that give a true and fair view of the consolidated financial position and performance in accordance with Ind AS 34 and other generally accepted accounting principles in India.
- Maintaining adequate accounting records.
- Safeguarding the Group's assets and preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls to support accurate and complete accounting records and financial statement presentation.
- Assessing the entities' ability to continue as a going concern.
- Disclosing applicable going-concern matters and using the going-concern basis of accounting unless liquidation, cessation of operations, or the absence of a realistic alternative makes that basis inappropriate.
- Overseeing the Group's financial reporting process.

## Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit of the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements

The auditor's objective was to obtain reasonable assurance that the interim condensed consolidated financial statements as a whole were free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an audit report containing its opinion.

The report states that reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but is not a guarantee that an audit will detect every material misstatement. Misstatements may arise from fraud or error and are material if they could reasonably be expected to influence users' economic decisions.

As part of the audit, the auditor:

- Identified and assessed risks of material misstatement and designed audit procedures responsive to those risks.
- Obtained audit evidence sufficient and appropriate to support the audit opinion.
- Considered that the risk of failing to detect a material misstatement is higher for fraud than for error because fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or management override of internal controls.
- Obtained an understanding of relevant internal financial controls to design appropriate audit procedures; this work was not performed to express an opinion on the effectiveness of those controls.
- Evaluated the appropriateness of accounting policies and the reasonableness of accounting estimates.
- Evaluated management's use of the going-concern basis of accounting and whether events or conditions created a material uncertainty that could cast significant doubt on the Group's ability to continue as a going concern.
- Evaluated the overall presentation, structure, content, and disclosures of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements.
- Obtained sufficient and appropriate audit evidence regarding the financial information of entities within the Group.
- Considered the planned scope and timing of the audit and significant audit findings, including significant deficiencies in internal financial controls.
- Communicated relevant ethical and independence matters to those charged with governance.

Materiality was described as the magnitude of misstatements that, individually or in aggregate, could make it probable that the economic decisions of a reasonably knowledgeable user would be influenced. The auditor considered both quantitative materiality and qualitative factors when planning and evaluating the audit.
