## Provident fund

Eligible employees of Infosys receive benefits from a provident fund, which is a defined benefit plan. Both eligible employees and the Company make monthly contributions equal to a specified percentage of the covered employee’s salary.

The Company contributes a portion to the Infosys Limited Employees’ Provident Fund Trust. The trust invests in designated instruments permitted by Indian law. The remaining portion is contributed to the government-administered pension fund. The Government of India administers the annual interest rate payable to beneficiaries by the trust. The Company is obligated to make good any shortfall between the return on the trust’s investments and the notified interest rate.

Eligible employees of Indian subsidiaries receive benefits from a defined contribution provident fund plan. Both eligible employees and the respective companies make monthly contributions equal to a specified percentage of the covered employee’s salary. Contributions are deposited in a government-administered provident fund, and the companies have no further obligation beyond their monthly contributions.

## Superannuation

Certain employees of Infosys, Infosys BPM, and EdgeVerve participate in a defined contribution plan. The Group has no further obligations beyond its monthly contributions, which are periodically contributed to a trust fund invested with the Life Insurance Corporation of India.

## Compensated absences

The Group’s compensated absence policy includes accumulating and non-accumulating absences. The expected cost of accumulating compensated absences is determined by an external actuary at each Balance Sheet date using the projected unit credit method. The valuation measures the additional amount expected to be paid or availed as a result of unused entitlement accumulated at the Balance Sheet date.

Expense for non-accumulating compensated absences is recognized in the period in which the absences occur.

## 2.19 Leases

## The Group as a lessee

The Group’s lease asset classes primarily consist of land, buildings, and computers. The Group assesses whether a contract contains a lease at the inception of the contract. A contract is, or contains, a lease if it conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration.

To assess whether a contract conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset, the Group assesses whether:

1. The contract involves the use of an identified asset.
2. The Group has substantially all of the economic benefits from using the asset during the lease period.
3. The Group has the right to direct the use of the asset.

At the commencement date, the Group recognizes a right-of-use asset (ROU) and a corresponding lease liability for all lease arrangements in which it is a lessee, except leases with a term of twelve months or less and low-value leases. For these short-term and low-value leases, lease payments are recognized as an operating expense on a straight-line basis over the lease term.

The Group determines the lease term as the non-cancellable period of a lease, adjusted for options to extend or terminate the lease when exercise of those options is reasonably certain. The assessment is performed on a lease-by-lease basis.

In evaluating the lease term, the Group considers factors such as:

- Significant leasehold improvements undertaken over the lease term.
- Costs relating to termination of the lease.
- The importance of the underlying asset to the Group’s operations, including its location and the availability of suitable alternatives.

The lease term is reassessed in future periods to ensure that it reflects current economic circumstances. ROU assets and lease liabilities include extension or termination options when it is reasonably certain that the options will be exercised.

ROU assets are initially recognized at cost. Cost comprises the initial lease liability, adjusted for lease payments made at or before the commencement date, initial direct costs, and lease incentives. ROU assets are subsequently measured at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment losses.

ROU assets are depreciated from the commencement date on a straight-line basis over the shorter of the lease term and the useful life of the underlying asset.

ROU assets are evaluated for recoverability whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. For impairment testing, the recoverable amount—the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use—is determined on an individual asset basis unless the asset does not generate cash flows largely independent of those from other assets. In such cases, the recoverable amount is determined for the cash-generating unit (CGU) to which the asset belongs.

Lease liabilities are initially measured at amortized cost at the present value of future lease payments. Lease payments are discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease or, when that rate is not readily determinable, the incremental borrowing rates in the country of domicile of the leases.

Lease liabilities are remeasured with a corresponding adjustment to the related ROU asset if the Group changes its assessment of whether it will exercise an extension or termination option.

Lease liabilities and ROU assets are presented separately in the Balance Sheet, and lease payments are classified as financing cash flows.

## The Group as a lessor

Leases for which the Group is a lessor are classified as finance or operating leases. A lease is classified as a finance lease when its terms transfer substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to the lessee. All other leases are classified as operating leases.

When the Group is an intermediate lessor, it accounts for its interests in the head lease and the sublease separately. The sublease is classified as a finance or operating lease by reference to the ROU asset arising from the head lease.

For operating leases, rental income is recognized on a straight-line basis over the relevant lease term.
