## 2.17 REVENUE FROM OPERATIONS

## Accounting Policy

The Company derives revenue primarily from IT services, including software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting, package implementation, and licensing of software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings. Customer contracts may be time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe arrangements.

Revenue is recognized when:

- The contract has been approved in writing and is legally enforceable.
- The parties are committed to performing their obligations.
- The promised products or services, or performance obligations, are transferred to the customer.
- The transaction price can be recognized without an unresolved collectability uncertainty.

The Company identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of standalone selling price. If that evidence is unavailable, the Company estimates standalone selling price using expected cost plus an appropriate margin.

Contracts may include variable consideration, including rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue.

Revenue recognition by contract type is as follows:

- Time-and-material and unit-of-work revenue is recognized as services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services consist of an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized using percentage of completion when the pattern of customer benefit and fulfillment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.
- Other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts satisfied over time are recognized using percentage of completion. Progress is measured using costs or efforts incurred to date divided by estimated total costs or efforts.
- Contract estimates are continuously monitored. Changes in transaction price, estimated costs, or efforts are recognized in net profit in the period of revision.
- Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when the losses become probable.

Billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billings or milestone-based progress billings. Revenue in excess of billings is classified as unbilled revenue, while billings in excess of revenue are classified as contract liabilities, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development and related services, the services generally represent distinct performance obligations. Revenue is allocated using relative standalone selling prices, or expected cost plus margin when standalone selling prices cannot be determined. These performance obligations are satisfied as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure arrangements include hardware, services, leasing elements, or other components subject to specific accounting guidance. Where hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated using relative standalone selling prices or, when unavailable, expected cost plus margin. When the arrangement is a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period using a measure of progress based on the contractual promise.

Software license revenue is recognized:

- At the time the license is made available when the customer obtains a right to use the license.
- Over the access period when the customer obtains a right to access the license.

Software product arrangements generally include a license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. If the implementation standalone selling price is unavailable, expected cost plus margin is used. When the license requires substantial customization as part of implementation, the license and implementation are treated as a single performance obligation and revenue is recognized using percentage of completion as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

For integrated services arrangements involving subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue is recorded net of costs when the Company acts as an agent and gross when it acts as principal. The Company evaluates whether it controls the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include responsibility for fulfillment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other indicators of control.

A contract modification is a change in scope, price, or both, approved by the parties. A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for as:

- A separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price.
- Termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract when the additional services are not priced at their standalone selling price.

A modification that does not create a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Company expects to recover them. Eligible nonrecurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, are recognized as an asset when they:

- Relate directly to the contract.
- Generate or enhance resources used to satisfy future performance obligations.
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to expenses over the contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored for impairment, and impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount.

The Company presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Statement of Profit and Loss.

## Trade receivables and Contract Balances

The timing of revenue recognition, billing, and cash collection results in trade receivables, unbilled revenue, and unearned revenue on the Company’s Balance Sheet. Amounts are billed as work progresses under agreed contractual terms, either periodically, such as monthly or quarterly, or upon achievement of contractual milestones.

Trade receivables are unconditional rights to consideration. Unbilled revenue from time-and-material contracts and fixed-price maintenance contracts is classified as a financial asset when the right to consideration is unconditional and payment is due only after the passage of time.

Billing for other fixed-price contracts is based on contractual milestones, so revenue recognition and invoicing may occur at different times. Unbilled revenue from these contracts is classified as a nonfinancial contract asset because the right to consideration depends on completing contractual milestones.

Invoicing in excess of earnings is classified as unearned revenue.

Trade receivables and unbilled revenue are presented net of impairment in the Balance Sheet.
