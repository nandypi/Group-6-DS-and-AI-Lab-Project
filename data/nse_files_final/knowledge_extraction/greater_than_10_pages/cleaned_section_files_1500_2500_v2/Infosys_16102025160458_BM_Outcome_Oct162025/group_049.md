## 2.6 Provisions and other contingencies

### 2.6.1 Provisions

A provision is recognized if, as a result of a past event, the Group has a present legal or constructive obligation that is reasonably estimable and it is probable that an outflow of economic benefits will be required to settle the obligation. Provisions are determined by discounting expected future cash flows at a pre-tax rate that reflects current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific to the liability.

The Group recognizes a reimbursement asset only when it is virtually certain that the reimbursement will be received if the Group settles the obligation.

A contingent liability is:

- a possible obligation arising from past events whose existence will be confirmed only by the occurrence or non-occurrence of one or more uncertain future events not wholly within the entity’s control; or
- a present obligation arising from past events that is not recognized because an outflow of resources embodying economic benefits is not probable or the amount cannot be measured with sufficient reliability.

### a. Post sales client support

The Group provides fixed-period post-sales support to clients under fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts. Costs associated with these services are accrued when the related revenues are recorded and included in cost of sales. The Group estimates these costs based on historical experience and periodically reviews the estimates for material changes in assumptions and likelihood of occurrence.

### b. Onerous contracts

Provisions for onerous contracts are recognized when the expected benefits from a contract are lower than the unavoidable costs of meeting future obligations. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

The provision is measured at the present value of the lower of the expected cost of terminating the contract and the expected net cost of continuing with the contract. Before establishing a provision, the Group recognizes any impairment loss on assets associated with the contract.

## 2.6.2 Legal proceedings

### McCamish Cybersecurity incident

In November 2023, certain systems of Infosys McCamish Systems LLC (“McCamish”), a subsidiary of Infosys BPM Limited and wholly owned subsidiary of Infosys Limited, were encrypted by ransomware. This resulted in the non-availability of certain applications and systems.

McCamish initiated incident response activities and engaged cybersecurity and other specialists to investigate, respond to, and remediate the incident. By December 31, 2023, McCamish, with assistance from external specialists, had substantially remediated and restored the affected applications and systems.

A third-party cybersecurity firm conducted investigative analysis to determine whether, and to what extent, company or customer data had been subject to unauthorized access or exfiltration. McCamish also engaged a third-party eDiscovery vendor to assess the extent and nature of the data involved. McCamish and the eDiscovery vendor identified corporate customers and individuals whose information was subject to unauthorized access and exfiltration. McCamish processes personal data on behalf of its corporate customers.

Between March 6, 2024 and July 25, 2024, six actions were filed in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Georgia against McCamish. The actions arose from the cybersecurity incident initially disclosed on November 3, 2023. The actions were consolidated, and a consolidated class action complaint was filed on November 7, 2024, purportedly on behalf of all persons residing in the United States whose personally identifiable information was compromised in the incident, including those who received an incident notice.

On December 20, 2024, the Court granted the parties’ joint motion to stay proceedings while they pursued mediation. On March 13, 2025, McCamish and the plaintiffs reached an in-principle agreement covering a proposed settlement of the class action lawsuits against McCamish and seven class action lawsuits arising from the incident that had been filed against McCamish’s customers.

On May 9, 2025, McCamish and the plaintiffs entered into a definitive settlement agreement, and the plaintiffs moved for preliminary approval. Under the settlement terms, McCamish agreed to pay $17.5 million, approximately ₹150 crore, into a settlement fund. On July 16, 2025, the Court granted preliminary approval. The settlement remains subject to final court approval. If approved, it will resolve all allegations made in the class action lawsuits without admission of liability.

During the three months ended March 31, 2025, McCamish recorded an accrual of $17.5 million, approximately ₹150 crore, related to the settlement and recognized an insurance reimbursement receivable of $17 million, approximately ₹145 crore. The reimbursement receivable was offset against the settlement expense in the statement of comprehensive income.

McCamish may incur additional costs, including costs arising from indemnities or damages and claims. The amount of these additional costs cannot currently be determined.

### Government Investigation

The U.S. Department of Justice is conducting an investigation into how the Company classified certain H-1B visa-recipient employees working for one of its clients in immigration documents filed with certain U.S. government authorities.

The Company is discussing the ongoing investigation with the DOJ and has commenced its own inquiry. At this stage, the Company is unable to predict the outcome, including whether it could have a material adverse effect on the Company’s business and results of operations.
