## Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit of the Interim Consolidated Financial Statements

The auditor's objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance that the interim consolidated financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report containing the auditor's opinion. Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but does not guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with applicable Standards on Auditing will always detect a material misstatement.

As part of the audit, the auditor exercises professional judgment and maintains professional scepticism. The auditor:

- Identifies and assesses risks of material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, designs procedures responsive to those risks, and obtains sufficient appropriate audit evidence. The risk of failing to detect a fraud-related misstatement is higher because fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or management override of internal controls.
- Obtains an understanding of internal financial controls relevant to the audit to design appropriate audit procedures, without expressing an opinion on the effectiveness of those controls.
- Evaluates the appropriateness of accounting policies, the reasonableness of accounting estimates, and related management disclosures.
- Assesses management's use of the going-concern basis of accounting and whether audit evidence indicates a material uncertainty related to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the Group's ability to continue as a going concern. Where such uncertainty exists, the auditor draws attention to the related disclosures or modifies the opinion if the disclosures are inadequate. The auditor's conclusions are based on evidence obtained up to the date of the auditor's report; future events or conditions may cause the Group to cease to continue as a going concern.
- Evaluates the overall presentation, structure, and content of the interim consolidated financial statements, including whether they represent the underlying transactions and events fairly.
- Obtains sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the financial information of entities within the Group to express an opinion on the interim consolidated financial statements. The auditor is responsible for the direction, supervision, and performance of the audit of financial statements of entities included in the interim consolidated financial statements where the auditor is the independent auditor.

Materiality is the magnitude of misstatements in the interim consolidated financial statements that, individually or in aggregate, makes it probable that the economic decisions of a reasonably knowledgeable user may be influenced. The auditor considers quantitative materiality and qualitative factors when planning the audit, evaluating audit results, and assessing identified misstatements.

The auditor communicates with those charged with governance regarding the planned scope and timing of the audit, significant audit findings, and significant deficiencies in internal financial controls identified during the audit. The auditor also communicates relevant ethical requirements regarding independence, together with relationships and other matters that may reasonably be considered to bear on independence and related safeguards.

From matters communicated with those charged with governance, the auditor determines which matters were of most significance in the audit of the financial statements for the current period and therefore constitute key audit matters. These matters are described in the auditor's report unless law or regulation precludes public disclosure, or unless the adverse consequences of disclosure would reasonably be expected to outweigh the public interest benefits.
