## 2.17 REVENUE FROM OPERATIONS

## Accounting Policy

The Company derives revenues primarily from IT services comprising software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting, package implementation, and licensing of software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings. Customer contracts may be on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe basis.

Revenue is recognized when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to their respective obligations, the contract is legally enforceable, and control of the promised products or services has transferred to the customer. Revenue is recognized at the transaction price, subject to collectability. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Company identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to them based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged when an item is sold separately is the best evidence of standalone selling price. When this is unavailable, the Company generally uses an expected cost-plus-margin approach.

Contracts may include variable consideration such as rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue.

Revenue recognition methods include:

- Time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts: recognized as services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance contracts: recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services consist of an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period, or using a percentage-of-completion method when services are discrete and the pattern of benefits and fulfilment costs is uneven.
- Other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts satisfied over time: recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Progress is measured using costs or efforts incurred to date relative to estimated total costs or efforts.
- Software development and related services: recognized as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.
- Licenses providing a right to use: recognized when the license is made available to the customer.
- Licenses providing a right to access: recognized over the access period.

Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are continuously monitored. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates change or are revised. Revenues and estimated total costs or efforts are subject to revision as contracts progress. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.

Billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billings or milestone-based progress billings. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

Certain cloud and infrastructure contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period based on the contractual promise and the related measure of progress.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include a license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When the license is substantially customized as part of implementation, the license and implementation fee is treated as a single performance obligation and recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

Contracts may include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software in integrated service arrangements. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Company acts as an agent and gross when it acts as principal. The Company considers responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors when determining whether it controls the specified goods or services.

A contract modification is a change in contract scope or price, or both, approved by the parties. A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for as a separate contract when the additional services are priced at standalone selling price. Otherwise, it is accounted for as termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract. A modification that does not create a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Company expects to recover them. Eligible nonrecurring costs, including setup, transition, and transformation costs that do not represent separate performance obligations, are recognized as assets when they:

1. Relate directly to the contract.
2. Generate or enhance resources used to satisfy future performance obligations.
3. Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to expenses over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are regularly monitored for impairment. Impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount.

The Company presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Statement of Profit and Loss.

## Trade receivables and Contract Balances

The timing of revenue recognition, billings, and cash collections results in trade receivables, unbilled revenue, and unearned revenue on the Company's Balance Sheet. Amounts are billed as work progresses under agreed contractual terms, either periodically, such as monthly or quarterly, or upon achievement of contractual milestones.

Trade receivables are unconditional rights to consideration. Unbilled revenue from time-and-material contracts and fixed-price maintenance contracts is classified as a financial asset when the right to consideration is unconditional and payment is due only after the passage of time.

For other fixed-price contracts, client invoicing is based on contractual milestones. Because revenue recognition and invoicing may occur at different times, unbilled revenue from these contracts is classified as a nonfinancial asset, or contract asset, when the right to consideration depends on completion of contractual milestones.

Invoicing in excess of earnings is classified as unearned revenue.

Trade receivables and unbilled revenue are presented net of impairment in the Balance Sheet.
