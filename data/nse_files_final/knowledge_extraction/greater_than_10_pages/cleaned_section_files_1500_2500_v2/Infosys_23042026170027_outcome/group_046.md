## Report on the Audit of the Interim Consolidated Financial Statements

## Opinion

The interim consolidated financial statements of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries (the Group) comprise:

- The Consolidated Balance Sheet as at March 31, 2026.
- The Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income for the three months and year ended March 31, 2026.
- The Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity for the year ended March 31, 2026.
- The Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows for the year ended March 31, 2026.
- Notes to the financial statements, including material accounting policies and explanatory information.

In the auditor's opinion, the interim consolidated financial statements give a true and fair view, in conformity with International Accounting Standard 34, *Interim Financial Reporting*, as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board, of:

- The Group's consolidated state of affairs as at March 31, 2026.
- Its consolidated profit and other comprehensive income for the three months and year ended March 31, 2026.
- Its consolidated changes in equity and cash flows for the year ended March 31, 2026.

## Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India. The auditors state that they are independent of the Group under the ICAI Code of Ethics and that they fulfilled their other ethical responsibilities. They believe the audit evidence obtained is sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their opinion.

## Key Audit Matters

Key audit matters were matters that, in the auditors' professional judgment, were of most significance in the audit of the consolidated financial statements for the current period. These matters were addressed in the context of the audit as a whole and did not result in separate opinions.

| Sr. No. | Key Audit Matter | Auditor's Response |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Revenue recognition.** The Group's customer contracts may include multiple products and services, including software development and related services, maintenance, consulting, package implementation, software-product and platform licensing, and business process management services. Significant judgment is required to identify distinct performance obligations and determine whether the Group acts as principal or agent for third-party vendor products or services. Revenue is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as agent and gross when it acts as principal. Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized on a straight-line basis when services consist of an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period, or using the percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and related costs is uneven because services are discrete and non-repetitive. These judgments were identified as a key audit matter. Refer to Notes 1.5 and 2.16 to the Consolidated Financial Statements. | The auditors tested controls over identifying distinct performance obligations, determining principal-versus-agent status, and selecting the straight-line or percentage-of-completion method for fixed-price maintenance revenue. They selected customer contracts, read master service agreements and related documents, reviewed significant terms and deliverables, and assessed management's conclusions on performance obligations, principal-versus-agent status, and the revenue-recognition method. |
| 2 | **Revenue recognition—fixed-price contracts using the percentage-of-completion method.** For fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts whose performance obligations are satisfied over time, revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Progress is measured using efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs, because of the direct relationship between input and productivity. Estimating total efforts or costs requires significant judgment and is reassessed throughout the contract. Provisions for estimated losses on uncompleted contracts are recorded when such losses become probable. | The auditors tested controls over recording incurred efforts or costs, estimating remaining efforts or costs, and access and application controls over time-recording, allocation, and budgeting systems. For selected contracts, they evaluated management's ability to estimate progress by comparing actual efforts or costs incurred with prior estimates and budgeted amounts. They investigated significant variations, assessed whether revised estimates reflected contract status, and compared estimates with milestone delivery and customer acceptance or sign-off to identify delays requiring changes to estimated costs or efforts. |

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Interim Consolidated Financial Statements

The Company's Board of Directors is responsible for preparing and presenting interim consolidated financial statements that give a true and fair view of the Group's consolidated financial position, financial performance, other comprehensive income, changes in equity, and cash flows in accordance with IAS 34.

The respective boards of directors of the entities in the Group are responsible for:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records and safeguarding Group assets.
- Preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls operating effectively to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting records.
- Preparing financial statements that are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error.
- Assessing the ability of the respective entities to continue as a going concern.
- Disclosing going-concern matters where applicable.
- Using the going-concern basis of accounting unless the relevant board intends to liquidate the entity, cease operations, or has no realistic alternative.
- Overseeing the Group's financial reporting process.
