## Credit risk

Credit risk refers to the risk of default by a counterparty resulting in a financial loss. The maximum exposure to credit risk at the reporting date was primarily from:

- Trade receivables of ₹35,234 crore as at March 31, 2026 and ₹31,158 crore as at March 31, 2025.
- Unbilled revenues of ₹17,221 crore as at March 31, 2026 and ₹15,083 crore as at March 31, 2025. The credit exposure table reports March 31, 2025 unbilled revenues as ₹15,082 crore.

Trade receivables and unbilled revenues are typically unsecured and are derived primarily from customers located in the United States of America and Europe. The Group manages credit risk through credit approvals, credit limits, and continuous monitoring of customer creditworthiness. It uses the expected credit loss model and a provision matrix based on available credit reports and related information.

The Group’s exposure to credit risk is influenced mainly by individual customer characteristics and concentration among its largest customers. Exposure is diversified, with no single customer contributing more than 10% of outstanding trade receivables and unbilled revenues.

Days sales outstanding was 67 days and 69 days as of March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025, respectively.

Credit risk on cash and cash equivalents is limited because the Group generally invests in deposits with banks that have high ratings from international and domestic credit rating agencies. Ratings are monitored periodically, using the latest available ratings as at the date of approval of the Consolidated financial statements.

The Group’s investments primarily include mutual fund units, target maturity fund units, tax-free bonds, certificates of deposit, commercial paper, treasury bills, government securities, non-convertible debentures, and quoted bonds issued by government and quasi-government organizations. The Group considers counterparty risk using criteria including Tier I capital, capital adequacy ratio, credit rating, profitability, non-performing asset levels, and deposit base. These risks are monitored regularly under the Group’s risk management program.

## Liquidity risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that the Group will not be able to settle or meet its obligations on time.

The Group’s principal sources of liquidity are cash and cash equivalents and cash flow generated from operations. The Group has no outstanding borrowings and states that its working capital is sufficient to meet current requirements.

As at March 31, 2026, the Group had working capital of ₹51,167 crore, including cash and cash equivalents of ₹22,201 crore and current investments of ₹12,950 crore. As at March 31, 2025, working capital was ₹54,249 crore, including cash and cash equivalents of ₹24,455 crore and current investments of ₹12,482 crore.

Outstanding compensated absences were ₹3,641 crore as at March 31, 2026 and ₹3,007 crore as at March 31, 2025, and were substantially funded.

## 2.12 EQUITY

## Ordinary Shares

Ordinary shares are classified as equity share capital. Incremental costs directly attributable to issuing new ordinary shares, share options, and buybacks are recognized as a deduction from equity, net of related tax effects.

### Treasury Shares

When an entity within the Group purchases the company’s ordinary shares, the consideration paid, including directly attributable incremental costs, is presented as a deduction from total equity until the shares are cancelled, sold, or reissued. When treasury shares are subsequently sold or reissued, the amount received is recognized as an increase in equity, and the resulting surplus or deficit is transferred to or from securities premium.
