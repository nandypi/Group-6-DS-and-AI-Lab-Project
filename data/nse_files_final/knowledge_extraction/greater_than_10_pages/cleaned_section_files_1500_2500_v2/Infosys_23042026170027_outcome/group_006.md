## Basis  for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing (“SAs”) specified under Section 143(10) of the Act. The auditor’s responsibilities are described in the “Auditor's Responsibilities for audit of the consolidated financial results” section.

The auditor is independent of the Group in accordance with the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (“ICAI”) and other ethical requirements relevant to the audit of the consolidated financial results for the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026. The auditor states that these ethical responsibilities were fulfilled and that the audit evidence obtained was sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for the audit opinion.

## Management's and Board  of Directors' Responsibilities for the Statement

The Statement, including the Consolidated Financial Results, is the responsibility of the Company’s Board of Directors and was approved by them for issuance. It was compiled from the related audited interim condensed consolidated financial statements for the three months and year ended March 31, 2026.

The board is responsible for preparing and presenting the Statement so that it gives a true and fair view of the Group’s consolidated net profit, consolidated other comprehensive income, and other financial information, in accordance with the recognition and measurement principles under Ind AS 34, Section 133 of the Act, relevant rules, other accounting principles generally accepted in India, and Regulation 33 of the Listing Regulations.

## Deloitte Haskins &amp; Sells LLP

The respective boards of directors of the companies included in the Group are responsible for:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records in accordance with the Act.
- Safeguarding the Group’s assets.
- Preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls.

These controls must operate effectively to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting records relevant to preparing and presenting financial results that give a true and fair view and are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error.

In preparing the Consolidated Financial Results, the respective boards are responsible for assessing each entity’s ability to continue as a going concern, disclosing relevant going-concern matters where applicable, and using the going-concern basis of accounting unless the board intends to liquidate the entity, cease operations, or has no realistic alternative.

The respective boards are responsible for overseeing the Group’s financial reporting process.

## Auditor's  Responsibilities  for  audit  of the  Consolidated  Financial  Results  for the  quarter and year ended  March 31, 2026

The auditor’s objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance that the Consolidated Financial Results for the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026, as a whole, are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor’s report containing the opinion.

Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but does not guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with SAs will always detect a material misstatement. Misstatements may arise from fraud or error and are considered material if, individually or in aggregate, they could reasonably be expected to influence users’ economic decisions based on the Consolidated Financial Results.

As part of the audit, the auditor exercises professional judgment and maintains professional skepticism throughout the process. The auditor also:

- Identifies and assesses risks of material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error; designs procedures responsive to those risks; and obtains sufficient and appropriate audit evidence.
- Recognizes that the risk of failing to detect a fraud-related material misstatement is higher than for one arising from error because fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or management override of internal control.
- Obtains an understanding of relevant internal control to design appropriate audit procedures, but not to express an opinion on the effectiveness of those controls.
- Evaluates the appropriateness of accounting policies and the reasonableness of accounting estimates made by the Board of Directors.
- Evaluates the appropriateness and reasonableness of disclosures made by the Board of Directors under Regulation 33 of the LODR Regulations.
