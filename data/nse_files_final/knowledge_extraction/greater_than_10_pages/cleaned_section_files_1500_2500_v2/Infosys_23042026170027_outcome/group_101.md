## (iii) Financial assets carried at fair value through profit or loss (FVTPL)

A financial asset that is not classified in any of the above categories is subsequently measured at fair value through profit or loss.

## (iv) Financial liabilities

Financial liabilities are subsequently carried at amortized cost using the effective interest method, except for contingent consideration recognized in a business combination, which is subsequently measured at fair value through profit or loss.

## (v) Investment in subsidiaries

Investments in subsidiaries are carried at cost in the separate financial statements.

## b. Derivative financial instruments

The Company holds derivative financial instruments, such as foreign exchange forward and option contracts, to mitigate the risk of changes in exchange rates on foreign currency exposures. The counterparty for these contracts is generally a bank.

## (i) Financial assets or financial liabilities, carried at fair value through profit or loss.

This category includes derivative financial assets or liabilities that are not designated as hedges.

Derivatives that are not designated as hedges, or that are designated as hedges but are ineffective under Ind AS 109, Financial Instruments, are categorized as financial assets or financial liabilities at fair value through profit or loss.

Derivatives not designated as hedges are initially recognized at fair value, with attributable transaction costs recognized in net profit in the Statement of Profit and Loss when incurred. Subsequently, these derivatives are measured at fair value through profit or loss, and the resulting exchange gains or losses are included in other income. Assets and liabilities in this category are presented as current assets or current liabilities if they are held for trading or expected to be realized within 12 months after the Balance Sheet date.

## (ii) Cash flow hedge

The Company primarily designates certain foreign exchange forward and option contracts as cash flow hedges to mitigate foreign exchange exposure on highly probable forecast cash transactions.

For a derivative designated as a cash flow hedging instrument, the effective portion of changes in fair value is recognized in other comprehensive income and accumulated in the cash flow hedge reserve. The ineffective portion is recognized immediately in net profit in the Statement of Profit and Loss.

If the hedging instrument no longer meets the criteria for hedge accounting, hedge accounting is discontinued prospectively. If the instrument expires or is sold, terminated, or exercised, the cumulative gain or loss recognized in the cash flow hedge reserve remains there until the forecast transaction occurs. Upon occurrence of the related forecast transaction, the cumulative gain or loss is transferred to net profit in the Statement of Profit and Loss. If the forecast transaction is no longer expected to occur, the accumulated amount is reclassified to net profit in the Statement of Profit and Loss.

## 2.11.3 Derecognition of financial instruments

The Company derecognizes a financial asset when the contractual rights to its cash flows expire or when it transfers the financial asset and the transfer qualifies for derecognition under Ind AS 109.

A financial liability, or part of a financial liability, is derecognized from the Company's Balance Sheet when the contractual obligation is discharged, cancelled, or expires.

## 2.11.4 Fair value of financial instruments

The Company determines the fair value of financial instruments using methods and assumptions based on market conditions and risks existing at each reporting date. Methods include discounted cash flow analysis, option pricing models, market multiples, available quoted market prices, and dealer quotes.

All fair-value measurement methods provide general approximations of value, and the resulting value may not be realized.

For financial assets and liabilities maturing within one year from the Balance Sheet date and not carried at fair value, carrying amounts approximate fair value because of their short maturity.

## 2.11.5 Impairment

The Company recognizes loss allowances using the expected credit loss (ECL) model for financial assets and unbilled revenues that are not measured at fair value through profit or loss.

For trade receivables and unbilled revenues with no significant financing component, loss allowances are measured at lifetime ECL. For all other financial assets, ECL is measured at 12-month ECL unless there has been a significant increase in credit risk since initial recognition; in that case, lifetime ECL is recognized.

The Company determines credit-loss allowances using historical loss experience adjusted for current and estimated future economic conditions, including conditions relating to the industries with which it deals and the countries where it operates.

The ECL amount, or reversal, required to adjust the loss allowance at the reporting date is recognized as an impairment loss or gain in the Statement of Profit and Loss.
