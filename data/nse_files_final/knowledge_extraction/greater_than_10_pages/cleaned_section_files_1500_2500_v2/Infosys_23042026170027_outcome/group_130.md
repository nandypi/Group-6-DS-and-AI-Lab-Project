## 1.2 Basis of preparation of financial statements

These interim condensed standalone financial statements are prepared in compliance with Indian Accounting Standard (Ind AS) 34, Interim Financial Reporting, under the historical cost convention on an accrual basis, except for certain financial instruments measured at fair value and defined benefit liabilities or assets recognized at the present value of the defined benefit obligation less the fair value of plan assets. They are also prepared in accordance with the Companies Act, 2013, and guidelines issued by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI).

The interim condensed standalone financial statements do not include all information required for a complete set of financial statements and should be read with the standalone financial statements and related notes in the Company's Annual Report for the year ended March 31, 2025. Ind AS is prescribed under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013, read with Rule 3 of the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015, and relevant amendment rules issued thereafter.

Accounting policies have been applied consistently, except when a newly issued accounting standard is initially adopted or a revision to an existing standard requires a change in accounting policy. Material accounting policy information used in preparing the audited interim condensed standalone financial statements is discussed in the respective notes.

Quarter and year-to-date figures are taken from the source and rounded to the nearest digits. Consequently, quarter figures may not always add up to the year-to-date figures reported in the statement.

## 1.3 Use of estimates and judgments

Preparation of the interim condensed standalone financial statements in conformity with Ind AS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies, reported amounts of assets and liabilities, disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities, and reported revenues and expenses.

The application of accounting policies involving critical accounting estimates and complex or subjective judgments is disclosed in Note 1.4. Estimates, judgments, and assumptions may change from period to period, and actual results may differ from estimates. Changes in estimates are reflected in the period in which the changes are made; material effects are disclosed in the notes.

## 1.4 Critical accounting estimates and judgments

## a. Revenue recognition

The Company's customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is considered for recognition and measurement when the contract has been approved in writing by the parties, the parties are committed to performing their respective obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable.

The Company assesses the services promised in each contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying distinct performance obligations, determining deliverables and whether the customer can benefit independently from them, and allocating the transaction price to those obligations involve significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and the Company's fulfillment costs is uneven because services are discrete rather than repetitive, fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method. The method used depends on the contractual promises and nature of the deliverables.

For other fixed-price contracts, the Company uses the percentage-of-completion method. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs. Efforts or costs incurred are used as an input measure because of the direct relationship between input and productivity. Estimated total efforts or costs are reassessed throughout the contract period using the latest available information.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Company acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Company first assesses whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include primary responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other indicators of control.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts, if any, are recorded in the period in which the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs required to complete the contract.

## b. Income taxes

The Company's two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. It also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is involved in determining income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, management considers whether some or all of the assets will be realized. Ultimate realization depends on generating future taxable income during the periods in which temporary differences become deductible. Management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax planning strategies.

Based on historical taxable income and projections for future taxable income during the periods in which deferred income tax assets are deductible, management believes the benefits of the deductible differences will be realized. The amount considered realizable could be reduced in the near term if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period are reduced. Refer to Note 2.16.

## c. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Company's asset base. Periodic depreciation is determined using estimates of an asset's expected useful life and residual value at the end of its life.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including technological changes. Refer to Note 2.1.

## 2. Notes to the Interim Condensed Standalone Financial Statements

## 2.1 PROPERTY, PLANT AND EQUIPMENT

## Accounting Policy

Property, plant and equipment are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment, if any. Costs directly attributable to acquisition are capitalized until the assets are ready for use as intended by management.

The Company depreciates property, plant and equipment over their estimated useful lives using the straight-line method. Estimated useful lives are as follows:

| Asset category | Estimated useful life |
|---|---|
| Building (1) | 22–25 years |
| Plant and machinery (1) | 5 years |
| Office equipment | 5 years |
| Computer equipment (1) | 3–5 years |
| Furniture and fixtures (1) | 5 years |
| Vehicles (1) | 5 years |
| Leasehold improvements | Lower of |
