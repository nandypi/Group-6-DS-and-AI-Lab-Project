## 1.3 Basis of consolidation

Infosys consolidates entities that it owns or controls. The interim consolidated financial statements comprise the financial statements of the Company, its controlled trusts, and its subsidiaries.

Control exists when the parent:

- Has power over the entity through existing rights that direct relevant activities.
- Is exposed, or has rights, to variable returns from its involvement with the entity.
- Can use its power to affect those returns.

Subsidiaries are consolidated from the date control commences until the date control ceases.

Group company financial statements are consolidated on a line-by-line basis. Intra-group balances and transactions, including unrealized gains or losses, are eliminated on consolidation. The Group applies uniform accounting policies.

Non-controlling interests represent the portion of the net profit or loss and net assets of subsidiaries that is not directly or indirectly owned or controlled by the Company. Refer to Note 2.14 for the list of subsidiaries and controlled trusts.

## 1.4 Use of estimates and judgments

The preparation of interim consolidated financial statements in conformity with IFRS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect:

- The application of accounting policies.
- Reported amounts of assets and liabilities.
- Disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities at the reporting date.
- Reported amounts of revenue and expenses during the period.

The application of accounting policies requiring critical accounting estimates and complex or subjective judgments is disclosed in Note 1.5. Estimates, judgments, and assumptions may change from period to period, and actual results may differ from those estimates.

Changes in estimates and judgments are reflected in the financial statements in the period in which they are made. Their effects are disclosed in the notes when material.

### a. Revenue recognition

The Group’s customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue recognition and measurement are considered when:

- The contract has been approved in writing by the parties.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- The contract is legally enforceable.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to those obligations. Identifying deliverables, determining whether customers can benefit independently from them, and allocating the transaction price involve significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When services are discrete and the pattern of benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven, revenue is recognized using a percentage-of-completion method. The method depends on the contractual promises and the nature of the deliverables.

For other fixed-price contracts, the Group uses the percentage-of-completion method. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs, because input and productivity have a direct relationship. Estimates of total efforts or costs are reassessed throughout the contract period using the latest available information.

Some integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded:

- Net of costs when the Group acts as an agent.
- Gross when the Group is the principal.

The Group assesses whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other indicators of control.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded in the period when the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs required to complete the contract.

### b. Income taxes

The Group’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. The Company also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is required in determining income-tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing whether deferred income-tax assets are realizable, management considers whether some or all of the assets may not be realized. Ultimate realization depends on generating future taxable income during the periods when temporary differences become deductible. Management considers:

- Scheduled reversals of deferred income-tax liabilities.
- Projected future taxable income.
- Tax-planning strategies.

Based on historical taxable income and projected future taxable income during the periods in which deferred tax assets are deductible, management believes the Group will realize the benefits of those deductible differences. The realizable amount could be reduced in the near term if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. Refer to Note 2.12.

### c. Business combinations and intangible assets

Business combinations are accounted for under IFRS 3 (Revised), Business Combinations. Identifiable intangible assets and contingent consideration are fair-valued to determine the net fair value of the acquiree’s identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities.

External valuation experts conduct these valuations. Estimates are required to determine the value of contingent consideration, option arrangements, and intangible assets. Measurements are based on information available at the acquisition date and on management’s reasonable expectations and assumptions. Refer to Notes 2.10 and 2.9.2.

### d. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Group’s asset base. Periodic depreciation is determined using estimates of an asset’s expected useful life and expected residual value.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including technological changes. Refer to Note 2.7.

### e. Impairment of Goodwill

Goodwill is tested for impairment annually and whenever there is an indication that the recoverable amount of a cash-generating unit (CGU) is less than its carrying amount.

For impairment testing, goodwill is allocated to the CGU or group of CGUs that benefits from acquisition synergies and represents the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes.

The recoverable amount of CGUs is the higher of value in use and fair value less costs to sell. Key assumptions in cash-flow projections include estimated:

- Long-term growth rates.
- Weighted average cost of capital.
- Operating margins.

Refer to Note 2.9.1.

### IFRS 18 - Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements

On April 9, 2024, the IASB issued IFRS 18, Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements, which will replace IAS 1, Presentation of Financial Statements, from its effective date.

IFRS 18 introduces new requirements for information presented in the primary financial statements and disclosed in the notes. The requirements focus on the statement of profit or loss and introduce three categories for income and expenses:

- Operating
- Investing
- Financing

The standard is effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2027. Early adoption is permitted. The Group is evaluating the impact of the amendment.

### Amendments to IFRS 9 Financial Instruments and IFRS 7 Financial Instruments: Disclosures

On May 30, 2024, the IASB issued amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7 addressing:

- Classification of financial assets with environmental, social, and governance (ESG) and similar features.
- Derecognition of financial liabilities settled through electronic payment systems.
- Additional disclosures concerning equity instruments designated at fair value through other comprehensive income.
- Additional disclosures for financial instruments with contingent features.

The amendments are effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026. Early adoption is permitted. The Group evaluated the amendments and determined that they have no impact on its consolidated financial statements.

On December 18, 2024, the IASB issued further amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7 addressing:

- Factors entities must consider when assessing the own-use requirements for contracts to buy and take delivery of nature-dependent renewable electricity.
- Hedge-accounting treatment for nature-dependent renewable electricity.
- Related disclosures.

These amendments are effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026. Early adoption is permitted. The Group evaluated the amendments and determined that they have no impact on its consolidated financial statements.
