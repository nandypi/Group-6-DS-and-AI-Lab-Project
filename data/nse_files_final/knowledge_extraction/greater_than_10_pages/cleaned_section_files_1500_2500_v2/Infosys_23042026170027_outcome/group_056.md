## Financial risk factors

The Group’s activities expose it to market risk, credit risk, and liquidity risk. Its primary market risk is foreign exchange risk. The Group uses derivative financial instruments to mitigate foreign exchange exposures.

Credit risk is influenced mainly by the individual characteristics of each customer and the concentration of risk among the top few customers.

## Market risk

The Group operates internationally, with a major portion of its business transacted in several currencies. It is exposed to foreign exchange risk through sales and services in the United States and elsewhere, purchases from overseas suppliers in various foreign currencies, and intercompany transactions in foreign currencies.

The Group holds foreign exchange forward and option contracts to mitigate the risk of changes in exchange rates. The exchange rate between the Indian rupee and foreign currencies has changed substantially in recent years and may fluctuate substantially in the future. The Group’s operating results may be adversely affected by appreciation or depreciation of the rupee against these currencies.

During the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025, the Group designated certain foreign exchange forward and option contracts as cash flow hedges to mitigate foreign exchange exposure on highly probable forecast cash transactions.

The related hedge transactions for the balance in the cash flow hedge reserve as of March 31, 2026 are expected to occur and be reclassified to the statement of comprehensive income within three months.

The Group determines whether an economic relationship exists between the hedging instrument and hedged item based on the currency, amount, and timing of forecast cash flows. Hedge effectiveness is determined at inception and through periodic prospective effectiveness assessments. These assessments consider whether an economic relationship exists and whether the hedging instrument is expected to offset changes in the cash flows of the hedged items.

If the hedge ratio is no longer optimal for risk-management purposes, but the risk-management objective remains unchanged and the hedge continues to qualify for hedge accounting, the hedge relationship is rebalanced by adjusting the volume of the hedging instrument or hedged item. Any hedge ineffectiveness is calculated and accounted for in profit or loss when the hedge relationship is rebalanced.
