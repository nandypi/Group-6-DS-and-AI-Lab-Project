## 2.3 LEASES

## The Company as a lessee

The Company's lease asset classes primarily consist of leases for land, buildings, and computers.

The Company assesses whether a contract contains a lease at contract inception. A contract is, or contains, a lease if it conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration. The Company assesses whether:

1. The contract involves the use of an identified asset.
2. The Company obtains substantially all of the economic benefits from use of the asset during the lease period.
3. The Company has the right to direct the use of the asset.

At lease commencement, the Company recognizes a right-of-use asset and corresponding lease liability for all lease arrangements in which it is a lessee, except leases with a term of 12 months or less and low-value leases. Payments for short-term and low-value leases are recognized as operating expenses on a straight-line basis over the lease term.

The lease term is the non-cancellable period, adjusted for extension or termination options when it is reasonably certain that those options will be exercised. The Company assesses the expected lease term on a lease-by-lease basis, considering factors including:

- Significant leasehold improvements undertaken during the lease term.
- Costs associated with terminating the lease.
- The importance of the underlying asset to Infosys's operations.
- The location of the underlying asset.
- The availability of suitable alternatives.

The lease term is reassessed in future periods to reflect current economic circumstances. Right-of-use assets and lease liabilities include extension or termination options when it is reasonably certain that the options will be exercised.

Right-of-use assets are initially recognized at cost, comprising the initial lease liability adjusted for lease payments made at or before commencement, initial direct costs, and lease incentives. They are subsequently measured at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment losses.

Right-of-use assets are depreciated on a straight-line basis from the commencement date over the shorter of the lease term and the useful life of the underlying asset.

Right-of-use assets are evaluated for recoverability when events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. The recoverable amount, being the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use, is determined on an individual asset basis unless the asset does not generate cash flows that are largely independent of other assets. In such cases, recoverability is assessed for the cash-generating unit to which the asset belongs.

Lease liabilities are initially measured at amortized cost at the present value of future lease payments. Payments are discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease or, when that rate is not readily determinable, the incremental borrowing rate in the country of domicile of the lease. Lease liabilities are remeasured, with a corresponding adjustment to the related right-of-use asset, when the Company's assessment of extension or termination options changes.

Lease liabilities and right-of-use assets are presented separately in the balance sheet. Lease payments are classified as financing cash flows.

## The Company as a lessor

Leases for which the Company is a lessor are classified as finance or operating leases. A lease is classified as a finance lease when its terms transfer substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to the lessee. All other leases are classified as operating leases.

As an intermediate lessor, the Company accounts for its interests in the head lease and sublease separately. The sublease is classified as a finance or operating lease by reference to the right-of-use asset arising from the head lease.

For finance leases, finance income is recognized over the lease term based on a pattern reflecting a constant periodic rate of return on the lessor's net investment in the lease. For operating leases, rental income is recognized on a straight-line basis over the relevant lease term.
