## Basis for Opinion

The audit of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under Section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013. The auditors state that they are independent of the Group under the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI) Code of Ethics and applicable ethical requirements. They believe that the audit evidence obtained provides a sufficient and appropriate basis for their audit opinion.

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements

The Company’s Board of Directors is responsible for preparing and presenting interim condensed consolidated financial statements that give a true and fair view of the Group’s:

- Consolidated financial position
- Consolidated financial performance
- Consolidated other comprehensive income
- Consolidated changes in equity
- Consolidated cash flows

The statements are prepared in accordance with Ind AS 34 and other accounting principles generally accepted in India.

The respective Boards of Directors of the entities included in the Group are responsible for:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records
- Safeguarding the Group’s assets
- Preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting statements and prevent material misstatement due to fraud or error
- Assessing the ability of the respective entities to continue as going concerns
- Disclosing applicable going-concern matters
- Using the going-concern basis of accounting unless liquidation or cessation of operations is intended or there is no realistic alternative
- Overseeing the Group’s financial reporting process

## Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit of the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements

The auditors’ objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance that the interim condensed consolidated financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor’s report containing their opinion.

Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but does not guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing will always detect a material misstatement. Misstatements may arise from fraud or error and are considered material if they could reasonably be expected to influence users’ economic decisions based on the financial statements.

As part of the audit, the auditors exercise professional judgment and maintain professional skepticism. Their responsibilities include:

- Identifying and assessing risks of material misstatement due to fraud or error, designing responsive audit procedures, and obtaining sufficient and appropriate audit evidence.
- Recognizing that the risk of failing to detect a material misstatement resulting from fraud is higher than for one resulting from error because fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or management override of internal control.
- Obtaining an understanding of internal financial controls relevant to the audit to design appropriate audit procedures. This work is not performed to express an opinion on the effectiveness of those controls.
- Evaluating the appropriateness of accounting policies and the reasonableness of accounting estimates and related disclosures made by management.
- Assessing the appropriateness of management’s use of the going-concern basis of accounting.
- Determining, based on audit evidence obtained, whether a material uncertainty exists relating to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the Group’s ability to continue as a going concern.
- Drawing attention in the auditor’s report to relevant going-concern disclosures when a material uncertainty exists, or modifying the audit opinion if those disclosures are inadequate.
- Recognizing that audit conclusions are based on evidence obtained up to the date of the auditor’s report, while future events or conditions may cause the Group to cease to continue as a going concern.
- Evaluating the overall presentation, structure, and content of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements, including disclosures, and whether they represent the underlying transactions and events in a manner that achieves fair presentation.
- Obtaining sufficient and appropriate audit evidence regarding the financial information of entities within the Group to express an opinion on the interim condensed consolidated financial statements.
- Directing, supervising, and performing the audit of the financial statements of entities included in the interim condensed consolidated financial statements for which the auditors are independent.
