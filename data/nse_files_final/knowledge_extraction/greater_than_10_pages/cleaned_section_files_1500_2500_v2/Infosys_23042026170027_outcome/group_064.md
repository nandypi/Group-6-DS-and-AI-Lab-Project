## 2.9.2 Intangible assets

## Accounting Policy

Intangible assets are stated at cost less accumulated amortization and impairment. They are amortized over their respective estimated useful lives on a straight-line basis from the date they are available for use.

The estimated useful life of an identifiable intangible asset is based on factors including obsolescence, demand, competition, industry stability, known technological advances, and the maintenance expenditures required to obtain expected future cash flows. Amortization methods and useful lives are reviewed periodically, including at each financial year end.

Research costs are expensed as incurred. Software product development costs are expensed as incurred unless technical and commercial feasibility is demonstrated, future economic benefits are probable, the Group intends and is able to complete and use or sell the software, and the costs can be measured reliably. Capitalizable costs include materials, direct labor, and directly attributable overhead costs required to prepare the asset for its intended use.

## Impairment

Intangible assets are evaluated for recoverability whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. The recoverable amount—the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use—is determined on an individual asset basis unless the asset does not generate cash flows largely independent of other assets. In that case, recoverability is assessed for the cash-generating unit (CGU) to which the asset belongs.

An impairment loss recognized in net profit in the statement of comprehensive income equals the amount by which the asset’s carrying value exceeds its estimated recoverable amount. An impairment loss is reversed in net profit if there has been a change in the estimates used to determine recoverable amount, subject to the carrying amount not exceeding the amount that would have been determined, net of accumulated amortization, had no impairment loss been recognized in prior years.
