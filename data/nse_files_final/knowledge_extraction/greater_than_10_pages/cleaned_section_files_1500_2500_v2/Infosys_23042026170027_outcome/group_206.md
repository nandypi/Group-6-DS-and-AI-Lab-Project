## Provident fund

Eligible employees of Infosys receive benefits from a provident fund classified as a defined benefit plan. Both eligible employees and the Company make monthly contributions equal to a specified percentage of the covered employee’s salary.

The Company contributes a portion to the Infosys Limited Employees’ Provident Fund Trust, which invests in designated instruments permitted under Indian law. The remaining portion is contributed to the government-administered pension fund. The annual interest rate payable to beneficiaries by the trust is administered by the Government of India. The Company is obligated to make good any shortfall between the trust’s investment returns and the notified interest rate.

Eligible employees of Indian subsidiaries receive benefits from provident funds classified as defined contribution plans. Both employees and the respective companies make monthly contributions equal to a specified percentage of covered employees’ salaries. Contributions are deposited in a government-administered provident fund, and the companies have no further obligation beyond their monthly contributions.

## Superannuation

Certain employees of Infosys, Infosys BPM, and EdgeVerve participate in a defined contribution plan. The Group has no further obligation beyond its monthly contributions, which are periodically contributed to a trust fund invested with the Life Insurance Corporation of India.

## Compensated absences

The Group’s compensated absences policy includes both accumulating and non-accumulating absences. The expected cost of accumulating compensated absences is determined by an external actuary at each Balance Sheet date using the projected unit credit method. The calculation reflects the additional amount expected to be paid or availed because of unused entitlement accumulated at the Balance Sheet date.

Expense for non-accumulating compensated absences is recognized in the period in which the absences occur.

## 2.18.1 Impact of Labour Codes

On November 21, 2025, the Government of India notified provisions of the Code on Wages, 2019, the Industrial Relations Code, 2020, the Code on Social Security, 2020, and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 (the “Labour Codes”). These laws consolidate 29 existing labour laws into a unified framework governing employee benefits during and after employment.

The Labour Codes introduce changes including a uniform definition of wages and enhanced leave benefits. The Group’s assessment of the financial implications resulted in an increase of ₹1,289 crore in gratuity liability arising from past service cost and in leave liability.

Because the impact of the new legislation is non-recurring, the Group presented the incremental amount as “Impact of Labour Codes” under “Exceptional Item” in the Condensed Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss for the year ended March 31, 2026. The Group continues to monitor developments relating to the Labour Codes and will evaluate any impact on the measurement of employee benefit liabilities.

## 2.19 Leases

## The Group as a lessee

The Group’s lease asset classes primarily comprise land, buildings, and computers. A contract is, or contains, a lease if it conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration.

To determine whether it controls the use of an identified asset, the Group assesses whether:

1. The contract involves the use of an identified asset.
2. The Group receives substantially all the economic benefits from use of the asset during the lease period.
3. The Group has the right to direct the use of the asset.

At lease commencement, the Group recognizes a right-of-use asset and a corresponding lease liability for lease arrangements in which it is a lessee, except for leases with a term of 12 months or less and low-value leases. Payments for short-term and low-value leases are recognized as operating expenses on a straight-line basis over the lease term.

The lease term is the non-cancellable period adjusted for extension or termination options when exercise of those options is reasonably certain. The Group assesses the expected lease term on a lease-by-lease basis, considering factors such as significant leasehold improvements, termination costs, the importance and location of the underlying asset, and the availability of suitable alternatives. Lease terms are reassessed in future periods to reflect current economic circumstances.

Right-of-use assets and lease liabilities include extension or termination options when it is reasonably certain that the options will be exercised.

Right-of-use assets are initially recognized at cost, comprising the initial lease liability adjusted for lease payments made at or before commencement, initial direct costs, and lease incentives. They are subsequently measured at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment losses.

Right-of-use assets are depreciated from the commencement date on a straight-line basis over the shorter of the lease term and the useful life of the underlying asset.

Right-of-use assets are assessed for recoverability when events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. For impairment testing, the recoverable amount—the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use—is determined on an individual asset basis unless the asset does not generate cash flows that are largely independent of other assets. In those cases, recoverable amount is determined for the cash-generating unit to which the asset belongs.

Lease liabilities are initially measured at amortized cost at the present value of future lease payments. Payments are discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease or, when that rate is not readily determinable, the incremental borrowing rates in the country of domicile of the leases.

Lease liabilities are remeasured with a corresponding adjustment to the related right-of-use asset when the Group changes its assessment of whether it will exercise an extension or termination option. Lease liabilities and right-of-use assets are presented separately in the Balance Sheet, and lease payments are classified as financing cash flows.

## The Group as a lessor

Leases for which the Group is a lessor are classified as finance or operating leases. A lease is classified as a finance lease when its terms transfer substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to the lessee. All other leases are classified as operating leases.

When the Group is an intermediate lessor, it accounts for its interests in the head lease and sublease separately. The sublease is classified as a finance or operating lease by reference to the right-of-use asset arising from the head lease.

For finance leases, finance income is recognized over the lease term based on a pattern reflecting a constant periodic rate of return on the lessor’s net investment in the lease. For operating leases, rental income is recognized on a straight-line basis over the relevant lease term.
