## Moderator

Thank you. We will now begin the question-and-answer session. The first question is from Ankur Rudra of JP Morgan.

## Ankur Rudra

Organic growth was approximately 3.5% to 3.4% year over year for the quarter, compared with overall growth of about 5% last quarter. Why is guidance still 2% at the midpoint, and why was the upper end of the guidance reduced despite strong signings?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

At the beginning of the year, the lower end of guidance assumed heightened uncertainty, while the higher end assumed a steady to improving environment.

Although Q1 was strong, the underlying environment has not materially changed. In Q2, Infosys is not seeing signs of significant improvement. The tariff situation remains escalated, and the geopolitical situation has not changed.

Q1 and Q2 together are seasonally the strongest part of the year. Based on current conditions, the lower end of guidance assumes continuing or elevated uncertainty, while the upper end assumes a steady environment.

## Ankur Rudra

Are there margin or pricing trade-offs when engaging with clients on renewals or other contracts where the benefits of AI are incorporated? Is Infosys proactively taking enterprise AI solutions to clients?

Also, does increased vendor consolidation, partly driven by AI, increase the replaceability of vendors such as Infosys?

## Salil Parekh

Enterprise AI is producing productivity benefits in areas such as deploying agents and setting up enterprise AI platforms using foundation models. It is also creating new revenue opportunities.

Productivity gains are typically shared between clients and Infosys. In some cases, clients seek these improvements directly; in others, Infosys brings the opportunity to clients to improve efficiency. This work also creates opportunities to support clients with additional enterprise AI and other services.

Regarding vendor consolidation, Infosys has benefited from large deals where clients believed other companies had not provided strong AI solutions or solid delivery, or where clients were evaluating smaller companies.

Infosys's delivery strength, track record of bringing AI innovation to clients, consistent execution, and scale across multiple services are supporting its position with large clients. Management does not believe AI is making vendor replacement inherently easier or more difficult.

## Kumar Rakesh

Is the revision to the upper end of the organic growth guidance solely a reflection of changed macroeconomic assumptions, rather than changes in deal ramp-up, third-party costs, or operational issues?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

At the beginning of the year, Infosys had already indicated that third-party costs would be lower. That factor has not changed.

The upper end of the original guidance assumed a steady to marginally improving environment. The environment did not improve in Q1, and almost one month of Q2 had passed. Tariff and geopolitical challenges continue. Clients remain in a wait-and-watch mode regarding discretionary spending, including deal signing, and deal cycles remain elongated.

The upper end of the revised guidance assumes a steady environment.

On a year-over-year basis, third-party costs were flattish in Q1. Therefore, extrapolating Q1 year-over-year growth to the full year would create a headwind from third-party costs.

## Kumar Rakesh

The company mentioned pricing and productivity benefits of approximately 70 basis points in Q1. What contributed to this benefit? Will third-party costs decline further from the first-quarter level during the year?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

The pricing benefit includes Project Maximus, particularly its value-based selling initiatives. The approximately 70-basis-point benefit was a combination of Project Maximus and seasonality.

The quarter had a higher number of working calendar days, and some furlough reversal also contributed to the benefit.

In the previous year, Infosys reported approximately 3.5% in pricing benefits, including some low-hanging opportunities. Project Maximus is continuing to contribute.

Infosys is not providing quarterly guidance on third-party costs. Based on signed deals and the pipeline, the company expects FY'26 third-party costs to be lower than FY'25 third-party costs.

## Kumar Rakesh

Europe has outperformed overall company growth for the last four or five quarters. What is driving this performance, and how sustainable is it?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys identified Europe as an opportunity several years ago and made investments based on that view. These investments helped the company win very large and mega deals in Europe.

Consolidation deals have also contributed to European growth. In addition, Europe has gradually become more open to outsourcing.

There are sufficient opportunities in Europe, although Infosys is not providing guidance on whether the region will continue to grow faster than the company. The company is seeing opportunities in Europe, with many large deals and a significant portion of the pipeline located there.

## Abhishek Kumar

Vendor consolidation has been ongoing for at least two years. Has there been a shift in the vendors with which Infosys competes—from a longer tail of small vendors added after COVID-19 to larger peers? Does this make competition more challenging and put pressure on margins?

## Salil Parekh

Clients have a range of vendor consolidation options, and consolidation has been ongoing for longer than the last two or three years.

Infosys is benefiting from consolidation because of the type of work it brings to clients, its enterprise AI investments over the last couple of years, and its consistent delivery across other offerings. Automation and Lean also contribute to the company's proposition.

Clients are selecting Infosys in situations involving large, midsized, and small competitors.

Pricing discussions generally focus on productivity, whether they occur during consolidation or in new engagements. Over time, clients expect productivity improvements, and Infosys evaluates the benefits it can provide through Automation, Lean, and enterprise AI.
