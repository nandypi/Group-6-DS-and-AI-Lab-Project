## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Before COVID-19, Infosys's delivery mix was close to 30% onsite and 70% offshore. The mix typically changed by approximately 20–30 basis points each year. After COVID-19, the mix changed significantly, by 3%–4% or more within a year.

Management said that when delivery constraints arise, Infosys and its clients work together to establish an operating model that works. The extent to which increased nearshoring and offshoring could improve margins depends on the balance between nearshoring, offshoring, and additional local hiring.

## James Friedman

Management had previously indicated a 70-basis-point margin impact from higher post-sale customer support. Jayesh Sanghrajka said this was a quarter-on-quarter margin-walk impact: the previous quarter had included a benefit, while the current quarter was at a normalized level.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys is already seeing projects in which AI agents work alongside people on the project. AI agents are expected to become part of the evolving delivery model. The impact of visa-related changes and the development of AI-agent-based delivery are separate trends that may eventually affect delivery ratios as the business develops.

## Sumeet Jain

### AI impact on revenue growth and client budgets

Salil Parekh described two types of AI opportunities:

1. Growth opportunities, such as using AI in sales and marketing to drive client growth.
2. Productivity and efficiency opportunities in client processes and activities.

Given the current economic environment, clients are initially focused heavily on cost reduction. Modernization discussions are more focused on growth when they involve capabilities that clients are not currently using. Management did not provide a view on whether AI-driven productivity or growth opportunities will ultimately be larger, but said Infosys sees substantial growth opportunities from AI delivery.

Clients are also seeking cost reductions through consolidation and non-AI automation. These approaches are contributing to some of the large deals currently being pursued. As AI approaches demonstrate measurable business growth benefits, and as economic conditions support them, management expects more such opportunities to emerge.

### Macroeconomic outlook

Management said that an improvement in the macroeconomic environment typically improves the technology sector, based on past experience. However, it did not provide a specific view on the following year’s growth outlook or the effect of tariff-related uncertainty.

## Nitin Padmanabhan

### Volumes, realizations, and revenue growth

Volumes were softer, while a large part of growth came from RPP expansion. The increase was partly attributable to a higher number of working and calendar days during the quarter, which reflected in pricing. Project Maximus also contributed by driving effective pricing increases through various levers.

### Versent JV

The Versent JV had not yet closed because several regulatory approvals were pending. Infosys expects the transaction to close during the year but did not know the exact timeline. The transaction was not included in guidance at this stage. Versent's revenue in the previous year was approximately Australian $210 million. Infosys does not disclose acquisition margins.

### Furloughs and smaller deals

Furloughs were not expected to be significantly different from the previous year. Small deals remained similar to the previous year, while the overall pipeline continued to be strong.

## Abhishek Kumar

### Second-half outlook and guidance

Management said guidance is intended to reduce the information asymmetry between Infosys and investors. The guidance range is based on various models:

- The lower end includes an elevated level of uncertainty.
- The upper end assumes a stable environment.

The second half is seasonally softer because of fewer working days, fewer calendar days, and a higher impact from furloughs. Management also noted that Infosys had delivered a stronger first half compared with many peers, which affects the second-half outlook.

Abhishek Kumar asked whether the recently announced mega deal is expected to begin ramping up during the fiscal year and what its net new contribution could be.
