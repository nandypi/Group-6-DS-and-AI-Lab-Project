Consolidated cash and investments were $6.2 billion at the end of the quarter. Yield on the cash balance was 6.98%, and ROE stood at 29.1%.

Infosys has taken several strategic steps in recent years to reduce dependence on work visas, especially H-1Bs in the U.S. These include reducing the onsite mix, increasing near-shoring and local hiring, establishing university partnerships, and creating local hubs. Infosys has several delivery centers across the U.S. to serve clients and leverage local talent. These hubs focus on emerging technologies including artificial intelligence, machine learning, cloud computing, big data, and user experience design.

In line with its capital allocation policy, Infosys announced an ₹18,000 crore buyback through the tender route at ₹1,800 per share. The buyback is expected to be completed in Q3, subject to shareholder approval.

The Board approved an interim dividend of ₹23 per share, which is 9.5% higher than the FY '25 interim dividend.

## Coming to verticals:

### Financial Services

Clients are actively planning modernization and AI-driven initiatives, with a focus on cost efficiency, enhanced customer experience, and strategic business transformation. Infosys sees strong momentum in mortgages, capital markets, commercial banking, and wealth management.

Macroeconomic uncertainty and volatility are affecting spending, although the mortgage sector has seen some acceleration following recent reductions in interest rates. The pipeline and signings remain strong, reflected in six large deals signed during the quarter.

Banks have spent significantly to build AI infrastructure. Many initiatives are progressing from proofs of concept to full-scale projects, with notable traction in Agentic AI.

### Manufacturing

Manufacturing continues to face trade and macroeconomic uncertainties, creating pressure on discretionary spending, particularly in the automotive sector. Infosys continues to support clients with digital initiatives and the rationalization of application and infrastructure footprints.

The company is using AI and automation to increase productivity and offset pricing deflation. In aerospace, opportunities include helping clients resolve supply-chain bottlenecks using technology and products.

More than 90% of the large-deal total contract value for Q2 was net new, which is expected to support future growth.

### EURS

EURS clients are focused on cost reduction, operational efficiency, and cash preservation, creating opportunities for vendor consolidation.

In Resources, large-scale GenAI deployments remain limited, while Agentic AI adoption is growing in technology operations to reduce costs.

The rapid construction of data centers is driving utility companies to seek partners to meet accelerating electricity demand. Opportunities include renewable integration, grid modernization, and AI-driven optimization.

Year-on-year growth was affected by significantly higher third-party revenues in Q2 '25.

### Retail

Retail clients remain cautious because of ongoing tariff-related uncertainties. Across geographies, there is increased focus on AI, cloud, estate modernization, de-risking, and cost reduction.

Clients are increasingly focused on improving the productivity of operating models to offset inflationary pressures. Deal pipelines remain strong, although decision cycles remain elongated.

Infosys continues to use its Topaz and AI Next platform capabilities in areas including digital marketing, predictive analytics, and real-time insights to improve customer and employee experience.

### Communications

Communications continues to face growth headwinds and high operating-expense pressures. Discretionary spending remains subdued, with investment prioritized toward AI, automation, and consumer experience.

Global Capability Centers are becoming key buying centers, creating opportunities for IT companies to support their transition. Lower interest rates offer cautious optimism, while geopolitical tensions and tariff risks add uncertainty.

### Hi-Tech

The Hi-Tech segment has seen significant cost-reduction efforts, leading to budget cuts and program closures. Opportunities are emerging in areas such as semiconductors, with a strong focus on leveraging GenAI.

Infosys’s H1 performance reflected the resilience of its business model and the agility of its execution capabilities. For H2, seasonal factors are expected to affect growth, including fewer working days, furloughs, and the onset of the new calendar year.

Infosys revised its revenue guidance to 2%–3%. This guidance does not include revenue from the joint venture with Telstra, which is expected to close later this year. Margin guidance remains at 20%–22%.
