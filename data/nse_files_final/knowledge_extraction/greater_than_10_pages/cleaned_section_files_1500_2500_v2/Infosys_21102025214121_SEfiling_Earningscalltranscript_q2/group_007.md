## Kumar Rakesh

Over the last two quarters, large-deal TCV has exceeded $3 billion. Vendor consolidation has been identified as one of the trends driving deal signings. Kumar Rakesh asked how management views the margins of these deals as they ramp up and contribute to revenue.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys does not disclose margins for large deals separately. Management stated that it follows a disciplined approach when taking on these deals. Chief Delivery Officer Dinesh and Satish H. C. are involved early in many large deals to understand the issues before signing and to maintain the margin profile at the start of, and throughout, the program.

Management is comfortable with the margin profile and does not expect an unusual margin impact from the ramp-up of large deals.

## Moderator

The next question was from Bryan Bergin of TD Cowen.

## Bryan Bergin

Bergin asked about changes in smaller-deal activity and average deal sizes, as well as trends in ACV for newly booked work, given that large-deal TCV had been healthy for two quarters.

## Salil Parekh

There has been no meaningful change in smaller-deal activity compared with recent periods.

Most large deals are focused on cost reduction, vendor consolidation, AI-enabled productivity, and lean automation.

Infosys does not disclose ACV separately. Management has not seen a material difference in the ACV structure of large deals this quarter compared with the previous quarter.

## Bryan Bergin

Bergin asked how the delivery mix might change beyond Fiscal 2026 in the context of potential visa changes. He also asked whether the recent increases in subcontractor and offshore usage could continue and whether there were potential ceilings for either.

## Salil Parekh

Subcontracting is not viewed as a long-term lever for changing the delivery mix. Subcontractor usage may rise or fall in different periods.

Infosys has developed localization capabilities over several years in geographies outside India, especially the United States. These capabilities include:

- Local technology operations and recruiting
- Nearshore centers in locations such as Canada, Mexico, and parts of South America
- Offshore delivery

Infosys expects to use a combination of local, nearshore, and offshore delivery to maintain a consistent client delivery approach. Management does not have a target for the eventual offshore ratio, but early client discussions indicate that clients may seek to increase offshore work over time.

## Moderator

The next question was from Jonathan Lee of Guggenheim Partners.

## Jonathan Lee

Lee asked about the decline in utilization despite increased subcontractor usage and the skill or pyramid gaps that subcontractors help address.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Subcontractor usage was approximately 11% a couple of quarters ago and had declined to 8.5%. Management said there has been a consistent long-term effort to reduce subcontractor usage.

Quarter-to-quarter changes depend on client demand, available skills, and the location of those skills. Infosys may increase subcontractor usage to bridge skill gaps across client projects and reduce it when those needs decline.

Management does not currently expect subcontractor usage to increase significantly from current levels.

## Jonathan Lee

Lee asked about forward-deployed engineers, including how they compare with similar teams being scaled by software companies and whether they carry higher costs than traditional engineers.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys has used forward-deployed engineers across its AI landscape for some time. Management did not comment on their specific cost or structure.

The company has different groups within its delivery structure, including power programmers, with different cost profiles. Infosys uses the appropriate cost level based on the market in which the engineers operate and the work they perform for clients.

## Satish H. C.

AI is creating a new services or software stack for clients, involving changes in how businesses operate and how services are delivered. Because this represents a different operating model, Infosys is placing greater emphasis on co-creation with clients.

As clients move from proofs of concept to enterprise-scale AI adoption, management expects greater demand for forward-deployed engineers to work closely with clients and develop this new software stack.

## Moderator

The next question was from Vibhor Singhal of Nuvama.

## Vibhor Singhal

Singhal asked whether the recent H-1B visa fee increase had created short-term uncertainty, delayed deals, or increased client concerns. He also asked whether the change could lead to greater use of offshore delivery over the longer term.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys had not seen any short-term change following the H-1B visa fee increase.

Over the medium to long term, management expects the delivery model to evolve through greater use of:

- Local employees in technology hubs and centers outside India, including the United States
- Nearshore delivery
- Offshore delivery

Infosys does not have specific percentage targets for these changes. The objective is to maintain effective client delivery through a combination of delivery locations and staffing models.

## Vibhor Singhal

Singhal asked whether clients would accept a greater shift toward nearshore and offshore delivery, given that onsite presence may previously have reflected client, regulatory, or internal requirements.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys will work jointly with each client to determine how the delivery model should operate for that client. Management compared this adjustment with the shift to remote work during COVID-19, when delivery models were adapted over time.

Different clients may use different combinations of local, nearshore, and offshore delivery. Management is comfortable that the available delivery levers will not constrain client delivery.
