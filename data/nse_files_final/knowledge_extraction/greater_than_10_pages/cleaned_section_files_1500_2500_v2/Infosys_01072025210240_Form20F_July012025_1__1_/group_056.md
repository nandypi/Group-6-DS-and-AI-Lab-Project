## Distribution

If a U.S. holder is not treated under the Section 302 tests as recognizing gain or loss on an exchange of equity shares for cash, the holder will generally be treated as receiving a distribution from the Company.

The gross distribution will generally be treated as dividend income to the extent of the Company’s current or accumulated earnings and profits, as determined under U.S. federal income tax principles. Such dividends will not generally qualify for the dividends-received deduction available to corporate U.S. holders.

To the extent a buyback exceeds the Company’s current and accumulated earnings and profits, the excess will first be treated as a tax-free return of the U.S. holder’s tax basis in the equity shares and thereafter as capital gain.

The Company does not intend to calculate its earnings and profits according to U.S. tax accounting principles.

## Equity Share Buybacks and Section 302 Tests

An exchange of equity shares for cash will generally be a substantially disproportionate redemption for a U.S. holder if:

- The percentage of voting stock owned immediately after the exchange is less than 80% of the percentage owned immediately before the exchange; and
- After the exchange, the U.S. holder owns less than 50% of the total combined voting power of all classes of stock entitled to vote.

For purposes of the Section 302 tests, U.S. holders must account for equity shares and ADSs constructively owned under applicable attribution rules. These rules may attribute ownership of shares and ADSs held by certain family members and related entities, as well as shares and ADSs that the U.S. holder has the right to acquire through an option. Family attribution may be waived in certain circumstances.

If an exchange does not satisfy the substantially disproportionate test, it may still satisfy the “not essentially equivalent to a dividend” test if it produces a meaningful reduction in the U.S. holder’s equity interest, based on the holder’s particular facts and circumstances.

IRS published rulings indicate that a relatively minor reduction may satisfy this test when the U.S. holder’s relative equity interest is minimal and the holder does not exercise control over, or participate in, corporate management.

The Section 302 tests apply separately to each stockholder. Accordingly, the same buyback may be treated as a sale or exchange for some U.S. holders and as a distribution for others.

## Qualified Foreign Corporation Treatment

Subject to conditions and limitations, including the PFIC rules, dividends paid to non-corporate U.S. holders may qualify for a reduced tax rate if the Company is a qualified foreign corporation.

A qualified foreign corporation generally includes a foreign corporation whose shares are readily tradable on an established U.S. securities market or that is eligible for benefits under a comprehensive income tax treaty with the United States, including the applicable treaty.

Based on existing guidance, it is unclear whether dividends on an equity share will qualify for the reduced rate because the equity shares are not themselves listed on a U.S. exchange. The Company may nevertheless be eligible for treaty benefits. A corporation is not a qualified foreign corporation if it is a PFIC during the current or prior taxable year.

## Sale or Exchange of Equity Shares or ADSs

Subject to the PFIC rules, a U.S. holder generally recognizes gain or loss on the sale or exchange of equity shares or ADSs equal to the difference between the amount realized and the holder’s adjusted tax basis.

The gain or loss generally will be capital gain or loss and will be long-term capital gain or loss if the equity shares or ADSs were held for more than one year.

Gain or loss generally will be treated as U.S.-source passive-category income or loss for U.S. foreign tax credit purposes. The sale of equity shares or ADSs may be subject to Indian taxes. Due to limitations on the use of foreign tax credits, a U.S. holder may not be able to use those taxes as a credit against the U.S. federal income tax liability resulting from the sale.

## Estate Taxes

An individual U.S. holder generally will include the value of equity shares or ADSs held by the holder in the holder’s gross estate for U.S. federal estate tax purposes.

An individual holder who pays Indian estate tax on equity shares may be entitled to credit that tax against U.S. federal estate tax liability, subject to applicable conditions and limitations.

## Additional Tax on Investment Income

U.S. holders who are individuals, estates, or trusts and whose income exceeds specified thresholds may be subject to a 3.8% tax on certain net investment income, including dividends on, and capital gains from the sale or other taxable disposition of, equity shares or ADSs. Limitations and exceptions may apply.

## Backup Withholding and Information Reporting

Dividends paid on, and proceeds from the sale of, equity shares or ADSs may be subject to U.S. information reporting. Backup withholding may apply unless the holder is an exempt recipient, provides a correct U.S. taxpayer identification number, certifies under penalty of perjury that the number is correct and that the holder is not subject to backup withholding, and satisfies other applicable requirements.

Amounts withheld under the backup withholding rules may be claimed as a refund or credit against the holder’s U.S. federal income tax liability if the required information is timely provided to the IRS.

Certain U.S. holders must report information regarding investments in equity shares or ADSs not held through a custodial account with a U.S. financial institution on IRS Form 8938, attached to the holder’s annual income tax return. Failure to report required information may result in substantial penalties.

## Passive Foreign Investment Company

A non-U.S. corporation generally will be classified as a PFIC for U.S. federal income tax purposes if either:

- At least 75% of its gross income for the taxable year is passive income; or
- On average for the taxable year, at least 50% of the value of its assets, measured at the end of each quarter, is attributable to assets that produce or are held for the production of passive income.

The Company does not believe that it satisfied either PFIC test for fiscal 2025. PFIC status is determined annually, and no assurance can be given that the Company will not be classified as a PFIC in future taxable years.

If the Company were classified as a PFIC, U.S. holders:

- May be required to pay an interest charge and tax at ordinary income rates on excess distributions and gains from the sale or other disposition of equity shares;
- Could avoid the excess distribution rules through a qualified electing fund election by including their pro rata share of the Company’s undistributed income in taxable income. The Company does not plan to provide the information necessary for U.S. holders to make this election;
- May avoid the excess distribution rules if the applicable equity shares or ADSs are marketable and the holder makes a mark-to-market election. Under this election, the holder must mark the equity shares or ADSs to market each taxable year and recognize ordinary gain, and ordinary loss to the extent of prior ordinary gain, based on changes in market value; and
- Generally will be subject to additional annual return requirements and may be required to file IRS Form 8621, unless an exemption applies.

The Company’s ADSs are traded on the NYSE and its equity shares are traded on Indian stock exchanges. Accordingly, a U.S. holder may be able to make a mark-to-market election with respect to the ADSs or equity shares.

U.S. holders should consult their own tax advisors regarding the treatment of dividends, eligibility for reduced tax rates, the availability of foreign tax credits for Indian withholding tax, and the tax consequences of participating in a buyback. Additional tax consequences may arise under state, local, or non-U.S. tax laws and under estate, gift, and inheritance tax laws.
