## Income tax expense

Profits earned from providing software development and other services outside India are subject to tax in the countries where the work is performed. Most taxes paid outside India can be claimed as a credit against the Company’s tax liability in India.

As a resident company in India under the Income Tax Act, 1961, the Company is required to pay taxes in India on its global income under Section 5. Revenue geographies disclosed in Note 2.11 of Item 18 are based on customer domicile and do not indicate where delivery or revenue-related work occurs. Therefore, domestic and foreign income taxes will not necessarily correlate with the proportion of revenue generated from India and other geographical segments.

The applicable Indian corporate statutory tax rates were 25.17% for fiscal 2025 and 34.94% for fiscal 2024. The Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019 introduced Section 115BAA, which allows a domestic company to elect a reduced corporate tax rate without claiming certain deductions, including the deduction for SEZ units under Section 10AA. For fiscal 2025, the Company recognized income tax expense under Section 115BAA without claiming the Section 10AA deduction.

In prior years, certain Indian SEZ units benefited from tax incentives for exporting services. Eligible units could claim:

- A 100% deduction of profits or gains from export services for the first five years.
- A 50% deduction for the following five years.
- Up to a 50% deduction for an additional five years, subject to creating and using a Special Economic Zone Re-Investment Reserve to acquire new plant and machinery.

If the Company does not use the SEZ reserve for plant and machinery within the period specified under the Income Tax Act, the unused reserve becomes taxable, increasing the effective tax cost.

For fiscal 2024, these incentives reduced income tax expense by $363 million compared with the estimated tax amount without the incentives. The per-share effect, based on both basic and diluted weighted-average equity shares, was $0.09. The fiscal 2025 income tax expense did not claim the Section 10AA deduction.

The effective tax rate is generally influenced by differential tax rates, non-deductible expenses, exempt non-operating income, overseas taxes, tax reversals and provisions relating to prior periods, changes to tax regulations, and other tax deductions.

The fiscal 2024 effective tax rate was lower primarily because tax provisions were reversed following orders received for prior assessment years. Income tax expense included the following prior-period amounts:

| Fiscal year | Provision or reversal, net (dollars in millions) | Details |
|---|---:|---|
| Fiscal 2025 | Provision of 16 | Additional provision of 86 offset by reversal of 70 from earlier periods |
| Fiscal 2024 | Reversal of 113 | Reversal of 151 from earlier periods offset by additional provision of 38 |

These prior-period provisions and reversals primarily resulted from the adjudication of disputed matters, tax-return filings, and completion of assessments across various jurisdictions.

During fiscal 2025, the Company received orders under Section 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, for assessment years 2016–17 and 2019–20. The orders confirmed the Company’s position regarding the tax treatment of certain contentious matters. As a result:

- Pre-tax interest income of $38 million and the corresponding tax impact were recognized.
- An income tax provision of $21 million was reversed, with a corresponding credit to the Statement of Profit and Loss.
- Contingent liabilities were reduced by $125 million.

During fiscal 2024, the Company received orders under Sections 250 and 254 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, for assessment years 2007–08 to 2015–16, 2017–18, and 2018–19. The orders confirmed the Company’s position regarding the tax treatment of certain contentious matters. As a result:

- Pre-tax interest income of $232 million and the corresponding tax impact were recognized.
- An income tax provision of $63 million was reversed, with a corresponding credit to the Statement of Profit and Loss.
- Contingent liabilities were reduced by $196 million.

The claims primarily represent demands arising from assessment proceedings under the Income Tax Act, 1961. Issues include the disallowance of software expenditure treated as capital in nature and payments to Associated Enterprises treated as subject to withholding tax, among other matters. These matters are pending before various Income Tax Authorities.

The Company’s Advanced Pricing Arrangement with the Internal Revenue Service for U.S. branch income tax expired in March 2021. The Company has applied for renewal. U.S. taxable income is currently based on the Company’s best estimate using the expected value method.
