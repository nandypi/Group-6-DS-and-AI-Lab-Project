# Takeover Code and Delisting Regulations

Where public shareholding in a target company falls below the limit specified in the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957 (“SCRR”) and the Listing Regulations because of shares acquired pursuant to an open offer, the acquirer must take the steps permitted under SEBI Master Circular SEBI/HO/CFD/PoD2/CIR/P/0155 dated November 11, 2024. This circular rescinded SEBI circulars SEBI/HO/CFD/PoD2/P/CIR/2023/120 dated July 11, 2023 and SEBI/HO/CFD/PoD2/P/CIR/2023/18 dated February 3, 2023. The steps must facilitate compliance with the public shareholding threshold within the period prescribed in the SCRR.

Following amendments to the Takeover Code dated December 6, 2021, an acquirer may make an offer to delist the company if the acquirer declares the intention to do so:

- At the time of making the public announcement of an open offer; and
- At the time of making the detailed public statement, in accordance with the Takeover Code.

For an indirect acquisition that is not a deemed direct acquisition under Regulation 5(2) of the Takeover Code, the intention to delist is required to be declared initially only in the detailed public statement.

If the target company is not delisted through the delisting offer, but the acquirer’s shareholding exceeds the maximum permissible non-public shareholding threshold, the acquirer may make a further attempt to delist the target company under the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Delisting of Equity Shares) Regulations, 2021 (“Delisting Regulations”) within 12 months after completion of the open offer. This is subject to the acquirer continuing to exceed the maximum permissible non-public shareholding.

If the further delisting attempt also fails, the acquirer must ensure compliance with the target company’s minimum public shareholding requirement under the SCRR within 12 months after the end of that 12-month period.

Subject to these requirements, if an acquirer’s shareholding exceeds the maximum permissible non-public shareholding pursuant to an open offer, the acquirer is not eligible to make a voluntary delisting offer under the Delisting Regulations until 12 months have elapsed from the date of completion of the offer period.

As Infosys is listed in India, the Takeover Code applies to Infosys and to any person acquiring its equity shares or voting rights, including through ADSs.

ADS holders may exercise voting rights in respect of the deposited equity shares represented by the ADSs. Accordingly, acquisitions of ADSs are subject to the Takeover Code. An open offer for at least 26% of the company’s shares to existing shareholders would be triggered by an ADS holder and persons acting in concert with that holder if the shares underlying their ADSs represent 25% or more of the company’s shares or voting rights.

Under the Listing Regulations, Infosys has entered into listing agreements with each Indian stock exchange on which its equity shares are listed. Infosys must report to the stock exchanges any disclosures made to the company under the Takeover Code.

Infosys’s Articles of Association do not contain provisions that would delay, defer, or prevent a change in control of the company and that would operate only with respect to a merger, acquisition, or corporate restructuring involving Infosys.
