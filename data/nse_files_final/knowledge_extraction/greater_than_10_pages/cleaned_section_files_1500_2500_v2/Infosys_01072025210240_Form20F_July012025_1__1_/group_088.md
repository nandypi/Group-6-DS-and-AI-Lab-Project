## 2.18 Income taxes

## Accounting policy

Income tax expense comprises current and deferred income tax. It is recognized in net profit in the consolidated statement of comprehensive income, except for amounts relating to items recognized directly in equity, which are recognized in equity or other comprehensive income.

Current income tax for current and prior periods is recognized at the amount expected to be paid to or recovered from tax authorities, using tax rates and tax laws enacted or substantively enacted by the balance sheet date.

Deferred income tax assets and liabilities are recognized for temporary differences between the tax bases of assets and liabilities and their carrying amounts in the financial statements, except when deferred income tax arises from the initial recognition of goodwill or an asset or liability in a transaction that is not a business combination and affects neither accounting nor taxable profit or loss at the time of the transaction.

Deferred tax assets are reviewed at each reporting date and reduced to the extent that it is no longer probable that the related tax benefit will be realized. Deferred income tax assets and liabilities are measured using tax rates and tax laws enacted or substantively enacted by the balance sheet date and expected to apply when the temporary differences are recovered or settled.

A deferred income tax asset is recognized to the extent that future taxable profit is probable and available against which deductible temporary differences and tax losses can be utilized. Deferred income taxes are not provided on undistributed earnings of subsidiaries and branches when the earnings are not expected to be distributed in the foreseeable future.

The Group offsets current tax assets and liabilities, and deferred tax assets and liabilities, when it has a legally enforceable right to set off the recognized amounts and intends either to settle on a net basis or to realize the asset and settle the liability simultaneously. Tax benefits from deductions earned on the exercise of employee share options in excess of compensation charged to income are credited to equity.

The applicable Indian corporate statutory tax rate was 25.17% for fiscal 2025 and 34.94% for fiscal 2024 and fiscal 2023.

Income tax expense for fiscal 2025, 2024, and 2023 includes provisions, net of reversals, of $16 million, reversals, net of provisions, of $113 million, and reversals, net of provisions, of $13 million, respectively. These prior-period reversals primarily resulted from the adjudication of disputed matters, filing of tax returns, and completion of assessments across various jurisdictions.

During the year ended March 31, 2025, the Company received orders under section 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, from the Income Tax Authorities in India for assessment years 2016–17 and 2019–20. The orders confirmed the Company’s position on the tax treatment of certain contentious matters. As a result:

- Pre-tax interest income of $38 million was recognized.
- Income tax provisions totaling $21 million were reversed, with a corresponding credit to the Statement of Profit and Loss.
- Contingent liabilities were reduced by $125 million.

During the year ended March 31, 2024, the Company received orders under sections 250 and 254 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, from the Income Tax Authorities in India for assessment years 2007–08 to 2015–16, 2017–18, and 2018–19. These orders confirmed the Company’s position on the tax treatment of certain contentious matters. As a result:

- Pre-tax interest income of $232 million, along with the corresponding tax impact, was recognized.
- Income tax provisions totaling $63 million were reversed, with a corresponding credit to the Statement of Profit and Loss.
- Contingent liabilities were reduced by $196 million.

Foreign tax expense relates to income taxes payable overseas, principally in the United States.

In India, the Company benefited in prior years from income tax incentives for exports of services from SEZ units registered under the Special Economic Zones Act, 2005. SEZ units that began providing services on or after April 1, 2005, were eligible for:

- A 100% income tax deduction on profits or gains from export services for the first five years beginning with the relevant assessment year.
- A 50% deduction for a further five years.
- An additional deduction of up to 50% for a further five years, subject to creating and using a Special Economic Zone Re-investment Reserve to acquire new plant and machinery for business purposes, as provided under the Income Tax Act, 1961.

These incentives reduced income tax expense by $363 million in fiscal 2024 and $362 million in fiscal 2023 compared with estimated tax without the incentives. The per-share effect, based on both basic and diluted weighted-average equity shares, was $0.09 in each of fiscal 2024 and 2023.

The Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019 introduced section 115BAA, under which a domestic company may elect a reduced corporate tax rate of 22%, plus a 10% surcharge and 4% cess, without claiming certain deductions, including the deduction for SEZ units under section 10AA of the Income Tax Act. For fiscal 2025, the Company’s income tax expense was recognized by applying section 115BAA without claiming the section 10AA deduction for SEZ units.

Deferred income tax for fiscal 2025, 2024, and 2023 substantially relates to the origination and reversal of temporary differences.

Infosys is subject to a 15% U.S. Branch Profit Tax to the extent that its U.S. branch’s net profit during the year exceeds the increase in the U.S. branch’s net assets during the year, calculated under the Internal Revenue Code. As of March 31, 2025:

- Infosys’ U.S. branch net assets were approximately $907 million.
- The Company had a deferred tax liability for branch profit tax of $31 million, net of credits, because it expected the branch profits to be distributed in the foreseeable future.

Deferred income tax liabilities were not recognized on temporary differences associated with investments in subsidiaries and branches totaling $1,941 million as of March 31, 2025, and $1,292 million as of March 31, 2024. The Company can control the timing of reversal of these temporary differences and considers it probable that they will not reverse in the foreseeable future. The Group generally intends to repatriate earnings from subsidiaries and branches only to the extent that they can be distributed in a tax-free manner.

Deferred income tax assets were not recognized on accumulated losses of $538 million as of March 31, 2025, and $560 million as of March 31, 2024, because future taxable profit was not considered probable to be available against which the unused tax losses could be utilized in the foreseeable future.
