## VI.    Risks related to our operations

- Maintaining a hybrid working model may continue to expose us to various risks.
- Our reputation could be at risk, and we may be liable to clients or regulators for damages caused by unauthorized disclosure of confidential information and sensitive data.
- Our reputation could be at risk, and we may be liable to clients for damages caused by cybersecurity incidents.
- Our reputation may be impacted, and we may incur financial liabilities if privacy breaches or incidents under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) or other data privacy regulations are attributed to us. This includes failures to report breaches to regulators and data subjects within stipulated timeframes or to respond to data subject requests promptly. Client claims for losses caused by privacy breaches involving our employees could also affect our finances and reputation.
- The markets in which we operate are subject to earthquakes, floods, tsunamis, storms, pandemics, and other disasters.
- The safety of our employees, assets, and infrastructure may be affected by untoward incidents beyond our control, impacting business continuity or reputation.
- Terrorist attacks or war could adversely affect our business, results of operations, and financial condition.
- Our reputation, access to capital, and longer-term financial stability could be at risk if we are unable to meet our stated goals under our Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) 2030 vision.

## VII.     Risks related to legislation and regulatory compliance

- New and changing regulatory compliance, corporate governance, and public disclosure requirements add uncertainty to our compliance efforts and increase compliance costs.
- The intellectual property (IP) laws of India may not provide software and related IP rights with protection equivalent to that available in the United States. We may be unsuccessful in protecting our IP rights and may also face third-party claims of IP infringement.

## VIII.    Risks related to the ADSs

- Fluctuations in the U.S. dollar to Indian rupee exchange rate may negatively affect the price of our ADSs and the U.S. dollar value of declared dividends.
- ADS holders may be restricted in their ability to exercise voting rights.
- ADS holders may be restricted in their ability to participate in a buyback of shares offered by us.
- Increases in the dividend distribution tax rate, introduction of new taxes on profit distributions, changes to the application of these taxes, or changes to buyback regulations could adversely affect shareholder returns.

## I.       Risks related to the markets in which we and our clients operate

Spending on technology products and services by our clients and prospective clients fluctuates based on economic, geopolitical, monetary, tariff, trade restriction, fiscal policy, and regulatory conditions in the markets where they operate.

Technology and IT budgets are frequently affected by economic slowdowns, tariff and trade restriction changes, geopolitical conflicts, and market uncertainty. Reductions in IT spending have adversely affected, and may continue to adversely affect, our results of operations.

New or changing regulations and increased government intervention, including sanctions, tariff changes, and trade restrictions, may adversely affect our clients' industries and reduce demand for our services. They may also cause us to incur additional process or personnel costs. Client compliance requirements, including industry-specific, privacy, and security standards for handling data, may affect how we provide services. A general slowdown in key customer geographies or supply-chain locations may also affect revenue and profitability.

Regulators have imposed guidelines for cloud computing services that mandate specific controls or require enterprises in sectors such as financial services, insurance, and aerospace to obtain regulatory approval before outsourcing certain functions. Regulators may also establish boundaries for emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence or ban their use. Evolving regulatory frameworks may affect the revenue and profitability of technology-sector clients investing in emerging technologies.

Reduced or delayed IT spending, including spending affected by tariff and trade restrictions, may lead clients to cancel projects, request pricing discounts, seek extended payment terms, or consolidate technology service providers. Such events have adversely affected our utilization rates, revenue earned per billed person month, proposal competitiveness, gross margins, and cash flows.

Macroeconomic uncertainty, tariff and trade restriction changes, and geopolitical conflicts may increase consumer and wage inflation, energy prices, interest rates, and currency fluctuations, affecting the revenue and profitability of some clients. Some clients shut down operations in Russia due to the conflict in Eastern Europe. Clients may consequently reduce discretionary spending or delay contract decisions, affecting our revenue. Clients seeking to optimize costs may prioritize cost takeout projects, requiring us to alter offerings, talent mobility, and marketing efforts to pursue these opportunities.

Geopolitical conflicts, including the conflict between India and Pakistan in May 2025, may affect our ability to serve global clients if operations cannot be moved out of conflict zones promptly or if relocation increases operating costs. Conflicts may also affect clients with businesses in the impacted geographies and lead them to prioritize investments to decouple from conflict zones, reducing discretionary technology-services spending and affecting our revenue and profitability.

The sudden emergence or resurgence of a pandemic in geographies where we, our clients, or most of our employees are located may affect revenue and profitability.

## An economic slowdown or other factors  may  affect  the  economic  health  of  the  United  States,  the  United Kingdom, the European Union, Australia or various industries from where our revenues are derived.

Our revenues are concentrated in a few geographies and client industry segments. In fiscal 2025, 57.9% of revenue was derived from projects in North America, 29.8% from Europe, and 9.2% from the rest of the world. Financial services and insurance accounted for 27.7% of fiscal 2025 revenue.

Instability and uneven global economic growth have adversely affected the IT industry in the past and may continue to do so. If the economies of the United States, United Kingdom, or the European Union weaken or experience uneven growth, clients may significantly reduce or postpone technology spending, lowering demand for our services and negatively affecting revenue and profitability. Factors affecting clients include supply-chain issues, labor shortages, inflation, political elections, and other macroeconomic conditions.

Future global economic uncertainty, including uncertainty arising from pandemics, tariff or trade restriction changes, and geopolitical conflicts affecting the financial services, retail, consumer goods, energy, and manufacturing industries, may result in reduced, postponed, or consolidated IT spending; contract terminations; project deferrals; or delayed purchases. These effects may lower demand, reduce utilization, affect service pricing, and adversely affect revenue and profitability.

We also depend on clients in the energy sector to generate revenue. Oil price volatility, including volatility caused by conflicts in Eastern Europe and the Middle East, may create economic pressure on clients or lead them to suspend or close operations in affected geographies, impacting our revenue and profitability.

## Our clients may operate in sectors that are adversely impacted by climate change which could consequently impact our business and reputation.

Clients in affected sectors may need to transition from climate-impacting solutions to climate-friendly solutions, creating transition or investment risks. Clients in mining and oil exploration may face significant financial stress from investments in climate-friendly solutions, which could impede our growth because of our exposure to these sectors. Societal pressure or adverse media publicity against companies using or implementing climate-impacting solutions could also affect our reputation if we are a significant service provider to those clients.

In geographies where customers have begun transitioning to sustainable solutions, regulatory or regime changes may cause them to abandon or temporarily pause those investments, potentially affecting growth and profitability in the related service lines.

## Changes in immigration laws, their interpretation or enforcement, or delays in getting visas in the jurisdictions in which we operate, could result in increased compliance costs, business disruptions, an inability to bid for or fulfill projects, enforcement actions or penalties, any of which could adversely affect our business, results of operations and financial condition

A portion of our employees in the United States, United Kingdom, European Union, and other countries and regions rely on work visas and permits. This makes our business vulnerable to changes in immigration laws, which are numerous, frequently changing, and sometimes conflicting.

Changes in legal requirements or their interpretation or enforcement may increase compliance costs, disrupt operations, impair our ability to serve clients, and lead to enforcement actions or penalties. Protectionist interpretations in certain jurisdictions have increased regulatory scrutiny and could cause us to lose access to visas.

Changes to immigration laws and regulations, adverse revisions, or significant visa delays caused by operational challenges have caused, and may continue to cause, project delays, staffing shortages, additional costs, and an inability to bid for or fulfill client projects. These effects could adversely affect our business, results of operations, and financial condition.
