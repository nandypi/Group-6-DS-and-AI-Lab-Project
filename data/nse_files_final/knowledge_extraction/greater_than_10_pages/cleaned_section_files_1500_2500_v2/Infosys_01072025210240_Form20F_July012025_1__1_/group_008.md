## Our  client  contracts  are  often  conditional  upon  our  performance,  which,  if  unsatisfactory  due  to  reasons whatsoever, could result in lower revenues than previously anticipated.

A number of client contracts include incentive-based or other pricing terms that condition some or all fees on meeting defined performance goals or service levels. Certain client situations may also require higher contractual liability exposure limits.

Failure to meet performance goals or client expectations, particularly where contracts depend on client factors that are not clearly articulated, may prevent Infosys from billing for effort already incurred. This could result in less profitable or unprofitable engagements and may lead to penalties or fines.

Clients may seek more favorable contract terms, particularly regarding limitations of liability for damages resulting from unsatisfactory service performance. Damages that cannot be recovered from insurers could adversely affect the business, revenues, and operating margins.

Infosys’s ability to meet contractual commitments may be affected by limited talent availability or geopolitical conflicts, potentially resulting in client dissatisfaction and lost revenue.

With most clients expecting engagements to be AI-driven, Infosys faces risks in contracts for AI-led projects, including confidentiality, data privacy, intellectual property ownership, and model reuse.

## Some of our long-term client contracts contain benchmarking provisions which, if triggered, could result in lower future revenues and profitability under the contract.

As the size and duration of client engagements increase, clients may require benchmarking provisions. These provisions may allow a client to request a benchmark study by an agreed third party comparing Infosys’s pricing, performance, and efficiency gains with those of an agreed list of service providers delivering comparable services in comparable geographies.

Depending on the results and the reasons for any unfavorable variance, Infosys may be required to reduce service prices or give the client a right to terminate services without paying a termination fee. This could adversely affect revenues and profitability.

Benchmarking provisions may have a greater impact during an economic slowdown because pricing pressure and declining rates may reduce fees charged to clients whose engagements include these provisions.

## Our work with governmental agencies may expose us to additional risks.

Infosys bids for work with governments and governmental agencies in key geographies where it operates. Government projects may involve the following risks:

- Reduction in scope or termination due to changes in government, pending elections, inadequate or absent funding, or disputes with other government departments or agencies.
- More onerous terms and conditions, including higher liability exposure for direct or indirect damages, extensive audit rights, punitive service-level penalties, and other restrictive covenants.
- Delayed payments or changes in contract terms due to political and economic factors or the lack of timely closure of requirements.
- Greater scrutiny and publicity, where negative publicity may adversely affect the business and reputation regardless of its accuracy.
- Stricter regulatory requirements that may increase compliance costs.
- Multiple parties involved in service delivery, requiring greater project-management efforts; failure in this regard may adversely affect performance.

Infosys operates in jurisdictions where local business practices may be inconsistent with international regulatory requirements, including the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) and the U.K. Bribery Act 2010. These regulations prohibit, among other things, giving or offering anything of value with the intent to influence the awarding of government contracts.

Although Infosys states that it has policies and enforcement mechanisms intended to support compliance, employees, subcontractors, agents, or partners may violate these requirements. Such violations could expose Infosys to criminal or civil enforcement actions, penalties, and suspension or disqualification from U.S. federal procurement contracting.

## Our inability to execute contracts and / or amendments with clients on a timely basis can impact our revenues and profits, causing fluctuations in our reported results.

Infosys generally enters into client contracts before beginning work but may occasionally start projects before contracts or amendments to previously executed contracts are completed. This may affect the ability to recognize revenue for effort incurred during a specified period and may affect margins if costs and revenue are accounted for in different periods.

In rare cases of contract abandonment, Infosys may be unable to recover costs incurred before contract execution.

## VI.   Risks related to our operations

### Maintaining a hybrid working model may continue to expose us to various risks.

In 2022, Infosys transitioned to a hybrid work model for employees and service providers after an extended period of remote work during the COVID-19 pandemic and continued with that model.

Risks associated with hiring, training, and deploying talent remotely include:

- Virtual hiring may make it more difficult to assess candidates effectively because of fake profiles, fraudulent mechanisms used to answer interview questions, limited personal interaction, or technical infrastructure issues. This may result in hiring unqualified or inappropriate candidates and cause service-delivery disruptions.
- Fraud risks in virtual hiring may delay background verification, affecting talent deployment, client satisfaction, and reputation.
- Difficulty training employees without sufficient physical training sessions and personal interaction may affect project deployment, business requirements, productivity, client sentiment, and costs.
- Employees working remotely for extended periods may be difficult to integrate into the existing corporate culture and may face weaker social connections, affecting engagement and retention of high-performing employees.
- A large remote workforce may increase exposure to cybersecurity and data privacy breaches, potentially disrupting services and operations and affecting revenue, profitability, and reputation.
- Some employees may work for multiple employers simultaneously without required prior approvals, creating potential conflicts of interest, confidentiality breaches, and reduced productivity.
- Long-term employee preferences for remote work, combined with client expectations to return to office-based work, may affect attrition, client satisfaction, and the ability to grow profitably.
