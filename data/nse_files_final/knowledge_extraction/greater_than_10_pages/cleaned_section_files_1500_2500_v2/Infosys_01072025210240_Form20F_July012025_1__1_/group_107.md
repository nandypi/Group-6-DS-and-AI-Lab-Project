# Provisions on Changes in Capital

Infosys’ authorized capital may be altered by an ordinary resolution of shareholders at a general meeting. Additional share issues are subject to shareholders’ pre-emptive rights.

By ordinary resolution at a general meeting, the company may also:

- Increase its share capital.
- Consolidate its share capital into shares with a larger face value.
- Subdivide its shares by reducing their par value.

# Takeover Code

Under the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011 (the “Takeover Code”):

- Upon acquiring shares or voting rights in a public listed Indian company such that the acquirer’s aggregate holding reaches 5% or more, the acquirer and persons acting in concert with the acquirer must disclose their aggregate shareholding and voting rights to the company and the relevant stock exchanges within two working days of the acquisition or receipt of intimation of allotment.
- A person, together with persons acting in concert, holding shares or voting rights representing 5% or more must disclose the number of shares or voting rights held and any change exceeding 2% of the company’s total shareholding or voting rights. The disclosure must be made to the company and the relevant stock exchanges within two working days of receiving intimation of allotment or completing the acquisition or disposal.
- In the case of a sale, disclosure is required even if the acquirer’s shareholding, together with persons acting in concert, falls below 5%.
- For financial year 2020–2021, a promoter was permitted to acquire more than 5% and up to 10% of the voting rights if the acquisition was pursuant to a preferential issue of equity shares by the target company.

Acquisition of shares or voting rights that entitles the acquirer and persons acting in concert to exercise 25% or more of the voting rights in, or control over, the target company triggers a requirement to make an open offer for at least 26% of the target company’s total shares. The offer price is determined under the Takeover Code, and the acquirer must make a public announcement on the date on which the acquisition of the shares or voting rights is agreed.

If an open offer reduces public shareholding below the limit specified in the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957 (the “SCRR”) read with the Listing Regulations, the acquirer must take steps permitted under SEBI circular SEBI/HO/CFD/PoD2/P/CIR/2023/18 dated February 3, 2023. This circular superseded SEBI circular SEBI/HO/CFD/CMD/CIR/P/43/2018 dated February 22, 2018. The steps must facilitate compliance with the public-shareholding threshold within the period prescribed in the SCRR.

Following amendments to the Takeover Code dated December 6, 2021, an acquirer may make an offer to delist the target company if the intention to do so is declared both when making the public announcement of an open offer and when making the detailed public statement. For an indirect acquisition that is not treated as a deemed direct acquisition under Regulation 5(2) of the Takeover Code, the intention to delist need initially be declared only in the detailed public statement.

If the target company is not delisted through the delisting offer, but the acquirer’s shareholding exceeds the maximum permissible non-public shareholding threshold, the acquirer may make a further delisting attempt under the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Delisting of Equity Shares) Regulations, 2009 during the 12-month period following completion of the open offer, provided the acquirer continues to exceed the maximum permissible non-public shareholding.

If the further delisting attempt also fails, the acquirer must ensure compliance with the target company’s minimum public-shareholding requirement under the SCRR within 12 months after the end of that period. If the acquirer’s shareholding exceeds the maximum permissible non-public shareholding following an open offer, the acquirer is not eligible to make a voluntary delisting offer under the Delisting Regulations unless 12 months have elapsed from completion of the offer period.

As Infosys is listed in India, the Takeover Code applies to Infosys and to any person acquiring its equity shares or voting rights, including through ADSs.

Infosys ADSs entitle ADS holders to exercise voting rights in respect of the deposited equity shares. Accordingly, ADS acquisitions are subject to the Takeover Code. An obligation to make an open offer for at least 26% of the company’s shares to existing shareholders would be triggered for an ADS holder and persons acting in concert if the shares underlying their ADSs represent 25% or more of the company’s shares or voting rights.

Under the Listing Regulations, Infosys has entered into listing agreements with each Indian stock exchange on which its equity shares are listed. Infosys must report to the stock exchanges disclosures made to the company under the Takeover Code.

Infosys’ Articles of Association do not contain provisions that would delay, defer, or prevent a change in control of the company and that would operate only with respect to a merger, acquisition, or corporate restructuring involving Infosys.
