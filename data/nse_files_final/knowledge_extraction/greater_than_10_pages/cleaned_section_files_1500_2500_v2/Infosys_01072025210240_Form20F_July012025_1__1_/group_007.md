## Currency fluctuations and changes in interest rates may affect the results of our operations and yield on cash balances.

Infosys’ functional currency is the Indian rupee, while a majority of its expenses are denominated in U.S. dollars and Indian rupees. A majority of revenues are generated in foreign currencies, including the U.S. dollar, Euro, Australian dollar, and United Kingdom pound sterling, through sales in the United States and elsewhere. Infosys also obtains products and services from overseas suppliers in various currencies.

Infosys expects that most revenues will continue to be generated in foreign currencies and that a significant portion of expenses—including personnel costs, capital expenditures, and operating expenditures—will continue to be denominated in U.S. dollars and Indian rupees. Exchange-rate changes could affect revenues, other income, cost of sales, gross margin, net income, business, results of operations, and financial condition. During fiscal 2025, every percentage-point depreciation or appreciation in the exchange rate between the Indian rupee and the U.S. dollar affected incremental operating margins by approximately 0.43%.

Infosys uses foreign-exchange forward and option contracts to mitigate foreign-currency exposure, but these instruments may not fully protect against exchange-rate risks. Significant or unexpected intra-quarter currency movements may cause losses, and foreign-exchange volatility may make effective hedging difficult. Changes in Reserve Bank of India (RBI) policies may also limit the company’s ability to hedge adequately. Increased capital-account convertibility, if introduced, could increase volatility between the rupee and foreign currencies.

A majority of Infosys’ investments are in India-based assets and are exposed to India’s interest-rate environment, which depends substantially on RBI monetary policy. Interest-rate cuts could reduce interest income and affect profitability. Macroeconomic issues and geopolitical conflicts may also cause adverse currency movements and reduce profitability.

## IV. Risks related to our employee workforce

## Our success depends largely upon our highly skilled technology professionals and our ability to hire, attract, motivate, retain and train these personnel.

Infosys’ ability to execute projects—particularly large, complex, and integrated programs—maintain client relationships, acquire new clients, bid for and obtain new deals, develop service offerings and software platforms, and expand its business depends on attracting, hiring, training, motivating, and retaining skilled technology professionals, project managers, and mid-level professionals. Failure to do so could impair these activities and reduce revenue.

The availability of science and technology professionals in certain current or potential operating geographies may be insufficient to meet demand or support workforce localization. Travel or immigration restrictions and higher wage costs could further limit the company’s ability to hire or deploy personnel.

Worldwide competition for skilled technology professionals, including increased hiring by technology companies in India, may affect hiring and retention. Workforce attrition may fluctuate in India and other locations.

Changing technology, industry needs, and demographics require talent with skills in areas such as creative design, data science, statistical analysis, artificial intelligence, and machine learning. These talent pools may differ from traditional recruitment sources and may be more expensive. Failure to hire, retain, and redeploy employees with changing skills could affect Infosys’ ability to bid for and obtain new deals.

Failure to integrate new employees into the existing corporate culture under the hybrid working model could affect operations. Infosys hires and trains fresh college graduates each year; delays or failures in training and deploying them on client projects could affect utilization, client satisfaction, and profitability.

Inadequate increases in employee compensation, or reductions in compensation or variable pay, could increase attrition and replacement hiring costs.

Failure to attract, engage, retain, train, and deploy rebadged employees could affect profitability and project deliverables. Rebadged employees and negotiated agreements, with or without work councils, may limit Infosys’ ability to redeploy employees or change employment contracts.

Attrition and mobility restrictions may increase reliance on subcontractors to deliver committed client services, which could affect profitability.

Infosys hires fresh college graduates and lateral employees to meet business needs and invests significant resources in training, particularly at the entry level, for client engagements. Competency and talent quality are important for service delivery. Despite training and enablement efforts, some associates may not meet required competencies or skills. Exits of such associates, even when conducted according to applicable law, may attract media attention and regulatory scrutiny and could affect the company’s brand and reputation.

## Our success depends in large part upon our management team and key personnel and our ability to attract and retain them.

Infosys is highly dependent on its Board of Directors, executive officers, and other senior executive leaders. Disruptions in their continued service may affect future performance and client relationships. Competition for senior management is intense, and Infosys may be unable to retain existing leaders or attract and retain new senior management personnel. Targeted hiring of Infosys leaders could also occur.

Infosys does not maintain key-person life insurance for senior management or other key personnel. The loss or extended unavailability of senior management or other key personnel due to health issues, security threats, or other business issues could adversely affect the business, results of operations, and financial condition.

## V. Risks related to our contractual obligations

## Our failure to complete fixed-price and fixed-timeframe contracts, or transaction-based pricing contracts, within budget and on time, may negatively affect our profitability.

In response to client requirements and IT-budget pressures, Infosys offers an increasing portion of its services on a fixed-price, fixed-timeframe basis rather than a time-and-materials basis. Revenue from fixed-price, fixed-timeframe projects represented approximately 54% of total revenue in fiscal 2025 and 53% in fiscal 2024.

Client IT-budget pressures have also led Infosys to deviate from standard pricing policies and offer varied pricing models. The company enters into transaction-based pricing contracts with some clients to provide flexibility to pay for services as they are used.

If project pricing is estimated incorrectly, Infosys may earn lower profits or incur losses because of difficulties completing projects within the expected timeframe and labor budget. Although the company uses software engineering methodologies, processes, and past project experience to reduce estimation, planning, and execution risks, it bears the risks of cost overruns, completion delays, and wage inflation.

Profitability may suffer if Infosys misjudges required resources or time, automation and productivity gains, the complexity of large and multi-party programs, future wage inflation, or currency exchange rates, or fails to meet contractual deadlines. Infosys expects to continue using fixed-price, fixed-timeframe, and transaction-based pricing arrangements, which may increase relative to time-and-materials engagements and increase associated business risks.

## Our client contracts can typically be terminated without cause, which could negatively impact our revenues and profitability.

Clients typically retain Infosys on a non-exclusive, project-by-project basis. Many client contracts, including fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts, may be terminated with or without cause subject to the agreed notice period.

Factors outside Infosys’ control that could lead to termination, project descoping, or client loss include:

- Financial difficulties, limited access to credit markets, higher debt-service or operating costs, insolvency or bankruptcy, macroeconomic conditions, tariffs or trade restrictions, and geopolitical conflicts.
- Changes in strategic priorities resulting in reduced technology spending.
- Demands for price reductions or unwillingness to accept higher prices because of higher wage or operating costs.
- Changes in outsourcing strategy, including moving work to in-house technology departments or competitors.
- Replacement of existing software with packaged software supported by licensors.
- Mergers and acquisitions.
- Consolidation of a client’s technology spending, whether arising from mergers and acquisitions or otherwise.
- Sudden project ramp-downs caused by economic uncertainty or a pandemic.

Infosys cannot control clients’ decisions regarding contract termination. Such decisions could adversely affect financial condition and results of operations. There had been no material project terminations due to economic uncertainty, changes in tariffs or trade restrictions, or geopolitical conflicts; however, prolonged uncertainty could increase the risk that clients invoke termination clauses to reduce expenditure, affecting anticipated growth and profitability.
