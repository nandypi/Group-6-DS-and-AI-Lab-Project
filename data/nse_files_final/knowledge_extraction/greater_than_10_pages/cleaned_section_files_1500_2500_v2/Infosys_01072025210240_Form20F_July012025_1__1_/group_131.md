## Persons Covered by the Policy

The Policy is binding and enforceable against all “Covered Executives,” meaning each individual who is or was ever designated as an “Executive Officer” by the Board, as defined in U.S. Exchange Act Rule 10D-1(d).

Each Covered Executive is required to sign and return an acknowledgement agreeing to be bound by and comply with the Policy. Failure to obtain such acknowledgement does not affect the Policy’s applicability or enforceability.

## Administration of the Policy

The Nomination and Remuneration Committee (the “Committee”) of the Board has delegated authority to administer the Policy. The Committee may interpret and construe the Policy and make all determinations necessary, appropriate, or advisable for its administration.

At the Board’s discretion, the Policy may instead be administered by the independent members of the Board or another Board committee consisting of independent members. In that case, references to the Committee are deemed to refer to those independent members or the other Board committee.

All Committee determinations are final and binding and receive the maximum deference permitted by law.

## Accounting Restatements Requiring Application of the Policy

If the Company is required to prepare an accounting restatement due to material noncompliance with a financial reporting requirement under securities laws, the Committee must determine any Excess Compensation that must be recovered.

An “Accounting Restatement” includes a required restatement to correct an error in previously issued financial statements that:

- Is material to the previously issued financial statements; or
- Would result in a material misstatement if corrected in, or left uncorrected for, the current period.

The Company’s obligation to recover Excess Compensation does not depend on whether or when restated financial statements are filed. Applicable legal requirements in all relevant jurisdictions must be considered.

## Compensation Covered by the Policy

The Policy applies to certain Incentive-Based Compensation “Received” on or after October 2, 2023 (the “Effective Date”), during the Covered Period, while the Company has a class of securities listed on a U.S. national securities exchange.

Incentive-Based Compensation is “Clawback Eligible Incentive-Based Compensation” if it is Received by a person after that person became an Executive Officer and the person served as an Executive Officer at any time during the applicable performance period.

“Excess Compensation” is the amount of Clawback Eligible Incentive-Based Compensation that exceeds the amount that would otherwise have been Received had the compensation been determined using the restated amounts. Excess Compensation is calculated without regard to taxes paid and is referred to in the listing standards as “erroneously awarded incentive-based compensation.”

For Incentive-Based Compensation based on stock price or total shareholder return, when the amount cannot be mathematically recalculated directly from the Accounting Restatement, the amount must be based on a reasonable estimate of the restatement’s effect on the relevant stock price or total shareholder return. The Company must maintain documentation supporting that estimate and provide it to the Exchange.

“Incentive-Based Compensation” means compensation granted, earned, or vested wholly or partly upon attainment of a Financial Reporting Measure. Compensation potentially subject to recovery under the Policy is not considered earned until the Company’s right to recover under the Policy has lapsed.

The following are not Incentive-Based Compensation under the Policy:

- Salaries.
- Bonuses paid solely at the discretion of the Nomination and Remuneration Committee or Board, unless paid from a bonus pool determined by satisfying a Financial Reporting Measure.
- Bonuses paid solely upon satisfying subjective standards and/or completing a specified employment period.
- Non-equity incentive plan awards earned solely upon satisfying strategic or operational measures.
- Equity awards whose grant is not contingent on achieving a Financial Reporting Measure and whose vesting depends solely on completing a specified employment period and/or attaining one or more non-Financial Reporting Measures, including time-based vesting equity awards.

“Financial Reporting Measures” are measures determined and presented in accordance with the accounting principles used to prepare the Company’s financial statements, and measures derived wholly or partly from those measures. Stock price and total shareholder return are also Financial Reporting Measures. A Financial Reporting Measure does not need to be presented in the financial statements or included in a filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.

Incentive-Based Compensation is “Received” during the Company’s fiscal period in which the specified Financial Reporting Measure is attained, even if payment, vesting, settlement, or grant occurs after that period. The Policy does not apply to Incentive-Based Compensation for which the Financial Reporting Measure was attained before the Effective Date.

“Covered Period” means the three completed fiscal years immediately preceding the Accounting Restatement Determination Date. It may also include certain transition periods resulting from a change in the Company’s fiscal year.

“Accounting Restatement Determination Date” means the earliest of:

- The date the Board, a Board committee, or an authorized Company officer, where Board action is not required, concludes or reasonably should have concluded that the Company is required to prepare an Accounting Restatement; or
- The date a court, regulator, or other legally authorized body directs the Company to prepare an Accounting Restatement.

## Repayment of Excess Compensation

The Company must recover Excess Compensation reasonably promptly, and Covered Executives must repay Excess Compensation to the Company.

Subject to applicable law, the Committee may require repayment by direct payment or through another method or combination of methods. These determinations do not need to be identical for each Covered Executive. Recovery methods may include:

- Requiring reimbursement of cash Incentive-Based Compensation previously paid.
- Seeking recovery of any gain realized from, or equity held following, the vesting, exercise, settlement, sale, transfer, or other disposition of equity-based awards, regardless of whether the awards are Incentive-Based Compensation.
- Offsetting the amount against unpaid or future compensation payable by the Company or an affiliate, including severance payments that might otherwise be due upon termination, regardless of whether those amounts are Incentive-Based Compensation.
- Cancelling outstanding vested or unvested equity awards, regardless of whether the awards are Incentive-Based Compensation.
- Taking any other remedial or recovery action permitted by law, as determined by the Committee.

Covered Executives must repay Excess Compensation regardless of whether they believe, legitimately or otherwise, that the compensation was previously earned under applicable law and is not subject to clawback.

The Company or any affiliate may take legal action to enforce a Covered Executive’s obligations or discipline the Covered Executive. Failure to comply with the Policy could result in:

- Termination for cause for failure to comply with a Company policy.
- Civil proceedings.
- Reporting misconduct to appropriate governmental authorities.
- Reduction of future compensation opportunities.
- A change in role.

These actions do not require Committee approval and may be taken by the Board, a Board committee, or a duly authorized officer of the Company or an applicable affiliate.

Disciplinary or employment-termination decisions are independent of determinations under the Policy. The no-fault and non-discretionary clawback requirement should not affect whether a termination is considered for cause, except where termination results from the Covered Executive’s failure to comply with the Policy.

## Limited Exceptions to the Policy

The Company must recover Excess Compensation unless the conditions below are met and the Committee determines that recovery would be impracticable:

- The direct expense of paying a third party to assist in enforcing the Policy would exceed the amount to be recovered. Before reaching this conclusion, the Company must make a reasonable attempt to recover the Excess Compensation, document the attempt or attempts, and provide the documentation to the Exchange.
- Recovery would violate an India law adopted before November 28, 2022. Before making this determination, the Company must obtain an opinion from India counsel acceptable to the Exchange stating that recovery would result in such a violation, and provide the opinion to the Exchange.
- Recovery would likely cause an otherwise tax-qualified retirement plan, under which benefits are broadly available to Company employees, to fail to meet applicable legal requirements.

## Other Important Information in the Policy

The Policy is in addition to the requirements of Section 304 of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002 applicable to the Company’s Chief Executive Officer and Chief Financial Officer, as well as other applicable laws, regulatory requirements, rules, and existing Company policies or agreements providing for compensation recovery.

Regardless of the Company’s organizational documents, corporate policies, or contracts, including indemnification agreements:

- Neither the Company nor any affiliate will indemnify or provide an advance to a Covered Executive for any loss of Excess Compensation.
- Neither the Company nor any affiliate will pay or reimburse insurance premiums for insurance covering potential recovery obligations.

If the Company must recover Excess Compensation from a Covered Executive who is no longer an employee, it may seek recovery to comply with applicable law, regardless of any release of claims or separation agreement signed by that individual.

The Committee or Board may review and modify the Policy from time to time.

If any Policy provision, or its application to a Covered Executive, is adjudicated to be invalid, illegal, or unenforceable, that determination does not affect the remaining provisions or their application to another Covered Executive. The affected provision will be deemed amended to the minimum extent necessary to make it enforceable.

The Policy terminates and is no longer enforceable when the Company ceases to be a listed issuer within the meaning of Section 10D of the Exchange Act.

## Limitation, Review and Amendment

If the Policy conflicts with applicable law, applicable law prevails. Any subsequent amendment or modification to applicable law automatically applies to the Policy.

The Board may review and amend the Policy from time to time as deemed necessary.
