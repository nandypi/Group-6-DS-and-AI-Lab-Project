## Our engagements with clients are typically singular in nature and do not necessarily provide for subsequent engagements.

Our clients generally retain us on a short-term, engagement-by-engagement basis for specific projects rather than on a recurring basis under long-term contracts. Although a substantial majority of our revenues are generated from repeat business—defined as revenues from a client who also contributed to our revenues during the prior fiscal year—our engagements are typically for projects that are singular in nature.

Infosys must seek new engagements when current engagements are completed or terminated, expand business with existing clients, and secure new clients. Expanding the sales and marketing group could increase expenses without necessarily resulting in a substantial increase in business. If Infosys cannot continually generate a substantial number of new project engagements, its business and results of operations could be adversely affected.

Macroeconomic uncertainties, changes in tariffs, trade restrictions, and geopolitical conflicts may cause clients to reevaluate spending and decide not to extend or renew their business with Infosys.

## II.   Risks related to the investments we make for our growth

## Our business will suffer if we fail to anticipate and develop new services and enhance existing services in order to keep pace with rapid changes in technology and in the industries on which we focus.

The technology services market is characterized by rapid technological change, evolving industry standards, changing client preferences, and new product and service introductions. Infosys’s future success depends on anticipating these advances and developing new offerings to meet client needs. Services or technologies developed by Infosys may not be successful in the marketplace, and competitors may develop more attractive or competitively priced offerings that render Infosys services noncompetitive or obsolete.

Infosys continues to introduce new solutions involving generative AI, agentic AI, cloud, AI-based automation, robotic process automation, blockchain, IoT, autonomous vehicles, and other technologies. Failure to identify technological changes or disruptions, develop services around them, take them to global customers ahead of competitors, or enhance delivery capabilities could affect competitive positioning, market share, and revenues.

Developing these services and technologies may require significant upfront investments. If they fail or cannot be leveraged for their intended purposes, Infosys may be unable to recoup some or all of those investments. Failure to retire unsuccessful new services in a timely manner may result in additional losses.

Evolving regulatory frameworks in the technology sector may affect new service offerings and require additional investments to align offerings with regulatory standards. Macroeconomic conditions, tariffs, trade restrictions, geopolitical uncertainties, and economic downturns may also reduce expenditure on ideating, incubating, developing, and marketing new services, affecting long-term growth prospects.

## Our use of AI technologies may not be successful and may present business, financial, legal, and reputational risks.

AI, including generative AI and agentic AI, is increasingly used in Infosys’s client offerings and internal operations. Infosys has incurred and expects to continue incurring significant costs to develop and operate AI capabilities, including costs related to compliance with evolving AI and automation laws and regulations. Failure to develop and implement effective AI solutions or compete efficiently in the AI market could prevent Infosys from recouping these investments and negatively affect its financial performance, competitive standing, business, and reputation.

AI technologies are complex and rapidly evolving. Infosys faces significant competition, including from clients that may develop their own AI capabilities, which could reduce demand for Infosys services. Automation may also replace some professional services currently performed by Infosys employees, potentially adversely affecting the business, results of operations, and financial condition.

During the fiscal year, Infosys built its first set of small language models for banking, IT operations, and cybersecurity, providing a foundation for implementing and scaling enterprise AI. As more services incorporate AI, Infosys may need additional investment in initiatives such as Responsible AI to create safeguards for handling company and customer data.

AI may generate content that appears correct but is inaccurate or flawed. Reliance by Infosys, its customers, or others on such content could result in brand or reputational harm, competitive harm, or legal liability. AI tools may also use content subject to third-party restrictions, creating potential liability related to intellectual property infringement, cybersecurity breaches, and data protection violations.

Ineffective or inadequate AI development or deployment practices by Infosys, its clients, or third parties could result in employees relying on inaccurate outputs, unauthorized disclosure of sensitive information, deliberate misuse, or infringement of third-party intellectual property rights. These circumstances could harm Infosys’s reputation and expose it to legal liability or regulatory risk.

Evolving AI laws and regulations, including the EU AI Act and laws in various jurisdictions that may conflict with one another, could affect AI integration and create compliance challenges. Actual or perceived non-compliance may lead to fines, investigations, lawsuits, remediation costs, and reputational damage. Integrating AI into products or using AI applications may also increase regulatory scrutiny and litigation, intellectual property, confidentiality, privacy, security, and ethical risks.

## We may be unable to recoup investment costs incurred in developing our software products and platforms.

Developing software products and platforms requires significant investment, and the markets for these offerings are competitive. Current or newly developed products and platforms may not be commercially successful, and development costs may not be recouped. Because software product and platform revenues typically occur after development costs are incurred, delayed revenues may cause periodic fluctuations in results of operations.

## We may engage in acquisitions, strategic investments, strategic partnerships or alliances or other ventures that may or may not be successful.

Infosys has previously sought, and may in the future seek, to acquire or make strategic investments in complementary businesses, new and emerging technologies, services, or products, or enter into strategic partnerships or alliances with third parties to enhance its business.

Infosys may be unable to identify suitable acquisition targets, strategic investments, or partnerships, or may be unable to complete transactions on commercially acceptable terms. This could affect competitiveness and growth prospects. Even when a desired acquisition is identified, the transaction may not be completed on commercially reasonable terms, or the target may be acquired by another company.

Future acquisitions could result in Infosys:

- Issuing equity securities that dilute current shareholders’ percentage ownership.
- Incurring substantial debt.
- Incurring significant acquisition-related expenses.
- Assuming contingent liabilities.
- Expending significant cash.

These financing activities or expenditures could harm the business, results of operations, financial condition, or the price of Infosys equity shares and ADSs. Difficulties in the capital and credit markets may also prevent Infosys from securing capital on acceptable terms, if at all, to complete acquisitions.

Expected acquisition synergies may not materialize because of inadequate business alignment or because assumptions made at the time of acquisition do not hold due to internal or external factors. Even if acquisitions increase sales and earnings, benefits may be delayed relative to the expenses incurred.

Acquisitions may create difficulties in assimilating personnel, operations, products, services, solutions, technology, and software. Key personnel of acquired companies may decide not to work for Infosys. These difficulties could disrupt ongoing business, distract management and employees, and increase expenses.

Infosys has made, and may in the future make, strategic investments in early-stage technology start-ups to gain experience in or exploit niche technologies. These investments may not generate expected returns if economic uncertainty, tariff changes, trade restrictions, or geopolitical conflicts adversely affect the demand, supply, and cash flows of the companies. Lack of profitability in investments could adversely affect results of operations.

Infosys has entered into alliances with many technology companies to enhance services offered to clients. Most alliances are non-exclusive, and alliance partners may enter into agreements with competitors or compete against Infosys. Failure to enter into alliances with new partners could affect the ability to enhance service offerings. Reduced demand for alliance partners’ services or products may prevent Infosys from obtaining expected benefits and could affect revenue growth.
