# Foreign Portfolio Investment Regulations

A single FPI, including its investor group, may purchase equity shares of an Indian company only below 10% of the company’s paid-up equity capital on a fully diluted basis.

An investment by a person resident outside India in a listed Indian company is categorized as foreign portfolio investment if it is less than 10% of the post-issue paid-up equity share capital on a fully diluted basis, or less than 10% of the paid-up value of each series of equity instruments of the listed Indian company. This category is distinct from investment by an entity registered with SEBI as an FPI. All investments by a SEBI-registered FPI are foreign portfolio investments, but investments by entities not registered as FPIs with SEBI may also qualify as foreign portfolio investments if they remain within the prescribed limits.

Subject to applicable Indian laws, regulations, guidelines, approvals, and Regulation 21 of the FPI Regulations, an FPI may issue, subscribe to, or otherwise deal in offshore derivative instruments directly or indirectly only when:

1. The instruments are issued by persons registered as Category I FPIs.
2. The instruments are issued only to persons eligible for registration as Category I FPIs.
3. The instruments are issued after compliance with know-your-client requirements.
4. Other conditions prescribed by SEBI are satisfied.

An offshore derivative instrument is an instrument, regardless of its name, issued overseas by an FPI against securities held by it in India as the underlying securities. An FPI issuing offshore derivative instruments must ensure that any transfer is made only to persons meeting the applicable requirements. Prior consent of the issuing FPI is required for a transfer unless the transferees have been pre-approved by the FPI. Category II FPIs, and persons that would be categorized as Category II FPIs, cannot deal in offshore derivative instruments.

## Permitted FPI Transactions

The Non-Debt Rules permit an FPI to purchase equity instruments of a listed Indian company on a recognized stock exchange in India, through a public offer, or through a private placement, subject to applicable individual and aggregate limits and conditions.

FPI securities transactions must generally be conducted through stockbrokers registered with SEBI. Exceptions under the FPI Regulations include:

- Transactions in government securities and other securities under the purview of the RBI, carried out as specified by the RBI.
- Sales in response to an acquirer’s letter of offer under the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011.
- Sales in response to an offer by a promoter or acquirer under the erstwhile SEBI (Delisting of Equity Shares) Regulations, 2009.
- Sales under the SEBI (Buy-back of Securities) Regulations, 2018.
- Divestment in response to an offer by Indian companies under the operative guidelines for disinvestment of shares by Indian companies in overseas markets through American Depository Receipts or Global Depository Receipts.
- Bids for, or acquisitions of, securities in response to an offer for disinvestment of shares by the Central Government or a State Government.
- Transactions under an agreement with a merchant banker for market making or subscribing to an unsubscribed portion of an issue under Chapter IX of the SEBI (ICDR) Regulations, 2018.
- Transactions in corporate bonds.
- Transactions on the electronic book provider platform of a recognized stock exchange.
- Transactions to receive and hold unlisted securities referred to in Regulation 20(2) of the FPI Regulations.
- Transactions in unlisted securities received through involuntary corporate actions, including a merger or demerger scheme approved under the Companies Act, 2013 and applicable SEBI guidelines.
- Transactions in unlisted securities received through implementation of a resolution plan approved under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, or under guidelines issued by the Government of India, RBI, or another regulator for a debt-resolution scheme. Such unlisted holdings are treated as FDI.
- Transactions for the transfer of rights entitlements.
- Purchases of illiquid, suspended, or delisted securities.
- Transactions with other registered FPIs that have a multi-investment-manager structure, the same beneficial owner, and a common Permanent Account Number.
- Other transactions specified by SEBI.

## FPI Holding Limits and Reclassification

Under the Non-Debt Rules, the total holding of each FPI or investor group must remain below:

- 10% of the total paid-up equity capital of an Indian company on a fully diluted basis; or
- 10% of the paid-up value of each series of debentures, preference shares, or share warrants issued by an Indian company.

An FPI that breaches the 10% limit may divest its holdings within five trading days from the settlement date of the trades causing the breach. If the FPI does not divest:

- Its entire investment in the company, together with the investment of its investor group, is treated as FDI.
- The FPI and its investor group may not make further portfolio investments in the company.

Divestment and reclassification from FPI to FDI remain subject to any additional conditions specified by SEBI and the RBI.

## Aggregate Foreign Portfolio Investment Limits

Effective April 1, 2020, the aggregate limit for FPI investment is the sectoral cap applicable to the Indian company under the FDI route.

Before March 31, 2020, a company could set a lower aggregate limit of 24%, 49%, or 74%, as applicable, with approval from its board of directors and shareholders through a special resolution. A company that had reduced its limit could subsequently increase it to 49%, 74%, the applicable sectoral cap, or the statutory ceiling, as applicable, with the same approvals. Once increased to a higher threshold, the aggregate limit could not be reduced to a lower threshold. For a sector in which FDI is prohibited, the aggregate limit for an Indian company is 24%.

Aggregate foreign portfolio investment of up to 49% of paid-up capital on a fully diluted basis, or the applicable sectoral or statutory cap, whichever is lower, is not subject to government approval or sectoral conditions when the investment does not result in:

- Transfer of ownership and control of the resident Indian company from resident Indian citizens; or
- Transfer of ownership or control to persons resident outside India.

Other investments by persons resident outside India remain subject to government approval and sectoral conditions under the Non-Debt Rules.

## Income-Tax Provisions

Section 9 of the Income Tax Act was amended by the Finance Act, 2017 to clarify that Explanation 5 does not apply to an asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in an FII referred to in clause (a) of the explanation to Section 115AD.

The amendment applies retrospectively from April 1, 2012, for assessment year 2012–13 and subsequent assessment years, and applies to assessment years beginning on or after April 1, 2012 but before April 1, 2015.

Section 9 was further amended to clarify that Explanation 5 does not apply to an asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in a Category I or Category II FPI under the SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2014. This amendment applies retrospectively from April 1, 2015, for assessment year 2015–16 and subsequent assessment years.

The Finance Act 2020, dated March 27, 2020, further modified the proviso to Explanation 5. Effective April 1, 2020, and applicable for assessment year 2020–21 and subsequent assessment years, Explanation 5 does not apply to:

- An asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in an FII referred to in clause (a) of the explanation to Section 115AD for an assessment year beginning on or after April 1, 2012 but before April 1, 2015.
- An asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in a Category I or Category II FPI under the SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2014, before their repeal.
- An asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in a Category I FPI under the SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2019.
