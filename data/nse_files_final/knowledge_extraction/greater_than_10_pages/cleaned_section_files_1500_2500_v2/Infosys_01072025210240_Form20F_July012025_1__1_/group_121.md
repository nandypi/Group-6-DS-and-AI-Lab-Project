## SUSPECTED LEAK OF UNPUBLISHED PRICE SENSITIVEINFORMATION

### Preamble

This Policy establishes a structured procedure for investigating an actual or suspected leak of unpublished price-sensitive information (UPSI).

### Applicability

The Policy applies to all insiders and any other persons covered by applicable law.

### Process of inquiry in case of leak of UPSI or suspected leak of UPSI

1. Information, whether written, oral, or electronic, regarding an actual or suspected UPSI leak may be received from:

   - **Internal sources:**
     - Whistleblowers through the Infosys Whistleblower Policy process.
     - Internal controls implemented by the Company.

   - **External sources:** Any entity, including a registrar and share transfer agent, depository, stock exchange, Regional Director, Registrar of Companies, regulatory or statutory authority, or any department of the Central or State Government. The information may be based on a whistleblower complaint or otherwise.

   Such information is collectively referred to as a “Complaint” under the Policy.

2. The Chief Legal Officer and Chief Compliance Officer (“CLO”) or the Company Secretary must report the Complaint to the Audit Committee within a reasonable time after receiving it.

   The Audit Committee reviews the Complaint and discusses potential next steps with the CLO and Company Secretary. These steps may include:

   - Seeking additional information before deciding whether to investigate.
   - Considering disclosure requirements to regulatory authorities.
   - Appointing an investigation panel consisting of internal employees or external agencies.

   If the Complaint implicates the CLO or Company Secretary, the relevant person must recuse themselves from the inquiry.

3. If the Audit Committee mandates an investigation, the appointed investigators conduct the investigation and present their findings to the CLO. The CLO reports the investigation’s executive summary to the Audit Committee.

4. Based on the CLO’s update, the Audit Committee makes a recommendation to the Board. After review and deliberation, the Board decides the next steps.

5. The Board may amend any provision of the Policy, substitute provisions, or replace the Policy entirely with a new policy.

6. Words and expressions not defined in the Policy but defined in the Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992; the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956; the Depositories Act, 1996; the Companies Act, 2013; or rules and regulations made under those laws have the meanings assigned to them in the applicable legislation.

## ILLUSTRATIVE LIST OF LEGITIMATE PURPOSE

Sharing of UPSI is considered to be for a legitimate purpose in the following cases:

1. Proceedings before, or orders of, courts or tribunals, including the National Company Law Tribunal, National Company Law Appellate Tribunal, quasi-judicial authorities, other appellate tribunals, and arbitration proceedings.

2. Investigations, inquiries, or reviews, whether internal or external, or requests for information from statutory or governmental authorities or other administrative bodies recognized by law. Examples include requests from the Ministry of Corporate Affairs, Income Tax authorities, SEBI, stock exchanges, the Reserve Bank of India, and sectoral regulatory bodies.

3. Compliance with applicable laws, regulations, rules, and requirements, including company law, securities law, income tax law, and banking law.

4. Fulfilment of contractual obligations under any contract, agreement, arrangement, settlement, understanding, or undertaking.

5. Sharing information with intermediaries and fiduciaries, including auditors, merchant bankers, management consultants, partners, collaborators, and other advisers or consultants.

6. Obtaining legal, financial, or other professional advice, including advice for accounting, audits, or preparation of a defence for litigation or dispute resolution.

7. Transactions that would require an open offer under the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011, where the Board considers sharing the information to be in the best interests of the Company.

8. Transactions that do not require an open offer under the Takeover Regulations, where the Board considers sharing the information to be in the best interests of the Company. These transactions may include acquisitions, mergers, amalgamations, and other corporate restructurings. Sharing may also involve legal advice, due diligence on target or merging companies, and commercial advice on transaction structuring or valuation.

   Information constituting UPSI must be made generally available at least two trading days before the proposed transaction is effected, in a form adequate and fair to cover all relevant and material facts.

9. Sharing financial information for preparation of the holding company’s consolidated financial statements.

10. Sharing information with statutory auditors, secretarial auditors, internal auditors, or cost auditors in the course of their duties, or while obtaining any required certificate, comfort, or confirmation, including for placing a transaction before the Board for approval.

11. Activities undertaken by the Company in furtherance of its objects as listed in its memorandum of association.

## Infosys CODE OF CONDUCT

Infosys’ Code of Conduct sets out its core values, shared responsibilities, global commitments, and promises. The Code provides guidance on ethical conduct and states that employees are responsible for their own actions and should use judgment when the Code does not address a specific situation.

Employees may be required to confirm in writing that they have reviewed, understood, and agreed to follow the Code’s core values, shared responsibilities, global commitments, and promises.

The Code encourages employees to raise concerns about potential violations through the Office of Integrity and Compliance or the company’s reporting channels. It states that concerns will be reviewed and that employees will be protected against retaliation.

Infosys identifies its values through the acronym **C-LIFE**:

- **Client Value:** To surpass client expectations consistently.
- **Leadership by Example:** To act on the conviction that ordinary people can be inspired and mentored to do extraordinary things.
- **Integrity and Transparency:** To be ethical, sincere, and open in all transactions.
- **Fairness:** To be objective, empathetic, and caring in transactions.
- **Excellence:** To strive relentlessly, constantly learn, and improve employees, teams, and services.

### THE CEO &amp; MD

The CEO and Managing Director, Salil S. Parekh, states that employees are responsible for upholding Infosys’ values and maintaining and enhancing its culture. The Code emphasizes integrity and transparency in transactions and fairness in addressing stakeholder challenges.

### VALUES: C-LIFE

The C-LIFE values are described as the ethical foundation for the Company’s daily operations and workplace conduct.

Infosys also applies the principle of trusteeship. Employees are described as trustees of the Company’s resources, assets, and opportunities, with an obligation to pass on a stronger Infosys and meet or exceed commitments to stakeholders.

Trusteeship also includes using natural resources sustainably and improving the communities in which Infosys operates. The Company describes its CSR agenda and social stewardship as commitments to creating a positive impact in those communities.
