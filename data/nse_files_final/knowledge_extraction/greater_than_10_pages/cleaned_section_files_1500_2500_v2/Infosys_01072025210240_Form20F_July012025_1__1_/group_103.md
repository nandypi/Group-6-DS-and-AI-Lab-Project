## Consolidation and Subdivision of Shares

The Indian Companies Act permits a company to split or combine the par value of its shares at a general meeting, if authorized by its articles of association and approved by its shareholders. Shareholders on record on a fixed record date are entitled to receive the split or combination.

## Pre-emptive Rights and Issue of Additional Shares

The Indian Companies Act gives shareholders the right to subscribe to new shares in proportion to their existing shareholdings when a company makes a further issue of shares, unless otherwise determined by a special resolution of the shareholders.

For a pre-emptive issuance, the company must first offer the new shares to shareholders on a fixed record date. The offer must include:

- The right of shareholders on record to renounce the shares offered in favor of another person.
- The number of shares offered and the offer period, which may not be less than 15 days, or such lesser period as may be prescribed, and not more than 30 days from the date of the offer.

If the offer is not accepted, it is deemed to have been declined. The Board may then distribute the unsubscribed shares in a manner that is not disadvantageous to the shareholders or the company.

## Meetings of Shareholders

The company must convene an annual general meeting (AGM) each year within 15 months of the previous AGM or within six months of the end of the previous fiscal year, whichever is earlier. The Registrar of Companies may grant a three-month extension in certain circumstances.

The AGM is generally convened by the Company Secretary pursuant to a Board resolution. Ordinary business at the AGM includes:

- Consideration of the financial statements and the reports of the Board and auditors.
- Declaration of any dividend.
- Appointment of directors in place of those retiring.
- Appointment of auditors and fixing of their remuneration.

All other business is treated as special business.

The Board may convene an extraordinary general meeting (EGM) when necessary or at the request of shareholders holding at least 10% of the paid-up capital carrying voting rights. The EGM must be held at a place within India. The AGM must be held at the registered office or another place within the city where the registered office is located.

Written notice setting out the agenda of a general meeting must be given at least 21 days before the meeting, excluding the days of mailing and the meeting date.

The Indian Companies Act permits certain business to be transacted by postal ballot together with electronic voting. Matters required to be voted on only by postal ballot include:

- Alteration of the objects clause of the memorandum and articles of association.
- Alteration of the articles of association to convert the company into a private company.
- Change in the registered office outside the local limits of a city, town, or village.
- Change in the objects for which money was raised from the public through a prospectus when any amount remains unutilized.
- Issue of shares with differential voting, dividend, or other rights under Section 43(a)(ii) of the Indian Companies Act.
- Variation in the rights attached to a class of shares, debentures, or other securities under Section 48 of the Indian Companies Act.
- Buy-back of shares.
- Election of a director under Section 151 of the Indian Companies Act.
- Sale of the whole or substantially the whole of an undertaking under Section 180(1)(a) of the Indian Companies Act.
- Loans, guarantees, or security exceeding the limit specified under Section 186(3) of the Indian Companies Act.

## Voting Rights

The company provides remote e-voting for shareholder resolutions at general meetings, and voting by ballot is also available at the AGM. Shareholders who have not already voted through remote e-voting may vote at the AGM. The Chairperson has a casting vote in the event of a tie.

A shareholder entitled to attend and vote may appoint a proxy. The proxy instrument must be delivered to the company at least 48 hours before the meeting. Unless the Articles of Association provide otherwise, a proxy may vote only on a poll.

A corporate shareholder may appoint an authorized representative to vote through remote e-voting or at the general meeting. An authorized representative may also appoint a proxy.

Under the SEBI Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements Regulations, 2015, as amended in 2018, remote e-voting must be provided to all shareholders for shareholder resolutions in accordance with the Indian Companies Act.

Ordinary resolutions may be passed by a simple majority at a general meeting for which the required notice has been given. Special resolutions, including amendments to the Articles of Association, waiver of pre-emptive rights for issuing new shares, and reduction of share capital, require votes in favor to be at least three times the votes against.

Companies that must provide electronic voting may pass these resolutions through electronic voting instead of postal ballot. Companies with equity shares listed on a recognized stock exchange and at least 1,000 members are required to provide electronic voting. The company's equity shares are listed on a recognized stock exchange, and the company has more than 1,000 members.

Two-thirds of the non-independent directors on the Board must retire by rotation each year. One-third of the non-independent directors whose term of office is longest must retire by rotation. Retiring directors are eligible for reappointment.

Executive directors are appointed by shareholders for a maximum term of five years and are eligible for reappointment after completing their term. An independent director may hold office for up to two consecutive five-year terms and is eligible for reappointment after three years from ceasing to be an independent director, provided the director is not appointed by or associated with the company in any capacity during that period.

Cumulative voting for the election of directors is not recognized under applicable Indian law. The number of shares held determines the number of votes available. A shareholder may cast all or part of those votes for or against each resolution.

## Register of Shareholders; Record Dates; Transfer of Shares

The company maintains its shareholder register electronically through National Securities Depository Limited and Central Depository Services (India) Limited.

To determine shareholders entitled to rights such as dividends or rights issues, the company may close the shareholder register for a specified period of up to 30 days at one time. The date on which the closure begins is the record date. The Indian Companies Act requires at least seven days' prior public notice before closure, unless SEBI specifies a shorter period. The register may not be closed for more than 30 consecutive days at one time or more than 45 days in a year. The Listing Regulations require at least 30 days between two record dates.

Following the Depositories Act, 1996, and the repeal of Section 22A of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956, shares of a public company are generally freely transferable, subject to Section 58 of the Indian Companies Act and Regulation 40 of the Listing Regulations.

Under Section 58(2) of the Indian Companies Act, securities and other interests of a member are freely transferable, except where a contract or arrangement between two or more persons concerning the transfer of securities may be enforced as a contract. If the Board refuses to register a transfer, the shareholder may appeal to the National Company Law Tribunal.

Effective April 1, 2019, requests to transfer securities generally cannot be processed unless the securities are held in dematerialized form with a depository. Transmission or transposition of securities held in physical or dematerialized form can be effected only in dematerialized form.

If a transfer contravenes the Indian Companies Act, the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956, the Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992, related regulations, or other Indian laws, the National Company Law Tribunal may direct the company or depository to correct the contravention and rectify the relevant registers and records.

The registrar and share transfer agent for the company's equity shares is KFin Technologies Limited, Hyderabad, India.

Under a SEBI circular dated January 25, 2022, listed companies must issue securities only in dematerialized form when processing the following service requests:

- Issue of duplicate securities certificates.
- Claims from an unclaimed suspense account.
- Renewal or exchange of securities certificates.
- Endorsement.
- Subdivision or splitting of securities certificates.
- Consolidation of securities certificates or folios.
- Transmission.
- Transposition.
