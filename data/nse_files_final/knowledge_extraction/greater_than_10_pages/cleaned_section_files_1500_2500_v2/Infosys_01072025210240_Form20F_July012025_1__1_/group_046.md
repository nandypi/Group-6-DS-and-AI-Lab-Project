## Register of Shareholders, Record Dates, Transfer of Shares

We maintain a register of shareholders in electronic form through National Securities Depository Limited and Central Depository Services (India) Limited.

To determine which shareholders are entitled to specified rights, such as a dividend or rights issue, we may close the register of shareholders for a specified period not exceeding 30 days at one time. The date on which this period begins is the record date. The Listing Regulations require at least three working days’ prior public notice, excluding the date of intimation and the record date, before such closure. The register may not be closed for more than 30 consecutive days at one time or more than 45 days in a year. There must also be a gap of at least five working days between two record dates.

Following the introduction of the Depositories Act, 1996, and the repeal of Section 22A of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956, the equity shares of a public company are freely transferable, subject to Section 58 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013, and Regulation 40 of the Listing Regulations.

Under Section 58(2) of the Indian Companies Act, 2013, securities or other interests of a member are freely transferable, except where a contract or arrangement between two or more persons relating to the transfer of securities may be enforced as a contract. If the Board refuses to register a transfer of shares, the shareholder seeking to transfer the shares may appeal to the National Company Law Tribunal.

Under Section 59(4) of the Indian Companies Act, 2013, if a share transfer contravenes the Indian Companies Act, 2013, the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956, the Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992, regulations issued under those laws, or any other Indian law, the National Company Law Tribunal may, on application by the relevant company, a depository, a depository participant, the holder of securities, or SEBI, direct the company or depository to remedy the contravention and rectify the registers and records of members or beneficial owners.

SEBI clarified through its amendment notification dated June 8, 2018, and additional clarification dated May 27, 2019, that, effective April 1, 2019, requests to transfer securities would not be processed unless the securities were held in dematerialized form, except for transmission or transposition. A SEBI notification dated January 24, 2022, removed this exception. Transmission or transposition of securities held in physical or dematerialized form can therefore be effected only in dematerialized form.

Under the SEBI Master Circular for Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents dated May 7, 2024, which replaced and rescinded the SEBI circular dated January 25, 2022, listed companies must issue securities only in dematerialized form when processing the following service requests:

- Issue of duplicate securities certificates
- Claims from an unclaimed suspense account
- Renewal or exchange of securities certificates
- Endorsement
- Subdivision or splitting of securities certificates
- Consolidation of securities certificates or folios
- Transmission
- Transposition
