## Trading in Company Shares

### How is this relevant to me?

In the course of everyday work, employees, consultants, and vendors may have access to unpublished price-sensitive information (UPSI). Under applicable securities laws, a person who has UPSI may not trade in Company securities or disclose that information to anyone who may trade. Insider trading may also result in private lawsuits.

Employees must not disclose UPSI to anyone outside the Company, including family and friends. They must not trade in Infosys securities, or in the securities of any company involved with Infosys, while in possession of UPSI.

The Company imposes a quarterly trading blackout period. During this period, designated persons under the Infosys Internal Code of Conduct of Prohibition of Insider Trading may not trade in Company securities.

## Prohibition Against Short Selling of Company Securities

No Company director, officer, employee, or third-party agent may, directly or indirectly, sell any equity security, including derivatives, of the Company if he or she does not own the security sold.

## Export Control and Import Regulations

Many countries maintain controls on where items or data may be exported. Under these laws, an export occurs when a product, service, software, or technical knowledge is transferred to another country or to a foreign citizen or representative of another country, regardless of where that person is located.

An export can also occur when a foreign citizen is given access to controlled information or software through a network, visual inspection, or telephone conversation. Infosys may be exposed to export-controlled items or data while developing software, working with clients, or reselling third-party products.

United States export regulations apply both to exports from the United States and to exports from other countries when products contain U.S.-origin components or technology. Other countries, including countries in Europe, also maintain export control regulations.

Software created in the United States may remain subject to export regulations even if it is duplicated and packaged abroad. In some circumstances, an oral presentation containing technical data made to foreign nationals in the United States may constitute a controlled export.

At the beginning of a customer engagement, employees should consider the export-control obligations of Infosys and the customer. Export rules are complex, and employees should seek assistance from the Office of Integrity and Compliance at an early stage.

Infosys imports software and other intangible assets. Employees must comply with applicable import laws, regulations, and requirements when transferring goods and controlled software across borders.

## Understanding Regulated Trade Restrictions

### Anti-Boycott Laws

Employees may receive requests for Infosys to boycott certain countries, companies, or other entities. Boycott activity may include refusing to do business with certain groups or requesting information about boycotted entities.

Employees must not cooperate with a boycott that was not initiated by the U.S. or Indian governments. Such activity may constitute an illegal foreign boycott. Boycott requests may be included in larger documents such as master service agreements, invoices, or statements of work.

## Conflict of Interest

Infosys respects the laws and customs of every place where it does business. Employees must perform their jobs in compliance with applicable laws, policies, and procedures. Laws may vary by location, and conduct that is lawful in one place may be unlawful in another.

### What does conflict of interest mean?

A conflict of interest occurs when the interests or benefits of one person conflict with the interests or benefits of the Company.

Employees must avoid situations involving an actual or potential conflict of interest, including situations in which employees or their family members receive improper personal benefits or preferential treatment because of their position or a family member’s position in the Company. Such situations may affect judgment or responsibilities toward the Company, its shareholders, and its customers.

### Outside Directorships

It is a conflict of interest for employees or directors to serve as a director, partner, sole proprietor, or equivalent position in a company, firm, or business entity that competes with Infosys or has a similar line of business.

With prior approval from the Office of Integrity and Compliance, employees may serve on the boards or governing bodies of two other business entities, provided those entities do not compete with Infosys.

### Working with Family and Friends

Employees must not work directly for, supervise, or make employment decisions about a family member or relative. This includes positions or assignments within the same department and positions involving financial or other dependence or influence, such as auditing, control, or supervisor-subordinate relationships.

Employees should avoid conducting Company business with a family member or relative, or with a business in which a family member or relative holds a significant role.

### Outside Employment

Any activity that enhances or involves simultaneous employment with another company or business entity is considered outside employment and may create a conflict of interest. This includes performing services as an employee, agent, or contractor for a customer, supplier, or other entity that has a business relationship with the Company while working at Infosys.

### Outside Investments

Employees should not have a financial interest, including through a relative, in an organization if that interest would create or appear to create a conflict of interest with the Company. Particular care is required with financial interests in competitors, suppliers, customers, distributors, and strategic partners.

### Loans

Loans by the Company to, or guarantees by the Company of obligations of, any director or executive officer or their family members are prohibited by law. Loans and guarantees to employees may constitute improper personal benefits depending on the facts and circumstances.

### Relationships at Work

Personal or romantic involvement with a competitor, supplier, or another employee may affect an employee’s ability to exercise good judgment on behalf of the Company and may create a conflict of interest.

Personal relationships and romantic liaisons between employees in a manager-employee reporting structure may create team-management challenges and reduce morale. Such relationships must be disclosed to the manager immediately. The manager may take appropriate corrective action.

The Human Resources Department is responsible for determining whether an acknowledged relationship is covered by the policy.

### Related Party Transactions

Family members and relatives include spouses, siblings, children, parents, grandparents, grandchildren, aunts, uncles, nieces, nephews, cousins, step-relations, and in-laws.

Material transactions, particularly those involving family members or relatives, must be reviewed and approved in writing in advance. The Company is subject to legal obligations to report certain material related party transactions to regulators, and such transactions must be conducted at arm’s length and without preferential treatment.

### What should I do if I have any questions?

Potential conflicts of interest must be disclosed to the Office of Integrity and Compliance. Employees must also update their disclosures when circumstances change.

Because potential conflicts may arise in many situations, employees should raise any proposed transaction or situation that creates questions or doubts with the Office of Integrity and Compliance.
