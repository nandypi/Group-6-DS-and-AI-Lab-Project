## Bonus Shares

The Indian Companies Act, 2013 permits a company to issue bonus shares by capitalizing:

- Free reserves.
- The securities premium account.
- The capital redemption reserve account.

Reserves created through asset revaluation cannot be capitalized for bonus shares. Bonus shares are distributed to shareholders in the proportion recommended by the Board. Shareholders on record on a fixed record date are entitled to receive the bonus shares, and the Board's announced decision to issue bonus shares cannot subsequently be withdrawn.

Any bonus issue is subject to SEBI guidelines. The company must:

- Be authorized by its Articles of Association.
- Obtain stock exchange approval for listing and trading of all securities issued before the bonus issue, excluding employee stock options and convertible securities.
- Reserve equity shares of the same class for holders of outstanding compulsorily convertible debt instruments, if any, in proportion to the convertible portion.
- Issue the bonus shares from free reserves, the securities premium account, or the capital redemption reserve account, using genuine profits or share premium collected in cash.
- Make any partly paid shares fully paid before issuing bonus shares.
- Not have defaulted on interest or principal payments relating to fixed deposits or existing debentures.
- Not declare bonus shares in lieu of dividends.
- Make the allotment only in dematerialized form.
- Have sufficient reason to believe that it has not defaulted on statutory employee-related dues, including provident fund, gratuity, and bonus contributions.

The bonus issue must be implemented within 15 days of Board approval when shareholder approval is not required. If shareholder approval is required for capitalization of profits or reserves, the issue must be implemented within two months of Board approval. Once announced, the bonus issue cannot be withdrawn.

## Consolidation and Subdivision of Shares

The Indian Companies Act, 2013 permits a company to split or combine the par value of its shares at a general meeting, provided this is authorized by its Articles of Association and approved by shareholders. Shareholders on record on a fixed record date are entitled to receive the split or combination.

## Pre-emptive Rights and Issue of Additional Shares

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013, shareholders have the right to subscribe to new shares in proportion to their existing shareholdings when a company makes a further issue of shares, unless a general meeting passes a special resolution otherwise.

For a pre-emptive issue, the company must first offer the new shares to shareholders on record on a fixed record date. The offer must include:

1. The right to renounce the offered shares in favor of another person.
2. The number of shares offered.
3. An offer period of at least 15 days and no more than 30 days from the date of the offer.

If the offer is not accepted, it is deemed to have been declined. The Board may then distribute any unsubscribed shares in a manner that is not disadvantageous to the shareholders or the Company.

## Meetings of Shareholders

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013, a company must hold an annual general meeting each year within 15 months of the previous AGM or within six months of the end of the previous fiscal year, whichever is earlier. Because the Company is among India's top 100 listed entities by market capitalization, Regulation 44(5) of the SEBI Listing Regulations requires it to hold its AGM within five months of the close of each financial year. In certain circumstances, the Registrar of Companies may grant a three-month extension.

The AGM is generally convened by the Company Secretary pursuant to a Board resolution. The Board may convene an Extraordinary General Meeting when necessary or at the request of shareholders holding at least 10% of the Company's paid-up capital carrying voting rights.

Written notice stating the agenda must be provided to shareholders on record at least 21 days before a general meeting, excluding the days of mailing and the meeting date. An EGM must be held at a place in India. An AGM must be held at the registered office or another place within the city where the registered office is located.

## Voting Rights

At general meetings, the Company provides shareholders with remote e-voting facilities. Voting by ballot is also available at the AGM, and members who have not voted through remote e-voting may vote at the AGM. The Chairperson has a casting vote in the event of a tie.

A shareholder entitled to attend and vote may appoint a proxy. The proxy instrument must be delivered to the Company at least 48 hours before the meeting. Unless the Articles of Association provide otherwise, a proxy may vote only on a poll.

A corporate shareholder may appoint an authorized representative to vote on its behalf through remote e-voting or at the general meeting. An authorized representative may also appoint a proxy. Under the SEBI Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements Regulations, 2015, remote e-voting must be provided for all shareholder resolutions in accordance with the Indian Companies Act, 2013.

Ordinary resolutions may be passed by a simple majority at a general meeting for which the required notice has been given. Special resolutions, including amendments to the Articles of Association, waivers of pre-emptive rights for new share issuances, and reductions of share capital, require votes in favor to be at least three times the votes cast against by members entitled to vote and voting.

The following matters must be voted on by postal ballot:

- Alteration of the objects clause of the memorandum.
- Change in the registered office outside the local limits of a city, town, or village.
- Alteration of the Articles of Association to convert the Company into a private company.
- Change in the objects for which money was raised from the public through a prospectus when any amount remains unutilized.
- Issue of shares with differential voting, dividend, or other rights under Section 43(a)(ii) of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.
- Variation of rights attached to a class of shares, debentures, or other securities under Section 48 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.
- Buyback of shares.
- Sale of the whole or substantially the whole of an undertaking under Section 180(1)(a) of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.
- Election of a director under Section 151 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.
- Providing loans, guarantees, or security above the limit specified under Section 186(3) of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.
