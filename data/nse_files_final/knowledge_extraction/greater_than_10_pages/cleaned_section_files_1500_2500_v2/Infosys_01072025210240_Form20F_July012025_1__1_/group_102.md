## Description of Indian Equity Shares

This section summarizes material information concerning the share capital of Infosys Limited and selected provisions of its Articles of Association, Memorandum of Association, the Companies Act, 2013, and related Indian laws. The legal summary is based on laws applicable as of March 31, 2024 and is not a complete analysis of all applicable laws. The Articles of Association and Memorandum of Association filed as exhibits to the Annual Report on Form 20-F contain additional information.

## General

As of March 31, 2025, Infosys had the following securities registered pursuant to Section 12(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934:

| Title of each class | Trading symbol(s) | Name of each exchange on which registered |
|---|---|---|
| American Depositary Shares (“ADSs”), each represented by one equity share, par value ₹5 per share; equity shares, par value ₹5 per share* | INFY | New York Stock Exchange |

\* The equity shares are not for trading, but only in connection with the listing of ADSs on the New York Stock Exchange.

Infosys’ authorized share capital is ₹24,000,000,000, divided into 4,800,000,000 equity shares with a par value of ₹5 per share. The Company has not issued bearer shares. For purposes of this description, “shareholder” means a shareholder registered as a member in the register of members or whose name appears in the beneficiary position maintained by the depositories.

## Dividends

Under the Indian Companies Act, the Board recommends dividend payments for shareholder approval at a general meeting. The Board is not obliged to recommend a dividend.

At the annual general meeting, the Company may declare dividends according to shareholders’ respective rights and interests in profits, but dividends may not exceed the amount recommended by the Board. Dividends are generally declared per equity share and distributed in cash in proportion to the paid-up value of the shares within 30 days of shareholder approval.

The Board may declare and pay interim dividends without shareholder approval, subject to the conditions specified under the Indian Companies Act. The Company must inform the stock exchanges on which its equity shares and ADSs are listed of the dividend declared per equity share and the record date or book-closure date determining shareholder eligibility.

Under the Indian Companies Act, dividends may be paid only in cash to registered shareholders as of the record date. Payment may be made by cash, cheque, warrant, or electronic mode.

## Description of securities

Unpaid or unclaimed dividends remaining unpaid or unclaimed 30 days after declaration must be transferred to a special bank account maintained by the Company at an approved bank. Infosys transfers such amounts within seven days after the expiry of the 30-day period.

Amounts remaining unclaimed in that account for seven years from the date of transfer are transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund (“IEPF”). Shares for which dividends have not been paid or claimed for seven consecutive years or more are also transferred to the IEPF Authority. Shareholders may claim back unclaimed dividends, corresponding shares, and any benefits accruing on those shares from the IEPF in accordance with the IEPF Rules. No claim for these amounts or shares lies against the Company.

Dividends may be paid from:

- Profits for the year in which the dividend is declared, after providing for depreciation.
- Undistributed profits from previous financial years, after providing for depreciation.
- Accumulated profits transferred to free reserves, subject to the conditions below when profits are inadequate or absent.

Before declaring a dividend, a company may transfer an appropriate percentage of its profits to reserves.

When profits are inadequate or absent, a dividend may be declared from accumulated profits transferred to free reserves only if:

- The dividend rate does not exceed the average rate declared during the three immediately preceding years, unless no dividends were declared during those three years.
- The amount drawn from accumulated profits transferred to reserves does not exceed 10% of paid-up capital plus free reserves shown in the latest audited financial statements. The amount drawn must first offset losses incurred in the fiscal year in which the dividend is declared.
- The reserves remaining after withdrawal are at least 15% of paid-up capital shown in the latest audited financial statements.
- Previous losses and depreciation not provided for in prior years are set off against the current year’s profit before the dividend is declared.

## Bonus Shares

The Indian Companies Act permits a company to distribute amounts transferred from free reserves to shareholders as bonus shares. Bonus shares may also be issued by capitalizing the securities premium account or capital redemption reserve account. Reserves created through asset revaluation cannot be capitalized for this purpose.

Bonus shares are distributed to shareholders in the proportion recommended by the Board, and shareholders registered on the applicable record date are entitled to receive them. Once announced, the Board’s decision recommending a bonus issue cannot be withdrawn.

Bonus share issues are subject to applicable Securities and Exchange Board of India (“SEBI”) requirements. These include the following:

- The issue must be authorized by the Articles of Association.
- Equity shares of the same class must be reserved for holders of outstanding compulsorily convertible debt instruments, if any, in proportion to their convertible portion.
- The issue must be made from free reserves, the securities premium account, or the capital redemption reserve account, and must be supported by genuine profits or share premium collected in cash.
- Any partly paid shares must be made fully paid before the bonus issue.
- The company must not have defaulted on interest or principal payments relating to fixed deposits or existing debentures.
- Bonus shares cannot be issued in lieu of dividends.
- The company must have sufficient reason to believe that it has not defaulted on statutory employee payments, including provident fund contributions, gratuity, and bonus.
- The issue must be implemented within 15 days of Board approval when shareholder approval is not required, or within two months of Board approval when shareholder approval is required for capitalization of profits or reserves.

A bonus issue cannot be withdrawn after the decision to issue the bonus shares has been announced.
