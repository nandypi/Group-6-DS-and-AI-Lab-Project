## Negative media coverage and public scrutiny may divert the time and attention of our board and management and adversely affect our reputation and the prices of our equity shares and ADSs.

Infosys’s business practices, policies, and actions are subject to media coverage and public scrutiny, including negative or inaccurate posts and comments. Future negative coverage involving the business, board, or senior management could harm its reputation, regardless of the factual basis of the allegations.

Responding to media allegations may divert the time and attention of the board and senior management and disrupt operations. Infosys may be unable to respond publicly to certain comments because of obligations to employees, clients, and other stakeholders. Unfavorable publicity could reduce investor confidence and directly or indirectly cause the prices of its equity shares and ADSs to decline.

Employee actions that are perceived to be against the interests of local communities or that violate local regulations could harm Infosys’s reputation if they attract mainstream or social media attention or regulatory scrutiny. Regulatory requirements to respond to rumors within specified timeframes could also strain board and management capacity, divert attention from strategic work, and affect the company’s reputation.

## VII. Risks related to legislation and regulatory compliance

Geopolitical tensions, changes in tariffs or trade restrictions, and restrictive immigration legislation could adversely affect Infosys’s ability to service clients in affected geographies.

Changes in government administrations may increase political and economic uncertainty and protectionist immigration policies. Potential changes include higher wage requirements, more restrictive interpretations or applications of immigration laws, and more complex processes. These changes could increase immigration sponsorship costs, impair Infosys’s global delivery model, and affect its results of operations and financial condition.

## New and changing regulatory compliance, corporate governance and public disclosure requirements add uncertainty to our compliance efforts and increase our costs of compliance.

Infosys is subject to laws, regulations, and industry standards in the countries where it operates. These cover privacy, information security, labor and employment, immigration, data protection, import and export practices, marketing, and communications. Requirements and their interpretations may change as Infosys introduces new solutions and services and expands into new jurisdictions.

Actual or perceived breaches could result in investigations, regulatory inquiries, litigation, fines, tax demands, injunctions, negative client sentiment, impairment of existing or planned solutions and services, or other adverse effects on the business.

Changes relating to accounting, corporate governance, and public disclosure may increase compliance costs. In India, the Indian Companies Act, 2013, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015, and SEBI Insider Trading Regulations have added complexity to Infosys’s corporate compliance regime. Infosys is also subject to social regulations, including modern slavery legislation in countries such as the UK and Australia and the UK Corporate Criminal Offence Act.

Supplier non-compliance with applicable regulations, including regulations relating to human trafficking, could expose Infosys to sanctions, harm its reputation, and affect its ability to provide services to clients.

In connection with its Annual Report on Form 20-F, management assessed Infosys’s internal controls over financial reporting and determined that they were effective as of March 31, 2025. Management will continue to assess these controls in connection with each annual report. Identified deficiencies or an inability of auditors to issue an unqualified opinion regarding internal controls over financial reporting could harm Infosys’s reputation and the prices of its equity shares and ADSs.

Compliance with evolving laws, regulations, and standards has increased, and may continue to increase, general and administrative expenses and management time devoted to compliance activities.

Director and officer liability insurance may become more expensive or difficult to obtain. Board members and executive officers may face increased personal liability risks relating to their duties and regulatory reporting obligations, making it more difficult for Infosys to attract and retain qualified directors and executives. Failure to comply with new or amended laws and regulations could harm the business and reputation.

## The intellectual property (IP) laws of India may not give sufficient protection to software and the related IP rights to the same extent as those in the United States. We may be unsuccessful in protecting our IP rights. We may also be subject to third party claims of IP infringement.

Infosys relies on patents, copyrights, trademarks, design laws, trade secrets, confidentiality procedures, and contractual provisions to protect its intellectual property. Indian law may not protect proprietary rights to the same extent as U.S. law. Competitors may independently develop similar technology or duplicate Infosys products and services, and unauthorized parties may infringe or misappropriate its products, services, or proprietary information.

Misappropriation or duplication could disrupt operations, divert management and employee attention, reduce revenues, and increase expenses. Litigation to enforce Infosys’s intellectual property rights or determine the validity and scope of third-party rights could be time-consuming and costly.

As intellectual property rights in the software industry increase, infringement claims may become more frequent. Defending claims, including claims without merit, could be expensive and divert management resources. The growing use of AI introduces evolving risks relating to ownership of AI-generated output, the legality of using third-party intellectual property in training datasets, and memorization of training data by AI systems. Infosys will need to manage these risks contractually with clients and partners.

Third parties, including companies with greater resources, have asserted and may assert patent, copyright, trademark, trade secret, and other intellectual property claims against Infosys, its clients, or business partners.

On August 23, 2024, Cognizant TriZetto Software Group, Inc. filed a lawsuit against Infosys in the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas. The lawsuit alleges misappropriation of TriZetto trade secrets relating to its Facets and QNXT software products, breach of contract, and unfair competition. TriZetto seeks unspecified damages and, among other remedies, an injunction preventing Infosys from using TriZetto trade secrets.

If Infosys is found liable for infringing or misappropriating third-party intellectual property, it could be required to pay substantial damages, develop alternative technology, obtain a license, or stop selling applications or products containing the affected technology. Infosys may be unable to develop alternative technology or obtain a license on commercially reasonable terms, or at all. An unfavorable litigation, proceeding, or settlement outcome could materially adversely affect its business, results of operations, and financial position.

Non-practicing entities continue to bring patent claims in the software industry, seeking monetary damages and court-issued injunctions. Such claims may increase Infosys’s costs and disrupt operations if plaintiffs succeed in blocking sales of products or services.

Infosys cannot be certain that its services and solutions do not infringe or misappropriate third-party intellectual property rights. Increased work from home may increase the risk that employees reuse intellectual property belonging to different clients, despite safeguards and policies. Such incidents could result in third-party claims, reputational harm, substantial costs, or restrictions on offering services or solutions.

Under most contracts, Infosys agrees to indemnify clients for expenses and liabilities resulting from claimed infringement or misappropriation of third-party intellectual property rights. In some cases, indemnity amounts could exceed the revenues received from the client.

Claims and litigation could require substantial resources over extended periods and could require Infosys to obtain rights to continue offering a service or solution. If those rights cannot be obtained on reasonable terms, or alternative technology cannot be substituted, results of operations could be materially adversely affected. The risk may increase as Infosys expands its industry software solutions and platforms and develops and licenses software to multiple clients.

Infosys also relies on third-party software to provide certain services and solutions. If it loses the ability to use that software, including because the software is found to infringe third-party rights, it may need to obtain substitute software or alternative technology. Failure to replace the software, or inability to do so promptly and cost-effectively, could adversely affect results of operations.
