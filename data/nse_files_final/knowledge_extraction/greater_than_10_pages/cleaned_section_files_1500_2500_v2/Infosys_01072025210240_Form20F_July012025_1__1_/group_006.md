## Goodwill and acquired intangible that we carry on our balance sheet could give rise to significant impairment charges in the future.

Goodwill and acquired intangible assets are subject to impairment review. Impairment testing under International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB), may result in future impairment charges. Significant impairment charges could adversely affect results of operations.

## III. Risks related to our cost structure

## Our expenses are difficult to predict and can vary significantly from period to period, which could cause fluctuations in our profitability.

A significant part of total operating expenses, particularly personnel and facilities expenses, is fixed in advance for a given period. Unanticipated changes in the number and timing of projects, employee utilization rates, employee deployment locations, resource estimates for ongoing projects, or economic conditions may cause significant period-to-period variations in results of operations.

Profitability may also be affected by:

- Pricing pressure on services.
- Competition for digital capabilities.
- Exchange-rate volatility between the Indian rupee, U.S. dollar, and other currencies in which revenues are generated or expenses are incurred.
- Wage pressure in India and other operating locations.
- Regulatory changes affecting employee payments, including overtime.
- Tax increases or expiration of tax benefits.
- The size and timing of facilities expansion and related depreciation and amortization.
- Economic downturns.
- Changes in immigration laws, policies, and enforcement that restrict offshore outsourcing or the availability of visas.
- Costs associated with localization strategies.
- Wage increases resulting from market pressure or immigration regulations.

If a greater proportion of services is delivered at client sites, operating costs may increase. Technology and investment hubs, together with additional personnel for enabling functions and management teams, may also increase costs. Onsite operations cost more than offshore operations, and most bench capacity is therefore maintained offshore.

Economic uncertainty, tariffs, or trade restrictions may cause clients to reduce their business with Infosys. Profitability may be negatively affected if fixed or committed costs cannot be reduced in line with demand. Sudden changes in demand may also reduce short-term utilization and profits.

Large deals may have higher initial costs because of onsite efforts, transition costs, and other deal-specific expenses, creating volatility in margins. Infosys may acquire hardware, software, products, physical infrastructure, intellectual property, or other assets, and may rebadge employees as part of large deals. Pricing assumptions for these requirements are established when deals are signed, and changes to assumptions about costs or execution may affect profitability.

Competition for talent continues to affect wage costs. Wage increases may prevent Infosys from sustaining its competitive advantage and may reduce profits.

Competitive conditions and pricing pressure have led Infosys to commit to higher productivity improvements in client contracts. Clients have also begun demanding higher productivity commitments in connection with code-generation capabilities of artificial intelligence technologies. Failure to achieve anticipated productivity improvements because of an inability to identify automation opportunities, optimize processes, leverage technology, address service-delivery risks, or manage client requirements may affect profitability.

Increases in operating expenses that are not offset by higher pricing, acquisitions with lower profitability, or unplanned expenditures supporting the hybrid working model, including increased information-security requirements, may adversely affect operating margins and profitability.

## Any inability to manage our growth could disrupt our business, reduce our profitability and adversely impact our ability to implement our growth strategy.

Infosys has undertaken and continues to undertake major expansions of existing facilities and construction of new facilities globally. Total employee count increased from 242,371 on March 31, 2020, to 323,578 on March 31, 2025. Continued growth will place significant demands on management and other resources and will require ongoing development and improvement of global operational, financial, and other internal controls.

As of March 31, 2025, Infosys operated in 292 locations across 59 countries and had more than 100 group companies. Its scale creates challenges in maintaining uniform strategies, standards, policies, and procedures. Although Infosys has institutionalized processes, standard operating procedures, an enterprise risk-management framework, internal-control frameworks, information-security policies, a data-privacy program, and compliance programs, controls may have design or operating gaps or weaknesses because of the organization’s size.

Control failures could include fraud, cybersecurity attacks, and privacy breaches, potentially affecting reputation, financial condition, profitability, and stakeholder relationships. Inadequate financial controls could increase the possibility of fraud or reduce the accuracy of financial reporting, negatively affecting shareholder relationships.

Continued growth increases the challenges involved in:

- Recruiting, training, and retaining sufficient skilled technical, marketing, and management personnel.
- Maintaining and improving quality and process-execution standards.
- Preserving culture, values, and the entrepreneurial environment.
- Expanding the range of services offered to clients.
- Developing and improving financial, operational, communications, and other internal systems.
- Maintaining high levels of client satisfaction.
- Maintaining effective internal controls and training employees to mitigate unlawful or fraudulent activity, breaches of contractual obligations, and other unacceptable business risks.

The growth strategy depends on expanding operations globally. Costs of entering, establishing, and expanding operations in new markets may exceed expectations, and Infosys may face significant competition. Infosys may also face competition from companies with greater experience in particular countries or in international operations generally, as well as difficulties integrating facilities in different countries into existing operations.

Organizational structures, processes, and culture may not be sufficiently agile or adaptive to support the changes required to execute the strategy.

## Wage pressures and the hiring of employees and sub-contractors either outside or in India may prevent us from sustaining some of our competitive advantage and may reduce our profits.

Wage costs in India have historically been significantly lower than those in the United States and Europe for comparably skilled professionals, providing a competitive advantage. Indian wages have increased over time because of inflation, competition among information-technology services companies, broader economic growth, competition from traditional industries, and competition from multinational companies establishing offshore operations in India, including global capability centers (GCCs).

Higher levels of offshoring across the industry may further increase demand for talent in India, including at management levels and among skilled professionals.

Wages globally have also increased because of skill shortages and inflation. Prolonged conflicts, tariffs, trade restrictions, and political or macroeconomic uncertainty may increase inflation in key geographies and further increase wage costs. Higher visa-processing costs and increased wages for visa-dependent workers in certain geographies may also affect the cost structure.

Changes in employment laws, including in India, that increase employee compensation or social-security benefits may reduce profitability.

Infosys may need to increase compensation more rapidly to attract and retain employees with newer technology skills, remain competitive, or recruit in other lower-labor-cost jurisdictions. It may also issue incentive compensation, including stock-based compensation, to employees and management. Compensation increases may raise operating costs and reduce profitability.

In some years, Infosys may delay or not provide wage increases because of adverse market conditions while competitors continue to provide them. This could increase attrition and affect the ability to hire highly skilled technology professionals. Employee attrition may require higher salaries for new hires. Differences between new-hire and tenured-employee compensation may affect employee satisfaction if not addressed. New employees may also require additional time to reach required productivity, increasing fulfillment costs and potentially resulting in client penalties.

## We are investing substantial cash in creating and maintaining physical and technological infrastructure, and our profitability could be reduced if our business does not grow proportionately.

As of March 31, 2025, Infosys had contractual commitments of $109 million for capital expenditures, including commitments related to facility expansion and construction. Facility projects may experience cost overruns or delays. Continued investment in existing and new facilities to support growth may increase fixed costs.

Infosys is also developing new facilities, including in Tier 2 cities across India, to support its hybrid working model. These investments may increase facilities costs. If business and revenue growth do not occur proportionately, profitability may be adversely affected.

Changes in business and operating models, including continued hybrid working, may reduce utilization of physical infrastructure while operating costs to maintain facilities continue. Conversely, business conditions may lead Infosys not to expand or invest in certain areas, which could affect its ability to meet commitments made to federal or state governments and, in turn, its reputation and government relationships.
