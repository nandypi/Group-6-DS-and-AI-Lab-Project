## Basis for Opinion

Management is responsible for maintaining effective internal control over financial reporting and assessing its effectiveness. The auditor’s responsibility was to express an opinion based on its audit.

The audit was conducted in accordance with Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (PCAOB) standards. It included understanding internal control over financial reporting, assessing the risk of material weakness, testing and evaluating the design and operating effectiveness of controls, and performing other necessary procedures.

## Definition and Limitations of Internal Control over Financial Reporting

Internal control over financial reporting is designed to provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of financial reporting and the preparation of financial statements for external purposes in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles.

It includes policies and procedures that:

1. Maintain records that accurately and fairly reflect transactions and dispositions of company assets.
2. Provide reasonable assurance that transactions are recorded as necessary to prepare financial statements in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles, and that receipts and expenditures are made only in accordance with management and directors’ authorizations.
3. Provide reasonable assurance regarding the prevention or timely detection of unauthorized acquisition, use, or disposition of company assets that could materially affect the financial statements.

Because of its inherent limitations, internal control over financial reporting may not prevent or detect misstatements. Evaluations of effectiveness for future periods are also subject to the risk that controls may become inadequate because of changing conditions or that compliance with policies and procedures may deteriorate.

## Item 16E. Purchases of Equity Securities by the Issuer and Affiliated Purchasers

## Buyback completed in February 2023

Shareholders approved the buyback through a special resolution passed by postal ballot on December 2, 2022, with results declared on December 3, 2022. The company was authorized to buy back fully paid-up equity shares with a face value of ₹5 each through the open-market route on Indian stock exchanges, for an aggregate amount of up to ₹9,300 crore, excluding buyback tax, at a price not exceeding ₹1,850 per share.

The buyback was offered to all equity shareholders other than the Promoters, Promoter Group, and Persons in Control of the company. It commenced on December 7, 2022, and was completed on February 13, 2023.

During the buyback period, Infosys purchased and extinguished 60,426,348 equity shares at a volume-weighted average buyback price of ₹1,539.06 per share. The shares represented 1.44% of pre-buyback paid-up equity share capital. The buyback resulted in a cash outflow of ₹9,300 crore, excluding transaction costs and buyback tax, equivalent to $1,130 million. It was funded from free reserves, including Securities Premium, in accordance with Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013.

## Item 16G. Corporate Governance

Section 303A of the NYSE Listed Company Manual permits a foreign private issuer to follow home-country corporate governance practices instead of certain NYSE requirements, provided that it:

1. Maintains an Audit Committee satisfying Rule 10A-3 under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934.
2. Discloses significant differences between its corporate governance practices and those followed by domestic companies under NYSE listing standards in its annual Form 20-F.
3. Promptly notifies the NYSE of non-compliance with Section 303A.
4. Complies with the NYSE’s annual and interim certification requirements.

Infosys states that its corporate governance policies and practices are generally in compliance with the NYSE listing standards applicable to domestic companies, although Indian home-country requirements may differ.

Key differences between Indian requirements under the SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015, as amended, and NYSE requirements include:

1. For a company with a non-executive Chairman, such as Infosys, SEBI LODR requires at least one-third of the Board to be independent directors. NYSE requirements require a majority of the Board to be independent. As of March 31, 2025, the Board had nine members: one executive director, one non-executive and non-independent Chairman, and seven independent directors.
2. SEBI LODR requires two-thirds of Audit Committee members to be independent directors, while the NYSE requires all Audit Committee members to be independent. Infosys’ Audit Committee consists only of independent directors.
3. SEBI LODR requires at least one woman director and requires the boards of the top 500 listed entities to include at least one independent woman director. Effective April 1, 2020, the boards of the top 1,000 listed companies must include at least one independent woman director. As of March 31, 2025, Infosys’ Board had two independent women directors.
4. The criteria for determining director independence differ between NYSE listing standards and SEBI LODR. Infosys follows the criteria prescribed under both jurisdictions.

Section 402.04 of the NYSE Listed Company Manual requires actively operating NYSE-listed companies to solicit proxies for all shareholder meetings. Section 105 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013, prohibits companies incorporated under that Act from soliciting proxies. As a result, Infosys does not meet the NYSE proxy solicitation requirement. Infosys provides written notices of shareholder meetings to all shareholders and files those notices with the SEC.

Under its listing agreements with Indian stock exchanges, Infosys has a Stakeholders Relationship Committee, which is not required under the NYSE Listed Company Manual.
