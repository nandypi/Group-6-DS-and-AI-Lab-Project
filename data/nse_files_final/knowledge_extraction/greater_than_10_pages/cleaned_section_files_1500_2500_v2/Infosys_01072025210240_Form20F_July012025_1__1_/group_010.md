## Our reputation could be at risk and we may be liable to our clients for damages caused by cybersecurity incidents.

The increasing sophistication and complexity of cyber-attacks, cybercrime, and cyber insecurity have made the global cyber threat landscape highly volatile. Infosys and its third-party service providers have been, and may in the future be, targets of cybersecurity attacks. For example, certain McCamish systems were encrypted by ransomware, resulting in the non-availability of certain applications and systems.

Cyber threats evolve rapidly, and Infosys may be unable to adapt its threat detection and prevention measures to detect or prevent new, modified, or evolving threats.

Potential causes of cybersecurity breaches and other information security incidents include:

- Insider threats.
- Hackers and other state or non-state actors intending to harm Infosys or its clients, including government clients and clients in sensitive industries such as financial services, insurance, energy, utilities, and healthcare.
- Technological or human error and inadvertent actions by employees and contractors.
- Malware, ransomware, viruses, worms, and similar threats, including infection spreading between environments.
- Increased threat and attack surfaces arising from hybrid work and the emergence of the metaverse.
- Vulnerabilities in security automation systems.
- Malicious use of AI to create tailored, high-volume, high-impact cyberattacks that could overwhelm organizations and prevent effective operations.

Cybersecurity risks may increase as Infosys scales, expands its cloud-based offerings and AI-powered enterprise transformation services, stores and processes larger amounts of client data, and hosts or manages parts of clients’ businesses. The risks are especially relevant to industries involving sensitive data, including financial services, energy, utilities, and healthcare.

Increased dependence on cloud vendors creates additional business-continuity risk. A large-scale security failure at a cloud vendor, combined with difficulties porting data from one vendor to another, could jeopardize Infosys’ and its clients’ business continuity.

Because Infosys operates across continents, alleged or actual non-compliance with cybersecurity and information-security obligations in applicable jurisdictions could lead to regulatory investigations, claims, litigation, damages, fines, penalties, and other liabilities.

Cybersecurity breaches and other data-security incidents could adversely affect Infosys’ business, operations, financial performance, and reputation. Potential consequences include the unavailability or disruption of critical systems; unauthorized access to, loss, corruption, or theft of intellectual property, personal data, or sensitive information; claims and litigation; regulatory investigations and inquiries; damages, fines, penalties, and other liabilities; loss of business; reputational harm; and costs to investigate, remediate, and address the incident.

Infosys also expects to incur increased costs to prevent cybersecurity breaches and other information-security incidents. Failure to demonstrate strong corporate governance around cybersecurity following a breach could affect its reputation, particularly given increased focus on environmental, social, and governance concerns.

Cybersecurity insurance covers certain first-party losses resulting from a cybersecurity incident, including forensic costs, crisis consultants, and data restoration. It also covers business-interruption losses resulting from a system shutdown caused by a cyber incident. The insurance may not cover all losses, and current or future coverage may not remain available on commercially reasonable terms or at all.

Infosys’ reputation and financial position may be affected if privacy breaches under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) or other global data-privacy regulations are attributed to it. Risks include failure to report breaches to regulators and data subjects within required time periods, failure to respond to data-subject requests on time, and client claims for losses caused by employee-related privacy breaches.

The GDPR and similar laws impose additional obligations and increase Infosys’ risk exposure and potential penalties. Because the GDPR’s enforcement history in Infosys’ industry sector is limited, the application of certain obligations is difficult to predict. Regulators may determine that Infosys’ compliance measures are insufficient and impose fines or public censure.

The number of data-privacy incidents may increase as Infosys grows and operates under a hybrid working model. More of its work may also become subject to privacy regulations as countries adopt new laws or strengthen existing laws similar to the GDPR.

Infosys established its data-privacy function more than a decade ago. The function operates as an independent business-enabling function, reports to top management, and uses the globally recognized Privacy Information Management System (PIMS) framework. Infosys assesses its liabilities as a processor and controller and implements controls where required to mitigate risks. It has policies and procedures for identifying and reporting privacy breaches to affected data subjects and regulators, as applicable, within stipulated time periods. Infosys also maintains insurance coverage for certain eventualities, but remains exposed to privacy breaches that could adversely affect its financial results and reputation.

The increasing use of AI-based tools and technologies introduces new privacy threats that are still at an early stage of understanding for the industry and regulators. Infosys may be unable to anticipate or adequately manage privacy threats associated with greater AI adoption.

## We may be the subject of litigation which, if adversely determined, could harm our business and impact reputation, growth, profitability, and results of operations.

In the normal course of business, Infosys is and may in the future be subject to legal disputes and claims. An unfavorable outcome could require payment of substantial damages. Intellectual-property infringement claims could also require ongoing royalty payments or prevent Infosys from selling certain products. Settlements may result in significant costs. A settlement or unfavorable litigation outcome could materially adversely affect Infosys’ business, results of operations, reputation, financial position, or cash flows.

Economic downturns, changes in tariffs or trade restrictions, geopolitical conflicts, cybersecurity threats, data breaches, contractual suspensions, delayed payments, and similar events affecting clients, vendors, or other stakeholders may result in additional litigation and disputes, costs, or reputational harm.

Six actions were filed against McCamish arising from the McCamish cybersecurity incident. The actions were consolidated, and a consolidated class-action complaint was filed on behalf of persons residing in the United States whose personally identifiable information was compromised, including persons who received notice of the incident. On May 9, 2025, McCamish and the plaintiffs entered into a definitive settlement agreement. The settlement is subject to preliminary and final court approval and, if approved, will resolve the allegations in the class-action lawsuits without admission of liability.

Cognizant TriZetto Software Group, Inc. (TriZetto) filed a lawsuit against Infosys asserting claims for misappropriation of TriZetto trade secrets relating to its Facets and QNXT software products, breach of contract, and unfair competition. The lawsuit seeks unspecified damages and an injunction preventing Infosys from using TriZetto’s trade secrets.

Infosys filed counterclaims against TriZetto and its parent company, Cognizant Technology Services, collectively Cognizant. Infosys alleges that Cognizant monopolized and restrained trade in the markets for U.S. healthcare payor software products and related IT support, in violation of federal and state antitrust laws. Infosys seeks treble damages, attorneys’ fees, and an injunction to stop the alleged anticompetitive conduct. Discovery is ongoing, and the trial is scheduled for July 2026.

## Our insurance coverage may not be adequate to protect us against all potential losses to which we may be subject, which could adversely affect our business.

Infosys’ insurance policies generally cover loss of or damage to insured property and business-interruption losses following property loss or damage. The policies cover property and assets worldwide, including leased property. Infosys also maintains coverage for damage caused by disclosure of employee and client personally identifiable or confidential information, system failures, and errors or unsatisfactory performance of services where a third party claims damages or financial loss.

Insurance coverage may not remain available on reasonable terms or in sufficient amounts to cover one or more large claims. An insurer may disclaim coverage for a future claim. For example, certain insurers have indicated that they may disclaim coverage for business-interruption claims arising from geopolitical conflicts.

Large claims exceeding available coverage, or changes to insurance policies such as premium increases, large deductibles, or co-insurance requirements, could adversely affect results of operations. The hybrid working model increases the risk of claims involving errors and omissions and data breaches, which could make existing coverage inadequate. Increased claims could also increase premiums and adversely affect profitability.

Losses arising from events excluded from insurance policies could adversely affect financial condition and results of operations. Claims may not be honored fully or promptly, and Infosys may be adversely affected by losses that are uninsured or exceed its available coverage.
