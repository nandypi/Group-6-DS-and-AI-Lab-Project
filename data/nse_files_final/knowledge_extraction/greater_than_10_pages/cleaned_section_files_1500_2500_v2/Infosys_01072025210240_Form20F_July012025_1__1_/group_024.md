## Overview

Infosys provides consulting, technology, outsourcing, and next-generation digital services to clients across 59 countries.

Clients are responding to advances in software and computing technology, particularly artificial intelligence, by seeking to reinvent core offerings, processes, and systems and become AI-enabled. Infosys’ strategy is to help clients ideate, plan, and execute their AI transformation journeys by building data foundations and cloud capabilities. The company also emphasizes safeguards for privacy, ethics, controls, and responsible AI practices.

Beginning in 2018, Infosys adopted a four-pronged strategy:

1. Scale Agile Digital
2. Energize the core
3. Reskill our people
4. Expand localization

Its primary geographic markets are North America, Europe, Rest of the World, and India. Its industry sectors include:

- Financial services and insurance
- Retail, consumer packaged goods, and logistics
- Communication, telecom OEM, and media
- Energy, utilities, resources, and services
- Manufacturing
- Hi-tech
- Life sciences and healthcare

Infosys was founded in 1981 and is headquartered in Bengaluru, India. It completed its initial public offering of equity shares in India in 1993 and its initial public offering of American Depositary Shares (ADSs) in the United States in 1999. It completed sponsored secondary ADS offerings in August 2003, June 2005, and November 2006; it received none of the proceeds from these secondary offerings.

## ESG Vision and Ambitions

Infosys launched its ESG Vision 2030 in October 2020. In fiscal 2025, it refreshed those ambitions to address changing stakeholder priorities.

After six consecutive years of carbon neutrality, Infosys’ ESG Vision 2030 Refresh includes an ambition to become climate positive by 2030. The company plans to address Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3 greenhouse-gas emissions by reducing its carbon footprint and sequestering more carbon than it emits.

Its digital-skilling programs aim to empower more than 18 million people and enable employment opportunities for more than 500,000 people. Its sustainability and community initiatives include technology-for-good programs, CSR efforts, employee volunteering, healthcare, and women’s empowerment. The company also refers to a diverse and inclusive board and data privacy and information-management practices.

## Industry structure and developments

Fiscal 2025 included uncertainty related to interest rates, geopolitical conditions, elections across major economies, inflation, protectionism, and subdued economic growth. High inflation and interest rates reduced discretionary spending, although technology-led growth remained active in some areas. Artificial intelligence became a central focus for industry and government across geographies and sectors.

Global GDP was estimated to have grown by 3.2% in calendar year 2024, down 10 basis points from the previous year. IT-services growth during the same period was 4.7%.

Technology-services companies faced pressure to develop AI strategies and remain competitive. Businesses sought to reimagine cost structures, improve resilience and agility, personalize customer and employee experiences, and launch new products and services using enterprise AI. Client spending and investments were expected to continue shifting toward AI in the near term.
