## Item 16J. Insider trading policies

In May 2015, SEBI introduced the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015 (“PIT Regulations”), replacing the SEBI (Insider Trading) Regulations, 1992.

Infosys has adopted insider trading policies and procedures to regulate, monitor, and report trading by all insiders, including designated persons and their immediate relatives, as defined in the Insider Trading Policy and Code. These policies are intended to promote compliance with the PIT Regulations, as amended, and applicable U.S. securities laws.

The Code of Conduct for Prohibition of Insider Trading is filed as an exhibit to the Annual Report on Form 20-F.

## Item 16K. Cybersecurity

Infosys’s cybersecurity strategy combines a comprehensive framework with a governance program intended to protect the company and ensure business continuity by minimizing disruptions. The program includes continuous threat-landscape monitoring, cyber-event management, security compliance monitoring, secure-by-design principles, technological innovation, and measures to strengthen cyber resilience.

The cybersecurity program is designed to ensure that controls and processes are consistently implemented, monitored, measured, and improved across cybersecurity domains. Infosys promotes a security-first mindset and security culture to mitigate enterprise risks.

## Risk management and strategy

Infosys maintains an integrated Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) framework implemented across the organization by the risk management office. The framework references the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations (COSO) and ISO 31000 and is tailored to Infosys’s business requirements.

The ERM framework covers strategic, operational, legal, compliance, and other company risks. It identifies, analyzes, assesses, mitigates, monitors, and governs risks, vulnerabilities, and potential threats to the company’s strategic objectives. Risks are identified through top-down and bottom-up approaches and recorded in a multi-layer risk register.

Infosys has organization-wide risk assessment guidelines and an eight-layer governance structure covering risk categories. Risks identified across functions, including cybersecurity risks, are presented to the appropriate councils. Critical or cross-functional risks may be escalated through the governance structure. ERM also defines guidelines for the company’s incident management process. Cybersecurity is a key enterprise risk monitored by ERM, with periodic updates on risks and mitigations provided according to governance guidelines.

Infosys Cyber Risk Management (CRM) is designed to identify, analyze, prioritize, treat, and monitor cyber risks and vulnerabilities across the enterprise. Its cyber risk assessment framework aligns with ISO 31000, ISO 27001, and ISO 27005.

CRM is integrated with the ERM framework to support cross-functional risk analysis and management reporting. It protects information, information-processing assets, and facilities of Infosys and its subsidiaries, with the objective of supporting business continuity.

The vulnerability management program uses threat-based prioritization, vulnerability-aging analysis, and continuous tracking. Infosys’s third-party security risk management program addresses security risks and vulnerabilities throughout supplier engagement through governance processes, continuous monitoring of security metrics, threat-intelligence tracking, and periodic risk assessments.

Multiple committees and sub-councils, from the Board level to departmental functional levels, oversee and monitor cyber risks.

Infosys periodically works with third-party security consultants on cybersecurity maturity assessments, gap analyses, evaluations of security controls and processes, and tabletop exercises. Findings and recommendations from these engagements are reviewed with senior management to determine appropriate actions.

## Governance

Infosys’s cybersecurity program is managed by the Information Security Group (ISG) using a defense-in-depth approach supported by cybersecurity policies, processes, and preventive, detective, and corrective controls.

A full-time Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) has overall responsibility for the cybersecurity program. The CISO and ISG are responsible for identifying, detecting, assessing, and mitigating risks and threats related to information technology systems, networks, and data, and for establishing and implementing cybersecurity policies, processes, and practices.

The CISO coordinates the Cyber Defense Centre, Technical Operations, Business Security, and Risk Management teams within ISG. The CISO and ISG review the cyber threat landscape, security incidents and risks, cybersecurity metrics, and technology implementations.

Infosys has established a Board-level Risk Management Committee (RMC) composed exclusively of independent directors. The RMC assists the Board with identifying, evaluating, and mitigating strategic, operational, and external-environment risks and is responsible for monitoring, evaluating, and approving the ERM framework and related practices.

The Board has constituted a Cybersecurity Risk Subcommittee under the RMC. The Subcommittee includes four independent directors and assesses cybersecurity-related risks and the company’s preparedness to mitigate and respond to those risks.

The Information Security Council (ISC) governs and oversees Infosys’s Information Security Management System. It is the primary governing body for information security and establishes, directs, and monitors the information security governance framework. The ISC reports to both the Operational Risk Council and the Legal & Compliance Risk Council.

The ISC meets periodically to discuss cybersecurity and other information security matters. The Cybersecurity Risk Subcommittee receives periodic management updates, meets periodically, and reports its findings, if any, to the RMC. The Board receives periodic updates from the RMC on cybersecurity and other information security matters.

Infosys operates with a hybrid employee model. The company is certified against the Information Security Management System standard ISO 27001:2022 and has received independent-firm attestations for SSAE 18 and ISAE 3402 SOC 1 and SOC 2.

During fiscal 2025, Infosys strengthened its cybersecurity posture through technology, tools, processes, cybersecurity personnel training, and initiatives to foster collective security ownership. The company also provided the developer community with dedicated cybersecurity courses and resource kits.

Infosys continues to review and strengthen cybersecurity processes and controls across its network.

## Report of the Audit Committee

## To the Board of Directors and Shareholders of Infosys Limited

In connection with the March 31, 2025 consolidated financial statements prepared under International Financial Reporting Standards as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board, the Audit Committee:

1. Reviewed and discussed the consolidated financial statements with management.
2. Discussed with the auditors the matters required by PCAOB Auditing Standard 1301, as adopted by the PCAOB in Rule 3200.
3. Received the written disclosures and letter from the auditors required by applicable Public Company Accounting Oversight Board requirements concerning auditor communications with the Audit Committee regarding independence, and discussed the auditors’ independence with them.

Based on these reviews and discussions, the Audit Committee recommended to the Board of Directors that the audited consolidated financial statements be included in the Annual Report on Form 20-F filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission of the United States of America.
