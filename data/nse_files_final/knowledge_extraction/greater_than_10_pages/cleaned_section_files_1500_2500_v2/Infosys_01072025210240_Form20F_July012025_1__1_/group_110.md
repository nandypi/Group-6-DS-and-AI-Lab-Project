The depositary is not responsible if it determines that it is unlawful or impractical to make a distribution available to ADS holders. We have no obligation to register ADSs, shares, rights, or other securities under the Securities Act, or to take any other action to permit their distribution to ADS holders. Accordingly, ADS holders may not receive distributions made on our shares, or any value for them, if we or the depositary determines that making them available is illegal or impracticable.

## Deposit, Withdrawal and Cancellation

## How are ADSs issued?

The depositary will deliver ADSs if an ADS holder or broker deposits equity shares, or evidence of rights to receive equity shares, with the custodian. After receiving payment of its fees and expenses, applicable taxes or charges, and any required certifications, the depositary will register the appropriate number of ADSs in the requested name or names and deliver the ADSs to, or at the order of, the person or persons entitled to them.

## How do ADS holders cancel an American Depositary Share?

ADS holders may surrender their ADSs at the depositary’s corporate trust office or provide appropriate instructions to their broker. After receiving payment of its fees and expenses and applicable taxes or charges, the depositary will deliver the equity shares and any other deposited securities underlying the ADSs to the holder or a designated person at the custodian’s office. At the holder’s request, risk, and expense, the depositary may deliver the deposited securities at its corporate trust office to the extent permitted by law.

## How do ADS holders interchange between Certificated ADSs and Uncertificated ADSs?

An ADS holder may surrender an ADR to the depositary to exchange the ADR for uncertificated ADSs. The depositary will cancel the ADR and send a statement confirming ownership of the uncertificated ADSs. Alternatively, upon receiving proper instructions from a holder of uncertificated ADSs, the depositary will exchange them for certificated ADSs by executing and delivering an ADR evidencing those ADSs.

## How do you vote?

ADS holders may instruct the depositary to vote the equity shares or other deposited securities underlying their ADSs at any meeting at which they are entitled to vote under applicable law, the company’s memorandum and articles of association, and the provisions governing the deposited securities. Alternatively, an ADS holder may vote directly after withdrawing the underlying equity shares, although the holder may not receive sufficient advance notice of the meeting to do so.

If we timely request voting instructions and provide the required notice, the depositary will notify ADS holders of an upcoming meeting, including a vote by postal ballot, and arrange to deliver the voting materials. The materials will include the meeting or solicitation information, the ADS record-date eligibility requirements, instructions for submitting voting instructions, and information on the circumstances in which a discretionary proxy may be given if no instructions are received.

Voting instructions may be given only for a number of ADSs representing an integral number of equity shares or other deposited securities. For instructions to be valid, the depositary must receive them in writing by the specified deadline. Subject to applicable law and the company’s memorandum and articles of association, the depositary will try to vote, or cause to be voted, the underlying equity shares or other deposited securities as instructed. The depositary will vote or attempt to vote only as instructed.

If we timely request voting instructions but the depositary receives no instructions by its deadline, the holder will be deemed to have instructed the depositary to give a discretionary proxy to a person designated by us, subject to required legal opinions and representations. No deemed instruction or discretionary proxy will apply if we inform the depositary that we do not wish the proxy to be given, substantial opposition exists, or the matter would adversely affect the rights of equity-share holders.

Under Indian law, voting on equity shares is generally by show of hands unless a poll is demanded by a shareholder or shareholders present in person or by proxy who hold at least 10% of the total shares entitled to vote on the resolution, or hold shares on which an aggregate amount of at least ₹5,00,000, or such higher amount as may be prescribed, has been paid up. Every company whose equity shares are listed on a recognized stock exchange, and every company with at least 1,000 shareholders, must provide shareholders with electronic voting for resolutions proposed at a general meeting. A proxy, including a body corporate represented by an authorized representative, may vote on a resolution.

If the depositary receives instructions from ADS holders to demand a poll, it may, subject to receiving required legal opinions and representations, request our chairman or a designated person to demand a poll. The chairman or designated person will endeavor to do so.

We cannot assure ADS holders that they will receive voting materials in time to instruct the depositary to vote the underlying equity shares.

The depositary and its agents are not responsible for failing to carry out voting instructions or for the manner in which voting instructions are carried out. ADS holders may therefore be unable to exercise their voting rights and may have no recourse if the underlying equity shares are not voted as requested.
