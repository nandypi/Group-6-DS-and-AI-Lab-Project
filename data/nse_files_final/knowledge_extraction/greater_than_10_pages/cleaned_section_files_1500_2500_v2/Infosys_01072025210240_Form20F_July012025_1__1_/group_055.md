## U.S. Federal Income Tax Considerations for ADSs, Equity Shares, and Dividends

U.S. holders should consult their own tax advisors regarding the U.S. federal, state, local, and non-U.S. tax consequences of acquiring, owning, or disposing of Infosys equity shares or ADSs.

### Ownership of ADSs

For U.S. federal income tax purposes, holders of ADSs generally will be treated as holders of the equity shares represented by those ADSs.

### Dividends

Subject to the passive foreign investment company rules, the gross amount of cash or property distributions on ADSs or equity shares, before reduction for Indian withholding taxes, generally will be included in a U.S. holder’s income as ordinary dividend income when received. For a U.S. holder of ADSs, the receipt date generally should be the date the distribution is received by the Depositary.

A distribution generally will be treated as a dividend to the extent it is made from current or accumulated earnings and profits, as determined under U.S. federal income tax principles. Infosys does not expect to maintain earnings and profits under those principles. To the extent a distribution exceeds current and accumulated earnings and profits, the excess generally will be treated first as a tax-free return of capital up to the U.S. holder’s tax basis in the equity shares or ADSs, and thereafter as capital gain.

Dividends generally will not qualify for the dividends-received deduction available to corporate U.S. holders. Subject to limitations, dividends paid to non-corporate U.S. holders may qualify for a reduced tax rate if Infosys is treated as a qualified foreign corporation.

A qualified foreign corporation may include a foreign corporation whose shares, or according to legislative history, ADSs, are readily tradable on an established U.S. securities market, or that qualifies for benefits under a comprehensive income tax treaty with the United States. A corporation is not a qualified foreign corporation if it is or was a passive foreign investment company during the taxable year in which the dividend is paid or the preceding taxable year.

Infosys ADSs are traded on the NYSE. However, because there are no specific statutory provisions addressing ADSs, there is no assurance that Infosys qualifies as a qualified foreign corporation solely because of its NYSE listing, and it is unclear whether the shares will be considered readily tradable for this purpose. Infosys may nevertheless be eligible for benefits under the applicable treaty. U.S. holders should consult their tax advisors regarding dividend treatment and eligibility for a reduced tax rate.

### Indian Withholding Taxes and Foreign Tax Credits

Subject to conditions and limitations, Indian withholding taxes imposed on distributions to a U.S. holder with respect to ADSs or equity shares generally should be eligible for credit against the holder’s U.S. federal income tax liability. Alternatively, a U.S. holder may claim a deduction, but only for a year in which the holder does not claim a U.S. foreign tax credit for any foreign income taxes.

The overall limitation on creditable foreign taxes is calculated separately for specific classes of income. Distributions on ADSs or ordinary shares generally will be foreign-source income for purposes of calculating the U.S. foreign tax credit. The rules governing foreign tax credits are complex, and U.S. holders should consult their tax advisors regarding availability under their particular circumstances.

### Dividends Paid in Indian Rupees

If dividends are paid in Indian rupees, the amount included in a U.S. holder’s income will be the U.S. dollar value of the payment, determined using the spot exchange rate between Indian rupees and U.S. dollars on the date the dividend is included in income, regardless of whether the payment is converted into U.S. dollars.

Generally, any gain or loss resulting from currency exchange fluctuations between the dividend payment date and the date the payment is converted into U.S. dollars will be treated as U.S.-source ordinary income or loss.

## U.S. Federal Income Tax Considerations for Company Buybacks

Each prospective participant in a company buyback should consult their own tax advisor regarding the U.S. federal, state, local, and non-U.S. tax consequences of participating in the buyback.

### Ownership of ADSs

For U.S. federal income tax purposes, U.S. holders generally will be treated as owners of the equity shares represented by their ADSs. Accordingly, converting ADSs into equity shares to participate in the buyback, and converting equity shares that are not tendered or are not accepted in the buyback into ADSs, generally will not be subject to U.S. federal income tax.

### Tax Treatment of a Buyback

An exchange of equity shares for cash by a U.S. holder pursuant to a buyback will be a taxable transaction for U.S. federal income tax purposes. Depending on the holder’s circumstances, the tendering U.S. holder will be treated either as recognizing gain or loss from the disposition of the equity shares or as receiving a distribution from Infosys.

Under Section 302 of the Internal Revenue Code, a tendering U.S. holder will recognize gain or loss on the exchange of equity shares for cash if the exchange:

- Results in a “substantially disproportionate” redemption with respect to the U.S. holder; or
- Is “not essentially equivalent to a dividend” with respect to the U.S. holder.
