## 2.16 REVENUE FROM OPERATIONS

### Accounting policy

Income tax expense for the three months ended June 30, 2025 and June 30, 2024 includes provisions, net of reversals, of ₹116 crore and ₹60 crore, respectively. These provisions and reversals relating to prior periods primarily arose from the adjudication of disputed matters following tax return filings and completion of assessments across various jurisdictions.

Deferred income tax for the three months ended June 30, 2025 and June 30, 2024 substantially relates to the origination and reversal of temporary differences.

The Company's Advanced Pricing Arrangement (APA) with the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) for US branch income tax expired in March 2021. The Company has applied for renewal of the APA. Until then, US taxable income is based on the Company's best estimate using the expected value method.

The Group derives revenue primarily from:

- IT services, including software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting, package implementation, and licensing of software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings, collectively referred to as software-related services.
- Business process management services.

Contracts with customers may be on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe basis.

Revenue is recognized when the customer contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable. Revenue is recognized upon the transfer of control of promised products or services, or performance obligations, to customers at an amount reflecting the consideration the Group expects to receive. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations in each contract and allocates the transaction price to those obligations based on their relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. When such evidence is unavailable, the Group primarily estimates standalone selling prices using expected cost plus an appropriate margin based on similar services.

Contracts may include variable consideration, including rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue when the related uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue recognition by contract type is as follows:

- Time-and-material and unit-of-work contract revenue is recognized as the related services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services comprise an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. It is recognized using a percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of customer benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.
- Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts with performance obligations satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because they have a direct relationship with productivity.
- Progress toward completion is measured as costs or efforts incurred to date, representing work performed, divided by estimated total costs or efforts.
- Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are monitored throughout the contract term. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates change or are revised.
- Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable based on estimated costs or efforts required to complete the contracts.

Customer billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development, related services, and maintenance services, the Group generally treats software development and related services as distinct performance obligations when the revenue recognition criteria are applied to each obligation. Revenue is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. Software development and related-services performance obligations are satisfied as services are rendered because customers generally obtain control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. These contracts are accounted for under the applicable guidance. When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices are unavailable, the Group uses the expected cost-plus-margin approach. When the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period based on the promise in the contract, and the measure of progress is determined accordingly.

Revenue from licenses under which the customer obtains a right to use the licenses is recognized when the licenses are made available to the customer. Revenue from licenses under which the customer obtains a right to access is recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on their relative standalone selling prices. When the standalone selling price for implementation is unavailable, the Group uses the expected cost-plus-margin approach. If the license requires substantial customization as part of implementation, the license and implementation fee is treated as a single performance obligation, and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services related to software product sales is recognized as the relevant performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Group first assesses whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other indicators of control.

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price, or both, of a contract approved by the parties. A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for as a separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling prices. If they are not so priced, the modification is accounted for as a termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract. A modification that does not create a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract using a cumulative catch-up adjustment.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract, meaning costs that would not have been incurred if the contract had not been obtained, are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them.

Certain eligible, nonrecurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as assets when they:

1. Relate directly to the contract.
2. Generate or enhance resources that the Group will use to satisfy performance obligations in the future.
3. Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to expenses over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services to the customer. Capitalized costs are regularly monitored for impairment. Impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount of the capitalized costs.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss.
