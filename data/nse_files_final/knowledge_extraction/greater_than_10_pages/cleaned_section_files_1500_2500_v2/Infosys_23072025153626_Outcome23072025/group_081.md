## Fair value hierarchy

- **Level 1:** Quoted prices, unadjusted, in active markets for identical assets or liabilities.
- **Level 2:** Inputs other than quoted prices included within Level 1 that are observable for the asset or liability, directly or indirectly.
- **Level 3:** Inputs for assets or liabilities that are not based on observable market data.

During the year ended March 31, 2025, government securities and non-convertible debentures of ₹297 crore were transferred from Level 2 to Level 1 because they were valued using quoted prices. Further, non-convertible debentures and tax-free bonds of ₹554 crore were transferred from Level 1 to Level 2 because they were valued using market-observable inputs.

A one percentage point change in the unobservable inputs used in the fair valuation of Level 3 assets and liabilities does not have a significant impact on their value.

The majority of the Group’s investments are fair valued using Level 1 or Level 2 inputs. These investments primarily include liquid mutual fund units, target maturity fund units, tax-free bonds, certificates of deposit, commercial paper, treasury bills, government securities, non-convertible debentures, and quoted bonds issued by government and quasi-government organizations.

The Group assesses counterparty risk using multiple criteria, including Tier I capital, capital adequacy ratio, credit rating, profitability, non-performing asset levels, and deposit base of banks and financial institutions. These risks are monitored regularly under the Group’s risk management program.

## 2.11 EQUITY X

## Ordinary Shares

Ordinary shares are classified as equity share capital. Incremental costs directly attributable to issuing new ordinary shares and share options, and to buybacks, are recognized as a deduction from equity, net of related tax effects.

## Treasury Shares

When an entity within the Group purchases the Company’s ordinary shares, the consideration paid, including directly attributable incremental costs, is presented as a deduction from total equity until the shares are cancelled, sold, or reissued.

When treasury shares are subsequently sold or reissued, the amount received is recognized as an increase in equity, and the resulting surplus or deficit is transferred to or from securities premium.

## Capital Redemption Reserve

In accordance with section 69 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013, the Company creates a capital redemption reserve equal to the nominal value of shares bought back, through an appropriation from general reserve or retained earnings.

## Securities premium

Amounts received in excess of the par value of equity shares are classified as securities premium. The securities premium account has been used for bonus issues and share buybacks.

## Share options outstanding account

The share options outstanding account records the fair value of equity-settled share-based payment transactions with employees. Amounts recorded in this account are transferred to securities premium when stock options are exercised and to general reserve for stock options not exercised by employees.

## Special Economic Zone Re-investment reserve

The Special Economic Zone Re-investment reserve was created from the profit of the eligible SEZ unit under section 10AA(1)(ii) of the Income Tax Act, 1961.

The reserve is to be used by the Company to acquire new plant and machinery for its business in accordance with section 10AA(2) of the Income Tax Act, 1961.
