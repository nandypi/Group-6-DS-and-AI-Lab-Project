## Basis for Opinion

The audit of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements was conducted in accordance with applicable Standards on Auditing, the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI), the Companies Act, 2013, and related rules.

The auditors stated that they were independent of the Group, had fulfilled their ethical responsibilities, and believed that the audit evidence obtained was sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their audit opinion.

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements

The Company's Board of Directors was responsible for preparing and presenting interim condensed consolidated financial statements that give a true and fair view of the Group's:

- Consolidated financial position
- Consolidated financial performance
- Consolidated total comprehensive income
- Consolidated changes in equity
- Consolidated cash flows

The financial statements were prepared in accordance with Ind AS 34 and other accounting principles generally accepted in India.

The respective Boards of Directors of the entities included in the Group were responsible for:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records
- Safeguarding the Group's assets
- Preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls
- Assessing the ability of the respective entities to continue as a going concern
- Disclosing, where applicable, matters related to going concern
- Using the going-concern basis of accounting unless liquidation, cessation of operations, or the absence of a realistic alternative made that basis inappropriate
- Overseeing the Group's financial reporting process

## Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit of the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements

The auditors' objectives were to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the interim condensed consolidated financial statements as a whole were free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report containing their opinion.

Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but does not guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with Standards on Auditing will always detect a material misstatement.

As part of the audit, the auditors exercised professional judgment and maintained professional scepticism. Their responsibilities included:

- Identifying and assessing the risks of material misstatement due to fraud or error
- Designing and performing audit procedures responsive to those risks
- Obtaining sufficient and appropriate audit evidence
- Understanding internal financial controls relevant to the audit to design appropriate audit procedures
- Evaluating the appropriateness of accounting policies and the reasonableness of accounting estimates and related disclosures
- Evaluating management's use of the going-concern basis of accounting
- Determining, based on the audit evidence obtained, whether a material uncertainty existed relating to events or conditions that could cast significant doubt on the Group's ability to continue as a going concern
- Evaluating the overall presentation, structure, content, and disclosures of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements
- Assessing whether the financial statements represented the underlying transactions and events in a manner that achieved fair presentation
- Obtaining sufficient and appropriate audit evidence regarding the financial information of entities within the Group

The auditors' conclusions were based on audit evidence obtained up to the date of the auditor's report. Future events or conditions could cause the Group to cease to continue as a going concern.

### Materiality

Materiality was defined as the magnitude of misstatements in the interim condensed consolidated financial statements that, individually or in aggregate, could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of a reasonably knowledgeable user.

The auditors considered quantitative materiality and qualitative factors when:

- Planning the scope of audit work
- Evaluating the results of audit work
- Evaluating the effect of identified misstatements in the interim condensed consolidated financial statements

### Communications with Those Charged with Governance

The auditors communicated with those charged with governance of the Company and other entities included in the interim condensed consolidated financial statements regarding:

- The planned scope and timing of the audit
- Significant audit findings
- Significant deficiencies in internal financial controls identified during the audit
- Compliance with relevant ethical requirements regarding independence
- Relationships and other matters that could reasonably be thought to bear on auditor independence
- Related safeguards, where applicable
