## 2.16 Revenue from Operations

### Accounting Policy

The Group derives revenues primarily from:

- IT services comprising software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting and package implementation.
- Licensing of software products and platforms across the Group’s core and digital offerings.
- Business process management services.

Customer contracts may be on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe basis.

Revenue is recognized when:

- The contract has been approved in writing by the parties.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- The contract is legally enforceable.
- Control of promised products or services has transferred to the customer.
- The amount recognized reflects the consideration the Group expects to receive.

When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to each obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged when an item is sold separately is the best evidence of standalone selling price. When this evidence is unavailable, the Group generally uses an expected-cost-plus-margin approach.

Contracts may include variable consideration such as rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue.

Revenue recognition by contract type is as follows:

- Time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts: recognized as the related services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance contracts: recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services consist of an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When services are discrete and the pattern of benefits and fulfilment costs is uneven, revenue is recognized using a percentage-of-completion method.
- Other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts satisfied over time: recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Costs or efforts incurred are used to measure progress toward completion.
- Incomplete contracts with estimated losses: provisions are recorded when losses become probable.

Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are monitored throughout the contract term. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates change.

Customer billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development and related services and maintenance arrangements, software development and related services generally qualify as distinct performance obligations. Revenue is recognized as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices cannot be determined, the expected-cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the service period using a measure of progress based on the contractual promise.

License revenue is recognized as follows:

- Licenses providing a “right to use”: recognized when the license is made available to the customer.
- Licenses providing a “right to access”: recognized over the access period.

Software product arrangements generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When the license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When the license is substantially customized as part of implementation, the license and implementation are treated as a single performance obligation and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed.

Revenue from client training, support, and other services related to software products is recognized as the relevant performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

When integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when the Group acts as principal. The Group considers responsibility for fulfilment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors when determining whether it controls the specified goods or services.

A contract modification is a change in contract scope, price, or both that is approved by the parties. A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for as:

- A separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price.
- A termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract when the additional services are not priced at their standalone selling price.

A modification that does not add a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract using a cumulative catch-up adjustment.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them.

Eligible nonrecurring costs, such as set-up, transition, or transformation costs, are recognized as an asset when they:

- Relate directly to the contract.
- Generate or enhance resources that will be used to satisfy future performance obligations.
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to cost of sales over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored for impairment, with impairment losses recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its interim Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income.
