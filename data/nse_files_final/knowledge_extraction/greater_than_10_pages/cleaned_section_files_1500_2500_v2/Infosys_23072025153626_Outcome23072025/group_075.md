## 1.3 Basis of consolidation

Infosys consolidates entities that it owns or controls. The interim condensed consolidated financial statements comprise the financial statements of the Company, its controlled trusts, and its subsidiaries.

Control exists when the parent has power over an entity, is exposed or has rights to variable returns from its involvement with the entity, and can affect those returns through its power over the entity. Power is demonstrated through existing rights that provide the ability to direct relevant activities that significantly affect the entity’s returns. Subsidiaries are consolidated from the date control commences until the date control ceases.

The financial statements of Group companies are consolidated on a line-by-line basis. Intra-group balances and transactions, including unrealized gains and losses, are eliminated on consolidation. The consolidated financial statements apply uniform accounting policies across the Group. Non-controlling interests represent the portion of the net profit or loss and net assets of subsidiaries that is not directly or indirectly owned or controlled by the Company.

## 1.4 Use of estimates and judgments

The preparation of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements in conformity with Ind AS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies; the reported amounts of assets and liabilities; disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities; and reported revenues and expenses.

Critical accounting estimates involving complex and subjective judgments are disclosed in Note 1.5. Estimates, judgments, and assumptions may change from period to period, and actual results may differ from estimates. Changes are reflected in the financial statements in the period in which they are made, with material effects disclosed in the notes.

### a. Revenue recognition

The Group’s customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is considered for recognition and measurement when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to those obligations. Identifying distinct deliverables and determining whether customers can benefit independently from them involves significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and the Group’s fulfilment costs are uneven because the services are discrete rather than repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method. The method used depends on the promises in the contract and the nature of the deliverables.

The Group uses the percentage-of-completion method for other fixed-price contracts. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs expended to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs. Estimating total efforts or costs involves significant judgment and is reassessed throughout the contract period.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as principal. The Group considers responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors when determining whether it controls the goods or services.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.

### b. Income taxes

The Group’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States, although the Company also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgments are involved in determining income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies. The amount of deferred income tax assets considered realizable could be reduced if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. See Note 2.15.

### c. Business combinations and intangible assets

Business combinations are accounted for using Ind AS 103, Business Combinations. Identifiable intangible assets and contingent consideration are measured at fair value to determine the net fair value of the acquiree’s identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities. External valuation experts conduct these valuations.

Estimates are required to determine the value of contingent consideration, option arrangements, and intangible assets. These measurements are based on information available at the acquisition date and management’s reasonable expectations and assumptions. See Notes 2.1 and 2.3.2.

### d. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Group’s asset base. Periodic depreciation is based on estimates of an asset’s expected useful life and residual value. Management determines these estimates when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end.

Useful lives are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including technological changes. See Note 2.2.

### e. Impairment of Goodwill

Goodwill is tested for impairment annually and whenever there is an indication that the recoverable amount of a cash-generating unit (CGU) is less than its carrying amount.

For impairment testing, goodwill is allocated to the CGU or group of CGUs that benefits from acquisition synergies and represents the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes.

The recoverable amount of a CGU is the higher of its value in use and fair value less costs to sell. Key assumptions in cash flow projections include estimated long-term growth rates, weighted average cost of capital, and estimated operating margins. See Note 2.3.

## 2.1  BUSINESS COMBINATIONS X

## Accounting policy

Business combinations are accounted for using the acquisition method under Ind AS 103, Business Combinations.

The purchase price is measured at the fair value of assets transferred, equity instruments issued, and liabilities incurred or assumed at the acquisition date, when control is transferred to the Group. It also includes the fair value of contingent consideration.

Identifiable assets acquired and liabilities and contingent liabilities assumed are initially measured at fair value on the acquisition date. Contingent consideration is remeasured at fair value at each reporting date, with changes recognized in the interim condensed Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss.

Non-controlling interests are initially measured either at fair value or at the proportionate share of the acquiree’s identifiable net assets. The basis is selected for each acquisition. Subsequently, the carrying amount includes the initial amount plus the non-controlling interests’ share of subsequent changes in the subsidiary’s equity.

Business combinations between entities under common control are accounted for at the carrying values of the assets acquired and liabilities assumed in the Group’s consolidated financial statements.

Options issued by the Group over non-controlling interests in subsidiaries are accounted for as financial liabilities. They are initially recognized at the estimated present value of gross obligations and subsequently measured at fair value to reflect the amount payable when the option becomes exercisable. If the option expires unexercised, the liability is derecognized.
