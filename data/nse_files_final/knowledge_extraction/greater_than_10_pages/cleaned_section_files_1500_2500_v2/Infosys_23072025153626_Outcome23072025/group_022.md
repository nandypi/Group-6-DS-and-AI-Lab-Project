## 2.6 Provisions and other contingencies

### 2.6.1 Provisions

A provision is recognized if, as a result of a past event, the Group has a present legal or constructive obligation that is reasonably estimable and it is probable that an outflow of economic benefits will be required to settle the obligation.

Provisions are measured by discounting expected future cash flows at a pretax rate reflecting current market assessments of the time value of money and the risks specific to the liability. The Group recognizes a reimbursement asset only when it is virtually certain that reimbursement will be received if the Group settles the obligation.

A contingent liability is:

- A possible obligation arising from past events whose existence will be confirmed only by the occurrence or non-occurrence of one or more uncertain future events not wholly within the Group’s control; or
- A present obligation arising from past events that is not recognized because an outflow of resources embodying economic benefits is not probable, or because the amount cannot be measured with sufficient reliability.

#### a. Post sales client support

The Group provides fixed-period post-sales support to clients under fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts. The related costs are accrued when the associated revenue is recorded and included in cost of sales. The Group estimates these costs using historical experience and periodically reviews the estimates for material changes in assumptions and likelihood of occurrence.

#### b. Onerous contracts

Provisions for onerous contracts are recognized when the expected benefits from a contract are lower than the unavoidable costs of meeting future obligations.

Estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract. The provision is measured at the present value of the lower of:

- The expected cost of terminating the contract; and
- The expected net cost of continuing with the contract.

Before recognizing a provision, the Group recognizes any impairment loss on assets associated with the contract.

### 2.6.2 Legal Proceedings

#### McCamish Cybersecurity incident

In November 2023, certain systems of Infosys McCamish Systems LLC (“McCamish”), a subsidiary of Infosys BPM Limited and an indirect subsidiary of Infosys Limited, were encrypted by ransomware. This resulted in the non-availability of certain applications and systems.

McCamish initiated its incident response and engaged cybersecurity and other specialists to investigate, respond to, remediate, and restore the impacted applications and systems. By December 31, 2023, McCamish had substantially remediated and restored the affected applications and systems.

A third-party cybersecurity firm conducted an investigation into whether, and to what extent, company or customer data was subject to unauthorized access or exfiltration. McCamish also engaged a third-party eDiscovery vendor to assess the extent and nature of the data involved. McCamish and the eDiscovery vendor identified corporate customers and individuals whose information was subject to unauthorized access and exfiltration.

From March 6, 2024 through July 25, 2024, six actions were filed against McCamish in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Georgia. The actions arose from the cybersecurity incident initially disclosed on November 3, 2023. The actions were consolidated, and a consolidated class action complaint was filed on November 7, 2024.

On December 20, 2024, the Court granted a joint motion to stay proceedings while the parties pursued mediation. On March 13, 2025, McCamish and the plaintiffs reached an in-principle agreement covering the proposed settlement of the class action lawsuits against McCamish and seven class action lawsuits filed against McCamish’s customers.

On May 9, 2025, McCamish and the plaintiffs entered into a definitive settlement agreement, and the plaintiffs moved for preliminary approval. Under the settlement terms, McCamish agreed to pay $17.5 million into a settlement fund. The Court granted preliminary approval on July 16, 2025. The settlement remained subject to final court approval and, if approved, would resolve all allegations in the class action lawsuits without admission of liability.

During the three months ended March 31, 2025, McCamish recorded a $17.5 million accrual related to the settlement and recognized a $17 million insurance reimbursement receivable. The receivable was offset against the $17.5 million settlement expense in the Statement of Comprehensive Income. McCamish may incur additional indeterminable costs, including costs arising from indemnities or damages or claims.

#### Government Investigation

The U.S. Department of Justice (“DOJ”) is investigating how the Company classified certain H-1B visa-recipient employees working for one of its clients in immigration documents filed with certain U.S. government authorities.

The Company is discussing the ongoing investigation with the DOJ and has commenced its own inquiry. At this stage, the Company is unable to predict the outcome, including whether it could have a material adverse effect on the Company’s business and results of operations.

#### Others

The Group is subject to legal proceedings and claims arising in the ordinary course of business. Management expects that these actions, when ultimately concluded and determined, will not have a material adverse effect on the Group’s results of operations or financial condition.

## 2.7 Property, plant and equipment

### Accounting Policy

Property, plant and equipment are stated at cost, less accumulated depreciation and impairment, if any. Costs directly attributable to acquisition are capitalized until the assets are ready for use as intended by management.

The Group depreciates property, plant and equipment over their estimated useful lives using the straight-line method. Depreciation is determined based on the estimated useful life of an asset and its expected residual value at the end of its useful life.

| Asset class | Estimated useful life |
|---|---|
| Building | 22–25 years |
| Plant and machinery (1) | 5 years |
| Computer equipment | 3–5 years |
| Furniture and fixtures | 5 years |
| Vehicles | 5 years |
| Leasehold improvements | Lower of the useful life of the asset or the lease term |
