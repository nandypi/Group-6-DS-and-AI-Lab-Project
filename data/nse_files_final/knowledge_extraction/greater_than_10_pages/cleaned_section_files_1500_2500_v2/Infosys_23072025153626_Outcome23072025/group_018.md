## a. Revenue recognition

The Group’s contracts with customers may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is recognized and measured when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to those obligations. This requires judgment regarding the deliverables and the customer’s ability to benefit independently from them.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and fulfilment costs is uneven because the services are discrete rather than repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method. The method selected depends on the promises in the contract and the nature of the deliverables.

For other fixed-price contracts, the Group uses the percentage-of-completion method. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs. Estimates of total efforts or costs are reassessed throughout the contract period based on the latest available information.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as principal. The assessment considers whether the Group controls the specified goods or services before transfer, including responsibility for fulfilment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

## b. Income taxes

The Group’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. The Group also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is required to determine income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, Management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax planning strategies. The amount considered realizable could be reduced if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period are reduced. (Refer to note 2.12.)

## c. Business combinations and intangible assets

Business combinations are accounted for using IFRS 3 (Revised), *Business Combinations*. Identifiable intangible assets and contingent consideration are fair valued to determine the net fair value of the acquiree’s identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities.

External valuation experts conduct these valuations. Estimates are required for contingent consideration, option arrangements, and intangible assets, based on information available at the acquisition date and assumptions considered reasonable by Management. (Refer to notes 2.10 and 2.9.2.)

## d. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Group’s asset base. Periodic depreciation is based on estimates of expected useful lives and residual values.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates reflect historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including technological changes. (Refer to note 2.7.)

## e. Impairment of Goodwill

Goodwill is tested for impairment annually and whenever there is an indication that the recoverable amount of a cash-generating unit (CGU) is less than its carrying amount.

Goodwill is allocated to the CGU or group of CGUs that benefit from acquisition synergies and represent the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes.

The recoverable amount of CGUs is based on the higher of value-in-use and fair value less costs to sell. Key assumptions in the cash flow projections include estimated long-term growth rates, weighted average cost of capital, and estimated operating margins. (Refer to note 2.9.1.)

## IFRS 18 - Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements

On April 9, 2024, the IASB issued IFRS 18, *Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements*, which will replace IAS 1, *Presentation of Financial Statements*, from its effective date.

IFRS 18 introduces new requirements for information presented in the primary financial statements and disclosed in the notes, with a focus on the statement of profit or loss. It introduces three categories for income and expenses: operating, investing, and financing.

IFRS 18 is effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2027; early adoption is permitted. The Group is yet to evaluate the impact of the standard.

## Amendments to IFRS 9 Financial Instruments and IFRS 7 Financial Instruments: Disclosures

On May 30, 2024, the IASB issued amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7 addressing:

- Classification of financial assets with environmental, social, and corporate governance (ESG) and similar features.
- Derecognition of financial liabilities settled through electronic payment systems.
- Additional disclosure requirements for equity instruments designated at fair value through other comprehensive income and financial instruments with contingent features.

The amendments are effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026; early adoption is permitted. The Group is yet to evaluate their impact.

On December 18, 2024, the IASB issued further amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7 addressing:

- Factors relevant to assessing the own-use requirements for contracts to buy and take delivery of nature-dependent renewable electricity.
- Hedge accounting treatment for nature-dependent renewable electricity.
- Related disclosures.

These amendments are effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026; early adoption is permitted. The Group has evaluated the amendments and determined that they have no impact on its consolidated financial statements.
