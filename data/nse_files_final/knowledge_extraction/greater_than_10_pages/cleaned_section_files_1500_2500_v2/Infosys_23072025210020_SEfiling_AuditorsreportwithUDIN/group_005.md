The auditors consider materiality to be the magnitude of misstatements in the interim condensed consolidated financial statements that, individually or in aggregate, could influence the economic decisions of a reasonably knowledgeable user. They consider quantitative materiality and qualitative factors when planning the audit, evaluating audit results, and assessing identified misstatements.

## INDEPENDENT AUDITOR'S REPORT TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS OF INFOSYS LIMITED

### Report on the Audit of the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements

### Opinion

The auditors audited the interim condensed consolidated financial statements of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries for the three months ended June 30, 2025. These statements include:

- The condensed consolidated balance sheet as at June 30, 2025.
- The condensed consolidated statement of profit and loss, including other comprehensive income, for the three months ended June 30, 2025.
- The condensed consolidated statement of changes in equity.
- The condensed consolidated statement of cash flows.
- Notes to the financial statements, including a summary of material accounting policies and other explanatory information.

In the auditors' opinion, the interim condensed consolidated financial statements give a true and fair view, in conformity with Indian Accounting Standard 34, *Interim Financial Reporting* (“Ind AS 34”), the applicable provisions of the Companies Act, 2013, and other accounting principles generally accepted in India, of:

- The consolidated state of affairs of the Group as at June 30, 2025.
- The Group's consolidated profit and total comprehensive income for the three months ended on that date.
- The Group's consolidated changes in equity.
- The Group's consolidated cash flows for the three months ended on that date.

### Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013. The auditors state that they are independent of the Group in accordance with the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India and the ethical requirements relevant to the audit under the Companies Act, 2013 and related rules.

The auditors believe that the audit evidence obtained is sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their opinion.

### Financial Statements

The Company's Board of Directors is responsible for the preparation and presentation of interim condensed consolidated financial statements that give a true and fair view of the Group's consolidated financial position, financial performance, total comprehensive income, changes in equity, and cash flows in accordance with Ind AS 34 and other generally accepted accounting principles in India.

The respective Boards of Directors of the entities included in the Group are responsible for:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records.
- Safeguarding the assets of the Group.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls relevant to the preparation and presentation of the financial statements, including controls intended to prevent and detect fraud and error.

Management is also responsible for assessing each entity's ability to continue as a going concern, making applicable going-concern disclosures, and using the going-concern basis of accounting unless the respective entity's Board intends to liquidate the entity, cease operations, or has no realistic alternative.

The respective Boards of Directors are also responsible for overseeing the Group's financial reporting process.

### Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit of the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements

The auditors' objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the interim condensed consolidated financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report containing their opinion.

Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but does not guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing will always detect a material misstatement when it exists. Misstatements may arise from fraud or error and are considered material if they could reasonably be expected to influence users' economic decisions based on the financial statements.

As part of an audit conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing, the auditors exercise professional judgment and maintain professional scepticism throughout the audit. Their responsibilities include:

- Identifying and assessing the risks of material misstatement in the interim condensed consolidated financial statements, whether due to fraud or error.
- Designing and performing audit procedures responsive to those risks and obtaining sufficient and appropriate audit evidence.
- Recognizing that the risk of failing to detect a material misstatement resulting from fraud is higher than for one resulting from error because fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or management override of internal control.
- Obtaining an understanding of internal financial controls relevant to the audit to design appropriate audit procedures, without expressing an opinion on the effectiveness of those controls.
- Evaluating the appropriateness of the accounting policies used and the reasonableness of accounting estimates.
- Concluding on the appropriateness of management's use of the going-concern basis of accounting.
- Determining, based on the audit evidence obtained, whether a material uncertainty exists related to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the Group's ability to continue as a going concern.
- Drawing attention to related disclosures in the auditor's report when a material uncertainty exists, or modifying the opinion if the disclosures are inadequate.
- Evaluating the overall presentation, structure, and content of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements, including the disclosures.

The auditors' conclusions are based on audit evidence obtained up to the date of the auditor's report. Future events or conditions may cause the Group to cease to continue as a going concern.
