## Opinion

Based on the examination of relevant records, information provided by management, and management representations, the Company complied with the Corporate Governance conditions stipulated in regulations 17 to 27, clauses (b) to (i) of regulation 46(2), and paragraphs C and D of Schedule V of the Listing Regulations during the year ended March 31, 2026.

This compliance statement is not an assurance of the Company's future viability or of the efficiency or effectiveness with which management conducted the Company's affairs.

## Annexure 5 - Secretarial audit report for the financial year ended March 31, 2026 Form No. MR-3

[Pursuant to Section 204(1) of the Companies Act, 2013 and Rule No. 9 of the Companies (Appointment and Remuneration of Managerial Personnel) Rules, 2014]

The secretarial audit covered the period from April 1, 2025 to March 31, 2026. Based on the records examined and information provided by the Company, the auditors reported compliance with applicable statutory provisions and stated that proper Board processes and compliance mechanisms were in place.

### Auditor's responsibility

The audit was conducted in accordance with the applicable Auditing Standards issued by The Institute of Company Secretaries of India. The auditors reported that the Company complied with:

- The Companies Act, 2013 and the rules made under it.
- The Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 and related rules.
- The Depositories Act, 1996 and related regulations and bye-laws.
- The Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 and related rules and regulations, to the extent of Foreign Direct Investment and Overseas Direct Investment. External Commercial Borrowings were not applicable during the audit period.
- The following regulations and guidelines under the Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992:
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011.
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015.
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018. Not applicable during the audit period.
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Share Based Employee Benefits and Sweat Equity) Regulations, 2021.
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Issue and Listing of Non-Convertible Securities) Regulations, 2021. Not applicable during the audit period.
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents) Regulations, 1993, and the 2025 regulations relating to the Companies Act and dealing with clients.
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Delisting of Equity Shares) Regulations, 2021. Not applicable during the audit period.
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Buy-back of Securities) Regulations, 2018.

The audit also examined compliance with:

- Secretarial Standards issued by The Institute of Company Secretaries of India.
- Securities and Exchange Board of India (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015, including amendments.

On a test-check basis, the Company generally complied with the following laws applicable specifically to it:

- Special Economic Zones Act, 2005 and related rules.
- Software Technology Parks of India Rules and Regulations.

### We further report that

- The Board of Directors was duly constituted with a proper balance of Executive, Non-Executive, and Independent Directors.
- No changes took place in the composition of the Board during the audit period.
- Adequate notice was generally provided for Board meetings, with agendas and detailed notes sent at least seven days in advance. Two meetings were convened at shorter notice, with the necessary approvals obtained under applicable provisions.
- A system existed for obtaining further information and clarifications on agenda items before meetings.
- All decisions at Board and Committee meetings were carried out unanimously, as recorded in the minutes.
- The Company had systems and processes commensurate with its size and operations to monitor and ensure compliance with applicable laws, rules, regulations, and guidelines.

During the audit period:

- The Company issued and allotted 8,74,856 equity shares of face value ₹5 each under the 2015 Stock Incentive Compensation Plan.
- The Company issued and allotted 14,53,412 equity shares of face value ₹5 each under the Infosys Expanded Stock Ownership Program 2019.
- The Company bought back 10,00,00,000 fully paid-up equity shares of face value ₹5 each for an aggregate consideration of ₹18,000 crore, at ₹1,800 per share.

The secretarial audit report states that:

- Maintenance of secretarial records is the responsibility of management.
- The audit verification was conducted on a test basis.
- The correctness and appropriateness of the Company's financial records and books of account were not verified.
- Management representations were obtained where required.
- Responsibility for compliance with applicable corporate and other laws, rules, regulations, and standards rests with management.
- The report is not an assurance of the Company's future viability or of the efficacy or effectiveness with which management conducted the Company's affairs.

## Annexure A

## Annexure 6 - Annual report on CSR activities

[Pursuant to Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013 read with Companies (Corporate Social Responsibility Policy) Rules, 2014, as amended.]

## 1. Brief outline on CSR Policy of the Company:

Infosys' CSR policy focuses on sustainable development of society and the environment. Its CSR activities include:

- Education and skill development to enable sustained livelihoods.
- Healthcare, including preventive health.
- Environmental sustainability and ecological balance.
- Gender equality and empowerment of women.
- Promotion of national heritage, art, and culture.
- Rural development.
- Disaster relief and rehabilitation.

## CSR activities

Infosys undertakes CSR initiatives directly and through Infosys Foundation, which was established in 1996.

Key activities during the year included:

- Education-to-employment initiatives focused on skilling underserved youth through strategic partnerships and livelihood training programs.
- Scholarships for students pursuing undergraduate studies in National Institutional Ranking Framework-accredited colleges in Engineering and other STEM fields, including nursing, pharmacy, biochemistry, and biotechnology.
- The Infosys Springboard digital literacy program, which provides access to core digital and life skills through a technology platform.
- The Universal Cornea Care Mission at L.V. Prasad Eye Institute, including model optimization, operationalization, validation, and development of a strategy for replication across India.
- Construction of a mother and child healthcare center at the Government Hospital, Kunigal.
- Tree plantation initiatives intended to promote environmental sustainability, maintain ecological balance, and improve farmer livelihoods and income.
- Co-implementation of the Gear for Gold program to support sustainable, inclusive, and high-performing sports academies through coaching, infrastructure, sports science, governance, and administration.
- Support for the operationalization of an advanced mother and child center through the provision of medical equipment at PGIMER Chandigarh.
