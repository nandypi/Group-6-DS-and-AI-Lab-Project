## Management's discussion and analysis

## Overview

Infosys provides AI-first business consulting and technology services. It has more than four decades of experience managing the systems and operations of global enterprises and uses an AI-first value framework, domain expertise, and an AI-native partner ecosystem to support client transformation.

The company’s strategic objective is to build a sustainable and resilient organization, remain relevant to client needs, create growth opportunities for employees, generate profitable returns for investors, and contribute to the communities in which it operates. Risks and challenges affecting the business are discussed in the Risk management report of the Integrated Annual Report.

## I. Industry structure and developments

Fiscal 2026 saw lower interest rates and stable economic sentiment, while policy changes and geopolitical uncertainties weighed on the technology services industry.

The year was marked by subdued growth, with pockets of economic improvement supporting technology-led growth. AI-driven technology transformation changed discretionary spending patterns and increased pressure on traditional IT services.

Global GDP growth remained flat at 3.3% in CY 2025. During the same period, IT services contracted by 10 basis points and grew by 4.6%. Client spending and investments were expected to continue shifting toward AI in the near term.

Businesses continued to use enterprise AI to reimagine cost structures, improve resilience and agility, personalize customer and employee experiences, and launch new products and services.

## II. Opportunities and threats

## Our strategy

Infosys’ clients and prospective clients are addressing opportunities created by advances in software and computing technology, particularly AI. They are reimagining core offerings, processes, and systems and seeking to strengthen their positions in their industries.

Infosys’ strategy is to act as a navigator for clients on their AI journeys and help them unlock business value. The company supports clients in ideating, planning, and scaling their AI futures by building data foundations and cloud capabilities. It also emphasizes safeguards for privacy, ethics, and controls through Responsible AI practices.

## Our strengths

Infosys has more than four decades of experience managing the systems and operations of global enterprises. The company states that this experience, its AI-first strategy, and continued investment in AI capabilities position it to support clients’ AI transformations.

## Our competition

Infosys operates in an environment characterized by intense competition in traditional services, rapid technological change, and the emergence of specialized niche providers.

The company has invested in proprietary intellectual property, including:

- **Infosys Cobalt™:** Services, solutions, and platforms designed to accelerate enterprise cloud journeys.
- **Infosys Topaz™:** AI-first services, solutions, and platforms using generative and agentic AI technologies.
- **Infosys Aster™:** An AI-amplified marketing suite.

## III. Financial condition

Refer to the Standalone and Consolidated financial statements in this Integrated Annual Report for detailed schedules and notes.

## 1. Equity share capital

Infosys has one class of shares: equity shares with a par value of ₹5 each.

During the year, movements in share capital resulted from:

- Buyback of 10,00,00,000 shares, involving a cash outflow of ₹18,000 crore, excluding transaction costs.
- Shares issued on exercise of stock options.

## 2. Other equity

Retained earnings changed due to profit earned during the year, payment of dividends, and the buyback of equity shares.

The company funded the buyback from free reserves, including securities premium, in accordance with Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013. As at March 31, 2026, it created a Capital Redemption Reserve of ₹50 crore, equal to the nominal value of the shares bought back, by appropriating the general reserve in accordance with Section 69 of the Companies Act, 2013.

The increase in securities premium was mainly due to the exercise of stock options. The Group made an irrevocable election to present subsequent changes in the fair value of certain instruments in other comprehensive income.

The Special Economic Zone Re-Investment Reserve was created from the profit of an eligible SEZ unit under Section 10AA(1)(ii) of the Income-Tax Act, 1961. The reserve is to be used for acquiring new plant and machinery for business purposes under Section 10AA(2) of the Income-Tax Act, 1961.

## 3. Property, plant and equipment

Additions to the gross block mainly related to computer equipment and infrastructure.

## 4. Goodwill and other intangible assets

On a consolidated basis, the carrying value of goodwill was ₹12,117 crore as at March 31, 2026, compared with ₹10,106 crore as at March 31, 2025.

The increase was primarily attributable to:

- Goodwill of ₹444 crore recognized on the acquisition of MRE Consulting Ltd. and The Missing Link Security Pty. Ltd., The Missing Link Security Limited, and The Missing Link Automation Pty. Ltd., collectively referred to as “The Missing Link”.
- A translation difference of ₹1,567 crore arising from foreign currency fluctuations.

The consolidated carrying value of intangible assets was ₹2,825 crore as at March 31, 2026, compared with ₹2,766 crore as at March 31, 2025. During the year ended March 31, 2026, intangible assets of ₹297 crore, mainly customer-related intangibles, were added through acquisitions.

## 5. Financial assets

### A. Investments

On a standalone basis, Infosys made additional investments in subsidiaries during the year for the acquisition of entities and operations and for expansion.

Infosys also invests in the startup ecosystem to access innovations that can be combined with its services and solutions for clients. These investments are typically minority equity positions in startup companies and/or venture capital funds.

The company’s investments include:

- Mutual fund units
- Tax-free bonds
- Non-convertible debentures
- Certificates of deposit
- Commercial paper
- Quoted bonds and securities issued by government and quasi-government organizations

Certificates of deposit and commercial paper represent marketable securities issued by banks, NBFCs, and eligible financial institutions for specified periods. These instruments have high credit ratings from domestic credit rating agencies.

Government securities are liquid and marketable instruments issued across different tenures, backed by the Government, and carrying sovereign credit. Investments in non-convertible debentures consist of debt instruments issued by government-aided institutions and financial institutions with high credit ratings.

The majority of the company’s investments are fair valued using Level 1 or Level 2 inputs. Investment decisions consider counterparty risk based on criteria including Tier-I capital, capital adequacy ratio, credit rating, profitability, non-performing asset levels, and the deposit base of banks and financial institutions. These risks are monitored regularly as part of the risk management program.

### B. Trade receivables

Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) in USD terms was 67 days for the year ended March 31, 2026, compared with 69 days in the previous year.

### C. Cash and cash equivalents

Cash and cash equivalents comprise deposits with banks that have high credit ratings assigned by international and domestic credit rating agencies. The deposits can be withdrawn at any time without prior notice or penalty on principal. Ratings are monitored periodically.

### D. Loans

Loans are provided to subsidiaries based on business requirements. Infosys had no loans outstanding as at March 31, 2026.

### E. Other financial assets

Deposits with corporations represent restricted deposits used to settle employee-related obligations as they arise during the normal course of business.

Unbilled revenues are classified as financial assets when the right to consideration is unconditional and payment is due only after the passage of time.

Foreign currency forward and options contracts are entered into to mitigate the risk of changes in exchange rates on foreign currency exposures. The counterparties for these contracts are generally banks.

## 6. Other assets

Unbilled revenues are classified as non-financial assets when the right to consideration depends on the completion of contractual milestones.

Withholding taxes and other amounts represent credits that can be applied against local taxes payable in various countries.

Deferred contract costs mainly comprise the costs of obtaining and fulfilling contracts, recorded in accordance with Ind AS 115, *Revenue from Contracts with Customers*.

## 7. Deferred tax assets / liabilities

Deferred tax assets primarily arise from temporary differences relating to property, plant and equipment, lease liabilities, allowances for trade receivables, credits related to branch profits, and compensated absences.

These assets are partially offset by deferred tax liabilities relating to the Special Economic Zone Re-Investment Reserve, branch profit tax, and intangible assets arising from business combinations.

A deferred income tax asset is recognized to the extent that it is probable that future taxable profit will be available against which deductible temporary differences and tax losses can be utilized.
