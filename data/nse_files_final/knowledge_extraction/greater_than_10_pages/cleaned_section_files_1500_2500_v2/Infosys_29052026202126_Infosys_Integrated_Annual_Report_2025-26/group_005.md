# Chairman's message Performance overview

## Performance indicators

- Free cashflow growth (2): 33,097 cr
- FCF conversion: 112.3% of net profit
- Adjusted operating margin (3): 21.0%
- Consolidated cash and investments (1): ₹43,075 cr
- Return on equity: 31.6%, an improvement of 2.6% over the last fiscal
- Large deal TCV: US$14.9 billion, with net new business representing 55%
- Clients with more than US$50 million in annual revenue: 88, an increase of 3 clients year over year
- Buyback completed: ₹18,000 cr at a price of ₹1,800 per share

## Sustainability, workforce, and recognition

- 84% of office space was certified with the highest green-building rating.
- 81.8% of Infosys’ India operations was powered by renewable energy.
- Carbon offset programs continued to benefit more than 300,000 rural families.
- Infosys was carbon neutral for 7 consecutive years across Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions.
- Approximately 14 million saplings were planted through the agroforestry program.
- 84% of the workforce was enabled on AI.
- Infosys was recognized as one of the 2026 World’s Most Ethical Companies by Ethisphere for the sixth consecutive year.
- Infosys was recognized as a Global Top Employer 2026 for the sixth consecutive year.

## The age of orchestration

*Nandan M. Nilekani, Chairman*

Whenever there is a major technology transition, questions are asked of Infosys: whether it will master the transition, remain relevant, establish a defensible position, and maintain growth and margins. Given that AI is a larger and more disruptive technology transition, these questions are louder. More than three years after the launch of generative AI, Infosys states that it remains relevant and has a future ahead of it.

Infosys plans to adopt coding tools and improve productivity while continuing to address the broader software development life cycle. This includes testing and validation, architecture design for speed, scale, and resilience, cybersecurity, data protection, company rules, and governance policies. The company identifies a deployment gap among large enterprise customers.

Continuous learning and talent transformation have been core to Infosys for decades. The company plans to prepare its talent for the AI era and redistribute employees released through productivity improvements to grow new accounts and offerings.

The AI transition has increased customer urgency around legacy modernization, eliminating data silos, addressing AI-identified cyber vulnerabilities, and reducing technical debt accumulated over decades. The preference to build rather than buy software is identified as an additional opportunity.

The Chairman identifies the integration of AI models and agents with traditional deterministic enterprise systems as a major opportunity. New capabilities can provide intelligence, simpler user experiences, and automation, while highly scalable and reliable transaction systems remain relevant.

Supply chains continue to change, and AI regulation is being developed in real time across multiple jurisdictions. Many enterprises stall when moving from AI pilots to scaled deployment because of legacy architecture, organizational inertia, and changing regulatory requirements. Closing this deployment gap requires redesigning business processes, customer journeys, and operating models rather than simply adding AI to existing structures.

Technology developed for an earlier era has created substantial enterprise technical debt. These systems are increasingly expensive to maintain and can restrict the agility required for AI adoption. The Chairman identifies organizational readiness, data integrity, governance, and the pace of decision-making as factors that distinguish enterprises that unlock AI value from those that do not.

The World Economic Forum projects that by 2030, 92 million jobs will be displaced and 170 million jobs will be created, representing a net gain of 78 million jobs. Capturing this gain requires attention to affected people, clarity about desired outcomes, and willingness to correct course when technology and human judgment diverge.

Enterprise AI implementation depends heavily on context because each company has different legacy systems, data, and undocumented dependencies. Modernizing existing “brownfield” systems is described as more difficult than greenfield development.

The future enterprise will be enabled by firms with long-standing client relationships, a successful execution record, and the ability to combine existing systems with new capabilities. A trusted partner can provide optionality among AI tools, orchestrate a complex technology landscape, preserve client strategic autonomy and control over context, and commit to business outcomes.
