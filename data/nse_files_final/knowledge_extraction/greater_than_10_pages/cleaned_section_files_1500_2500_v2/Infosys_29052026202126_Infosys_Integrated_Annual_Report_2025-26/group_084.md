## Overview and Notes to the Standalone Financial Statements

## 1. Overview

## 1.1 Company overview

Infosys Limited (“the Company” or “Infosys”) provides AI-first business consulting and technology services to help organizations unlock AI value at scale. The Company has over four decades of experience managing the systems and operations of global enterprises. Its capabilities include business transformation, domain expertise, and orchestration of innovations from its AI-native partner ecosystem.

Infosys Limited is a public limited company incorporated and domiciled in India. Its registered office is in Electronics City, Hosur Road, Bengaluru 560100, Karnataka, India. The Company’s primary listings are on BSE Ltd. and the National Stock Exchange of India Limited. Its American Depositary Shares (ADSs), representing equity shares, are listed on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE).

The standalone financial statements were approved for issue by the Company’s Board of Directors on April 23, 2026.

## 1.2 Basis of preparation of financial statements

These standalone financial statements were prepared in accordance with the Companies Act, 2013, guidelines issued by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), and Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS), under the historical cost convention and on an accrual basis, except for:

- Certain financial instruments measured at fair value.
- Defined benefit liabilities or assets recognized at the present value of the defined benefit obligation less the fair value of plan assets.

Ind AS is prescribed under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013, read with Rule 3 of the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015, and subsequent amendment rules.

Accounting policies have been applied consistently, except when a newly issued accounting standard is initially adopted or a revision to an existing standard requires a change in accounting policy. Material accounting policy information used in preparing the audited standalone financial statements is discussed in the respective notes.

Year-end figures are taken from the source and rounded to the nearest digits. Consequently, figures reported for previous quarters may not always add up to the reported year-end figures.

## 1.3 Use of estimates and judgments

Preparing financial statements in conformity with Ind AS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies and the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, contingent assets and liabilities, revenue, and expenses.

Critical accounting estimates and judgments involving complex or subjective assumptions are disclosed in Note 1.4. These estimates and judgments may change from period to period, and actual results may differ from the estimates. Changes in estimates are reflected in the period in which management becomes aware of changes in the relevant circumstances. If material, their effects are disclosed in the notes to the standalone financial statements.

## 1.4 Critical accounting estimates and judgments

## a. Revenue recognition

Customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is recognized and measured when:

- The contract has been approved in writing by the parties.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- The contract is legally enforceable.

The Company assesses the services promised in each contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying distinct performance obligations, determining the deliverables, assessing whether the customer can benefit independently from those deliverables, and allocating the transaction price to the performance obligations involve significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits from the services and the costs to fulfill the contract is uneven because the services are discrete rather than repetitive, revenue is recognized using a percentage-of-completion method. The method selected requires judgment based on the contractual promises and the nature of the deliverables.

For other fixed-price contracts, the Company uses the percentage-of-completion method. Progress toward completion is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs. Efforts or costs incurred are used as a measure of progress because there is a direct relationship between inputs and productivity. Estimated total efforts or costs are reassessed throughout the contract period using the latest available information.

Certain integrated service arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Company acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Company first assesses whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include primary responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other indicators of control.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts, if any, are recorded in the period when the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs required to complete the contracts.

## b. Income taxes

The Company’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States, although it also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is required to determine income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, management considers whether some or all of the assets may not be realized. Ultimate realization depends on generating future taxable income during the periods in which temporary differences become deductible. Management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax planning strategies.

Based on historical taxable income and projections for future taxable income during the periods in which deferred income tax assets are deductible, management believes the Company will realize the benefits of the deductible differences. The realizable amount could be reduced in the near term if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. (Refer to Note 2.17.)

## c. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Company’s asset base. Periodic depreciation is determined using estimates of an asset’s useful life and residual value at the end of its life.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. (Refer to Note 2.1.)

## 2. Notes to the Standalone Financial Statements

## 2.1 Property, plant and equipment

## Accounting Policy

Property, plant and equipment are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment, if any. Costs directly attributable to acquisition are capitalized until the assets are ready for use as intended by management.

Property, plant and equipment are depreciated over their estimated useful lives using the straight-line method. Estimated useful lives are as follows:

| Asset | Estimated useful life |
|---|---|
| Building (1) | 22–25 years |
| Plant and machinery (1) | 5 years |
| Office equipment | 5 years |
| Computer equipment (1) | 3–5 years |
| Furniture and fixtures (1) | 5 years |
| Vehicles (1) | 5 years |
| Leasehold improvements | Lower of the useful life of the asset or the lease term |

(1) Based on technical evaluation, management believes these useful lives best represent the period over which the assets are expected to be used. The useful lives for these assets therefore differ from those prescribed under Part C of Schedule II of the Companies Act, 2013.

Depreciation methods, useful lives, and residual values are reviewed periodically, including at each financial year-end. Useful lives are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events that may affect asset lives, such as changes in technology.

Advances paid toward the acquisition of property, plant and equipment outstanding at each balance sheet date are classified as capital advances under other non-current assets. The cost of assets not ready for use by that date is disclosed as capital work-in-progress.

Subsequent expenditure relating to property, plant and equipment is capitalized only when future economic benefits are probable and the cost can be measured reliably. The cost and related accumulated depreciation are removed from the financial statements upon sale or retirement of the asset.
