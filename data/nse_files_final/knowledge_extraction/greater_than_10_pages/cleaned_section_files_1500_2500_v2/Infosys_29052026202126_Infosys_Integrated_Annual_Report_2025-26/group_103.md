## 2.18 Revenue from operations

## Accounting policy

The Company derives revenues primarily from IT services, including software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting, package implementation, and licensing of software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings. Contracts with customers may be time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe arrangements.

Revenue is recognized when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable. Revenue is recognized when control of the promised products or services transfers to the customer, at an amount reflecting the consideration the Company expects to receive. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Company identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to each obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged when an item is sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. When this evidence is unavailable, the Company generally uses an expected-cost-plus-margin approach.

Contracts may include variable consideration, such as rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue recognized will not occur when the uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue from time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed. Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and fulfilment costs is uneven because services are discrete and non-repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts whose performance obligations are satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because of the direct relationship between inputs and productivity. Progress is measured as costs or efforts incurred to date divided by estimated total costs or efforts. Transaction price and total cost or effort estimates are continuously monitored and changes are recognized in net profit in the period of revision. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.

Billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

In software development, related services, and maintenance arrangements, software development and related services generally constitute distinct performance obligations. Revenue is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices, using the expected-cost-plus-margin approach when standalone selling prices cannot be determined. These performance obligations are satisfied as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, such as leasing guidance. When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected-cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period and progress is measured based on the contractual promise.

Revenue from licenses where the customer obtains a “right-to-use” license is recognized when the license is made available to the customer. Revenue from licenses where the customer obtains a “right-to-access” license is recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When the implementation standalone selling price is unavailable, the expected-cost-plus-margin approach is used. If the license requires substantial customization as part of implementation, the license and implementation fee is treated as a single performance obligation and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

Some integrated service arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Company acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Company evaluates control of the specified goods or services, including responsibility for fulfilment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors.

A contract modification is a change in contract scope, price, or both, approved by the parties. A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for as a separate contract when the additional services are priced at standalone selling prices. Otherwise, it is accounted for as termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract. A modification that does not create a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Company expects to recover them. Certain eligible, non-recurring setup, transition, or transformation costs that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as assets when they:

- relate directly to the contract;
- generate or enhance resources used to satisfy future performance obligations; and
- are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to expenses over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored for impairment, and impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover their carrying amount.

The Company presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Statement of Profit and Loss.

## Products & platforms

The Company derives revenue from the sale of products and platforms, including Infosys Applied AI, which applies next-generation artificial intelligence and machine learning.

Revenue from fixed-price contracts represented 58% of revenue in each of the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025.

## Trade receivables and Contract Balances

The timing of revenue recognition, billing, and cash collection results in trade receivables, unbilled revenue, and unearned revenue on the Company’s Balance Sheet. Amounts are billed as work progresses under agreed contractual terms, either periodically, such as monthly or quarterly, or upon achievement of contractual milestones.

Trade receivables are unconditional rights to consideration. Unbilled revenue from time-and-material contracts and fixed-price maintenance contracts is classified as a financial asset when the right to consideration is unconditional and payment is due only after the passage of time.

Billing for other fixed-price contracts is based on contractual milestones. Because revenue recognition may occur at a different time from invoicing, unbilled revenue for these contracts is classified as a non-financial contract asset when the right to consideration depends on completion of contractual milestones.

Invoicing in excess of earnings is classified as unearned revenue. Trade receivables and unbilled revenue are presented net of impairment in the Balance Sheet.

During the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025, the Company recognized revenue of ` 5,276 crore and ` 4,404 crore, respectively, from opening unearned revenue balances as of April 1, 2025 and April 1, 2024.

During the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025, ` 4,413 crore and ` 4,448 crore, respectively, of unbilled revenue relating to other fixed-price and fixed-timeframe contracts as of April 1, 2025 and April 1, 2024 was reclassified to trade receivables upon billing customers after completion of milestones.
