## 2.18 Revenue from operations

## Accounting policy

The Group derives revenues primarily from IT services comprising software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting and package implementation, licensing of software products and platforms across the Group’s core and digital offerings (together, “software-related services”), and business process management services.

Contracts with customers are entered into on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe basis.

Revenue from customer contracts is recognized when:

- the contract has been approved in writing by the parties;
- the parties are committed to performing their respective obligations; and
- the contract is legally enforceable.

Revenue is recognized upon transfer of control of promised products or services (“performance obligations”) to customers, at an amount reflecting the consideration the Group has received or expects to receive in exchange. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group assesses the services promised in a contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. The transaction price is allocated to each distinct performance obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. In the absence of such evidence, the Group generally estimates standalone selling price using the expected-cost-plus-margin approach, under which it estimates the cost of satisfying the performance obligation and adds an appropriate margin based on similar services.

Contracts may include variable consideration, including rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue recognized will not occur when the related uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue from time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably:

- on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period; or
- using a percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits from the services rendered and the Group’s costs to fulfill the contract are uneven because the services are generally discrete in nature and not repetitive.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts in which performance obligations are satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Efforts or costs expended are used to measure progress toward completion because there is a direct relationship between inputs and productivity. Progress is measured as the ratio of costs or efforts incurred to date, representing work performed, to estimated total costs or efforts.

Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are continuously monitored over the contract term. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates change or are revised. Revenues and estimated total costs or efforts are subject to revision as the contract progresses. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded in the period in which the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

Billing schedules agreed with customers include periodic performance-based billing and/or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

In arrangements for software development, related services, and maintenance services, the arrangements generally meet the criteria for treating software development and related services as distinct performance obligations. Revenue is allocated to each performance obligation based on its relative standalone selling price. Where the standalone selling price cannot be determined, the expected-cost-plus-margin approach is used.

For software development and related services, performance obligations are satisfied as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements that may be subject to specific accounting guidance, such as leasing guidance. These contracts are accounted for in accordance with the applicable specific guidance.

When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated to them based on their relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices are not available, the expected-cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is considered a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period, with progress measured based on the promise in the contract.

Revenue from licenses is recognized as follows:

- Licenses providing the customer with a “right-to-use” are recognized when the licenses are made available to the customer.
- Licenses providing the customer with a “right-to-access” are recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS).

When implementation services are provided with the licensing arrangement and the license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated to each performance obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. When the standalone selling price for implementation cannot be determined, the expected-cost-plus-margin approach is used.

When the license requires substantial customization as part of the implementation service, the entire arrangement fee for the license and implementation is treated as a single performance obligation. Revenue is then recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed.

Revenue from client training, support, and other services arising from the sale of software products is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the period in which the services are rendered.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent between the customer and vendor, and gross when the Group is the principal.

The Group first evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before they are transferred to the customer. Factors considered include whether the Group is primarily responsible for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors indicating whether the Group controls the specified goods or services.

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price, or both, of a contract that is approved by the parties.

- A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for as a separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price.
- If the additional services are not priced at their standalone selling price, the modification is accounted for as a termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract.
- If the modification does not result in a distinct performance obligation, it is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract, meaning costs that would not have been incurred if the contract had not been obtained, are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them.

Certain eligible, non-recurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as an asset when they:

- relate directly to the contract;
- generate or enhance resources that the Group will use to satisfy performance obligations in the future; and
- are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to expenses over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services to the customer.

Capitalized costs are regularly monitored for impairment. Impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount of the capitalized costs.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss.

## Products and platforms

The Group derives revenue from products and platforms including Finacle, a core banking solution; the Edge Suite of products; the Panaya platform; the Stater digital platform; and Infosys McCamish, an insurance platform.
