# Guidance, AI Productivity, Headcount, and Subcontractor Outlook

## Guidance range and growth outlook

Management stated that the fiscal-year guidance was provided in a 200-basis-point band, compared with a 3-point range in the prior year. The narrower range reflects greater visibility into client behavior after several quarters of observing the effects of tariff changes. Management noted that client behavior could still change if the environment changes.

The guidance is approximately 2.25% organic growth at the midpoint, compared with 2.4% organic growth in Fiscal '26. Management identified the following factors in the guidance:

- Strong growth and traction in AI services.
- A program targeting large companies where Infosys currently has a smaller footprint, with expansion progressing well.
- Large deals with 55% net-new business, expected to contribute significantly during the fiscal year.
- Productivity benefits from AI on existing client portfolios, which clients are seeking and which create compression.
- Specific factors related to Manufacturing in Europe and the onsite mix.

Management described the outlook as a balance between AI-related and other growth drivers on one side and productivity-related compression on the other. The prior year’s reported growth was 3.1%; after adjusting for prior-year one-time items, management said the underlying growth rate was greater than the compression observed. Management said the current-year guidance also reflects growth that is not offset by compression.

Management described the large-deal pipeline as good and the overall environment as favorable. It also stated that it had not seen a major change requiring a wider 3-point guidance range.

## Potential growth acceleration

Management stated that organic growth could accelerate if:

- The situation in the Middle East reaches a favorable resolution.
- Underlying economic trends remain strong in Infosys's major markets, creating a more stable macroeconomic environment.
- AI traction and partnerships continue to perform well.

Management said the tariff situation became better understood as the prior year progressed. The subsequent war had a constraining effect on the macroeconomic environment. Although there was a general expectation of a resolution and economic indicators were forecast to improve, management said the improvement had not yet fully entered the operating environment.

## AI productivity and margin compression

Management said AI services activity was strong, while competitive intensity remained high. It had not observed a specific change in productivity compression over the preceding one or two quarters attributable solely to recent model and technology advances. However, competitors periodically demonstrated capabilities that appeared outside management’s current expectations of what the models could do.

Management did not disclose the proportion of services directly exposed to productivity pass-throughs. It referred to previously disclosed service-line data as a basis for estimating exposure.

Productivity improvements are largely expected to be passed back to clients because of competitive intensity in the market. This pass-through can affect margins even when productivity improves.

## Headcount and hiring

Infosys plans to recruit approximately 20,000 college graduates for fiscal '27. The hiring model includes some recruitment at a particular time and the remainder throughout the year, providing flexibility if demand changes.

Management said demand for higher-level talent and lateral recruitment remained strong for the current and following quarter and was expected to continue. It did not have a plan for total headcount to be lower at the end of the year, while noting that hiring would depend on how the demand environment develops.

## Subcontractor usage

Subcontractor usage as a percentage of revenue has declined over the last few years. Subcontractors are typically used to meet immediate demand when Infosys does not yet have the required employees or skill sets; employees are then recruited to backfill that work.

Management did not expect subcontractor usage to change significantly from current levels in the near term. Over the medium term, it expected subcontractor usage as a percentage of revenue to decline.
