## Mansee Dave

Mansee Dave asked about margin sustainability despite multiple headwinds and about the outlook for FY27 amid geopolitical tensions and cautious client spending. She asked whether Infosys expects growth acceleration or another year of consolidation.

## Salil Parekh

Salil Parekh said Infosys’ margin expansion and margin protection program has been a key reason for resilient margins. He said the program has been executed consistently over several years and that the company remained confident in its guidance of a 20%–22% margin for the year.

At the start of the calendar year, the global environment and growth in Infosys’ strong markets appeared favorable. Markets where Infosys is expanding, including Japan, were also growing well. The situation changed after the Iran war affected the economic environment, although Parekh said there appeared to be paths toward stabilization.

Based on discussions with clients and market participants, Parekh said the underlying resilience of major economies remained strong. He also cited continued investment, AI growth, and Infosys’ strategic approach to AI services. The company’s growth guidance was based on conditions visible at the time, with the possibility that conditions could change during the year.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Jayesh Sanghrajka said Infosys expanded margins by 50 basis points in the first year of its margin program. In the current year, the company maintained a 21% margin despite absorbing multiple headwinds and investing in the business.

Sales and marketing costs increased by 40 basis points. Infosys also invested in AI-related capabilities, AI partnerships, and talent. Sanghrajka said these investments were absorbed while delivering a 21% margin.

He identified market competitiveness and acquisition-related costs as additional headwinds. Acquisition-related costs are expected to affect margins by another 60–70 basis points. Infosys’ objective remains to improve margins over the medium to long term, while its margin confidence for the year remains 20%–22%.

## Chandra R. Srikanth

Chandra R. Srikanth asked why Infosys was confident about the Financial Services, Energy, and Utilities sectors despite client-specific issues and continued challenges in discretionary spending. He also asked about the normal business, the AI business, AI revenue, potential acquisitions of AI start-ups, the impact of Mythos, wage hikes, and the decline in employee headcount.

## Salil Parekh

Parekh said that across several industry groups, Infosys is already the AI partner of choice for many of its largest clients. He specifically mentioned Financial Services, Energy, Utilities, Manufacturing, and Telecommunications.

Infosys has large programs in its pipeline involving:

- Building AI agents
- Legacy modernization
- Combined technology services and operations

Parekh said the pipeline appeared consistent with the company’s existing client relationships and had not changed suddenly.

Infosys had completed two acquisitions. Parekh said the company evaluates acquisitions based on strategic fit, cultural fit, value fit, and integration requirements. The acquisition pipeline remained active, although the number of transactions could vary significantly between periods.

The healthcare acquisition was intended to expand Infosys’ presence in a market where it already had a strong business. The insurance acquisition involved a company working with the Guidewire platform, an area Infosys wanted to expand.

Regarding Mythos, Parekh said the company appeared to expose more vulnerabilities than previously expected, although other models were also exposing vulnerabilities. He said this could create opportunities for Infosys to help clients identify and protect against such vulnerabilities. Discussions were described as very early.

## Chandra R. Srikanth

Chandra R. Srikanth asked about AI revenue.

## Salil Parekh

Parekh said Infosys was not sharing the current AI revenue figure but confirmed that it was growing. He said the company had disclosed AI revenue during its AI Investor Day to communicate that it was material and growing, but would not disclose the current percentage. He also declined to confirm whether AI revenue had reached 10% of Infosys’ total revenue.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys’ sequential headcount declined by 8,000 employees, but headcount increased by 5,000 employees year over year. Sanghrajka said quarterly seasonality affects headcount, which also depends on employee numbers, utilization, and business volumes.

Volumes were softer during the quarter, affecting the demand-and-supply equation and net hiring, including the addition of freshers. Sanghrajka said the company did not expect headcount to decline sequentially every quarter and that the full-year view remained more relevant.

Infosys had not yet decided the quantum or timing of wage hikes. The company would communicate the decision once made.
