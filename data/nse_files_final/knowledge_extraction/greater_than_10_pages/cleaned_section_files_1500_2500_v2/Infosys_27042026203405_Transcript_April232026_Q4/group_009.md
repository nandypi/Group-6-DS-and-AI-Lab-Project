## Salil Parekh

Productivity compression in deals depends on the mix of first-year, second-year, and later-year contracts. Productivity benefits may be spread across multi-year deals, including three-year and five-year deals, rather than occurring entirely in the first year.

Infosys has assessed what foundation models and other tools can support and is using that understanding when structuring forward deals. The effectiveness of different models and tools varies across different parts of the AI work conducted for clients.

## Vibhor Singhal

For recent deals, Infosys was asked whether clients had returned to request additional productivity benefits after deals were signed six or twelve months earlier.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys has not seen situations in which clients returned a few months after signing a deal and requested additional productivity assumptions to be incorporated. The productivity discussion applies when a deal comes up for bid or when Infosys is bidding for a new deal.

## Vibhor Singhal

The quarter benefited from INR depreciation. The question was whether the temporary margin benefit from INR depreciation had stopped accruing, was being invested elsewhere, or was being passed immediately to clients.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Rupee benefits are sometimes, and most of the time, offset by cross-currency headwinds. When the US dollar appreciates against most currencies, the resulting effects offset each other. As the non-US portfolio grows, this offset becomes larger.

The US previously represented more than 70%–75% of the business, but that share has declined significantly, increasing the impact of headwinds from other currencies.

For the quarter, acquisition-related intangible amortization created a headwind of approximately 50 basis points. The previous quarter had included a 30-basis-point gain, so the comparable effects moved in opposite directions and both affected the margin walk. Employee-related costs created an additional 20-basis-point headwind. These headwinds were offset by a 40-basis-point currency benefit and a 30-basis-point contribution from Project Maximus.

## Abhishek Pathak

The question concerned deals left on the table and whether Infosys was being disrupted by leaner, AI-native companies with lower pricing, changing delivery models, or irrational pricing by traditional vendors.

## Salil Parekh

Unusual competitor pricing has occurred in individual cases, but it is not widespread. The pricing may sometimes be associated with client expectations regarding AI productivity, although similar situations have occurred for other reasons in the past.

Infosys signed 96 large deals with a total contract value close to $15 billion during the last financial year. The outcome was broad-based, and the pipeline remained strong. In some cases, productivity assumptions appeared outside the range indicated by Infosys’s understanding of what foundation models can support, but Infosys does not view this as a broad industry trend.

## Keith Bachman

The question concerned whether pricing competitiveness was higher than in previous years and whether the uncertain cost curves associated with deploying AI were creating additional pricing risk.

## Salil Parekh

Competitive intensity has increased. Infosys grew during the last financial year while some other players reported negative revenue.

Overall realization was better during the year than previously. This reflected execution and a portfolio that Infosys considered less risky.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Despite softer volume during the year, most growth came from realization. This reflected the value created for clients through AI and, to some extent, contributions from Project Maximus, including lean automation and value-based selling.

Despite market competitiveness, Infosys maintained its margins for the year. It reinvested approximately 50 basis points, or roughly 40 basis points, in sales and marketing, AI talent, AI capabilities, and other AI-related investments. These investments were absorbed while maintaining margins.

## Keith Bachman

The question concerned how AI efficiency gains on the supply side could change the growth algorithm, particularly the volume growth required to achieve a given level of revenue growth.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys expects some deflation in existing services, with much of the impact being offset by new AI-driven services. Overall volumes remained broadly flat during the last year.

Going forward, Infosys expects volumes to remain flat or marginally positive, consistent with the lower end of its guidance. The upper end assumes a better macroeconomic environment, which would result in better volumes.
