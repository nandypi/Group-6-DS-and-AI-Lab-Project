## Information Other than the Financial Statements and Auditor's Report Thereon

The Company's Board of Directors is responsible for preparing the other information, comprising:

- Management Discussion and Analysis
- Board's Report and its annexures
- Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report
- Corporate Governance and Shareholder's Information

The other information excludes the Consolidated Financial Statements, standalone financial statements, and the auditor's report thereon.

The auditor's opinion on the Consolidated Financial Statements does not cover the other information. During the audit, the auditors read the other information and considered whether it was materially inconsistent with the Consolidated Financial Statements, their audit knowledge, or otherwise materially misstated.

The auditors reported that they had nothing to report regarding material misstatements in the other information.

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Consolidated Financial Statements

The Company's Board of Directors is responsible under Section 134(5) of the Act for preparing and presenting Consolidated Financial Statements that give a true and fair view of the Group's:

- Consolidated financial position
- Consolidated financial performance, including other comprehensive income
- Consolidated changes in equity
- Consolidated cash flows

The statements are prepared in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in India, including Ind AS specified under Section 133 of the Act.

The respective Boards of Directors or Trustees of entities included in the Group are responsible for:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records under the Act
- Safeguarding Group assets
- Preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining effective internal financial controls
- Ensuring the accuracy and completeness of accounting records
- Preparing financial statements that are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error

Management is also responsible for assessing each entity's ability to continue as a going concern, making applicable going-concern disclosures, and using the going-concern basis of accounting unless liquidation or cessation of operations is intended or unavoidable.

The respective Boards of Directors or Trustees are responsible for overseeing the Group's financial reporting process.

## Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit of the Consolidated Financial Statements

The auditors' objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance that the Consolidated Financial Statements are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report containing their opinion. Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but is not a guarantee that an audit will detect every material misstatement.

As part of the audit, the auditors:

- Identify and assess risks of material misstatement, and design audit procedures responsive to those risks.
- Obtain sufficient and appropriate audit evidence.
- Obtain an understanding of internal financial controls relevant to the audit.
- Under Section 143(3)(i) of the Act, assess whether the Company and its Indian-incorporated subsidiary companies have adequate internal financial controls relating to the Consolidated Financial Statements and whether those controls operated effectively.
- Evaluate the appropriateness of accounting policies, the reasonableness of accounting estimates, and related disclosures.
- Assess the appropriateness of management's use of the going-concern basis of accounting.
- Determine whether a material uncertainty exists relating to events or conditions that could cast significant doubt on the Group's ability to continue as a going concern.
- Evaluate the overall presentation, structure, and content of the Consolidated Financial Statements.
- Assess whether the statements represent the underlying transactions and events in a manner that achieves fair presentation.
- Obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the financial information of entities within the Group.

The risk of failing to detect a material misstatement resulting from fraud is higher than for an error because fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or management override of internal controls.

If the auditors conclude that a material uncertainty exists regarding going concern, they are required to draw attention to the related disclosures or modify their opinion if those disclosures are inadequate. Their conclusions are based on audit evidence obtained up to the date of the auditor's report; future events or conditions may cause the Group to cease to continue as a going concern.

Materiality is the magnitude of misstatements that, individually or in aggregate, could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of users of the Consolidated Financial Statements. The auditors consider quantitative materiality and qualitative factors when:

1. Planning the scope of audit work
2. Evaluating audit results
3. Assessing the effect of identified misstatements

The auditors communicate with those charged with governance regarding the planned scope and timing of the audit, significant audit findings, and significant deficiencies in internal financial controls identified during the audit.

They also provide those charged with governance with a statement confirming compliance with relevant ethical requirements relating to independence, and communicate relationships and other matters that may reasonably be considered relevant to independence, along with related safeguards where applicable.

The auditors determine which matters were of most significance in the current-period audit and therefore constitute key audit matters. Such matters are described in the auditor's report unless disclosure is prohibited by law or regulation, or the adverse consequences of disclosure would reasonably be expected to outweigh the public-interest benefits.

## Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements

### 1. Section 143(3) of the Act

Based on the audit, the auditors reported that:

- They sought and obtained all information and explanations necessary for the audit of the Consolidated Financial Statements.
- Proper books of account and relevant records required by law were maintained by the Group.
- The Consolidated Balance Sheet, Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss including Other Comprehensive Income, Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity, and Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows agreed with the relevant books of account.
- The Consolidated Financial Statements complied with the Ind AS specified under Section 133 of the Act.
- Based on written representations received from the directors as of March 31, 2026, taken on record by the Board, and reports of statutory auditors of Indian-incorporated subsidiary companies, none of the directors of Group companies incorporated in India was disqualified as of March 31, 2026 under Section 164(2) of the Act.
- The separate report in Annexure A expressed an unmodified opinion on the adequacy and operating effectiveness of internal financial controls relating to the Consolidated Financial Statements of the Company and its Indian-incorporated subsidiary companies.
- Director remuneration paid by the Company during the year complied with Section 197 of the Act.

### 2. Rule 11 of the Companies (Audit and Auditors) Rules, 2014

The auditors reported that:

- The Consolidated Financial Statements disclosed the impact of pending litigations on the Group's consolidated financial position. Refer to Note 2.24 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.
- The Group made provisions required under applicable law or accounting standards for material foreseeable losses. Refer to Note 2.16 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.
- The Group did not have any long-term derivative contracts.
- There was no delay in transferring amounts required to be transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund by the Company and its Indian-incorporated subsidiary companies.
- Management of the Company and its Indian-incorporated subsidiaries represented that, to the best of their knowledge and belief:
  - No funds were advanced, loaned, or invested, from borrowed funds, share premium, or any other source, to persons or entities outside the Group, including foreign entities, with an understanding that intermediaries would lend or invest in ultimate beneficiaries identified by or on behalf of the Company or its subsidiaries, or provide guarantees, securities, or similar support on behalf of such beneficiaries.
  - No funds were received from persons or entities, including foreign entities, with an understanding that the Company or its subsidiaries would lend or invest in ultimate beneficiaries identified by or on behalf of the funding parties, or provide guarantees, securities, or similar support on behalf of such beneficiaries.
- Based on audit procedures considered reasonable and appropriate, nothing came to the auditors' attention indicating that these representations contained any material misstatement.

- As stated in Note 2.12 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.
