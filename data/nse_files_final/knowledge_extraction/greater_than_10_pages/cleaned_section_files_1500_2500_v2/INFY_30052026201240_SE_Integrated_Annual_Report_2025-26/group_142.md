## 2.18 Revenue from operations

## Accounting policy

The Group derives revenues primarily from:

- IT services, including software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting, package implementation, and licensing of software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings;
- business process management services; and
- products and platforms.

Customer contracts may be based on time and material, unit of work, fixed price, or a fixed timeframe.

Revenue is recognized when a customer contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable. Revenue is recognized when control of the promised products or services is transferred to the customer, at an amount reflecting the consideration the Group expects to receive. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies the distinct performance obligations in each contract and allocates the transaction price to those obligations based on their relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. When that evidence is unavailable, the Group generally estimates standalone selling price using expected cost plus an appropriate margin.

Contracts may include variable consideration, including rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue when the uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue recognition by contract type is as follows:

- Revenue from time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and related costs is uneven because the services are discrete, revenue is recognized using a percentage-of-completion method.
- Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts whose performance obligations are satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Progress is measured using costs or efforts incurred to date relative to estimated total costs or efforts.
- Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are monitored throughout the contract. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which they arise. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.
- Billing may be based on periodic performance or milestones. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development and related services, performance obligations are generally distinct and are satisfied as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses. Transaction prices are allocated based on relative standalone selling prices, using the expected cost-plus-margin approach when standalone selling prices cannot be determined.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, such as leasing guidance. Where hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period based on the measure of progress specified in the contract.

Revenue from licenses is recognized as follows:

- Licenses providing a right to use are recognized when the licenses are made available to the customer.
- Licenses providing a right to access are recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include a license, implementation services, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When the license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on their relative standalone selling prices. When the license is substantially customized as part of implementation, the license and implementation fees are treated as one performance obligation and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

In integrated services arrangements involving subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as principal. The Group assesses control by considering whether it is primarily responsible for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other relevant factors.

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price of a contract, or both, approved by the parties. A modification that adds distinct performance obligations is accounted for as a separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling prices. Otherwise, it is accounted for as a termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract. A modification that does not add a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them. Eligible non-recurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, are recognized as an asset when they relate directly to the contract, generate or enhance resources used to satisfy future performance obligations, and are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to expenses over the contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored for impairment, and impairment losses are recorded when projected remaining operating cash flows are insufficient to recover their carrying amount.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss.

## Products and platforms

The Group derives revenue from products and platforms including Finacle, a core banking solution; the Edge Suite of products; the Panaya platform; the Stater digital platform; and Infosys McCamish, an insurance platform.
