# Income Tax

The applicable Indian corporate statutory tax rate was 25.17% for the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025.

Income tax expense included a reversal, net of provisions, of `877 crore for the year ended March 31, 2026 and provisions, net of reversals, of `132 crore for the year ended March 31, 2025. These prior-period provisions and reversals primarily resulted from the adjudication of disputed matters, tax-return filings, and completion of assessments across various jurisdictions.

During the year ended March 31, 2026, Infosys received orders under Sections 250 and 254 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, from the Income Tax Authorities in India for assessment year 2013-14 and assessment years 2017-18 to 2021-22. The orders confirmed the Company’s position on the tax treatment of certain matters. Consequently, the Company recognized pre-tax interest income of `381 crore and reversed income tax provisions totaling `869 crore, with a corresponding credit to the Statement of Profit and Loss. The resolution of these disputes also reduced contingent liabilities by `86 crore.

During the year ended March 31, 2025, Infosys received orders under Section 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, for assessment years 2016-17 and 2019-20. These orders confirmed the Company’s position on the tax treatment of certain matters. Consequently, the Company recognized pre-tax interest income of `327 crore and reversed income tax provisions totaling `183 crore, with a corresponding credit to the Statement of Profit and Loss. Contingent liabilities were reduced by `1,068 crore following resolution of the disputes.

Foreign tax expense primarily relates to income taxes payable overseas, principally in the United States.

In India, the Group benefited in prior years from tax incentives provided for exports of software and services by units registered under the Special Economic Zones (SEZs) Act, 2005. Eligible SEZ units that began providing services on or after April 1, 2005 could claim:

- A deduction of 100% of profits or gains from export services for the first five years from the financial year in which service provision commenced.
- A deduction of 50% of such profits or gains for the subsequent five years.
- A further deduction of up to 50% for another five years, subject to creating a Special Economic Zone Re-investment Reserve and using it to acquire new plant and machinery for business purposes in accordance with the Income Tax Act, 1961.

Deferred income tax for the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025 substantially related to the origination and reversal of temporary differences.

Infosys is subject to a 15% US Branch Profit Tax (BPT) when the net profit of its US branch during the year exceeds the increase in the branch’s net assets during the year, calculated in accordance with the Internal Revenue Code.

As at March 31, 2026, Infosys’ US branch net assets were approximately `7,736 crore. The Company recognized a deferred tax liability for Branch Profit Tax of `207 crore, net of credits, because it estimated that the branch profits would be distributed in the foreseeable future.

Deferred income tax liabilities were not recognized on temporary differences associated with investments in subsidiaries and branches amounting to `19,270 crore as at March 31, 2026 and `16,593 crore as at March 31, 2025. The Company can control the timing of reversal of these temporary differences and considers it probable that they will not reverse in the foreseeable future. The Group generally intends to repatriate earnings from subsidiaries and branches only to the extent that they can be distributed in a tax-free manner.

Deferred income tax assets were not recognized on accumulated losses of `4,868 crore as at March 31, 2026 and `4,597 crore as at March 31, 2025 because it was probable that future taxable profit would not be available against which the unused tax losses could be utilized in the foreseeable future.
