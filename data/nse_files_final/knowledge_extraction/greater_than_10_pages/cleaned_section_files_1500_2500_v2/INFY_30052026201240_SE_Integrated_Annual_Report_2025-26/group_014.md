## Employee stock options / Restricted Stock Units (RSUs) / Performance stock units (PSUs)

The Company grants share-based benefits to eligible employees to attract and retain talent, align employee performance with Company objectives, and encourage participation in the Company's growth.

## Infosys Expanded Stock Ownership Program 2019 ('the 2019 Plan')

On June 22, 2019, following shareholder approval at the AGM, the Board was authorized to introduce, offer, issue, and provide share-based incentives to eligible employees of the Company and its subsidiaries under the 2019 Plan.

The maximum number of shares under the 2019 Plan shall not exceed 5,00,00,000 equity shares. To implement the plan, up to 4,50,00,000 equity shares may be issued through secondary acquisition of shares by the Infosys Expanded Stock Ownership Trust.

RSUs granted under the 2019 Plan vest based on defined annual performance parameters determined by the administrator, the Nomination and Remuneration Committee. These parameters are based on a combination of:

- Relative Total Shareholder Return (TSR) against selected industry peers.
- TSR against selected broader domestic and global market indices.
- Operating performance metrics of the Company.

Each performance parameter is distinct for calculating the number of shares to vest. Instruments generally vest between a minimum of one year and a maximum of three years from the grant date.

Based on the recommendation of the Nomination and Remuneration Committee, the Board approved an amendment to extend the grant period by seven years from the date of shareholder approval. This would extend the plan's validity to a total period of ten years from shareholder approval for granting stock incentives to eligible employees of the Company and its subsidiaries. The amendment is subject to shareholder approval and is described in the explanatory statement to the Notice of the 45th Annual General Meeting.

## 2015 Stock Incentive Compensation Plan ('the 2015 Plan')

On March 31, 2016, following shareholder approval through postal ballot, the Board was authorized to introduce, offer, issue, and allot share-based incentives to eligible employees of the Company and its subsidiaries under the 2015 Plan.

The maximum number of shares under the 2015 Plan shall not exceed 2,40,38,883 equity shares, not adjusted for the bonus issue. The instruments generally vest over four years and are exercisable within the period approved by the administrator.

- The exercise price of RSUs is equal to the par value of the shares.
- The exercise price of stock options is the market price on the date of grant.

Following the September 2018 bonus issue, all outstanding options granted under the stock option plan were adjusted for bonus shares.

The total number of equity shares and American Depositary Receipts (ADRs) allotted to employees of the Company and its subsidiaries under the 2015 Plan does not cumulatively exceed 1% of issued capital. For shares and ADRs issued under the 2019 Plan, the cumulative amount does not exceed 1.15% of issued capital.

The 2019 Plan and 2015 Plan comply with the SEBI (Share Based Employee Benefits and Sweat Equity) Regulations, 2021, as amended. There was no material change to the plans during fiscal 2026, except for the proposed changes to the 2019 Plan described above.

Details of both plans, including their terms of reference and requirements under Regulation 14 of the SEBI (Share Based Employee Benefits and Sweat Equity) Regulations, 2021, are available in the Company's disclosures pursuant to SEBI regulations. The plans also form part of the Notes to Accounts in this Integrated Annual Report.

## 4. Corporate governance

## Our corporate governance philosophy

Infosys' corporate governance practices are based on its value system, culture, policies, and stakeholder relationships. The Company states that integrity and transparency are central to its corporate governance practices and that its governance approach seeks to maximize shareholder value legally, ethically, and sustainably while respecting minority rights.

The Board exercises its fiduciary responsibilities broadly, and the Company's disclosures seek to follow international corporate governance practices. The Corporate governance report for fiscal 2026 forms part of this Integrated Annual Report.

## Board diversity

The Company recognizes Board diversity across thought, perspective, regional and industry experience, cultural and geographical background, age, ethnicity, race, gender, knowledge, and skills.

The Board Diversity Policy covers expertise including finance, global business, leadership, information technology, mergers and acquisitions, Board service and governance, sales and marketing, Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG), risk management, and cybersecurity.

Additional details on Board diversity are provided in the Corporate governance report forming part of this Integrated Annual Report.

## Number of meetings of the Board

The Board met eight times during the financial year. The maximum interval between any two meetings did not exceed 120 days, as prescribed by the Act.

## Policy on directors' appointment and remuneration

The Company's policy is to maintain an appropriate mix of executive, non-executive, and independent directors to preserve Board independence and separate governance from management.

As of March 31, 2026, the Board had nine members:

- One executive director.
- One non-executive and non-independent director.
- Seven independent directors, including two women.

After fiscal 2026, Diane Enberg Jurgens was appointed as an Additional and Independent Director with effect from April 22, 2026, subject to shareholder approval.

The Company's policy on directors' appointment and remuneration covers qualifications, positive attributes, director independence, and other matters required under Section 178(3) of the Act. The Company states that director remuneration is paid according to the Nomination and Remuneration Policy.

## Declaration by independent directors

The Company received the required declaration from each independent director confirming compliance with the independence criteria under Section 149(6), the Code for Independent Directors under the Act, and Regulation 16(1)(b) of the Listing Regulations.

## Board evaluation

The Nomination and Remuneration Committee engaged Egon Zehnder as an external consultant to conduct the Board evaluation for the year.

The evaluation covered all directors, Board committees, the Chairman of the Board, and the Board as a whole. It was conducted using criteria and a framework adopted by the Board and was completed during fiscal 2026. The evaluation parameters and process are described in the Corporate governance report.

## Familiarization program for independent directors

All new independent directors attend an orientation program. At appointment, the Company issues a formal letter describing the independent director's role, functions, duties, and responsibilities.

Details of the training and familiarization program are provided in the Corporate governance report.

## Committees of the Board

As of March 31, 2026, the Board had six committees:

- Audit Committee
- Corporate Social Responsibility Committee
- Nomination and Remuneration Committee
- Risk Management Committee
- Stakeholders Relationship Committee
- Environment, Social and Governance (ESG) Committee

All committees comprised only independent directors, with one independent director serving as chairperson of each committee.

A Cybersecurity Risk Sub-Committee of the Risk Management Committee was constituted to assess and enhance preparedness to mitigate cybersecurity risks. The subcommittee comprised only independent directors, with one serving as chairperson.

All recommendations made by the committees during the year were approved by the Board.

Detailed information on the composition of the Board and its committees is provided in the Corporate governance report forming part of this Integrated Annual Report.
