## Independent Auditor's Report

To the Members of Infosys Limited

## Report on the Audit of the Standalone Financial Statements

## Opinion

The auditors audited the standalone financial statements of Infosys Limited (the “Company”), comprising:

- Balance Sheet as at March 31, 2026
- Statement of Profit and Loss, including Other Comprehensive Income, for the year ended March 31, 2026
- Statement of Changes in Equity for the year ended March 31, 2026
- Statement of Cash Flows for the year ended March 31, 2026
- Notes to the financial statements, including material accounting policies and other explanatory information

In the auditors’ opinion, the standalone financial statements provide the information required by the Companies Act, 2013, and give a true and fair view, in conformity with the Indian Accounting Standards prescribed under Section 133 of the Act (Ind AS) and other generally accepted accounting principles in India, of:

- The state of affairs of the Company as at March 31, 2026
- Its profit and other comprehensive income for the year ended on that date
- Its changes in equity for the year ended on that date
- Its cash flows for the year ended on that date

## Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under Section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013. The auditors stated that they were independent of the Company in accordance with the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India’s Code of Ethics and applicable ethical requirements under the Act and related rules.

The auditors stated that they had fulfilled their other ethical responsibilities and that the audit evidence obtained was sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their opinion.

## Key Audit Matters

Key audit matters were those matters that, in the auditors’ professional judgment, were of most significance in the audit of the standalone financial statements for the current period. They were addressed in the context of the standalone financial statements as a whole, and no separate opinion was provided on them.

### 1. Revenue recognition

The Company’s customer contracts include multiple products and services. Revenue from IT services comprises:

- Software development and related services
- Maintenance
- Consulting and package implementation
- Licensing of software products and platforms across the Company’s core and digital offerings
- Business process management services

The Company assesses the services promised in a contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying distinct performance obligations, deliverables, and whether customers can benefit independently from those deliverables involves significant judgment.

For integrated services arrangements involving subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Company acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Company evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer.

The assessment of principal-versus-agent status considers whether the Company is primarily responsible for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors indicating control over the products or services.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using either:

- A straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period; or
- The percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of customer benefits and the Company’s fulfilment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.

The selection of the recognition method requires judgment based on the contractual promises and the nature of the deliverables. Revenue recognition was identified as a key audit matter because of the judgments involved in:

1. Identifying distinct performance obligations
2. Determining whether the Company acts as a principal or an agent
3. Determining whether fixed-price maintenance revenue should be recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method

Refer to Notes 1.4 and 2.18 to the Standalone Financial Statements.

#### Auditor’s Response

The principal audit procedures included:

- Testing the effectiveness of controls over identifying distinct performance obligations, determining principal-versus-agent status, and selecting the revenue-recognition method for fixed-price maintenance contracts.
- Selecting customer contracts and reading the contract documents, including master service agreements and other documents forming part of the agreements.
- Identifying significant contractual terms and deliverables to assess management’s conclusions regarding performance obligations, principal-versus-agent status, and the revenue-recognition method.

### 2. Revenue recognition — Fixed-price contracts using the percentage-of-completion method

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably either:

1. On a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period; or
2. Using the percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and fulfilment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts, where performance obligations are satisfied over time, is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.

Under this method, actual efforts or costs incurred to date are determined as a proportion of the estimated total efforts or costs required. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress toward completion because of the direct relationship between input and productivity.

Estimating total efforts or costs involves significant judgment and is reassessed throughout the contract period using the latest available information. This matter was identified as a key audit matter because estimating total efforts or costs to complete fixed-price contracts involves significant judgment and inherent uncertainty. The assessment considers:

- Progress of the contract
- Efforts or costs incurred to date
- Estimated efforts or costs required to complete the remaining performance obligations

The matter required a high degree of auditor judgment and additional audit effort to evaluate the reasonableness of the total estimated revenue recognized on fixed-price contracts.

Refer to Notes 1.4 and 2.18 to the Standalone Financial Statements.

#### Auditor’s Response

The principal audit procedures included:

- Testing controls over recording efforts or costs incurred and estimating efforts or costs required to complete remaining contract performance obligations.
- Testing access and application controls for time-recording, allocation, and budgeting systems that prevent unauthorized changes to recorded efforts incurred.
- Selecting a sample of fixed-price contracts measured using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Evaluating management’s ability to estimate progress toward satisfying performance obligations by comparing actual efforts or costs incurred with prior-year estimates or budgeted efforts and costs for fulfilled obligations.
- Comparing efforts or costs incurred with the Company’s estimates to identify significant variations and assessing whether those variations were appropriately considered in estimating remaining costs or efforts to complete the contracts.
- Testing estimates for consistency with delivery milestones and customer acceptances and sign-offs to identify possible delays requiring changes to estimated costs or efforts needed to complete the remaining performance obligations.
