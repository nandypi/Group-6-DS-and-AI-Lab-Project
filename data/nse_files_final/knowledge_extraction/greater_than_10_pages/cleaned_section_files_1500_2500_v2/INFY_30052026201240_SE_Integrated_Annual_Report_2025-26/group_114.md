## Independent Auditor's Report

To The Members of Infosys Limited

## Report on the Audit of the Consolidated Financial Statements

## Opinion

We audited the consolidated financial statements of INFOSYS LIMITED (the “Company”) and its subsidiaries, together referred to as the “Group.” The financial statements comprise:

- The Consolidated Balance Sheet as at March 31, 2026.
- The Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss, including Other Comprehensive Income, for the year ended March 31, 2026.
- The Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity.
- The Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows.
- Notes to the financial statements, including material accounting policies and other explanatory information.

In the auditors’ opinion, the consolidated financial statements:

- Give the information required under the Companies Act, 2013.
- Present a true and fair view of the Group’s consolidated state of affairs as at March 31, 2026.
- Fairly present the Group’s consolidated profit, other comprehensive income, changes in equity, and cash flows for the year ended March 31, 2026.
- Conform with Indian Accounting Standards prescribed under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013, and other generally accepted accounting principles in India.

## Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under Section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013.

The auditors stated that:

- They were independent of the Group in accordance with the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India and relevant legal and ethical requirements.
- They fulfilled their other ethical responsibilities under these requirements.
- The audit evidence obtained was sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their opinion.

## Key Audit Matters

Key audit matters were those matters that, in the auditors’ professional judgment, were of most significance in the audit of the current-period consolidated financial statements. They were addressed in the context of the financial statements as a whole, and no separate opinion was provided on them.

### 1. Revenue recognition

The Group’s customer contracts may include multiple products and services. Revenue is derived from:

- Software development and related IT services.
- Maintenance.
- Consulting and package implementation.
- Licensing of software products and platforms across the Group’s core and digital offerings.
- Business process management services.

The Group assesses the services promised in each contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. This requires significant judgment, including assessing the deliverables and whether customers can benefit independently from them.

Some integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from these products or services is recorded:

- Net of costs when the Group acts as an agent between the customer and vendor.
- Gross when the Group is the principal in the transaction.

The Group evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include whether the Group is primarily responsible for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other relevant factors.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably:

- On a straight-line basis when services involve an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
- Using the percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and fulfilment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.

The method used for recognizing fixed-price maintenance revenue depends on the contractual promises and the nature of the deliverables.

Revenue recognition was identified as a key audit matter because it involves management judgment in:

1. Identifying distinct performance obligations.
2. Determining whether the Group acts as principal or agent.
3. Determining whether fixed-price maintenance revenue should be recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method.

Refer to Notes 1.5 and 2.18 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.

#### Auditor’s Response

The auditors’ procedures included:

- Testing the effectiveness of controls over:
  - Identification of distinct performance obligations.
  - Determination of whether the Group acts as principal or agent.
  - Determination of the appropriate recognition method for fixed-price maintenance revenue.
- Selecting a sample of customer contracts.
- Reading contract documents, including master service agreements and other documents forming part of each agreement.
- Identifying significant contractual terms and deliverables.
- Assessing management’s conclusions regarding performance obligations, principal-versus-agent determinations, and the revenue recognition method for fixed-price maintenance contracts.

### 2. Revenue recognition — Fixed-price contracts using the percentage-of-completion method

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably either:

- On a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
- Using the percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and fulfilment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts, where performance obligations are satisfied over time, is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.

The percentage-of-completion method requires the Group to determine actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs required to complete the contract. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because of the direct relationship between input and productivity.

Estimating total efforts or costs requires significant judgment and is reassessed throughout the contract period based on the latest available information. Provisions for estimated losses on uncompleted contracts are recorded in the period in which they are identified.

This was identified as a key audit matter because estimates of total efforts or costs to complete fixed-price contracts have high inherent uncertainty and require consideration of:

- Contract progress.
- Efforts or costs incurred to date.
- Estimated efforts or costs required to complete the remaining performance obligations.

The matter required a high degree of auditor judgment and substantial audit effort to evaluate the reasonableness of revenue recognized on fixed-price contracts.

Refer to Notes 1.5 and 2.18 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.

#### Auditor’s Response

The auditors’ procedures included:

- Testing the effectiveness of controls over:
  - Recording efforts or costs incurred.
  - Estimating efforts or costs required to complete remaining contract performance obligations.
  - Access and application controls for time-recording, allocation, and budgeting systems designed to prevent unauthorized changes to effort records.
- Selecting a sample of fixed-price contracts measured using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Assessing management’s ability to estimate progress by comparing actual efforts or costs incurred with prior-year estimates for completed performance obligations.
- Comparing efforts or costs incurred with the Group’s estimates to identify significant variations and assessing whether those variations were appropriately considered in estimating remaining costs or efforts.
- Testing estimates against delivery milestones, customer acceptances, and customer sign-offs to identify potential delays requiring changes to estimated costs or efforts for the remaining performance obligations.
