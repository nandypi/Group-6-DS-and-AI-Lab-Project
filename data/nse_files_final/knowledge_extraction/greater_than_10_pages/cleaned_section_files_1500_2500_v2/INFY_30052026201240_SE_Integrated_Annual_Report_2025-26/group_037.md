## Selection and appointment of new directors

The Board delegates the screening and selection process to the Nomination and Remuneration Committee. The Committee presents a diverse slate of eligible candidates to the Board for approval under the criteria in the Nomination and Remuneration Policy. The Board then recommends the candidates to shareholders for approval.

## Succession planning

The Nomination and Remuneration Committee works with the Board on leadership succession planning for Board and senior management appointments. The company seeks to maintain an appropriate balance of skills and experience while introducing new perspectives and maintaining continuity. Promoting senior management within the organization supports future leadership development.

## Training of Board members

New non-executive directors receive orientation on the company’s culture, operations, values, commitments, organizational and Group structure, subsidiaries, constitution, Board procedures, matters reserved for the Board, major risks, and risk management strategy.

Details of the familiarization program are available in the company’s [training program document](https://www.infosys.com/investors/reports-filings/Documents/training-board-members2026.pdf).

Non-executive and independent directors are familiarized through activities including periodic management strategy retreats. These retreats cover strategic planning, progress of strategic initiatives, risks to strategy execution, potential new strategic programs, competitive differentiation, execution plans, and implementation challenges. They also provide opportunities for Board members to interact with senior leadership.

During fiscal 2026, Board training covered the following topics:

### Geopolitical impact and preparedness

Board members received briefings on foreign trade policy, technical and artificial intelligence competition, immigration, and international relations that may affect the business. Management also briefed the Board on the company’s preparedness for evolving geopolitical conditions.

### Evolving Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) landscape

Training covered the changing global ESG landscape and its implications for the IT sector, including:

- AI-driven emissions growth
- Increasing exposure to extreme weather risks
- Expansion of green technology markets
- Changes in workforce skill requirements
- Peer practices in sustainability solutions, inclusive hiring, employee development, and governance disclosures
- Stakeholder expectations for transparent ESG reporting and verified outcomes
- The EU’s simplified CSRD requirements
- Emerging climate disclosure regulations in California and New York
- India’s phased climate finance taxonomy
- SEBI’s revised timelines for BRSR value chain reporting

The training emphasized the Board’s role in ESG oversight, including strengthening governance structures, improving ESG competencies and information flows, integrating sustainability into strategy and enterprise risk management, and aligning leadership remuneration with ESG performance metrics.

Each director is entitled to a training fee of US $5,000 per year. The company supports independent directors who choose to attend educational programs in Board and corporate governance.

During the year, one independent director attended the NACD “AI in Action” event on artificial intelligence and Board decision-making. Another independent director participated in the Master Class® on Technology & Innovation Oversight – an AI Tools workshop covering technology oversight, operations, strategy, governance, and artificial intelligence.

## Effective corporate governance

The company’s corporate governance framework includes the constitution, governance, and working of Board committees.

## Availability of information to Board members

The Board has unrestricted access to company-related information, including information relating to employees. Managers and representatives may be invited to Board meetings to provide additional insight on matters under discussion.

The Board receives information continuously for review, input, and approval. This includes strategic and operational plans, quarterly and annual financial statements, acquisition matters, important managerial decisions, material positive or negative developments, and statutory matters. Committee matters are presented to the relevant Board committees and subsequently to the Board for approval based on committee recommendations.

Information is submitted to directors with the agenda in advance of Board meetings. Board members’ inputs and feedback are considered when preparing meeting agendas and documents, and directors may provide suggestions on strategic and operational matters.
