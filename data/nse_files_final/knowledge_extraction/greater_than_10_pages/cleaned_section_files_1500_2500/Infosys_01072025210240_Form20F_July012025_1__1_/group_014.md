## VIII. Risks related to the ADSs

## Historically, our ADSs have traded at a premium to the trading prices of our underlying equity shares. Currently, they do not do so, and they may not continue to do so in the future.

In the past, Infosys ADSs traded at a premium to the trading prices of the underlying equity shares on Indian stock exchanges. This premium was attributed to the relatively small portion of market capitalization represented by ADSs, restrictions under Indian law on converting equity shares into ADSs, and some investors’ preference for dollar-denominated securities.

Infosys has completed three secondary ADS offerings, significantly increasing the number of outstanding ADSs. Restrictions under Indian law on issuing ADSs have also been relaxed over time. As a result, ADSs currently do not command a premium and may not trade at a premium in the future.

If a substantial amount of ADSs is converted into underlying equity shares in India, the liquidity and price of ADSs on the New York Stock Exchange could be affected.

## Sales of our equity shares may adversely affect the prices of our equity shares and ADSs.

Sales of substantial amounts of equity shares, including sales by insiders, or the perception that such sales may occur, could adversely affect the market prices of Infosys equity shares and ADSs and the company’s ability to raise capital through securities offerings.

Infosys may sponsor the sale of shares held by certain shareholders, as it has done in the past, or issue new shares. The timing and effect of any future sales, or the availability of equity shares for future sale, cannot be predicted.

## The price of our ADSs and the U.S. dollar value of any dividends we declare may be negatively affected by fluctuations in the U.S. dollar to Indian rupee exchange rate.

Fluctuations in the exchange rate between the Indian rupee and the U.S. dollar affect the dollar conversion by Deutsche Bank Trust Company Americas, the ADS depositary, of cash dividends paid in Indian rupees on the equity shares represented by ADSs. Exchange-rate fluctuations may also negatively affect ADS prices and the U.S. dollar value of dividends.

## An investor in our ADSs may not be able to exercise pre-emptive rights for additional shares and may thereby suffer dilution of such investor's equity interest in us.

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013, an Indian company must offer equity shareholders pre-emptive rights to subscribe and pay for a proportionate number of shares to maintain their existing ownership percentages before issuing new equity shares, unless three-fourths of the shareholders voting on the resolution waive those rights.

ADS holders may be unable to exercise pre-emptive rights for the equity shares underlying their ADSs unless a registration statement under the U.S. Securities Act of 1933 is effective for those rights or an exemption from registration is available. Infosys is not obligated to prepare or file such a registration statement. Any decision to do so would depend on associated costs, potential liabilities, perceived benefits, and other relevant factors. If securities are issued in the future, they may be issued to the Depositary, which may sell them for the benefit of ADS holders. The value received by the Depositary on such sales cannot be assured.

If ADS holders cannot exercise pre-emptive rights relating to the equity shares represented by their ADSs, their proportional interests in Infosys would be reduced.

## ADS holders may be restricted in their ability to exercise voting rights.

The SEBI Listing Regulations and the Indian Companies Act, 2013 require an e-voting facility for shareholder resolutions in accordance with prescribed procedures. This may allow ADS holders to vote on resolutions regardless of their location or ability to attend shareholder meetings.

At Infosys’s request, the Depositary will electronically send ADS holders notices of shareholder meetings received from Infosys, together with instructions for directing the Depositary to exercise voting rights attached to the securities represented by ADSs. If voting instructions are received in time, the Depositary will endeavor to vote in accordance with those instructions.

The Depositary’s ability to carry out voting instructions may be limited by practical and legal restrictions and the terms of the deposited securities. ADS holders may not receive voting materials in time to submit instructions. Securities for which no voting instructions are received will not be voted.

Other communications, notices, or offerings may be provided only to equity shareholders and not forwarded to ADS holders. Accordingly, ADS holders may not be able to participate in all offerings, transactions, or votes available to equity shareholders.

## ADS holders may be restricted in their ability to participate in a buy-back of shares offered by us.

Under Indian law, a company may acquire its own equity shares without court or tribunal approval if it complies with the prescribed requirements of the Indian Companies Act, 2013. Public companies listed on recognized Indian stock exchanges must also comply with the SEBI (Buy-back of Securities) Regulations, 2018.

As a public company listed on two recognized Indian stock exchanges, Infosys would need to comply with the Indian Companies Act, 2013 and the Buy-back Regulations. For ADS holders to participate in a purchase of Infosys shares under the tender-offer route, Infosys must obtain exemptive relief from the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, and ADS holders must convert their ADSs into equity shares and tender those shares into the offer.

## It may be difficult for holders of our ADSs to enforce any judgment obtained in the United States against us.

Infosys is incorporated under Indian law and is primarily located outside the United States. ADS holders may therefore find it difficult to serve process on Infosys outside the United States or enforce judgments obtained in U.S. courts, including judgments based solely on U.S. federal securities laws.

The United States and India do not currently have a treaty providing for reciprocal recognition and enforcement of judgments, other than arbitration awards, in civil and commercial matters. A final U.S. federal or state court judgment for the payment of money based on civil liability would not be directly executable by an Indian court. The judgment holder may instead bring a new suit in a competent Indian court based on the U.S. judgment.

That suit must be brought within three years from the date of the U.S. court judgment and would proceed like any other civil-liability suit in India. An Indian court may not award damages on the same basis as a foreign court and may not enforce a foreign judgment if it considers the damages inconsistent with Indian law. RBI approval under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, would also be required to repatriate amounts outside India as damages, including amounts recovered through execution of a judgment.

## Holders of ADSs are subject to the Securities and Exchange Board of India's Takeover Code with respect to their acquisitions of ADSs or the underlying equity shares, and this may impose requirements on such holders with respect to disclosure and offers to purchase additional ADSs or equity shares.

The SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011 (the Takeover Code) applies to publicly listed Indian companies, including Infosys, and to persons acquiring its equity shares or voting rights, including through ADSs.

An acquisition of shares or voting rights that, together with holdings of persons acting in concert, gives the acquirer 25% or more of the voting rights in or control over Infosys triggers a requirement to make an open offer for at least 26% of the target company’s total shares. The offer price must be determined under the Takeover Code, and a public announcement must be made on the date the acquisition is agreed.

If completion of the open offer causes the acquirer’s shareholding, together with persons acting in concert, to exceed the maximum permissible non-public shareholding, the acquirer must reduce the non-public shareholding using methods permitted by SEBI within the prescribed timeline to comply with public shareholding requirements.

An acquirer and persons acting in concert that already hold 25% or more of the shares or voting rights must make an open offer if an additional acquisition would entitle them to exercise more than 5% additional voting rights.

When an acquisition causes an acquirer’s aggregate shareholding to reach 5% or more of the company’s shares, the acquirer must disclose the aggregate shareholding and voting rights to Infosys and the relevant stock exchanges within two working days.

An acquirer and persons acting in concert holding 5% or more of the shares or voting rights must disclose any change in shareholding or voting rights exceeding 2% of the company’s total shares or voting rights, measured from the last reported holding. The disclosure must be made within two working days of the acquisition, disposal, sale, or receipt of allotment information, even if the change causes the shareholding to fall below 5%.
