## Foreign Direct Investment Issuances by the Company

The Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, together with related rules, regulations, and notifications, regulates foreign direct investment (FDI) in India. FDI includes investments through equity instruments by a person residing outside India in an unlisted Indian company, or investments of 10% or more of the post-issue paid-up equity capital, on a fully diluted basis, of a listed Indian company.

Regulatory approval is required for investment in certain sectors, including defense and certain strategic industries. Prior approval is also required for:

- Foreign investments, including transfers of shares, exceeding applicable foreign investment limits.
- Proposals involving the transfer of control or ownership through amalgamation, merger, or acquisition of an Indian company owned or controlled by resident Indian citizens or companies controlled by resident Indian citizens to a non-resident entity, where the activities are not covered by the automatic route under India’s foreign investment policy.

Under the Consolidated FDI Policy effective October 15, 2020, FDI in most sectors does not require prior approval from the Government of India or the RBI if foreign ownership remains within specified sector-specific thresholds and applicable conditions are met. These conditions include minimum pricing requirements, compliance with the Takeover Code, and ownership restrictions based on the type of foreign investor.

Purchases of ADSs by foreign investors are treated as direct foreign investment in the equity issued by Indian companies for such offerings. Indian law currently permits foreign investment of up to 100% of Infosys’ share capital.

Under the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Amendment Rules, 2020, issued on April 22, 2020, prior Government approval is required for foreign investments, including subsequent transfers of ownership, made on or after April 22, 2020, from countries sharing a land border with India. This requirement also applies where the beneficial owner of the investment, either at the time of investment or following a change in ownership, is situated in or is a citizen of such a country.

## Subsequent Transfers

Restrictions on subsequent transfers of shares of Indian companies between residents and non-residents were significantly relaxed in October 2004. For a private-arrangement transfer between a resident and a non-resident involving securities of an Indian IT-sector company, no prior approval from the RBI or the Government of India is required if applicable pricing guidelines, the Takeover Code, and ownership restrictions are complied with.

For a resident’s sale of shares in a listed Indian company to a non-resident, the minimum price per share cannot be lower than the higher of:

- The 90-trading-day volume-weighted average price of the related equity shares quoted on a recognized stock exchange before the relevant date.
- The 10-trading-day volume-weighted average price of the related equity shares quoted on a recognized stock exchange before the relevant date.

If the issuer’s Articles of Association prescribe a method that produces a higher floor price, that higher price applies.

For a non-resident’s sale of shares in a listed Indian company to a resident, the transfer price cannot exceed the price calculated under the relevant SEBI guidelines.

A non-resident may not acquire shares of a listed company on a stock exchange unless the non-resident is:

- Registered as a foreign portfolio investor (FPI) with SEBI.
- An NRI.
- An individual resident outside India registered as an Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) cardholder under the Citizenship Act, 1955.
- An individual resident outside India investing through the FDI route who has already acquired control of the company under the Takeover Code and continues to hold that control.

The conditions for a non-resident investing through the FDI route on a stock exchange include:

1. The investor must have acquired and continue to hold control in accordance with the Takeover Code.
2. Consideration for the transfer may be paid through a mode specified by the RBI or from dividends payable by the Indian investee company, provided the right to receive the dividend is established and the dividend has been credited to a specially designated non-interest-bearing rupee account for acquiring shares on the recognized stock exchange.
3. The original and resulting investments must comply with the applicable FDI policy and the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, including sectoral caps, entry routes, reporting requirements, and documentation.

Transfers of the company’s shares or convertible debentures by sale or gift between two non-residents, other than NRIs, OCIs, or erstwhile OCBs, are not subject to RBI approval or pricing restrictions. However, where FDI requires prior Government approval, such approval is required for a transfer between two non-residents. FDI in the information technology sector does not require prior Government approval.

## Investment by Non-Resident Indians and Overseas Citizens of India

NRIs and OCIs may purchase or sell equity instruments of a listed Indian company on a repatriation basis through a recognized stock exchange in India and through a branch designated by an authorized dealer.

An individual NRI or OCI may acquire up to:

- 5% of the total paid-up equity capital, on a fully diluted basis; or
- 5% of the paid-up value of each series of debentures, preference shares, or share warrants issued by an Indian company.

The combined holdings of all NRIs and OCIs may not exceed:

- 10% of the total paid-up equity capital, on a fully diluted basis; or
- 10% of the paid-up value of each series of debentures, preference shares, or share warrants.

The 10% ceiling may be increased to an overall ceiling of 24% if the company passes a special resolution at a general meeting of shareholders. NRIs may also make FDI in Indian companies under the FDI route described above.

The RBI no longer recognizes overseas corporate bodies controlled by NRIs as an eligible investment vehicle under the applicable foreign-exchange routes and schemes.

1. Rule 2(k) of the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019, as amended, defines “equity instruments” to include equity shares, convertible debentures, preference shares, and share warrants issued by an Indian company. Equity shares issued under the Companies Act, 2013, or other applicable law include partly paid equity shares. Convertible debentures and preference shares must be fully and mandatorily convertible and fully paid. Share warrants are those issued in accordance with SEBI regulations, the Companies Act, 2013, or other applicable law. Equity instruments may contain an optionality clause subject to a minimum lock-in period of one year, or the period prescribed for the specific sector, whichever is higher, but may not provide an option or right to exit at an assured price. Partly paid shares issued to a person resident outside India must be fully called up within twelve months of issue or within a period specified by the RBI. Twenty-five percent of the total consideration, including any share premium, must be received upfront. For share warrants, at least 25% of the consideration must be received upfront and the balance within 18 months of issuance.

## Investment by Foreign Portfolio Investors ("FPI")

Investments by FPIs are governed by the SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2019; the SEBI Master Circular for FPIs, designated depository participants, and eligible foreign investors dated May 30, 2024; the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules, 2019; and the Foreign Exchange Management (Debt Instruments) Regulations, 2019.

FPIs must be registered with a designated depository participant acting on behalf of SEBI and must comply with Know Your Customer requirements. FPIs may invest only in permitted securities, including:

- Shares, debentures, and warrants issued by a body corporate that is listed or proposed to be listed on a recognized stock exchange in India.
- Units of schemes launched by mutual funds under Chapters V, VI-A, and VI-B of the SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996.
- Units of schemes operated by a collective investment scheme under the SEBI (Collective Investment Schemes) Regulations, 1999.
- Derivatives traded on a recognized stock exchange.
- Units of REITs, InvITs, and Category III Alternative Investment Funds registered with SEBI.
- Indian depository receipts.
- Dated government securities and treasury bills.
- Non-convertible debentures and bonds issued by an Indian company.
- Commercial papers issued by an Indian company.
- Units of domestic mutual funds or exchange-traded funds that invest no more than 50% in equity.
- Security receipts issued by asset reconstruction companies.
- Debt instruments issued by banks that are eligible for inclusion in regulatory capital.
- Credit-enhanced bonds.
- Listed non-convertible or redeemable preference shares or debentures issued under Regulation 6 of the Debt Instruments Regulations, 2019.
- Securitized debt instruments, including certificates or instruments issued by a special purpose vehicle established to securitize assets originated by banks, financial institutions, or non-banking financial institutions.
- Rupee-denominated bonds or units issued by Infrastructure Debt Funds, including instruments issued on or after November 22, 2011, and held by FPIs.
- Municipal bonds.
- Debt securities issued by InvITs and REITs.
- Other instruments specified by SEBI from time to time.
