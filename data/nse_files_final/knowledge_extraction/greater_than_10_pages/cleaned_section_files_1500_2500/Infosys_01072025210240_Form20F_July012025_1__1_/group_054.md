## Taxation for buyback of equity shares -

- All domestic companies were subject to tax on the buyback of shares at 23.296%, including a 12% surcharge and 4% Health and Education cess.
- As a result of this buyback tax, shareholders were provided an income tax exemption under section 10(34A) of the Income Tax Act.
- The tax obligations of non-resident shareholders on gains, if any, in their country of tax residence were separate and independent from the company's buyback tax obligations. Shareholders were advised to consult their own tax advisors.

Under the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024, section 115QA of the Income Tax Act does not apply to buybacks taking place on or after October 1, 2024. The related shareholder exemption under section 10(34A) is also unavailable.

For buybacks on or after October 1, 2024:

- Consideration received by shareholders is taxable as dividend income under “Income from other sources.”
- The amount is subject to withholding tax at applicable rates under the Income Tax Act and relevant tax treaties, including the Multilateral Instrument where applicable.
- Non-resident shareholders may need to provide tax forms, including a Tax Residency Certificate and electronic Form 10F.
- For computing capital gains on shares bought back, the consideration received is deemed to be nil under section 46A of the Income Tax Act, resulting in a capital loss.
- The capital loss may be eligible for set-off and carry-forward under section 74 of the Income Tax Act.

Any buyback undertaken on the NSE or BSE is subject to Securities Transaction Tax (STT).

### Taxation for American Depositary Shares (ADSs) Holders

ADS holders with underlying equity shares may withdraw those equity shares and participate in a buyback by selling the equity shares on Indian stock exchanges.

There is no assurance that equity shares offered by an ADS holder in a buyback will be accepted. Withdrawal of underlying equity shares is subject to the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, and related rules and regulations; the Income Tax Act and related rules and regulations; the Depository Receipts Scheme, 2014; and other applicable approvals from the Reserve Bank of India.

ADS holders were advised to consult legal, financial, and tax advisors before participating in a buyback, including on regulatory approvals and tax matters.

### Income tax implications on withdrawal and re-deposit of equity shares

- Conversion of ADSs into shares may not be subject to capital gains tax, but this position is uncertain because the law is not clear.
- Shares not accepted by the Company in a buyback may be reconverted into ADSs. Two views may apply to the tax treatment of this reconversion: it may be a taxable transfer subject to capital gains tax, or it may be a non-taxable transfer. The view that reconversion is not a taxable transfer may be considered the better view.

### Income tax implications of sale of equity shares pursuant to conversion of ADSs

Prior to October 1, 2024, domestic companies were subject to buyback tax under section 115QA of the Income Tax Act at 23.296%, including a 12% surcharge and 4% Health and Education cess. Shareholders consequently received an income tax exemption under section 10(34A). The tax obligations of non-resident shareholders in their country of tax residence remained separate and independent from the company's buyback tax obligations.

For buybacks on or after October 1, 2024, section 115QA and the section 10(34A) shareholder exemption do not apply. Buyback consideration is taxable as dividend income under “Income from other sources,” subject to applicable withholding tax, tax treaties, and required tax forms. Consideration for capital-gains purposes is deemed to be nil under section 46A, resulting in a capital loss that may be eligible for set-off and carry-forward under section 74.

### Stamp Duty and Transfer Tax

A transfer of ADSs is not subject to Indian stamp duty.

A sale of equity shares in physical form by a non-resident holder is subject to Indian stamp duty at 0.015% of the market value of the equity shares on the trade date. Customarily, this tax is borne by the transferee. Shares must be traded in dematerialized form.

Under the Finance Act 2019, stamp duty applies to transfers of shares in dematerialized form:

- Delivery basis: 0.015% of the total market value of the shares, as defined under the applicable framework.
- Non-delivery basis: 0.003% of the total market value of the shares, as defined under the applicable framework.

This provision applies from July 1, 2020.

### Goods and Service Tax

Brokerage or commission paid to stockbrokers in connection with the sale or purchase of shares is subject to GST at 18%, effective July 1, 2017. The stockbroker is responsible for collecting GST from the shareholder and paying it to the relevant authority.

Under the former service tax regime, the rate was 15%, inclusive of all cess.

## Material U.S. Federal Income and Estate Tax Consequences

This section summarizes material U.S. federal income and estate tax consequences that may be relevant to the ownership and disposition of equity shares or ADSs, including a company buyback. It is general information for U.S. holders who hold equity shares or ADSs as capital assets, generally for investment, and who are not resident in India for purposes of the India-U.S. tax treaty.

U.S. holders include:

- Individuals who are citizens or residents of the United States.
- Corporations or other entities treated as corporations for U.S. federal tax purposes that were created in or under the laws of the United States, a U.S. state, or the District of Columbia.
- Estates whose income is subject to U.S. federal income taxation regardless of source.
- Trusts for which a U.S. court exercises primary supervision and a U.S. person controls all substantial decisions, or trusts with a valid election under applicable U.S. Treasury Regulations to be treated as U.S. persons.

If a partnership, including an entity treated as a partnership for U.S. federal income tax purposes, holds equity shares or ADSs, the tax treatment of a partner generally depends on the partner's status and the partnership's activities. Partners were advised to consult their own tax advisors.

The summary does not address holders subject to special tax rules, including:

- Banks, insurance companies, financial institutions, and securities or currency dealers.
- Tax-exempt entities.
- Persons holding equity shares or ADSs in a straddle or as part of a hedging or conversion transaction for tax purposes.
- Persons with a functional currency other than the U.S. dollar.
- Holders owning 10% or more of the company's shares by voting power or value.

The summary is based on the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended and in effect on the date of the Annual Report on Form 20-F, U.S. Treasury Regulations in effect or proposed as of that date, and judicial and administrative interpretations available on or before that date. It also assumes that all obligations under the deposit agreement and related agreements will be performed according to their terms.

The tax rules and interpretations described may change, including retroactively. The Internal Revenue Service or a court may interpret existing authorities differently, which could affect the tax consequences described. The summary does not address U.S. federal taxes other than income or estate tax, or U.S. state, local, or non-U.S. tax laws.
