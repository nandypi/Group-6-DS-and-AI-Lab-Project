## Disclosure of Ownership Interest

Section 89 of the Indian Companies Act requires holders of record who do not hold beneficial interests in shares of Indian companies to declare certain details to the company, including the nature of the holder's interest and details of the beneficial owner.

A person who fails to make the required declaration within 30 days may be liable for a fine of up to ₹50,000. For continuing failure, an additional fine of ₹200 per day may apply, subject to a maximum of ₹500,000. Failure to comply does not affect the company's obligation to pay dividends to the registered holder.

Beneficial interest in a share includes, directly or indirectly, through any contract, arrangement, or otherwise, the right or entitlement of a person, alone or together with another person, to:

1. Exercise or cause to be exercised any or all rights attached to the share.
2. Receive or participate in any dividend or other distribution relating to the share.

It is unclear under Indian law whether Section 89 applies to ADS holders. Investors who exchange ADSs for the underlying equity shares will be subject to Section 89. ADS holders may also be required to comply with notification and disclosure obligations under the Deposit Agreement between the holders, the company, and the Depositary.

## Declaration of Significant beneficial ownership in the Company

Section 90 of the Indian Companies Act requires individual shareholders who, acting alone or together, or through one or more persons or trusts, including persons resident outside India, hold a beneficial interest of at least 10% in a company's shares or have the right to exercise, or actually exercise, significant influence or control over the company to declare the nature of that interest to the company.

The Companies (Significant Beneficial Ownership) Rules, 2018, as amended from time to time, prescribe the thresholds and rules for identifying a significant beneficial owner and require filings and disclosures when ownership details change.

A person who fails to submit the required disclosures is subject to a minimum fine of ₹50,000, which may extend to ₹200,000. For continuing failure, an additional fine of up to ₹1,000 per day may apply after the first day.

## Audit and Annual Report

Under the Indian Companies Act, a company must file its financial statements with the Registrar of Companies within 30 days of the AGM.

Under the Listing Regulations, copies of the annual report sent to shareholders, together with the AGM notice, must be submitted to the stock exchanges on which the company's shares are listed and published on the company's website no later than the date dispatch to shareholders begins.

At least 21 days before the AGM, a listed company must distribute:

- Soft copies of the full annual report to shareholders who have registered their email addresses.
- Physical copies to shareholders who have not registered their email addresses and to shareholders who request physical copies.

The company must also file an annual return containing a list of shareholders and other company information within 60 days after the conclusion of the AGM.

## Reduction of Share Capital

Under the Indian Companies Act, a company may reduce its share capital by special resolution and approval of the court or tribunal of the state in which its registered office is situated, provided the reduction is authorized by the company's articles of association.

These provisions do not apply to a company share buyback.

## Company Acquisition of Equity Shares

A company may acquire its own equity shares without court or National Company Law Tribunal approval if it complies with the prescribed rules, regulations, and conditions of the Indian Companies Act.

Public companies listed on recognized Indian stock exchanges must also comply with the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Buy-back of Securities) Regulations, 2018. Infosys, as a public company listed on two recognized Indian stock exchanges, must comply with the relevant provisions of both the Indian Companies Act and the Buy-back Regulations.

The Buy-back Regulations provide two methods for buying back shares:

1. Tender Offer method.
2. Open Market method through book building or the stock exchange.

For ADS holders to participate in a tender-offer buyback, they must withdraw the equity shares underlying their ADSs before the applicable record date and become holders of the equity shares on that record date.

Under the open-market method, the buyback must be conducted through Indian stock exchanges. There is no assurance that shares offered by an ADS investor in a company buyback will be accepted.

Conversion of ADSs into underlying equity shares, and reconversion of those equity shares into ADSs, is subject to the limits of permissible foreign shareholding in the company. Indian law currently permits foreign investment of up to 100% of the company's share capital.

ADS holder participation in a buyback is also subject to, as applicable:

- The Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, and related rules and regulations.
- The Income Tax Act, 1961, and related rules and regulations.
- The Depository Receipts Scheme, 2014.
- The Circular on framework for issue of Depositary Receipts dated October 10, 2019.
- SEBI/HO/MRD2/DCAP/CIR/P/2 dated November 28, 2019.
- SEBI/HO/MRD/DCAP/CIR/P/2020/190 dated October 1, 2020.
- SEBI/HO/MRD2/DCAP/CIR/P/2020/243 dated December 18, 2020.
- Any required approvals from relevant authorities, including the Reserve Bank of India under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999.

## Liquidation Rights

Under the Indian Companies Act and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, certain payments have priority over payments to equity shareholders. These include payments to:

- Employees.
- Tax authorities.
- Secured and unsecured lenders.
- Holders of shares entitled by their terms to preferential repayment over equity shares.

In a winding-up, equity shareholders are entitled to repayment of the paid-up capital, or capital credited as paid-up, after the prioritized payments have been made. Subject to those payments, any surplus assets are distributed to equity shareholders in proportion to their shareholdings.

## Redemption of Equity Shares

Subject to the share buyback provisions described under “Company Acquisition of Equity Shares,” equity shares are not redeemable under the Indian Companies Act.

## Discriminatory Provisions in Articles

The Articles of Association contain no provisions discriminating against or in favor of an existing or prospective holder of the securities because that shareholder owns a substantial number of shares.

There are no restrictions on the company's securities imposed by another class of securities or by contracts.

## Alteration of Shareholder Rights

Under the Indian Companies Act, and subject to the company's memorandum or articles of association, the rights of a class of shareholders may be altered or varied by either:

1. Written consent from holders of at least three-fourths of the issued shares of that class.
2. A special resolution passed at a separate meeting of holders of the issued shares of that class.

If a variation by one class of shareholders affects the rights of another class, the consent of three-fourths of that other class is also required.

If the articles contain no applicable provision, an alteration or variation is permitted provided it is not prohibited by the terms of issue of the relevant class of shares.

## Limitations on the Rights to Own Securities

The subscription, purchase, and sale of shares of an Indian company are governed by Indian laws that restrict the issuance of shares to non-residents and the subsequent transfer of shares by or to non-residents.

Non-residents may invest in Indian company securities subject to conditions prescribed by the Reserve Bank of India. A non-resident cannot acquire shares of a listed company on a stock exchange unless the non-resident is:

1. Registered as a foreign portfolio investor with SEBI.
2. An individual resident outside India who is a citizen of India (NRI).
3. An individual resident outside India registered as an Overseas Citizen of India cardholder under the Citizenship Act, 1955 (OCI).
4. A person resident outside India investing under the FDI route who has acquired control of the company in accordance with the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeover) Regulations, 2011, and continues to hold that control.

Subject to specified conditions, FDI in most industry sectors does not require prior approval from the Government of India or the RBI if foreign investors' equity holding does not exceed applicable industry-specific thresholds. Conditions may include minimum pricing requirements, compliance with the Takeover Code, and ownership restrictions based on the nature of the foreign investor.

Purchases of ADSs by foreign investors are treated as direct foreign investment in the equity issued by Indian companies for such offerings. Indian law currently permits foreign investment of up to 100% of the company's share capital.

Under the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Amendment Rules, 2020, issued on April 22, 2020, prior Government approval is required for foreign investments, including subsequent transfers of ownership, made on or after April 22, 2020, from countries sharing a land border with India. The requirement also applies when the beneficial owner of the foreign investment, both at the time of investment and after any subsequent transfer of ownership, is situated in or is a citizen of a country sharing a land border with India.

NRIs and OCIs may purchase or sell equity instruments of a listed Indian company on a recognized Indian stock exchange on a repatriation basis through a branch of an authorized dealer designated for this purpose.

An NRI or OCI may purchase up to 5% of:

- The company's total paid-up equity capital on a fully diluted basis.
- The paid-up value of each series of debentures, preference shares, or share warrants issued by an Indian company.

The combined holdings of all NRIs and OCIs may not exceed 10% of the total paid-up equity capital on a fully diluted basis or 10% of the paid-up value of each series of debentures, preference shares, or share warrants.

The 10% aggregate ceiling may be increased to 24% if a special resolution is passed at a general meeting of the company's shareholders. NRIs may also make FDI in Indian companies through the FDI route described above.
