## ADSs Issue of securities through the depository receipt mechanism

Issue of securities through the depository receipt mechanism by Indian companies is governed by the Indian Companies Act, 2013, the Companies (Issue of Global Depository Receipts) Rules, 2014, the Depository Receipts Scheme, 2014 (the “DR Scheme”), and the SEBI Master Circular for Depositories dated December 3, 2024.

The Government of India approved the DR Scheme on October 21, 2014, and it came into force on December 15, 2014. The Issue of Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds and Ordinary Shares (Through Depository Receipt Mechanism) Scheme, 1993 was repealed except to the extent relating to foreign currency convertible bonds.

Under the DR Scheme, an Indian company, whether listed or unlisted and whether private or public, may issue or transfer permissible securities to a foreign depository for the issue of a depository receipt if the company has not been specifically prohibited from accessing capital markets or dealing in securities and satisfies the prescribed conditions.

However, SEBI’s circular dated October 10, 2019, subsequently rescinded and replaced by the Master Circular for Depositories dated October 6, 2023, read with the Master Circular dated December 3, 2024, restricted the scope of depository receipt issuances to companies incorporated in India and listed on a recognized stock exchange in India.

“Permissible securities” are equity shares and debt securities in dematerialized form that rank pari passu with securities issued and listed on a recognized stock exchange.

The SEBI Master Circulars establish a framework for depository receipt issuances by listed Indian companies, including eligibility requirements and issuer obligations. Under the October 1, 2020 framework for monitoring foreign holdings in depository receipts, listed companies must appoint an Indian depository as the “Designated Depository” to monitor applicable depository receipt limits.

The Depository Receipts (Amendment) Scheme, 2019, notified on October 7, 2019, amended the definition of “permissible jurisdiction” to include the International Financial Services Centre in India.

The current framework includes additional eligibility requirements, including that the issuer must not have been declared a willful defaulter or a fugitive economic offender.

A permissible holder of depository receipts, including its beneficial owner, excludes an Indian citizen and a Non-Resident Indian. An exception applies to depository receipts issued to NRIs under share-based employee benefit schemes implemented under the SEBI (Share Based Employee Benefits) Regulations, 2014, and to bonus and rights issues.

The Company has complied with the provisions of the Depository Receipts Circular and subsequent SEBI notifications applicable to the Company.

For purposes of the SEBI Master Circulars, a “permissible jurisdiction” means a jurisdiction notified by the Central Government under Rule 9(1) of the Prevention of Money-Laundering (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005, pursuant to notification no. G.S.R. 669(E) dated September 18, 2019. The Central Government subsequently notified a list of permissible jurisdictions on November 28, 2019, and SEBI notified that list through its circular dated November 28, 2019.

A listed company issuing depository receipts must file with the Indian stock exchanges any public disclosure made to an international stock exchange within 24 hours of filing. For an initial issue of depository receipts, the issuer must also file the offer document with SEBI and the stock exchanges for their comments, if any.

A listed company issuing depository receipts must comply with applicable laws, including minimum public shareholding requirements and foreign investment limits under FEMA. The price of a depository receipt issuance must be at least equivalent to the price determined for the corresponding mode of issue to domestic investors.

Under the revised framework, voting rights attached to the shares underlying depository receipts must be exercised only by depository receipt holders.

The summary above is based on laws applicable as of March 31, 2025, and is not intended to be a complete analysis of all laws applicable to the Company and its securities.

## TAXATION

## Indian Taxation

### General

The following summary is based on the Income Tax Act, 1961, including the special tax regimes under Sections 115AC and 115ACA, read with the Issue of Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds and Ordinary Shares (Through Depository Receipt Mechanism) Scheme, 1993, as amended.

The Income Tax Act is amended annually by the Finance Act of the relevant year. Some or all of the tax consequences under Sections 115AC and 115ACA may be amended or changed by future amendments.

The summary is not intended to constitute a complete analysis of the individual tax consequences to non-resident holders or employees relating to the acquisition, ownership or sale of ADSs and equity shares.

### Residence

For purposes of the Income Tax Act, an individual is considered a resident of India during a fiscal year if the individual:

- Is in India for at least 182 days during that year; or
- Is in India for at least 60 days during that year and has been in India for at least 365 days during the preceding four years.

The 60-day period is treated as 182 days:

- For an Indian citizen who leaves India during the previous year for employment outside India; or
- For an Indian citizen or person of Indian origin living abroad who visits India.

For an Indian citizen or person of Indian origin living abroad who visits India and has total income, other than income from foreign sources, exceeding Rs. 15 lakhs, the applicable period of stay is 120 days instead of 182 days. Such a person is treated as Not Ordinarily Resident (“NOR”) if the stay in India is less than 182 days.

“Foreign source” includes income accruing or arising outside India, except income derived from a business controlled in India or a profession set up in India and income deemed to accrue or arise in India. This provision does not apply to a person resident in India.

An Indian citizen is deemed to be a resident of India if the citizen has income other than income from foreign sources exceeding Rs. 15 lakhs during the previous year and is not liable to income tax in any other country or territory because of domicile, residence or similar criteria. Such a deemed resident is treated as NOR.

A company is resident in India if it is an Indian company or if its place of effective management during the year is in India. Individuals and companies that do not meet these criteria are treated as non-residents for purposes of the Income Tax Act.

### Taxation of Distributions

The Finance Act 2020 replaced Dividend Distribution Tax with a classical dividend taxation system under which dividend income is taxed in the hands of shareholders at their applicable tax rates.

A company paying dividends to shareholders must withhold tax at the applicable rates under the Income Tax Act and the applicable tax treaty, together with the Multilateral Instrument where applicable, subject to the conditions specified under the Income Tax Act.

The Finance Act 2020 amended Section 57 of the Income Tax Act. No deduction is allowed from dividend income other than a deduction for interest expense incurred to earn the dividend. The deduction cannot exceed 20% of the dividend income included in total income for that year, before any deduction under Section 57.

### Taxation of Capital Gains

The relevant provisions for capital gains taxation of non-resident holders and resident employees relating to the sale of ADSs and equity shares received upon conversion of ADSs are contained mainly in Sections 2(42A), 45, 47(viia), 47(x), 49(2ABB), 115AC and 115ACA of the Income Tax Act, together with the Scheme.

Shares, including shares issuable upon conversion of ADSs, held by a non-resident investor for more than 12 months are treated as long-term capital assets. Shares held for less than 12 months from the date of conversion are treated as short-term capital assets.

Capital gains are taxed as follows:

- Under the applicable scheme, gains from a sale of ADSs outside India by a non-resident to another non-resident are not taxable in India.
- Long-term capital gains realized by a resident from the transfer of ADSs were subject to tax at 10%, plus applicable surcharge and health and education cess. Under the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024, transfers taking place on or after July 23, 2024, are taxable at 12.5%, plus applicable surcharge and health and education cess. Short-term capital gains on such transfers are taxed at graduated rates, with a maximum rate of 30%, plus applicable surcharge and health and education cess.
- Long-term capital gains realized by a non-resident from the sale of equity shares obtained upon conversion of ADSs were subject to tax at 10%, plus applicable surcharge and health and education cess. Under the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024, transfers taking place on or after July 23, 2024, are taxable at 12.5%, plus applicable surcharge and health and education cess. Short-term capital gains on such transfers are taxed at the rate applicable to the seller.
