A single FPI, including its investor group, may purchase equity shares of an Indian company only below 10% of the company’s total paid-up equity capital on a fully diluted basis.

An investment by a person resident outside India through equity instruments in a listed Indian company is categorized as foreign portfolio investment if it is less than 10% of the listed company’s post-issue paid-up equity share capital on a fully diluted basis, or less than 10% of the paid-up value of each series of equity instruments.

Foreign portfolio investment and investment by an entity registered with SEBI as an FPI are distinct concepts. All investments by a SEBI-registered FPI are foreign portfolio investments, but investments by entities not registered as FPIs with SEBI may also qualify as foreign portfolio investments if they remain within the prescribed limits.

Subject to applicable Indian laws, regulations, guidelines, and approvals under Regulation 21 of the FPI Regulations, an FPI may issue, subscribe to, or otherwise deal in offshore derivative instruments only if:

- The instruments are issued by persons registered as Category I FPIs.
- The instruments are issued only to persons eligible for registration as Category I FPIs.
- Applicable know-your-client requirements have been met.
- Other conditions prescribed by SEBI from time to time are satisfied.

An FPI issuing offshore derivative instruments must ensure that transfers are made only to persons meeting these requirements. The issuing FPI’s prior consent is required for a transfer unless the transferee has been pre-approved by the FPI. Category II FPIs and persons that would be categorized as Category II FPIs cannot deal in offshore derivative instruments.

The Non-Debt Rules permit FPIs to purchase equity instruments of a listed Indian company through a recognized stock exchange in India, a public offer, or a private placement, subject to applicable individual and aggregate limits and conditions.

FPI securities transactions must generally be conducted through SEBI-registered stock brokers. Exceptions include:

1. Transactions in Government securities and other securities under the purview of the RBI, when conducted as specified by the RBI.
2. Sales in response to an acquirer’s letter of offer under the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011.
3. Sales in response to an offer by a promoter or acquirer under the SEBI (Delisting of Equity Shares) Regulations, 2009.
4. Sales under the SEBI (Buy-back of Securities) Regulations, 2018.
5. Divestment in response to offers by Indian companies under the applicable guidelines for disinvestment through American Depository Receipts or Global Depository Receipts.
6. Bids for, or acquisitions of, securities in response to offers for disinvestment by the Central Government or a State Government.
7. Transactions under an agreement with a merchant banker for market making or subscribing to an unsubscribed portion of an issue under Chapter IX of the SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018.
8. Transactions in corporate bonds.
9. Transactions on the electronic book provider platform of a recognized stock exchange.
10. Transactions to receive, hold, and sell unlisted securities referred to in Regulation 20(2) of the FPI Regulations.
11. Transactions in unlisted securities received through involuntary corporate actions, including mergers or demergers approved under the Indian Companies Act, 2013 and applicable SEBI guidelines.
12. Transactions in unlisted securities received through implementation of a resolution plan approved under the Indian Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016.
13. Transactions under guidelines issued by the Government of India, RBI, or another regulator for a debt-resolution scheme.
14. Transfers of rights entitlements.
15. Purchases or sales of illiquid, suspended, or delisted securities.
16. Transactions between registered FPIs with a multi-investment-manager structure, the same beneficial owner, and a common Permanent Account Number.
17. Any other transactions specified by SEBI.

Under the Non-Debt Rules, the total holding of each FPI or investor group must remain below 10% of an Indian company’s total paid-up equity capital on a fully diluted basis. The limit is also below 10% of the paid-up value of each series of debentures, preference shares, or share warrants issued by an Indian company.

An FPI that breaches the prescribed 10% limit may divest the excess holdings within five trading days from settlement of the trades causing the breach. If the FPI does not divest, its entire investment in the company, together with the investment of its investor group, is treated as foreign direct investment. The FPI and its investor group may not make further portfolio investments in that company. Divestment and reclassification as FDI remain subject to conditions specified by SEBI and the RBI.

Effective April 1, 2020, the aggregate limit for FPI investment is the sectoral cap applicable to the Indian company under the FDI route.

Before March 31, 2020, a company could choose a lower aggregate limit of 24%, 49%, or 74%, instead of the applicable sectoral cap, with approval from its board of directors and shareholders through a special resolution. A company that had reduced its aggregate limit could subsequently increase it to 49%, 74%, the sectoral cap, or the statutory ceiling, as applicable, with the same approvals. Once increased to a higher threshold, the aggregate limit could not be reduced to a lower threshold. For a sector in which FDI was prohibited, the aggregate limit was 24%.

Aggregate foreign portfolio investment of up to 49% of paid-up capital on a fully diluted basis, or the applicable sectoral or statutory cap, whichever was lower, was not subject to Government approval or sectoral conditions if the investment did not transfer ownership or control of the resident Indian company from resident Indian citizens or to persons resident outside India. Other investments by persons resident outside India were subject to Government approval and sectoral conditions under the Non-Debt Rules.

Section 9 of the Income Tax Act was amended by Finance Act, 2017 to clarify that explanation 5 did not apply to an asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in an FII referred to in section 115AD(a) for assessment years commencing on or after April 1, 2012, but before April 1, 2015. The amendment applied retrospectively from April 1, 2012, including assessment year 2012–13 and subsequent assessment years.

Section 9 was further amended to clarify that explanation 5 did not apply to an asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in a Category I or Category II FPI under the SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2014. This amendment applied retrospectively from April 1, 2015, including assessment year 2015–16 and subsequent assessment years.

Finance Act 2020, dated March 27, 2020, modified the proviso to explanation 5 with effect from April 1, 2020, applying from assessment year 2020–21 and subsequent assessment years. Following the amendment, explanation 5 did not apply to:

- An asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in an FII referred to in section 115AD(a) for an assessment year commencing on or after April 1, 2012, but before April 1, 2015.
- An asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in a Category I or Category II FPI under the SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2014, before those regulations were repealed.
- An asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in a Category I foreign portfolio investor under the SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2019.
