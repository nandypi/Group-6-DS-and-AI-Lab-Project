## Trade Receivables and Contract Balances

The timing of revenue recognition, billings, and cash collections results in receivables, unbilled revenue, and unearned revenue on the Group's consolidated balance sheet. Amounts are billed as work progresses under agreed contractual terms, either at periodic intervals, such as monthly or quarterly, or upon achievement of contractual milestones.

Receivables are rights to consideration that are unconditional. Unbilled revenue comprising revenue in excess of billings from time-and-material contracts and fixed-price maintenance contracts is classified as a financial asset when the right to consideration is unconditional and payment is due only after the passage of time.

Invoicing for other fixed-price contracts is based on contractually defined milestones. Because the timing of revenue recognition differs from the timing of invoicing, unbilled revenue for these contracts is classified as a non-financial contract asset when the right to consideration depends on completion of contractual milestones.

Invoicing in excess of earnings is classified as unearned revenue. Trade receivables and unbilled revenue are presented net of impairment in the consolidated statements of financial position.

During fiscal 2025, 2024, and 2023, the Group recognized revenue of $671 million, $656 million, and $669 million, respectively, arising from opening unearned revenue as of April 1, 2024, April 1, 2023, and April 1, 2022, respectively.

During fiscal 2025, 2024, and 2023, $579 million, $850 million, and $739 million, respectively, of unbilled revenue pertaining to other fixed-price, fixed-time-frame contracts as of April 1, 2024, April 1, 2023, and April 1, 2022, respectively, was reclassified to trade receivables upon billing to customers after completion of milestones.

## Remaining performance obligations

The remaining performance obligation disclosure provides the aggregate transaction price yet to be recognized as of the end of the reporting period and explains when the Group expects to recognize these amounts as revenue.

Applying the practical expedient under IFRS 15, the Group has not disclosed remaining performance obligations for contracts where recognized revenue corresponds directly with the value to the customer of the entity's performance completed to date. These typically include time-and-material contracts and unit-of-work-based contracts.

Remaining performance obligation estimates are subject to change and are affected by terminations, changes in contract scope, periodic revalidation, adjustments for revenue that has not materialized, and currency fluctuations.

The aggregate value of performance obligations that were completely or partially unsatisfied as of March 31, 2025, excluding contracts meeting the criteria above, was $12,258 million. The Group expected to recognize approximately 50% within the next year and the remainder thereafter.

The aggregate value of performance obligations that were completely or partially unsatisfied as of March 31, 2024, was $10,869 million. Contracts can generally be terminated by customers and typically include an enforceable termination penalty payable by the customer. Generally, customers have not terminated contracts without cause.

## 2.14 Employee benefits

## Gratuity and Pensions

The Group provides gratuity, a defined benefit retirement plan covering eligible employees, primarily of Infosys and its Indian subsidiaries. The plan provides a lump-sum payment to vested employees upon retirement, death, incapacitation, or termination of employment, based on the employee's salary and tenure with the Group.

The Company contributes gratuity liabilities to the Infosys Limited Employees' Gratuity Fund Trust. Infosys BPM and EdgeVerve contribute to the Infosys BPM Employees' Gratuity Fund Trust and EdgeVerve Systems Limited Employees' Gratuity Fund Trust, respectively. Trustees administer contributions to the trusts, which are invested in a scheme with the Life Insurance Corporation of India as permitted by Indian law.

The Group operates defined benefit pension plans in certain overseas jurisdictions in accordance with local laws. These plans are managed by third-party fund managers and provide periodic payouts after retirement and/or a lump-sum payment under the rules of each fund, including death and disability benefits. Contributions are based on a percentage of salary that varies according to employee age.

Liabilities for these defined benefit plans are determined by actuarial valuation performed by an external actuary at each balance sheet date using the projected unit credit method. The plans expose the Group to actuarial risks, including longevity risk, interest rate risk, and market risk.

The Group recognizes the net obligation of a defined benefit plan as an asset or liability on its balance sheet. Re-measurement gains and losses on the net defined benefit liability or asset are recognized in other comprehensive income and are not reclassified to profit or loss in subsequent periods. The actual return on plan assets in excess of the yield calculated using the discount rate applied to measure the defined benefit obligation is recognized in other comprehensive income. The effect of plan amendments is recognized in net profit in the consolidated statement of comprehensive income.
