## Risk Management Procedures

Infosys manages market risk through treasury operations. Senior management and the Audit Committee approve the objectives and policies of treasury operations, which include:

- Managing cash resources.
- Implementing hedging strategies for foreign currency exposures.
- Managing borrowing strategies, if any.
- Ensuring compliance with market-risk limits and policies.

## Components of Market Risk

### (1) Exchange rate risk

Infosys’ primary market-risk exposure is exchange-rate risk. Its functional currency is the Indian rupee, while a major portion of revenue is generated in foreign currencies, particularly the U.S. dollar, euro, Australian dollar, and United Kingdom pound sterling. A significant portion of expenses is incurred in U.S. dollars and Indian rupees.

U.S. dollar-denominated revenue represented 64.2% of total revenue in fiscal 2025 and 66.2% in fiscal 2024. The corresponding figures were:

| Currency | Fiscal 2025 | Fiscal 2024 |
|---|---:|---:|
| U.S. dollar | 64.2% | 66.2% |
| Euro | 16.8% | 14.8% |
| Australian dollar | 4.6% | 5.0% |
| United Kingdom pound sterling | 3.8% | 4.1% |

Exchange-rate risk primarily arises from foreign-currency revenues, receivables, and payables. Changes in the exchange rate between the Indian rupee and the U.S. dollar may adversely affect operating results, including when the Indian rupee appreciates against the U.S. dollar.

Infosys uses foreign-exchange forward and option contracts to mitigate changes in exchange rates on foreign-currency exposures. Counterparties are generally banks. The outstanding foreign-exchange forward and option contracts are disclosed in Note 2.3 in Item 18 of the Annual Report.

These contracts typically mature within 12 months, must be settled on the maturity date, and may be cancelled subject to payment or receipt of gains or losses based on the difference between the contract exchange rate and the market exchange rate on the cancellation date. Infosys uses these derivatives for hedging and not for speculative purposes.

Infosys may not purchase adequate instruments to fully insulate its foreign-exchange exposures, and the instruments may not perform adequately as hedges. Changes in Reserve Bank of India policies may limit its ability to hedge foreign-currency exposures. Infosys may adopt more active hedging policies in the future and has done so in the past.

### (2) Fair value

Note 2.3 in Item 18 of the Annual Report contains disclosures on the carrying values and fair values of financial assets and liabilities.

## Size and composition of the Board

The Securities Exchange Board of India (SEBI) Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements Regulations, 2015, as amended, mandate that, for a company with a non-executive Chairman who is a Promoter, at least half of the Board should comprise independent directors. The Listing Regulations also require the Boards of the top 1,000 listed companies, effective April 1, 2020, to have at least one independent woman director.

As of March 31, 2025, the Board comprised nine members:

- One non-executive and non-independent Chairman.
- One Chief Executive Officer and Managing Director.
- Seven independent directors.

Independent directors constituted 77.78% of the Board. Two of the nine directors were independent women, representing 22.22% of the Board. The Board periodically evaluates whether changes are needed in its size and composition.

## Definition of Independent Directors

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013, and the Listing Regulations, an independent director is not a promoter, employee, or key managerial personnel of the company or its subsidiaries. The person must also not have a material pecuniary relationship exceeding 10% of total income, or another prescribed amount, with the company or its holding, subsidiary, or associate company, or with their promoters or directors, during the two immediately preceding financial years or the current financial year, except for remuneration as an independent director.

Infosys also follows the definitions of an independent director under the NYSE Listed Company Manual, the Sarbanes-Oxley Act, and applicable U.S. securities laws.

Based on disclosures received from all independent directors and the Board’s assessment, the independent directors fulfilled the conditions specified in the Indian Companies Act, 2013, the Listing Regulations, and the NYSE Listed Company Manual, and were independent of management.

## Key Board qualifications, expertise and attributes

The qualifications, skills, and attributes considered when nominating Board candidates include:

- **Financial:** Leadership of a financial firm or management of an enterprise’s finance function, including complex financial management, capital allocation, and financial reporting, or experience supervising senior finance, accounting, audit, or related personnel.
- **Diversity:** Gender, ethnic, geographic, cultural, or other perspectives that broaden understanding of customers, partners, employees, governments, and other stakeholders worldwide.
- **Global Business:** Experience driving business success in international markets and understanding diverse business environments, economic conditions, cultures, regulatory frameworks, and global market opportunities.
- **Leadership:** Leadership of a significant enterprise, including organizational understanding, process management, strategic planning, risk management, talent development, succession planning, change management, and long-term growth.
- **Information Technology:** Technology experience, including anticipating technological trends, generating disruptive innovation, and extending or creating business models.
- **Mergers and Acquisitions:** Experience leading growth through acquisitions and business combinations, assessing build-or-buy decisions, evaluating strategic and cultural fit, valuing transactions, and assessing operational integration plans.
- **Board Service and Governance:** Public-company Board service and experience with Board and management accountability, shareholder interests, and governance practices.
- **Sales and Marketing:** Experience developing strategies to grow sales and market share, build brand awareness and equity, and enhance enterprise reputation.
- **Sustainability, and Environment, Social and Governance (ESG):** Experience leading organizational sustainability and ESG initiatives and integrating them into company strategy.
- **Risk Management:** Experience identifying and evaluating significant strategic risk exposures and assessing mitigation of strategic, legal, compliance, and operational risks.
- **Cybersecurity:** Experience assessing and managing cybersecurity risks and implementing cybersecurity policies, procedures, and strategies.
