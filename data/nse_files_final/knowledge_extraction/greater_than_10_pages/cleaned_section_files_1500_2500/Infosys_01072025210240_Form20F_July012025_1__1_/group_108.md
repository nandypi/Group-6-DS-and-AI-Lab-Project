## Maintenance of Minimum Public Shareholding as a Condition for Continuous Listing

The Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957 (SCRR) were amended on June 4, 2010, to require all listed companies in India to maintain minimum public shareholding of 25%.

For this purpose, “public shareholding” means equity shares held by the public, including shares underlying depository receipts if:

1. The holder of the depository receipts has the right to issue voting instructions; and
2. The depository receipts are listed on an international exchange in accordance with the Depository Receipts Scheme.

Equity shares held by a trust established to implement employee benefit schemes under SEBI regulations are excluded from public shareholding.

Existing listed companies with public shareholding below 25% may meet the requirement through the following methods:

- Sale of shares held by promoters or the promoter group in the open market, subject to applicable conditions:
  - Promoters or the promoter group may sell up to 2% of the listed entity’s total paid-up equity share capital in each financial year, subject to the sale not exceeding five times the entity’s average monthly trading volume, until the SCRR compliance deadline.
  - Promoters or the promoter group may sell up to 5% of paid-up capital during a financial year if public shareholding reaches 25% after the sale. The sale may occur in one or multiple tranches over a period not exceeding 12 months, and the shares sold may not exceed the entity’s trading volume during the preceding 12 months.
- Allotment of eligible securities through a qualified institutional placement under Chapter VI of the SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018.
- Issuance of shares to the public through a prospectus.
- An offer for sale of promoter or promoter-group shares to the public through a prospectus.
- Sale of promoter shares through the stock exchange mechanism, including the secondary market, under SEBI circular no. SEBI/HO/MRD/MRD-PoD-3/P/CIR/2023/10 dated January 10, 2023.
- Rights issues to public shareholders, with promoters and promoter-group shareholders forgoing their entitlement to equity shares arising from the issue.
- Bonus issues to public shareholders, with promoters and promoter-group shareholders forgoing their entitlement to equity shares arising from the issue.
- Increased public shareholding resulting from the exercise of options and allotment of shares under an employee stock option scheme, subject to a maximum of 2% of the listed entity’s paid-up equity share capital.
- Transfer of promoter or promoter-group shares to an exchange-traded fund managed by a SEBI-registered mutual fund, subject to a maximum of 5% of the listed entity’s paid-up equity share capital.
- Any other method approved by SEBI on a case-by-case basis.

## Voting Rights of Deposited Equity Shares Represented by ADSs

Under Indian law, voting on equity shares is generally by show of hands unless a poll is demanded by members present in person or by proxy who hold at least 10% of the total shares entitled to vote on the resolution or shares with aggregate paid-up capital of at least ₹5,00,000.

Every company whose equity shares are listed on a recognized stock exchange, and every company with at least 1,000 members, must provide members with an electronic voting facility for resolutions considered at a general meeting. The Listing Regulations require electronic voting for all shareholder resolutions in accordance with the Indian Companies Act.

A proxy, including a body corporate represented by an authorized representative, may vote on resolutions.

The voting rights of ADS holders are described under “Description of American Depositary Shares Voting Rights.”

## Issue of Securities through the Depository Receipt Mechanism

The issue of securities through the depository receipt mechanism by Indian companies is governed by the Indian Companies Act, the Companies (Issue of Global Depository Receipts) Rules, 2014, the Depository Receipts Scheme, 2014 (DR Scheme), and SEBI circulars dated October 10, 2019, November 28, 2019, October 1, 2020, and December 18, 2020.

The Government of India approved the DR Scheme on October 21, 2014, and it came into force on December 15, 2014. The Issue of Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds and Ordinary Shares (Through Depository Receipt Mechanism) Scheme, 1993, was repealed except for provisions relating to foreign currency convertible bonds.

Under the DR Scheme, an Indian company may issue or transfer permissible securities to a foreign depository for the issue of depository receipts if it has not been prohibited from accessing capital markets or dealing in securities and satisfies the prescribed conditions. Following SEBI’s October 10, 2019 circular, the framework applies to companies incorporated in and listed on a recognized stock exchange in India.

“Permissible securities” are equity shares and debt securities in dematerialized form that rank pari passu with securities issued and listed on a recognized stock exchange.

The framework effective October 10, 2019 establishes eligibility requirements and issuer obligations. Listed companies must appoint an Indian depository as the “Designated Depository” to monitor depository receipt limits. The Depository Receipts (Amendment) Scheme, 2019, notified on October 7, 2019, expanded the definition of “permissible jurisdiction” to include the International Financial Services Centre in India.

Additional eligibility requirements include not being declared a willful defaulter or a fugitive economic offender.

A permissible holder of depository receipts, including its beneficial owner, generally excludes an Indian resident and a non-resident Indian (NRI). An NRI is not excluded when depository receipts are issued under a company’s share-based employee benefit scheme in accordance with the SEBI (Share Based Employee Benefits) Regulations, 2014, or in connection with a bonus issue or rights issue.

A listed company issuing depository receipts must:

- File with the Indian stock exchanges any public disclosure made to an international stock exchange within 24 hours of filing.
- File the offer document for an initial issue of depository receipts with SEBI and the stock exchanges for their comments, if any.
- Comply with applicable laws, including minimum public shareholding requirements and foreign investment limits under FEMA.
- Price depository receipt issuances at not less than the price determined for the corresponding issue to domestic investors.

Voting rights on shares underlying depository receipts must be exercised by depository receipt holders under the revised framework. Previously, voting rights could be governed contractually under the deposit agreement; if ADS holders could not exercise those rights, the underlying shares would not count toward minimum public shareholding.

## Description of American Depositary Shares (ADS)

Deutsche Bank Trust Company Americas acts as depositary for the ADSs. Each ADS represents ownership of one equity share deposited with ICICI Bank Limited as custodian for the depositary. An ADS also represents ownership of any other securities, cash, or property held by the depositary.

As of March 31, 2023, the ADSs were administered through the depositary’s corporate trust office and principal executive office in New York.

The Direct Registration System (DRS) is administered by The Depository Trust Company (DTC). Under the DRS, the depositary may register ownership of uncertificated ADSs, evidenced by periodic statements issued to entitled ADS holders.

ADS holders are not treated as shareholders of Infosys and do not have the rights of holders of the underlying equity shares. Shareholder rights are governed by the Indian Companies Act and the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015.

The depositary holds the equity shares underlying the ADSs. ADS holders have rights under the deposit agreement among Infosys, the depositary, ADS holders, and beneficial owners of ADSs. The laws of the State of New York govern the deposit agreement and the ADSs.

The deposit agreement and the form of American Depositary Receipt were filed as exhibits to the company’s Annual Report on Form 20-F.
