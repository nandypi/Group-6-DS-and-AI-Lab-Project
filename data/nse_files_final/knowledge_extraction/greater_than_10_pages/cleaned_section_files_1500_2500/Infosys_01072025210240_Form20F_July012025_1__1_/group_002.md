## Risk Factors

Infosys's revenues and expenses may vary significantly from period to period, making period-to-period comparisons unreliable indicators of future performance. Results may fall below market expectations, investor expectations, or company guidance.

### I. Risks related to the markets in which we and our clients operate

- Spending on technology products and services by clients and prospective clients fluctuates based on economic, geopolitical, monetary, tariff, trade, fiscal, and regulatory conditions in the markets where they operate.
- Economic slowdowns and other factors may affect the economic health of the United States, the United Kingdom, the European Union, Australia, and the industries from which Infosys derives revenue.
- Changes in immigration laws, their interpretation or enforcement, or delays in obtaining visas may increase compliance costs, disrupt operations, prevent Infosys from bidding for or fulfilling projects, or result in enforcement actions or penalties.
- Government or regulatory sanctions against clients in key operating geographies may limit Infosys's ability to grow those relationships and increase its exposure to penalties or consequential sanctions.
- A significant portion of revenue depends on a limited number of clients. The loss of any major client could materially affect the business.
- Client financial stability may be affected by demand and supply challenges, currency fluctuations, regulatory sanctions, tariff or trade restrictions, geopolitical conflicts, and other macroeconomic conditions. These factors may impair Infosys's ability to recover fees for services rendered.
- Intense competition in technology services may affect win rates and pricing, reduce market share, and decrease revenue and profits.

### II. Risks related to the investments we make for our growth

- Failure to anticipate and develop new services or enhance existing services in response to rapid technological and industry changes could harm the business.
- Acquisitions, strategic investments, strategic partnerships, alliances, and other ventures may not be successful.

### III. Risks related to our cost structure

- Expenses are difficult to predict and may vary significantly from period to period, causing fluctuations in profitability.
- Failure to manage growth could disrupt the business, reduce profitability, and impair execution of the growth strategy.
- Wage pressures and the hiring of employees and subcontractors outside or in India may reduce competitive advantages and profits.
- Infosys invests substantial cash in creating and maintaining physical and technological infrastructure. Profitability could decline if the business does not grow proportionately.
- Currency fluctuations and changes in interest rates may affect operating results and the yield on cash balances.

### IV. Risks related to our employee workforce

- Success depends substantially on highly skilled technology professionals and the ability to hire, attract, motivate, retain, and train them.

### V. Risks related to our contractual obligations

- Failure to complete fixed-price, fixed-timeframe, or transaction-based pricing contracts within budget and on time may negatively affect profitability.
- Client contracts are often conditional on performance. Unsatisfactory performance may result in lower-than-anticipated revenue.
- Work with governmental agencies may expose Infosys to additional risks.
- Inability to execute contracts or amendments with clients on a timely basis may affect revenue and profits and cause fluctuations in reported results.
