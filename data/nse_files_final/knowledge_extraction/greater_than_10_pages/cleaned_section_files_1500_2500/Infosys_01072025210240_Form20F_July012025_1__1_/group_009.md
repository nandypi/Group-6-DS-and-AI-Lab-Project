## Our reputation could be at risk and we may be liable to our clients or to regulators for damages caused by unauthorized disclosure of confidential information and sensitive data.

We are dependent on our information technology networks and systems to process, transmit, host, and securely store electronic information and to communicate among our locations around the world and with our clients, suppliers, and partners. We are often required to collect and store sensitive or confidential client data.

Security breaches, employee misappropriation, unauthorized access, and human or technological error could lead to unauthorized disclosure of sensitive data. Such events could jeopardize projects critical to the operations of our clients’ businesses. Theft or unauthorized use or publication of our confidential information, or our clients’ confidential or proprietary business information, could adversely affect our reputation and competitive position. Failures in the networks or computer systems used by us or our clients could result in substantial-damages claims and significant reputational harm. Many client agreements do not limit our potential liability for breaches of confidentiality.

As a global service provider serving clients across a broad range of industries, we access, manage, use, collect, and store sensitive data subject to multiple regulatory regimes, including U.S. federal and state laws governing personal financial and health data and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). These laws and regulations are increasing in complexity and number and change frequently. Their scope and coverage may extend across jurisdictions and stakeholders, increasing compliance risk and cost.

If any person, including an employee, negligently disregards or intentionally breaches established controls over our data or client data, or otherwise mismanages or misappropriates that data, Infosys could face significant litigation, monetary damages, regulatory enforcement actions, fines, and criminal prosecution in one or more jurisdictions, in addition to reputational damage. Monetary damages may not be subject to contractual liability limits or exclusions of consequential or indirect damages and could be significant. Liability insurance, including cyber insurance, may not be sufficient in type or amount to cover claims related to security breaches, cyberattacks, and related incidents. Some contracts involve projects critical to clients’ operations and provide benefits that may be difficult to quantify.

Failures in a client’s system or security breaches, regardless of Infosys’ responsibility, could result in substantial-damages claims, significant defense expenses, or large settlement payments. Unauthorized access to or disclosure of data in Infosys’ possession or control, or failure to comply with applicable laws and regulations, could expose Infosys to civil or criminal enforcement actions, penalties, and lawsuits by clients, clients’ customers, or others for breaches of contractual confidentiality and security provisions or data-protection laws.

For example, in November 2023, certain systems of Infosys McCamish Systems LLC (“McCamish”), a subsidiary of Infosys BPM Limited and wholly owned subsidiary of Infosys Limited, were encrypted by ransomware. This resulted in the non-availability of certain applications and systems.

Six actions were filed against McCamish in connection with the incident. The actions were consolidated, and a consolidated class-action complaint was filed on November 7, 2024, purportedly on behalf of all persons residing in the United States whose personally identifiable information was compromised, including those sent an incident notice.

On March 13, 2025, McCamish and the plaintiffs engaged in mediation, resulting in an in-principle agreement setting out the terms of a proposed settlement of the class-action lawsuits against McCamish and seven class-action lawsuits arising from the incident filed against McCamish’s customers. On May 9, 2025, McCamish and the plaintiffs entered into a definitive settlement agreement. The settlement is subject to preliminary and final court approval. If approved, it will resolve all allegations in the class-action lawsuits without admission of liability.

Laws and expectations relating to data protection continue to evolve. They may limit Infosys’ access to, use of, and disclosure of sensitive data, require increased expenditures, or prevent Infosys from offering certain types of services.

Given the hybrid working model, there is relatively higher potential for confidential data exposure. Infosys seeks to strengthen and automate controls and educate employees on data security and best practices. Non-material data breaches have occurred, and future material or non-material breaches cannot be completely ruled out. Such incidents could adversely affect the brand, financial condition, and results of operations.
