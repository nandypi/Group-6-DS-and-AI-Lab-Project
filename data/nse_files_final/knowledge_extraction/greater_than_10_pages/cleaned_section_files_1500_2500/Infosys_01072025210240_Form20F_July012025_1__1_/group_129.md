## Conflicts of interest

Infosys provides whistleblower reporting channels through its integrity and compliance function, the Chief Compliance Officer, and the Audit Committee. Reports may be made without fear of reprisal.

Retaliation, threats, or retribution against anyone who reports in good faith a suspected violation of law, the Code of Conduct and Ethics, or company policies, or anyone assisting with an investigation, is prohibited.

Employees may consult their manager, the Office of Integrity & Compliance, Human Resources, or the Legal Department about the meaning or application of the Code of Conduct and Ethics, company policies, and applicable legal or regulatory requirements. Questions and reports to these sources are maintained in confidence.

Concerns relating to fellow employees’ behavior may be reported through the company’s designated reporting process. Grievances relating to sexual harassment may also be raised through the designated grievance process.

## Form of Acknowledgment of Receipt of Code of Conduct and Ethics

Employees acknowledge that additional policies or laws may apply to their job or posting location. They agree to follow Infosys values and comply with the Company Code of Conduct and Ethics.

The acknowledgment form includes fields for the employee’s signature, name, date, and employee number. Employees are instructed to return the signed form to Human Resources.

## CERTIFICATION OF CHIEF EXECUTIVE OFFICER PURSUANT TO SECTION 302 OF THE SARBANES-OXLEY ACT OF 2002

Salil Parekh certified that he had reviewed the Annual Report on Form 20-F of Infosys Limited and, based on his knowledge:

1. The report did not contain an untrue statement of a material fact or omit a material fact necessary to make the statements, in light of the circumstances, not misleading for the period covered.
2. The financial statements and other financial information fairly presented, in all material respects, the company’s financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows for the periods presented.
3. The CEO and the company’s other certifying officer were responsible for establishing and maintaining disclosure controls and procedures and internal control over financial reporting.
4. Disclosure controls and procedures were designed to ensure that material information relating to the company and its consolidated subsidiaries was made known to the certifying officers during preparation of the report.
5. Internal control over financial reporting was designed to provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of financial reporting and the preparation of financial statements for external purposes in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles.
6. The effectiveness of disclosure controls and procedures was evaluated as of the end of the reporting period, with conclusions presented in the report.
7. Any change in internal control over financial reporting during the reporting period that materially affected, or was reasonably likely to materially affect, those controls was disclosed.
8. Based on the most recent evaluation of internal control over financial reporting, significant deficiencies and material weaknesses reasonably likely to adversely affect the company’s ability to record, process, summarize, and report financial information were disclosed to the auditors and the audit committee of the board.
9. Any fraud, whether or not material, involving management or other employees with a significant role in internal control over financial reporting was disclosed to the auditors and the audit committee.

The certification was signed by Salil Parekh, Chief Executive Officer and Managing Director.
