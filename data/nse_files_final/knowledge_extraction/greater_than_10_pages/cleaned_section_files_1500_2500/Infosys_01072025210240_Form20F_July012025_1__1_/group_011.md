## The markets in which we operate are subject to the risk of earthquakes, floods, tsunamis, storms, pandemics and other disasters.

Some regions in which Infosys operates are prone to earthquakes, floods, tsunamis, storms, pandemics, and other disasters. If business centers or telecommunications networks are affected, Infosys may incur costs to redeploy personnel and property, sustain damage to operations and assets, experience financial losses, or be unable to complete client engagements on time or at all.

Disasters affecting locations where significant clients are based may cause client losses or business interruptions, impair Infosys's ability to provide services, and limit clients' ability to continue purchasing its products or services. Prolonged disruption may also affect business continuity and employee productivity, with adverse effects on business, financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows.

## The safety of our employees, assets and infrastructure may be affected by untoward incidents beyond our control, impacting business continuity or reputation.

Acts of violence or vandalism, disease pandemics, geopolitical conflicts, political demonstrations, civil unrest, random acts of rage, and public health crises may affect the health and safety of employees and other personnel, the security of physical infrastructure, business continuity, and reputation. These incidents may also expose Infosys to lawsuits from employees.

Infosys operates in 292 locations across 59 countries. Its global development centers are connected through a telecommunications network architecture using multiple service providers, satellite and optical links, and alternate routing. Although Infosys has infrastructure and business continuity plans to address communication-network failures, disasters affecting these networks may still disrupt operations and service delivery.

As an international company, Infosys's offshore and onsite operations may also be affected by disease, epidemics, and local social instability, which could adversely affect revenues and profitability.

## Terrorist attacks or a war could adversely affect our business, results of operations and financial condition.

Terrorist attacks, violence, war, military coups, and threats of warfare may directly affect Infosys or its clients. Events affecting the United States or Europe could have a significant impact because a majority of Infosys's revenues are derived from clients in those regions. Geopolitical instability may affect clients, project execution, work visas, and travel for technology professionals serving the United States and Europe. Such disruptions could increase expenses and negatively affect results of operations.

Attacks in India could disrupt service delivery and negatively affect Infosys's business, personnel, assets, and results of operations. They could also lead existing or potential clients to select other service providers.

Regional conflicts in South Asia could adversely affect the Indian economy, disrupt operations, and harm the business. South Asia has experienced civil unrest and hostilities among neighboring countries, including China and Pakistan. Military confrontations between India and Pakistan have occurred in Kashmir and along the India-Pakistan border, including as recently as May 2025.

Continued conflict may affect the safety of employees and development centers, increase cybersecurity threats, and hinder service to global clients if operations cannot be moved out of conflict zones promptly. Relocating work to alternate locations may increase operating costs. Military activity or terrorist attacks could disrupt communications and travel, make it more difficult to service clients, and increase perceived risks associated with investments in Indian companies.

Heightened geopolitical conflicts may affect Infosys's ability to operate in affected geographies. Prolonged conflict may also delay economic recovery from the pandemic and compound adverse effects on the business.

## Climate change risks are increasingly manifesting in our business as strategic, physical and transitional (market and compliance) risks, which if not managed adequately, can affect our operations, reputation and profitability.

Climate change-related risks are described as strategic, physical, market, and regulatory risks.

### Strategic climate change risks

As a member of the global IT and digital supply chain ecosystem, Infosys may face strategic risks if its climate-action goals are not aligned with evolving climate-change trends and global treaties such as the Paris Agreement.

### Physical climate change risks

Extreme weather events may cause:

1. Physical damage to buildings and other assets, disrupting business continuity.
2. Disruption to transport networks, utilities, and other city functions in operating locations.
3. Decreased employee morale.

Drought may increase food prices or cause shortages of certain food items. Flooding may cause diseases and epidemics. Extreme weather may also reduce productivity and increase operating costs.

Changes in the availability of natural resources, including water, may directly affect operations and employee livelihoods. Large operating campuses in major urban cities may face disruptions to power and water supplies, affecting business continuity.

### Climate-related market risks

Clients increasingly request climate-action updates and emissions-performance disclosures, including Carbon Disclosure Project (CDP) scores, during evaluations. Clients and potential clients have asked about Infosys's Net Zero commitment, commitment to Science Based Targets, and supply-chain emissions-reduction initiatives.

Climate performance may become an important factor in clients' decisions. If Infosys does not manage performance in these areas, its ability to compete for and win or renew contracts may be adversely affected.

### Climate-related regulatory risks

Climate-related regulations may require businesses to meet emissions-reduction goals and other environmental targets and provide detailed disclosures concerning climate action, performance, risks, and opportunities. New and emerging regulations may increase compliance costs.

Incorrect environmental data or metrics, or misinterpretation of evolving global standards, may result in penalties, reputational effects, and other consequences. Violations or non-conformance with climate laws and regulations could affect reputation and profitability, limit operations in certain geographies, and adversely affect business performance.

## Our reputation, access to capital and longer-term financial stability could be at risk if we are unable to meet our stated goals under our Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) 2030 vision.

Infosys is an early signatory to the United Nations Global Compact. In 2020, after becoming carbon neutral, Infosys published its ESG Vision and Ambitions 2030, describing its roadmap for responsible and sustainable business. Annual ESG publications report performance against ESG ambitions and are mapped to the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals.

In 2025, at the midpoint of its ESG Vision 2030 journey, Infosys had an opportunity to refresh its ESG ambitions to address changing stakeholder priorities and reinforce its commitment to responsible business.

Infosys's ESG performance and leadership are monitored internally and by external agencies, including global ESG assessments. Failure to meet ESG goals or unfavorable assessments could adversely affect reputation, access to capital, longer-term financial stability, brand reputation, and share price.

Regulators, lawmakers, and stakeholders have also expressed or pursued contrary views, legislation, and investment expectations concerning ESG ambitions and measures. Proposed or enacted anti-ESG legislation, regulation, or policies may expose Infosys to additional legal, financial, or reputational risks based on its ESG ambitions and disclosures.
