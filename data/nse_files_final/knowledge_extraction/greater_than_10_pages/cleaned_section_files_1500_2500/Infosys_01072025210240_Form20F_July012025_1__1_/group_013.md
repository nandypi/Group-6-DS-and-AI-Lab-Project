The software industry is increasingly using open-source software in development. Infosys incorporates open-source technology into its services, proprietary products, and platforms. Open-source licenses may require component or integrated source code to be made publicly accessible under open-source terms. Unknown security vulnerabilities or third-party claims could require disclosure of source code or confidential product information and may adversely affect product development, sales, competitive position, operations, financial condition, and client relationships.

## Our net income would decrease if the Government of India reduces or withdraws tax benefits and other incentives it provides to us.

Many Infosys development centers in India are registered as Special Economic Zone units under the Special Economic Zone Act, 2005. Under Section 10AA of the Income Tax Act, 1961, eligible SEZ units that began providing services on or after April 1, 2005, may receive:

- A 100% income-tax deduction on profits or gains from exported services for the first five years beginning with the relevant assessment year.
- A 50% deduction for the following five years.
- Up to a 50% deduction for a further five years, subject to creating and using a Special Economic Zone Re-investment Reserve to acquire new plant and machinery for business purposes.

Changes to Indian SEZ policies could adversely affect Infosys’s results of operations and financial condition. If Infosys cannot use the SEZ Re-investment Reserve to invest in plant and machinery within the timeline specified under the Income Tax Act, it may have to pay tax on the unused reserve after the specified period, increasing its effective tax rate.

## In the event that the government of India or the government of another country changes its tax policies in a manner that is adverse to us, our tax expense may materially increase, reducing our profitability.

The General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR), addressing arrangements related to the OECD’s Base Erosion and Profit Shifting project, applied from fiscal 2018. An arrangement may be treated as an impermissible avoidance arrangement when obtaining a tax benefit is its main purpose or one of its purposes and at least one of the following applies:

- It creates rights and obligations not normally created between parties dealing at arm’s length.
- It misuses or abuses tax-law provisions.
- It lacks, or is deemed to lack, commercial substance.
- It is carried out in a manner not normally employed for a bona fide purpose.

If Infosys transactions are treated as impermissible avoidance arrangements under GAAR, its business, financial condition, and results of operations may be adversely affected.

The Finance Act, 2023, effective April 1, 2023, increased the withholding tax rate on payments to nonresidents for royalties or fees for technical services from 10% to 20%, plus applicable surcharge and cess, subject to furnishing an Indian Permanent Account Number or alternative documents where a PAN is unavailable. A lower rate may apply where a Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement, read with the Multilateral Instrument, exists.

Based on a Supreme Court ruling, payments to nonresidents for software purchases are not taxable as royalties when the payments are not characterized as royalties under an applicable Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement. Such payments are not subject to withholding when relevant tax documents are furnished. Infosys procures software licenses and technical services from nonresidents to deliver products and services to clients. If nonresidents do not provide the required documents, Infosys may incur additional withholding-tax costs, including gross-up costs.

Infosys has entered into Advance Pricing Agreements in multiple jurisdictions to provide predictability regarding tax obligations for overseas operations. Material changes to the critical assumptions underlying these agreements could affect taxes. When the agreements expire or are renewed, there is no certainty that they will be renewed or that renewed agreements will have the same or similar terms.

Infosys operates in multiple countries, and changes in tax rates or tax laws could affect its taxes. International tax-law and tax-practice changes arising from the OECD Pillars of the Inclusive Framework on Base Erosion and Profit Shifting, including global minimum tax and digital-services taxes, may increase tax costs.

## We operate in jurisdictions that impose transfer pricing and other tax-related regulations on us, and any failure to comply could adversely affect our profitability.

Infosys must comply with transfer-pricing regulations in India and other applicable countries, as well as local and municipal tax regimes in several jurisdictions. Noncompliance could result in additional taxes, penalties, and enforcement actions, adversely affecting profitability.

## Changes  in  the  policies  of  the  government  of  India  or  political  instability  may  adversely  affect  economic conditions in India generally, which could impact our business and prospects.

Changes in Indian laws and policies affecting technology companies, foreign investment, currency exchange, or investment in Infosys securities could adversely affect Indian economic conditions and Infosys’s business. Infosys depends on the Reserve Bank of India to make foreign-exchange payments and pay dividends. Exchange controls that restrict the remittance of funds could affect its ability to deliver services and pay dividends.

Changes to SEZ policies could adversely affect facilities established or operated in SEZs, as well as Infosys’s business, results of operations, and financial condition. Political instability could delay reforms and adversely affect the market for securities of Indian companies, including Infosys equity shares and ADSs, and the market for its services.

## Attempts to fully address concerns of activist shareholders may divert the time and attention of our management and Board of Directors and may impact the prices of our equity shares and ADSs.

Responding to activist shareholder queries and concerns may divert the time and attention of Infosys’s Board and management and require significant costs. These interactions may affect reputation, client and investor sentiment, and the prices of Infosys equity shares and ADSs.

## Our international expansion plans subject us to risks inherent to doing business internationally.

Infosys’s global presence exposes it to risks associated with compliance with treaties and national and local laws, including multiple or overlapping tax regimes, privacy and data-protection laws, export controls, restrictions on importing and exporting technologies, and labor laws concerning immigration, employee health and safety, wages, and benefits.

Infosys may face litigation or administrative actions brought by current or former employees, individually or as part of a class action, involving alleged wrongful termination, discrimination based on nationality, ethnicity, race, faith, gender, marital status, age, or disability, misclassification, redundancy payments under Transfer of Undertakings—Protection of Employment-type legislation, or other labor-law violations. Liability for unpaid compensation, redundancy payments, statutory penalties, or other damages could adversely affect operating profitability.

## Our ability to acquire companies organized outside India may depend on the approval of the RBI and the Government of India and failure to obtain this approval could negatively impact our business.

The Reserve Bank of India permits acquisitions of companies outside India by an Indian party under the automatic route, without approval, subject to conditions including:

- The transaction consideration is paid in cash.
- The transaction value does not exceed 400% of the acquiring company’s net worth as of the latest audited balance sheet date.
- The acquisition is funded with cash from the acquiring company’s existing foreign-currency accounts or proceeds from issuing ADRs or GDRs.
- The acquisition structure is otherwise permitted.

Financial commitments exceeding $1 billion or its equivalent in a financial year, and certain acquisition structures, require prior RBI approval even when the total financial commitment is within 400% of the acquiring company’s net worth.

Failure to obtain required approval from the RBI or another government agency could restrict Infosys’s international growth and adversely affect its business and prospects.

## Indian laws limit our ability to raise capital outside India, which could prevent us from operating our business or entering into a transaction that is in the best interests of our shareholders.

Indian foreign-exchange laws constrain Infosys’s ability to raise capital outside India through equity or convertible debt securities. Foreign investment in, or acquisition of, an Indian company generally does not require approval from Indian government authorities, including the RBI. However, restrictions apply in certain industries.

Changes in foreign-investment policies could restrict Infosys’s ability to raise capital. Limits on foreign ownership of Indian technology companies or pricing restrictions on ADR or GDR issuances could constrain additional equity investment by foreign investors. Such restrictions could also prevent transactions such as an acquisition by a non-Indian company that might otherwise benefit Infosys and holders of its equity shares and ADSs.
