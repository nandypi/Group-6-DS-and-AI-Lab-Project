## Maintenance of Minimum Public Shareholding ('MPS') as a Condition for Continuous Listing

The Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957 were amended on June 4, 2010, to require listed companies in India to maintain minimum public shareholding of 25%.

For this purpose, public shareholding includes equity shares held by the public and shares underlying depository receipts when:

1. The depository receipt holder has the right to issue voting instructions; and
2. The depository receipts are listed on an international exchange under the Depository Receipt Scheme, 2014.

Equity shares held by a trust established to implement employee benefit schemes under SEBI regulations are excluded from public shareholding.

Existing listed companies with public shareholding below 25% may meet the requirement through the following methods:

- Sale of shares held by promoters or the promoter group in the open market:
  - Sale of up to 2% of total paid-up equity share capital in a financial year, subject to the sale not exceeding five times the average monthly trading volume, until the MPS compliance due date under the SCRR.
  - Sale of up to 5% of paid-up capital during a financial year, provided public holding reaches 25% after the sale. The sale may occur in one or more tranches over a period not exceeding 12 months, and the shares sold may not exceed the listed entity’s trading volume during the preceding 12 months from the announcement date.
- Allotment of eligible securities through qualified institutional placement under Chapter VI of the SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018.
- Issuance of shares to the public through a prospectus.
- Offers for sale of promoter or promoter-group shares to the public through a prospectus.
- Sale of promoter shares through the stock exchange mechanism or secondary market under SEBI circular no. SEBI/HO/MRD/MRD-PoD-3/P/CIR/2023/10 dated January 10, 2023.
- Rights issues to public shareholders, with promoter and promoter-group shareholders foregoing their entitlement to equity shares arising from the issue.
- Bonus issues to public shareholders, with promoter and promoter-group shareholders foregoing their entitlement to equity shares arising from the issue.
- Increase in public holding through the exercise of options and allotment of shares under an employee stock option scheme, subject to a maximum of 2% of paid-up equity share capital.
- Transfer of promoter or promoter-group shares to an exchange-traded fund managed by a SEBI-registered mutual fund, subject to a maximum of 5% of paid-up equity share capital.
- Any other method approved by SEBI on a case-by-case basis.

As of March 31, 2025, Infosys’ public shareholding was approximately 85.14%.

## Voting Rights of Deposited Equity Shares Represented by ADSs

Under Indian law, voting on equity shares is conducted by a show of hands unless a poll is demanded by members present in person or by proxy who:

- Hold at least 10% of the total shares entitled to vote on the resolution; or
- Hold shares on which an aggregate amount of at least ₹5,00,000, or such higher amount as may be prescribed, has been paid up.

Companies whose equity shares are listed on a recognized stock exchange, and companies with at least 1,000 members, must provide electronic voting for resolutions considered at general meetings. The Listing Regulations require an e-voting facility for shareholders’ resolutions in accordance with the Indian Companies Act, 2013.

After receiving notice of a general meeting or a solicitation of consents or proxies from holders of shares or other deposited securities, the Depositary will set a record date to determine which ADS holders may give voting instructions.

The Depositary will mail ADS holders a notice stating:

1. The information contained in the meeting notice and related solicitation materials.
2. That each holder on the Depositary’s record date may instruct the Depositary on the exercise of voting rights relating to the deposited securities represented by the holder’s ADRs.
3. How voting instructions may be provided, including instructions to grant a discretionary proxy to a person designated by Infosys.
4. That, under the deposit agreement, failure to receive instructions may be treated as an instruction to grant a discretionary proxy to a person designated by Infosys, subject to specified conditions.

ADS holders may instruct the Depositary how to exercise voting rights for the shares underlying their ADSs. Instructions must be received by the Depositary by the specified deadline.

Subject to the deposit agreement, Indian law, and Infosys’ Memorandum and Articles of Association, the Depositary will seek to vote, or have its agents vote, in accordance with ADS holder instructions. The Depositary will not exercise voting discretion itself.

Neither the Depositary nor its agents are responsible for failure to carry out voting instructions, the manner in which a vote is cast, or the effect of a vote. ADS holders may not receive voting materials in time, and ADS holders or persons holding ADSs through brokers, dealers, or other third parties may not have an opportunity to vote.

## Insider Trading Regulations

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015 apply to listed and proposed-to-be-listed securities. They also prohibit communicating or procuring unpublished price-sensitive information in violation of the regulations.

Promoters, members of the promoter group, key managerial personnel, and directors must disclose their holdings of company securities within seven days of appointment or becoming a promoter or promoter-group member.

Promoters, promoter-group members, designated persons, and directors must disclose securities acquired or disposed of to the company within two trading days when the traded value, in one transaction or a series of transactions during a calendar quarter, exceeds ₹1,000,000 or another prescribed threshold.

The company must notify the stock exchanges where its securities are listed within two trading days of receiving the disclosure or becoming aware of the information.

A listed company may also require other connected persons or classes of connected persons to disclose their holdings and trading activity at specified intervals to monitor compliance with the Insider Trading Regulations.

## MATERIAL CONTRACTS

At the 2022 AGM, the Board and shareholders approved the reappointment of Salil Parekh as Chief Executive Officer and Managing Director, for a term beginning July 1, 2022, and ending March 31, 2027. They also approved a new employment agreement.

The employment agreement provides for:

- Annual salary
- Variable pay
- Stock compensation
- Employee benefits
- Vacation
- Expenses
- Minimum and maximum remuneration
- Ninety days’ notice by both Salil Parekh and Infosys before termination for reasons other than Cause or Good Reason, as defined in the agreement
- Potential severance benefits depending on the circumstances of termination

Infosys had not entered into any material contracts, other than contracts with its CEO, during the two years immediately preceding publication of the Form 20-F. The employment agreement had previously been filed with the Annual Report on Form 20-F.

Infosys also entered into agreements to indemnify its directors and officers for claims brought against them to the fullest extent permitted by applicable law. These agreements cover certain expenses, judgments, fines, and settlement amounts incurred in actions or proceedings, including actions by or on behalf of Infosys arising from the individual’s service as a director or officer. They also cover expenses related to public relations consultation when required. The form of these indemnification agreements had previously been filed with the Annual Report on Form 20-F.

## General

The subscription, purchase, and sale of shares of an Indian company are governed by Indian laws that restrict the issuance of shares to non-residents and the subsequent transfer of shares by or to non-residents. These restrictions have been relaxed in recent years.
