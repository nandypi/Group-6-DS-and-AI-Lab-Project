## Item 10. Additional Information

## MEMORANDUM AND ARTICLES OF ASSOCIATION

Infosys adopted new Articles of Association through a postal ballot concluded on March 31, 2017, to align them with the Indian Companies Act, 2013. Through a postal ballot concluded on August 22, 2018, the Company altered clause V of its Memorandum of Association to reflect an increase in authorized capital for the issue of bonus shares.

The corporate legal framework governing the Company is the notified provisions of the Companies Act, 2013. The Memorandum and Articles of Association were previously filed as exhibits to the Annual Report on Form 20-F.

The Articles of Association provide for a minimum of three directors and a maximum of 15 directors, unless shareholders approve the appointment of more than 15 directors by special resolution. As of March 31, 2025, Infosys had nine directors.

Unless the Articles of Association provide otherwise, at least two-thirds of the non-independent directors of a public company are liable to retire by rotation, with one-third of those directors retiring each year. Infosys’ Articles require one-third of the directors liable to retire by rotation, excluding independent directors, to retire each year. Directors who have held office longest retire first, and retiring directors are eligible for reappointment.

Executive directors are appointed by shareholders for a maximum term of five years and are eligible for reappointment. Independent directors may hold office for up to five consecutive years and may be reappointed through a shareholder special resolution. An independent director may serve only two consecutive terms and may be reappointed after three years from ceasing to be an independent director, provided the individual has no direct or indirect association with the Company during that period.

Infosys’ Articles do not require directors to hold Company shares.

A director with a personal interest in a transaction under discussion by the Board must disclose the interest and must not participate in the relevant meeting. Directors must disclose personal interests annually and at the first Board meeting after an interest arises.

Director remuneration may be fixed by the Board in accordance with the Indian Companies Act, 2013 and applicable Government of India provisions. Directors may not vote on their own remuneration. Remuneration is recommended by the Nomination and Remuneration Committee.

The Company may borrow money for legitimate corporate purposes. Shareholder approval by special resolution is required when borrowings, together with outstanding debt, exceed the aggregate of paid-up capital, securities premium, and free reserves. Temporary loans from bankers in the ordinary course of business are excluded from this calculation.

## General

Infosys’ authorized share capital is ₹24,000,000,000, divided into 4,800,000,000 equity shares with a par value of ₹5 per share.

As of March 31, 2025, 4,153,263,455 equity shares were issued, outstanding, and fully paid. A controlled trust held 9,655,927 equity shares under the 2015 Plan. Equity shares are the Company’s only class of share capital. Infosys had no convertible debentures or warrants outstanding.

As of March 31, 2025, 7,277,018 options were outstanding under the 2015 Plan and 8,072,635 options were outstanding under the 2019 Plan.

## Buyback completed in February 2023

On October 13, 2022, the Board approved an open-market share buyback through Indian stock exchanges for up to ₹9,300 crore, excluding buyback tax, at a price not exceeding ₹1,850 per share, subject to shareholder approval by postal ballot.

Shareholders approved the buyback through e-voting, with results declared on December 3, 2022. The offer was available to all equity shareholders other than the Promoters, Promoter Group, and persons in control of the Company.

The buyback commenced on December 7, 2022, and was completed on February 13, 2023. Infosys purchased and extinguished 60,426,348 equity shares at a volume-weighted average buyback price of ₹1,539.06 per share. The shares represented 1.44% of pre-buyback paid-up equity share capital.

The buyback resulted in a cash outflow of ₹9,300 crore, excluding transaction costs and buyback tax, equivalent to $1,130 million. It was funded from free reserves, including securities premium, under Section 68 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.

As of March 31, 2023, the Company had created a Capital Redemption Reserve of $3 million, equal to the nominal value of the shares bought back, through an appropriation from general reserve and retained earnings, in accordance with Section 69 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.

## Dividends

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013, the Board recommends dividends for shareholder approval at a general meeting, but the Board is not required to recommend a dividend. Shareholders may not declare a dividend exceeding the amount recommended by the Board.

Dividends are generally declared per equity share and paid in cash in proportion to the paid-up value of the shares within 30 days of the approving AGM. The Board may declare and pay interim dividends without shareholder approval, subject to the conditions of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.

Infosys must notify the stock exchanges on which its equity shares and ADSs are listed of the dividend declared per equity share and the applicable record date or book-closure date. Dividends may be paid in cash, by cheque or warrant, or through electronic mode to registered shareholders as of the record date.

Unpaid or unclaimed dividends remaining after 30 days from declaration must be transferred to a special bank account at an approved bank. Infosys transfers such amounts within seven days after the expiry of the 30-day period.

Amounts remaining unclaimed in that account for seven years from the transfer date are transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF) established by the Government of India. Equity shares for which dividends have not been paid or claimed for seven consecutive years or more are also transferred to the IEPF Authority.

Shareholders may claim unclaimed dividends and corresponding shares transferred to the IEPF, including any benefits accruing on those shares, by following the procedure prescribed under the Investor Education and Protection Fund Authority (Accounting, Audit, Transfer and Refund) Rules, 2016, as amended.

Dividends may be paid out of profits for the year in which the dividend is declared, after providing for depreciation, or out of undistributed profits from previous financial years after providing for depreciation. Before declaring a dividend, a company may transfer an appropriate percentage of its profits to reserves.

If profits are inadequate or absent, a dividend may be declared from accumulated profits transferred to free reserves, subject to applicable conditions. The declared dividend rate may not exceed the average dividend rates declared during the three financial years immediately preceding that year. This rate restriction does not apply if the Company had not declared dividends during the three immediately preceding financial years.
