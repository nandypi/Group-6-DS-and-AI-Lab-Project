## Functional currency, presentation currency and foreign exchange

The functional currency of Infosys, its Indian subsidiaries, and controlled trusts is the Indian rupee. The functional currencies of all other subsidiaries are their respective local currencies.

The consolidated financial statements included in the Annual Report on Form 20-F are presented in U.S. dollars, rounded to the nearest million. Assets and liabilities of foreign subsidiaries are translated using the exchange rate at the balance sheet date. Revenue, expenses, and cash flow items are translated using the monthly average exchange rate for the respective periods. Translation gains and losses are included in other comprehensive income and presented as currency translation reserves under other components of equity.

Generally, Indian law requires residents of India to repatriate foreign currency earnings to India. Section 8 of the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) requires an Indian company to take reasonable steps to realize and repatriate foreign currency earned outside India within the periods and in the manner specified by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).

RBI guidelines require realized foreign currency to be repatriated to a foreign currency account, such as an Exchange Earners’ Foreign Currency (EEFC) account with an authorized dealer in India. Subject to permitted use of balances and forward commitments, the total accruals in the account during a calendar month must be converted into rupees on or before the last day of the succeeding calendar month.

Infosys generally collects foreign-currency-denominated earnings through dedicated foreign currency accounts in the local countries of operation. Maintaining these accounts requires approval from an authorized dealer acting on behalf of the RBI. Failure to comply with RBI regulations could result in RBI enforcement actions.

Infosys generates substantially all its revenues in foreign currencies, particularly the U.S. dollar, United Kingdom pound sterling, euro, and Australian dollar. A significant portion of expenses is incurred in U.S. dollars and Indian rupees. Foreign exchange gains and losses arise from movements in the Indian rupee against currencies in which Infosys transacts business and from foreign exchange forward and option contracts.
