## 37 | Code of Conduct and Ethics

## COMMITTED TO OUR CUSTOMERS AND OUR SUPPLIERS

Employees who work with current or potential customers or suppliers represent Infosys and should act in a manner consistent with the Company’s C-LIFE values. Employees are expected to preserve and enhance the Company’s reputation through their actions.

## Fair Dealings

Employees must deal fairly with customers, suppliers, partners, service providers, competitors, and others with whom they interact at work. They must not take unfair advantage through manipulation, concealment, abuse of privileged information, misrepresentation of facts, or other unfair dealing practices.

## Confidential Information of Clients and Third Parties

The confidentiality and nondisclosure agreement signed when an employee joins Infosys describes confidentiality obligations to the Company and its clients. Employees have access to client information that may not be publicly available and must preserve its confidentiality.

Confidential, private, and sensitive information must be used responsibly and controlled and protected against prohibited, arbitrary, or careless disclosure. Unless the client has provided specific consent, preferably in writing, or there is a legal or professional right or duty to process or disclose the information, employees must not process or disclose confidential client information.

Confidential or proprietary information, including personal information about clients, Infosys, or other parties, must not be used for personal advantage or for the benefit of third parties. Infosys requires employees, agents, consultants, suppliers, and contractors to process personal information only when authorized by law or contract and to protect it through adequate technical and organizational measures.

## Free and Fair Competition

Infosys states that free and fair markets benefit clients and support the provision of quality products and services at competitive prices. Competition laws in many countries regulate relationships with customers, competitors, distributors, and resellers.

### What do we need to know? What is regulated? How do we comply?

Antitrust laws generally address:

- Unfair pricing practices, including price discrimination and price fixing
- Secret rebates
- Questionable entry barriers created through exclusive dealerships or distributorships
- Restrictions on carrying competing products
- Other practices that may undermine fair market competition

Employees who encounter questionable practices, including while bidding for services, should contact the Helpline. Employees must not enter into written or oral agreements or understandings intended to undermine fair market competition.

Employees must not knowingly make false or misleading statements about competitors or competitors’ products and services, customers, or suppliers.

Collusion among competitors is illegal. Communications with competitors should avoid subjects such as prices, other terms and conditions of sale, customers, and suppliers. Employees must not enter into written or oral agreements or understandings with competitors concerning these subjects.

For more information, refer to the Anti-Trust Policy.

## RESPONSE

An employee may compete for work involving a company owned by the employee’s sister, but the employee related to the owner must not participate in decisions about selecting the vendor or in managing the vendor if it is selected. The employee must not provide confidential Infosys information or any other advantage that could help the sister’s company be selected. Employees should consult their manager about actual or apparent conflicts of interest, particularly when family members are involved.

## Industrial Espionage

Infosys requires employees to respect competitors’ rights and comply with applicable laws. Employees must not appropriate or unlawfully use the information, materials, products, intellectual property, or proprietary or confidential information of suppliers, customers, business partners, or competitors.

## Governmental Relations

Employees must exercise additional care when dealing with government clients. Organizations that fail to follow applicable laws while working for government clients may face significant penalties, including debarment and monetary penalties.

Employees must not attempt to influence government employees outside the terms of the contractual arrangement with the government. Employment opportunities for former government officials must not be discussed without first seeking guidance and approval from the Office of Integrity and Compliance.

Employees must not initiate discussions for a contract with a business in which a government official or employee holds a significant interest without prior approval from the Office of Integrity and Compliance. Approvals may be requested from OIC@infosys.com.

## Selecting Suppliers

Where possible, Infosys selects significant suppliers and enters into significant supplier agreements through a competitive bid process. For more information, refer to the Supplier Code of Conduct.
