## Increase in the dividend distribution tax rate or introduction of new forms of taxes on distribution of profits or changes to the basis of application of these taxes and/or changes to Buyback regulations could adversely affect the returns to our shareholders.

Under the Corporate Policy on Capital Allocation, effective from fiscal 2025, the company expects to continue returning approximately 85% of cumulative free cash flow over a five-year period through a combination of semi-annual dividends, share buybacks and/or special dividends, subject to applicable laws and requisite approvals. The company expects to progressively increase its annual dividend per share, excluding special dividends, if any.

Free cash flow is defined as net cash provided by operating activities less capital expenditure, as reported in the consolidated statement of cash flows prepared under IFRS. Dividends and buybacks include applicable taxes. Dividends are declared and paid in Indian rupees.

The Finance Act 2020 replaced the Dividend Distribution Tax with the classical system of dividend taxation, under which dividend income is taxed in the hands of shareholders at their applicable tax rates. Companies paying dividends must withhold tax at applicable rates under the Indian Income Tax Act and applicable tax treaties, including the Multilateral Instrument where applicable, subject to the provision of required tax forms, including a Tax Residency Certificate for non-resident shareholders.

If the effective withholding tax rate on dividends increases, or new taxes on profit distributions are introduced, the amount received by shareholders after tax may decrease.

Indian listed companies that made a public announcement regarding a share buyback after July 5, 2019, were subject to additional income tax on buybacks under section 115QA of the Income Tax Act, while shareholders received an exemption under section 10(34A). Under the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024:

- Section 115QA does not apply to buybacks taking place on or after October 1, 2024.
- The shareholder exemption under section 10(34A) does not apply to such buybacks.
- Consideration received by shareholders for buybacks on or after October 1, 2024, is taxable as dividend income under section 2(22)(f), subject to applicable withholding tax and tax treaties, including the Multilateral Instrument where applicable.
- For capital-gains computation under section 46A, consideration received for the shares bought back is deemed to be nil, resulting in a capital loss for shareholders. The capital loss may be eligible for set-off and carry-forward under section 74.

From April 1, 2025, open-market buybacks through stock exchanges are no longer permitted. The company’s last three buybacks were open-market buybacks. The company stated that open-market buybacks had been its preferred mode because they allowed purchases at market prices, provided higher EPS accretion than tender-offer buybacks, and were easier to execute. The phase-out of open-market buybacks may affect the company’s ability to conduct buybacks and provide higher EPS accretion to remaining shareholders.

Each investor or prospective investor should consult their own tax advisors regarding the Indian and local tax consequences of acquiring, owning or disposing of equity shares or ADSs.

## Item 4. Information on the Company

## COMPANY OVERVIEW

Infosys provides consulting, technology, outsourcing and digital services to clients worldwide, including services supporting digital transformation.

Its primary geographic markets are North America, Europe, the Rest of World and India. These markets generated 57.9%, 29.8%, 9.2% and 3.1% of revenue, respectively, in fiscal 2025.

Infosys serves clients in the following industries:

- Financial services and insurance
- Retail, consumer packaged goods and logistics
- Communication, telecom original equipment manufacturer (OEM) and media
- Energy, utilities, resources and services
- Manufacturing
- Hi-tech
- Life sciences and health care

Revenue increased from $13,561 million in fiscal 2021 to $19,277 million in fiscal 2025, representing a compound annualized growth rate of 9.2%. Net profit increased from $2,623 million to $3,162 million over the same period, representing a compound annualized growth rate of 4.8%.

Total employees increased from 259,619 as of March 31, 2021, to 323,578 as of March 31, 2025, representing a compound annualized growth rate of 5.7%.

## A. HISTORY AND DEVELOPMENT OF THE COMPANY

Infosys was incorporated on July 2, 1981, in Pune, Maharashtra, India, as Infosys Consultants Private Limited under the Indian Companies Act, 1956. Its name changed to Infosys Technologies Private Limited in April 1992 and to Infosys Technologies Limited in June 1992, when it became a public limited company. In June 2011, the company changed its name to Infosys Limited.

Infosys completed an initial public offering of equity shares in India in February 1993 and was listed on Indian stock exchanges in June 1993. It completed its initial public offering of American Depositary Shares (ADSs) in the United States in 1999, followed by sponsored secondary ADS offerings in August 2003, June 2005 and November 2006.

After voluntarily delisting from the NASDAQ Global Select Market on December 11, 2012, Infosys began trading its ADSs on the NYSE on December 12, 2012, under the ticker symbol INFY. Infosys was inducted into the Dow Jones Sustainability Indices in fiscal 2018.

## Capital expenditure
