## A Safe Place to Work

To work effectively, all employees need a healthy and safe work environment. Substance abuse and the use or distribution of drugs and alcohol while at work are prohibited.

Unless required as part of an employee’s role, possession or use of weapons, firearms, or ammunition while conducting Company business is prohibited. Employees should report unsafe workplace situations and familiarize themselves with applicable emergency procedures and safety manuals.

## B ETHICS IN OUR BUSINESS ACTIVITIES

Infosys is subject to applicable regulations and requires compliance with them in both letter and spirit.

## Preventing Corruption

Corruption diverts public resources, undermines public accountability and the rule of law, increases business costs, and creates uncertainty. Bribery can expose the Company and its employees to legal, financial, and reputational risks.

In addition to the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (India), Infosys is subject to relevant anti-corruption laws, including the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act and the U.K. Bribery Act 2010. These laws prohibit bribery of government officials and commercial partners.

## Government Officials

Government officials include officers or employees of government agencies, departments, or instrumentalities; persons acting on behalf of governments; political party officers or employees; military, civil service, and judicial personnel; political or public office candidates; members of ruling or royal families; employees of government-controlled businesses; employees of public international organizations; and family members of government officials.

## Prevention Corruption

Employees must not directly or indirectly offer gifts, entertainment, or anything of value to government officials, commercial partners, customers, or their representatives to:

- Obtain or retain business.
- Influence business decisions.
- Secure an unfair advantage.

This includes bribes, kickbacks, and facilitation payments. Infosys employees and third parties acting on the Company’s behalf—including vendors, agents, customers, consultants, alliance partners, suppliers, and contractors—must not make such payments. The Company may be held responsible for the conduct of third parties acting on its behalf.

### What is a facilitation payment?

A facilitation payment is a payment to a government official to expedite or ensure routine actions, such as issuing visas, work permits, or licenses.

### What is a kickback?

A kickback involves two parties agreeing that a portion of sales or profits will be returned to the purchasing party in exchange for making a deal.

### What is a bribe?

A bribe is anything of value that may be viewed as an attempt to influence an action or decision to obtain or retain business or acquire an improper advantage. It may include money, gifts, favors, use of Company resources, entertainment, or other items of value.

## RESPONSE

Low-value holiday gift baskets received infrequently are unlikely to create an obligation or influence. Employees may continue to accept such gifts but are encouraged to share them with other employees. Employees should refer to the Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption Policy for permissible values.

## Anything of value? What does that mean?

“Anything of value” includes cash payments, gifts, entertainment, excessive business promotional activities, payment or reimbursement of expenses, investment opportunities, shares, securities, loans, contractual rights, promises of future employment, consulting-agreement payments, subcontracts, stock options, and similar items.

## Charitable Contributions

Infosys identifies education and research, social welfare, disaster relief, and similar social causes as typical areas for charitable support.

Before making a charitable contribution on behalf of Infosys:

- The recipient must be a registered, tax-paying, recognized organization.
- The contribution must be permissible under applicable local laws.
- The contribution must be made without a demand or expectation of business return.
- The beneficiary must not be related to an Infosys director or executive officer.
- Contributions must not be made in cash or to an individual’s private account.
- Contributions must comply with the Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption Policy.

Infosys maintains a diligence process for charitable contributions.

## Gifts and Entertainment

Gifts to customers, government officials, and other business-related parties must:

- Not be intended to obtain or retain business, influence a business decision, or gain an improper advantage.
- Be lawful in the country where they are given.
- Be permitted under applicable client policies.
- Constitute bona fide promotional or goodwill expenditure.
- Not be in cash.
- Be nominal in value, both individually and in aggregate.

Acceptance of gifts is not encouraged. Employees must comply with the Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption Policy.

## Transacting with Third Parties

Infosys seeks, where reasonably practicable, to interact directly with government officials. If a third party must interface with government authorities on behalf of Infosys, the Company should:

- Verify the third party’s credentials and reputation before engagement.
- Execute a formal contract requiring compliance with applicable anti-corruption and local laws.
- Ensure the engagement follows legal requirements and internal processes and guidelines.
- Provide the third party with a copy of the Supplier Code of Conduct.

The Company and individual directors, officers, or employees may be liable for payments made by a third-party agent if the Company makes a payment or transfers value to the agent knowing it will be given to a government official.

Fees, commissions, and other remuneration paid to intermediaries or third-party agents must be reasonable, bona fide, and commensurate with the functions and services performed. Related expenses should be tracked.

## Red Flags While Transacting with Third Parties

Red flags include:

- A background check reveals a flawed background or poor reputation.
- The transaction involves a country known for corrupt payments.
- The agent was suggested by a government official.
- The agent objects to anti-corruption compliance requirements.
- The agent has a personal or business relationship with a government official.
- The agent proposes unusual contract terms or payment arrangements, including cash payments, payment in another currency, or payment to a financial institution outside the country where the contract is performed.
- The agent requests that their identity be kept confidential.
- The agent requests false invoices or other false documentation.

## QUESTION

I have some shares of Infosys as part of my portfolio. I need to sell them as I am in need of money. How do I proceed?

## RESPONSE

Employees should read the Insider Trading Policy, follow the required pre-clearance procedures, and trade only when the trading window is open.

Infosys is a publicly listed company, and its securities—including shares and American Depository Receipts—may be traded by the public. Security prices may fluctuate based on publicly available information.

Unpublished Price Sensitive Information (UPSI), also known as material nonpublic information, includes information that is not generally available and that an investor would find useful in deciding whether to trade. Examples include information about dividends, changes in capital structure, mergers, de-mergers, acquisitions, delisting, disposal or expansion of the business, and changes in key managerial personnel. The list of UPSI is not exhaustive.

A person aware of UPSI may be subject to penalties under insider trading laws if they trade or communicate the information before it is made public.
