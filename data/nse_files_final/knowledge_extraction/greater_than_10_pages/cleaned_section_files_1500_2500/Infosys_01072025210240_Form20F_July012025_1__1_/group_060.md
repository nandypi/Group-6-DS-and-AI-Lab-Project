## Report of management

Management is responsible for preparing the company’s consolidated financial statements and related information included in the Annual Report. Management believes that the consolidated financial statements fairly reflect the form and substance of transactions and present the financial condition and results of operations of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries in conformity with IFRS Accounting Standards issued by the International Accounting Standards Board. The financial statements include amounts based on estimates and judgments that management considers reasonable under the circumstances.

The company maintains internal procedures and controls intended to provide reasonable assurance, at appropriate cost, that:

- Transactions are executed in accordance with company authorization.
- Transactions are properly recorded and reported in the consolidated financial statements.
- Assets are adequately safeguarded.

Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP audited the company’s consolidated financial statements for the years ended March 31, 2025, March 31, 2024, and March 31, 2023, in accordance with Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (United States) standards.

The Board of Directors appointed an Audit Committee composed of outside directors. The Committee meets with management, internal auditors, and independent auditors to review internal accounting controls and accounting, auditing, and financial reporting matters.

## REPORT OF INDEPENDENT REGISTERED PUBLIC ACCOUNTING FIRM

## To the Shareholders and the Board of Directors of Infosys Limited

## Opinion on the Financial Statements

The independent auditor audited the consolidated balance sheets of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries as of March 31, 2025 and March 31, 2024, the related consolidated statements of comprehensive income, changes in equity, and cash flows for each of the three years in the period ended March 31, 2025, and the related notes.

In the auditor’s opinion, the consolidated financial statements present fairly, in all material respects, the financial position of the Group as of March 31, 2025 and March 31, 2024, and its results of operations and cash flows for each of the three years in the period ended March 31, 2025, in conformity with IFRS Accounting Standards issued by the International Accounting Standards Board.

The auditor also audited the Group’s internal control over financial reporting as of March 31, 2025, based on the Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) issued by the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission. The auditor’s report dated July 1, 2025 expressed an unqualified opinion on the Group’s internal control over financial reporting.

## Basis for Opinion

The consolidated financial statements are the responsibility of Group management. The auditor’s responsibility is to express an opinion based on its audits.

The auditor is registered with the PCAOB and is required to be independent of the Group under U.S. federal securities laws and applicable Securities and Exchange Commission and PCAOB rules and regulations.

The audits were conducted in accordance with PCAOB standards. The audits included:

- Planning and performing procedures to obtain reasonable assurance that the financial statements were free of material misstatement due to error or fraud.
- Assessing risks of material misstatement and performing procedures responsive to those risks.
- Examining, on a test basis, evidence regarding amounts and disclosures in the consolidated financial statements.
- Evaluating the accounting principles used and significant estimates made by management.
- Evaluating the overall presentation of the consolidated financial statements.

The auditor stated that the audits provided a reasonable basis for its opinion.

## Critical Audit Matters

Critical audit matters are matters arising from the current-period audit that were communicated, or required to be communicated, to the Audit Committee; relate to accounts or disclosures material to the financial statements; and involved especially challenging, subjective, or complex judgments. Their communication does not alter the auditor’s opinion on the financial statements as a whole and does not constitute separate opinions on the matters or related accounts or disclosures.

## Revenue Recognition - Refer to Notes 1.5(a) and 2.11 to the consolidated financial statements

### Critical Audit Matter Description

The Group’s customer contracts may include multiple products and services. Revenue is derived from:

- Software development and related services
- Maintenance
- Consulting and package implementation
- Licensing of software products and platforms across core and digital offerings
- Business process management services

The Group assesses the services promised in each contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying distinct performance obligations requires significant judgment regarding the deliverables and the customer’s ability to benefit independently from them.

Some integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when the Group acts as the principal. The Group evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include:

- Responsibility for fulfilling the promise to provide the specified goods or services
- Inventory risk
- Pricing discretion
- Other factors relevant to determining control

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using either:

1. A straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
2. The percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits from the services and the Group’s costs to fulfill the contract are uneven because the services are generally discrete and non-repetitive.

The method used to recognize maintenance revenue requires judgment based on the promises in the contract and the nature of the deliverables.

Revenue recognition was identified as a critical audit matter because certain customer contracts require management judgment concerning:

1. The identification of distinct performance obligations.
2. Whether the Group is acting as a principal or an agent.
3. Whether fixed-price maintenance revenue should be recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method.

### How the Critical Audit Matter Was Addressed in the Audit

Audit procedures included:

- Testing the effectiveness of controls relating to:
  - Identifying distinct performance obligations.
  - Determining whether the Group acts as a principal or an agent.
  - Determining whether fixed-price maintenance revenue for certain contracts is recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Selecting a sample of customer contracts.
- Obtaining and reading contract documents for each selection, including master service agreements and other documents forming part of the agreement.
- Identifying significant contract terms and deliverables to assess management’s conclusions regarding:
  - Distinct performance obligations.
  - Whether the Group acts as a principal or an agent.
  - Whether fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method.

## Revenue recognition - Fixed price contracts using the percentage of completion method - Refer to Notes 1.5(a) and 2.11 to the consolidated financial statements

### Critical Audit Matter Description

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using either:

1. A straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
2. The percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits from services and the Group’s costs to fulfill the contract are uneven because the services are generally discrete and non-repetitive.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts, for which performance obligations are satisfied over time, is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.

Under the percentage-of-completion method, the Group determines actual efforts or costs expended to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs required to complete the contract. Efforts or costs expended are used to measure progress because there is a direct relationship between input and productivity.

Estimating total efforts or costs requires significant judgment and is reassessed throughout the contract period using the latest available information. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded in the period in which those losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

The estimate of total efforts or costs to complete fixed-price contracts was identified as a critical audit matter because it involves significant judgment and high inherent uncertainty. The estimate requires consideration of:

- Contract progress
- Efforts or costs incurred to date
- Estimated efforts or costs required to complete the remaining performance obligations over the contract term

The matter required a high degree of auditor judgment in evaluating the audit evidence and substantial audit effort to assess the reasonableness of the total estimated revenue recognized on fixed-price contracts.
