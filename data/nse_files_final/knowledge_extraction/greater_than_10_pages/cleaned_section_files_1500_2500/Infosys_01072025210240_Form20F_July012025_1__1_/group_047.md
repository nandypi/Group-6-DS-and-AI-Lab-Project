## Disclosure of Ownership Interest

Section 89 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013 requires holders of record who do not hold beneficial interests in shares of Indian companies to declare certain details to the company, including the nature of the holder’s interest and details of the beneficial owner.

A person who fails to make the required declaration within 30 days may be liable for a fine of up to ₹50,000. For a continuing failure, an additional fine of ₹200 per day may apply, subject to a maximum of ₹500,000.

Beneficial interest in a share includes, directly or indirectly, through any contract, arrangement, or otherwise, the right or entitlement, alone or together with another person, to:

1. Exercise or cause to be exercised any or all rights attached to the share; or
2. Receive or participate in any dividend or other distribution relating to the share.

Failure to comply with Section 89 does not affect the company’s obligation to pay dividends to the registered holder of shares for which the declaration has not been made.

It is unclear under Indian law whether Section 89 applies to holders of the company’s ADSs. Investors who exchange ADSs for the underlying equity shares will be subject to Section 89. ADS holders may also be required to comply with notification and disclosure obligations under the Deposit Agreement among the ADS holders, the company, and the Depositary.

## Declaration of Significant beneficial ownership in the Company

Section 90 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013 requires individual shareholders acting alone or together, or through one or more persons or trusts, including persons resident outside India, who hold a beneficial interest of not less than 10% in a company’s shares, or the right to exercise or the actual exercise of significant influence or control over the company, to declare the nature of their interest to the company.

The Companies (Significant Beneficial Ownership) Rules, 2018, as amended from time to time, prescribe thresholds and rules for identifying a “significant beneficial owner” and require filings and disclosures when relevant details change.

A person who fails to submit the required disclosures may be subject to a fine of at least ₹50,000 and up to ₹200,000. For a continuing failure, an additional fine of up to ₹1,000 per day may apply after the first day of the continuing failure.

Under the Companies (Management and Administration) Second Amendment Rules, 2023, a designated person within the company is responsible for providing the Registrar of Companies with information relating to beneficial interests in the company’s shares.

## Audit and Annual Report

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013, a company must file its financial statements with the Registrar of Companies within 30 days after the AGM.

Copies of the annual report and AGM notice must be submitted to the stock exchanges on which the company’s shares are listed and published on the company’s website no later than the day distribution to shareholders begins.

At least 21 days before the AGM, a listed company must distribute:

- Soft copies of the full annual report to shareholders who have registered their email addresses.
- Physical copies of the annual report to shareholders who have not registered their email addresses and to shareholders who request them.

A company must also file an annual return containing a list of shareholders and other company information within 60 days after the conclusion of the AGM.

## Reduction of Share Capital

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013, a company may reduce its share capital through a special resolution and approval of the court or tribunal of the state in which its registered office is situated, in the manner prescribed by the Act.

The reduction must be authorized by the company’s articles of association. These provisions do not apply to a company share buyback.

## Company Acquisition of Equity Shares

A company may acquire its own equity shares without court or National Company Law Tribunal approval if it complies with the prescribed rules, regulations, and conditions of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.

Public companies listed on recognized Indian stock exchanges must also comply with the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Buy-back of Securities) Regulations, 2018. As a public company listed on two recognized Indian stock exchanges, Infosys must comply with the applicable provisions of the Indian Companies Act, 2013 and the Buy-back Regulations.

For ADS holders to participate in a company share buyback, they must withdraw the equity shares underlying their ADSs before the record date fixed for the buyback so that they hold the equity shares on that record date.

There is no assurance that shares offered by an ADS investor in a company buyback will be accepted. Conversion of ADSs into underlying equity shares and reconversion of those equity shares into ADSs is subject to applicable limits on permissible foreign shareholding. Indian law currently permits foreign investment of up to 100% of the company’s share capital.

Participation by ADS holders in a buyback is also subject to applicable requirements under:

- The Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, and related rules and regulations.
- The Income Tax Act and related rules and regulations.
- The Depository Receipts Scheme, 2014.
- The SEBI Master Circulars for Depositories dated October 6, 2023 and December 3, 2023.
- Any required approvals from relevant authorities, including the Reserve Bank of India under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999.

ADS investors are advised to consult legal advisors before participating in a buyback, including regarding regulatory approvals and tax matters.

## Liquidation Rights

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013 and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, certain payments have priority over payments to equity shareholders. These include payments to:

- Employees.
- Tax authorities.
- Secured and unsecured lenders.
- Holders of shares whose terms provide for repayment priority over equity shares.

In a winding-up, holders of equity shares are entitled to repayment of the paid-up capital, or capital credited as paid-up, after the company has made the payments described above. Subject to those payments, any surplus assets are distributed to equity shareholders in proportion to their shareholdings.

## Redemption of Equity Shares

Subject to the share buyback provisions described under “Company Acquisition of Equity Shares,” equity shares are not redeemable under the Indian Companies Act, 2013.

## Discriminatory Provisions in Articles

The Articles of Association contain no provisions discriminating against or in favor of an existing or prospective holder of the company’s securities because that shareholder owns a substantial number of shares.

There are no restrictions on the company’s securities arising from any other class of securities or contracts.

## Alteration of Shareholder Rights

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013, and subject to the company’s memorandum or articles of association, the rights of a class of shareholders may be altered or varied by:

1. Written consent from holders of at least three-fourths of the issued shares of that class; or
2. A special resolution passed at a separate meeting of holders of the issued shares of that class.

If a variation by one class of shareholders affects the rights of another class, the consent of three-fourths of the affected class is also required.

If the articles of association contain no relevant provision, an alteration or variation is permitted provided it is not prohibited by the terms of issue of the shares of that class.

## Limitations on the Rights to Own Securities

Limitations on the ownership of securities of Indian companies, including the rights of non-resident or foreign shareholders to hold securities, are discussed in the sections titled “Currency Exchange Controls” and “Risk Factors” in Items 10 and 3 of the Annual Report.

## Provisions on Changes in Capital

The company’s authorized capital may be altered by an ordinary resolution of shareholders at a general meeting.

The additional issue of shares is subject to shareholders’ pre-emptive rights. A company may also:

- Increase its share capital.
- Consolidate its share capital into shares with a larger face value.
- Subdivide its shares by reducing their par value.

These actions are subject to an ordinary resolution of shareholders at a general meeting.

## Takeover Code

Under the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011 (the “Takeover Code”), an acquirer is a person who directly or indirectly acquires, or agrees to acquire, shares or voting rights in a target company, or acquires or agrees to acquire control over the target company, either alone or together with persons acting in concert.

When an acquirer and persons acting in concert acquire shares or voting rights representing 5% or more of the shares of a public listed Indian company, they must disclose their aggregate shareholding and voting rights to the company and the relevant stock exchanges within two working days after:

- The acquisition of the shares or voting rights; or
- Receipt of intimation of allotment of shares.

A person and persons acting in concert who hold shares or voting rights representing 5% or more of a target company must disclose the number of shares or voting rights held and any change exceeding 2% of the company’s total shareholding or voting rights. The disclosure must be made to the company and the relevant stock exchanges within two working days after receipt of intimation of allotment, or after the acquisition or disposal of shares or voting rights.

For a sale, disclosure is required even if the sale reduces the acquirer’s and persons acting in concert’s shareholding below 5%.

An acquisition of shares or voting rights that gives the acquirer and persons acting in concert 25% or more of the voting rights in, or control over, the target company triggers a requirement to make an open offer for at least 26% of the target company’s total shares. The offer price is determined under the Takeover Code.

The acquirer must make a public announcement of the open offer on the date on which the parties agree to acquire the shares, voting rights, or control.

For the financial year 2020–2021, a promoter could acquire more than 5% and up to 10% of the voting rights in a target company if the acquisition was made through a preferential issue of equity shares by the target company.
