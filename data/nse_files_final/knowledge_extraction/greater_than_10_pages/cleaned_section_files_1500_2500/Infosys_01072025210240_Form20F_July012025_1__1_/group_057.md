## Item 12. Description of Securities Other Than Equity Securities

The fees and charges payable by holders of Infosys ADSs include:

- A fee not exceeding U.S.$0.05 per ADS for each issuance of ADSs, including issuances resulting from share distributions, share splits, and rights distributions.
- A fee not exceeding U.S.$0.05 per ADS for each surrender of ADSs in exchange for the underlying deposited securities.
- A fee not exceeding U.S.$0.01 per ADS for each cash distribution.
- A fee not exceeding U.S.$0.01 per ADS for each stock dividend or other free or bonus distribution of the underlying deposited securities made in the form of ADSs.
- An annual fee not exceeding U.S.$0.02 per ADS for operating and maintaining the ADS program.
- For distributions of deposited securities, a fee equal to the fee for executing and delivering ADSs described above that would have been charged if the securities had instead been deposited with the depositary.

Under the deposit agreement, the depositary may also charge registered holders for:

- Taxes and other governmental charges incurred by the depositary or custodian on any ADS or underlying equity share.
- Transfer or registration fees for registering or transferring deposited securities, including fees charged by a central securities depository where applicable.
- Cable, telex, facsimile transmission, and delivery expenses.
- Customary foreign-currency conversion expenses, including expenses related to foreign-exchange controls and other regulatory requirements.
- Depositary fees and expenses related to regulatory requirements applicable to ADSs or the underlying deposited securities.
- Fees and expenses incurred by the depositary or its agents in servicing ADSs or the underlying deposited securities, selling securities, delivering deposited securities, or complying with applicable laws, rules, or regulations.

For cash distributions, fees are generally deducted from the cash distributed. Other fees may be collected in a manner determined by the depositary for ADSs registered in investors’ names and ADSs held in brokerage and custodian accounts through DTC. For non-cash distributions, such as stock dividends, the depositary charges the applicable ADS record-date holder when the distribution is made. For ADSs registered in an investor’s name, the depositary sends invoices to the applicable record-date ADS holder.

If ADS holders or beneficial owners owe taxes or other governmental charges, the depositary, custodian, or Infosys may withhold or deduct the amounts from distributions on deposited securities. They may also sell deposited securities on behalf of the holder or beneficial owner and apply the distributions or sale proceeds toward those taxes or charges, including applicable interest and penalties. The holder or beneficial owner remains liable for any deficiency.

## Fees and other payments made by the depositary

During fiscal 2025, the depositary incurred approximately $441,422 in expenses related to the ADS program:

- $429,446 was paid to proxy-processing firms for mailing notices, proxy cards, and other interim communications to ADS holders or their brokers, including dividend-reinvestment and transfer fees.
- $11,976 was paid to Ipreo for weekly and monthly market-intelligence ADR reports.

For the year ended March 31, 2025, the depositary paid Infosys approximately $7.33 million after withholding tax. Infosys may use these payments for investor-relations activities related to the ADR program, including annual reports and Form 20-F filings, listing fees, roadshows, investor targeting, peer analysis, perception studies, postage for annual and interim reports and other communications to ADR holders, broker conferences, and analyst events.

Under certain circumstances, including termination of the ADS program or removal of the depositary, Infosys may be required to repay a portion of amounts reimbursed in prior periods.

## Item 15. Controls and Procedures

## DISCLOSURE CONTROLS AND PROCEDURES

As of the end of the period covered by this Annual Report on Form 20-F, management, with the participation of the CEO and CFO, evaluated the effectiveness of Infosys’s disclosure controls and procedures as defined in Rules 13a-15(e) and 15d-15(e) under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, as amended. The evaluation stated that the relevant date was March 31, 2024.

Disclosure controls and procedures are designed to ensure that information required in Exchange Act reports is recorded, processed, summarized, and reported within the periods specified by SEC rules and forms. They also include controls designed to ensure that required information is accumulated and communicated to management, including the CEO and CFO, to support timely disclosure decisions.

Based on their evaluation, the CEO and CFO concluded that the disclosure controls and procedures were effective in providing reasonable assurance that:

- Information required in Exchange Act filings and submissions was recorded, processed, summarized, and reported within the periods specified by SEC rules and forms.
- Material information related to Infosys and its consolidated subsidiaries was accumulated and communicated to management, including the CEO and CFO, to support timely decisions about required disclosure.

## Management's Annual Report on Internal Control over Financial Reporting

Management is responsible for establishing and maintaining adequate internal control over financial reporting as defined in Rules 13a-15(f) and 15d-15(f) under the Exchange Act. Internal control over financial reporting is designed to provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of financial reporting and the preparation of financial statements for external purposes in accordance with International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB).

Internal control over financial reporting includes policies and procedures that:

- Maintain records that accurately and fairly reflect transactions and dispositions of assets in reasonable detail.
- Provide reasonable assurance that transactions are recorded as necessary to prepare financial statements in accordance with IFRS as issued by the IASB, and that receipts and expenditures are made only in accordance with authorizations of management and directors.
- Provide reasonable assurance regarding the prevention or timely detection of unauthorized acquisition, use, or disposition of assets that could materially affect the financial statements.

Because of its inherent limitations, internal control over financial reporting may not prevent or detect misstatements. Evaluations of effectiveness for future periods are also subject to the risk that controls may become inadequate because of changes in conditions or that compliance with policies and procedures may deteriorate.

Management assessed the effectiveness of internal control over financial reporting as of March 31, 2025, using the Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) issued by the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission (COSO). Management concluded that Infosys’s internal control over financial reporting was effective as of March 31, 2025.

Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP audited the consolidated financial statements included in this Annual Report on Form 20-F and issued a report on the effectiveness of internal control over financial reporting as of March 31, 2025.

## To the Shareholders and the Board of Directors of Infosys Limited

## Opinion on Internal Control over Financial Reporting

Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP audited the internal control over financial reporting of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries as of March 31, 2025, based on the COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013).

The auditor’s opinion was that the Group maintained, in all material respects, effective internal control over financial reporting as of March 31, 2025, based on the COSO framework.

The auditor also audited the Group’s consolidated financial statements as of and for the year ended March 31, 2025, in accordance with PCAOB standards. Its report, dated July 1, 2025, expressed an unqualified opinion on those consolidated financial statements.
