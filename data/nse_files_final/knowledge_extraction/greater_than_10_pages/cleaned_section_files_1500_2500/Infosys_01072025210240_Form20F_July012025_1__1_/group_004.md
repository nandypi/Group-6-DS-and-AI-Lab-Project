## Our clients may be the subject of economic or other sanctions by governments and regulators in key geographies that we operate in, limiting our ability to grow these relationships, and risking increased penalties and exposure of our business to consequential sanctions.

Infosys engages with clients operating across geographies and sectors, including conflict zones and highly regulated sectors. Sanctions may apply to clients, their investors, or key managerial personnel before or during an engagement.

Infosys takes reasonable precautions to determine whether potential clients appear on sanctions lists, but screening is limited by the availability and accuracy of public-domain data and third-party databases. If a client becomes subject to sanctions during an engagement, Infosys may face consequential sanctions, administrative action, or loss of government contracts or engagements.

## A large part of our revenues is dependent on a limited number of our clients, and the loss of any one of our major clients could significantly impact our business.

Infosys has historically derived, and expects to continue deriving, a significant portion of its revenues from a limited number of clients. In fiscal 2025:

- The five largest clients accounted for 13.2% of total revenues.
- The ten largest clients accounted for approximately 20.5% of total revenues.

The volume of work performed for individual clients may vary from year to year. A major client in one year may not provide the same level of revenue in a subsequent year.

Client business may decline or end if a client:

- Moves more work in-house.
- Establishes or expands a Global Capability Center (GCC).
- Replaces existing software with packaged software supported by the licensor.
- Reduces technology spending because of geopolitical, economic, or competitive conditions.

The loss of a major client or a significant reduction in its business could adversely affect Infosys’ revenues and profitability.

## Financial stability of our clients may be affected owing to several factors such as demand and supply challenges, currency fluctuations, regulatory sanctions, changes in tariffs or trade restrictions, geopolitical conflicts and other macroeconomic conditions which may adversely impact our ability to recover fees for the services rendered to them.

Client business pressures may affect clients’ ability to pay vendors on time, reduce their credit ratings, and limit their ability to raise funds. A credit-rating downgrade may adversely affect Infosys’ credit terms with those clients, cash flow, and results of operations.

Infosys evaluates client credit risks before onboarding clients, but sudden changes in clients’ financial health may impair fee recovery. Relevant factors include:

- Demand and supply challenges.
- Currency fluctuations.
- Regulatory sanctions.
- Changes in tariffs or trade restrictions.
- Volatility in key business sectors.
- Geopolitical conflicts.
- Other global macroeconomic challenges.

An adverse change in India’s debt rating or in Infosys’ credit rating from international or domestic rating agencies could also negatively affect the business and profitability.

## Outbreaks of contagious diseases, viruses or pandemics, could disrupt our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

Outbreaks of contagious diseases, viruses, or pandemics, together with related mitigation efforts, have disrupted and may in the future disrupt Infosys’ business, financial condition, and results of operations.

The effect of a future outbreak would depend on uncertain factors, including:

- Availability of effective vaccines.
- Regulatory actions affecting Infosys and its clients.
- Changes in consumer behavior or spending patterns.
- Effects on third-party providers.

A pandemic or similar public health threat could also heighten other risks described in the Risk Factors section.

## We may not be able to provide end-to-end business solutions for our clients, which could lead to clients discontinuing their work with us, which in turn could harm our business.

Infosys has expanded the breadth and scope of its client engagements. Its offerings include:

- Software applications.
- Automation solutions.
- Digital design and analytics services.
- Engineering services.
- Cloud-related services.
- Application development and maintenance.
- Consulting.
- Business process management.
- Systems integration.
- Security and infrastructure management.

Failure to anticipate and develop offerings using emerging technologies, or to enhance existing offerings in response to changes in technology and target industries, could adversely affect the business, financial condition, and results of operations. The relevant technologies include AI-based automation, generative AI, cloud, robotic process automation, the Internet of Things, and autonomous vehicles.

An inability to provide solutions in areas of client interest may lead clients to insource work, establish GCCs, or shift work to competitors.

Broader service offerings may result in larger and more complex projects. These projects require closer relationships with clients and potentially with other technology service providers and vendors, as well as a thorough understanding of client operations. Infosys’ ability to establish these relationships depends on the proficiency of its technology professionals and management personnel. If Infosys does not adequately understand client operations, its offerings may not meet client needs and could jeopardize client engagements, revenues, and financial condition.

Larger projects may involve multiple components, engagements, or stages. Clients may not retain Infosys for later stages, or may cancel or delay planned engagements for reasons unrelated to service quality and outside Infosys’ control, including:

- Changes in clients’ business or financial condition.
- General economic conditions.
- Cost pressures from tariffs or trade restrictions, including tariffs on IT services.
- Geopolitical conflicts in Eastern Europe and the Middle East.

Cancellations, terminations, or delays may complicate project resource planning and negatively affect profitability. Geopolitical conflicts may also impair Infosys’ ability to serve global clients in certain geographies, potentially resulting in client dissatisfaction, loss of future business, contract terminations, and litigation.

Business owners at client organizations increasingly make or influence technology-related purchasing decisions. Infosys’ revenues may be adversely affected if it cannot maintain or establish relationships with new buying centers or clearly communicate the value of its technology services to those business functions.

## Intense competition in the market for technology services could affect our win rates and pricing, which could reduce our market share and decrease our revenues and profits.

Infosys’ revenues and profits depend partly on continued demand from existing and new clients and its ability to meet that demand competitively and cost-effectively.

The technology services market includes competition from:

- Large global consulting firms.
- India-based technology services firms.
- Software and solution providers.
- Niche service providers.
- In-house IT departments.
- Global Capability Centers of large corporations.

Recent divestitures and acquisitions have contributed to industry consolidation. This may create larger competitors with significant resources or competitors with stronger offerings in emerging areas such as generative AI, agentic AI, digital design, cloud-based solutions, and cybersecurity.

Some competitors have added offshore capabilities and may deliver services through offshore and onsite models more efficiently. Competition may arise in countries where Infosys currently operates and in countries where it expects to expand, including from consulting, technology services, and outsourcing firms operating in Eastern Europe, China, and the Philippines.

Competitors may have greater:

- Financial, technical, and marketing resources.
- Revenues and international operating experience.
- Existing client relationships and technology partnerships.
- Brand recognition.
- Ability to recruit key technology and management talent.

Competitors’ investments in generative AI may improve productivity, lower clients’ total cost of ownership, and increase pricing pressure. Infosys may need to reduce prices to retain clients or win business. Its ability to compete may also be affected by competitor pricing and responsiveness to client needs.

Infosys has invested in building capabilities and training employees in AI, generative AI, and cloud technologies. Large existing competitors have made similar investments, while advancements in these technologies have reduced entry barriers for new software-services competitors. Failure to compete effectively and defend market share could negatively affect revenues and financial condition.

Clients may expect efficiency gains, volume discounts, or lower rates as their business with Infosys increases. This may limit Infosys’ ability to pass cost increases to clients. Existing and new clients are also increasingly using third-party consultants to negotiate contractual terms. Macroeconomic uncertainty may further limit price increases and encourage clients to renegotiate existing contracts.

Clients may increase their in-house IT capabilities or control over technology investments by establishing or expanding GCCs in India or in locations such as Eastern Europe, Latin America, and Southeast Asia. These centers may create partnership opportunities but may also erode part of Infosys’ addressable market and increase pricing pressure.
