## 1.4 Use of estimates and judgments

The preparation of financial statements in conformity with IFRS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies; the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses; and disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities.

Critical accounting estimates involving complex or subjective judgments and assumptions are disclosed in Note 1.5. Accounting estimates may change as circumstances change. Changes are reflected in the consolidated financial statements in the period in which they are made and, if material, are disclosed in the notes. Actual results may differ from these estimates.

## 1.5 Critical accounting estimates and judgements

### a. Revenue recognition

The Group's customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is considered for recognition and measurement when:

- The contract has been approved in writing by the parties.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- The contract is legally enforceable.

The Group assesses the services promised in each contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying distinct performance obligations, determining deliverables, assessing whether customers can benefit independently from those deliverables, and allocating the transaction price involve significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and the Group's costs to fulfill the contract are uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method. The method used depends on the promises in the contract and the nature of the deliverables.

The Group uses the percentage-of-completion method for other fixed-price contracts. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs. Estimating total efforts or costs involves significant judgment and is reassessed throughout the contract period using the latest available information.

Some integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Group assesses whether it controls the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer, considering factors including:

- Responsibility for fulfilling the promise to provide the goods or services
- Inventory risk
- Pricing discretion
- Other control-related factors

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded in the period in which the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs required to complete the contract.

### b. Income taxes

The Group's two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. The Group also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is involved in determining income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, management considers whether some or all of the assets may not be realized. Realization depends on generating future taxable income during the periods in which temporary differences become deductible. Management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies.

Based on historical taxable income and projections for future taxable income during the periods in which deferred tax assets are deductible, management believes the Group will realize the benefits of those deductible differences. The realizable amount could be reduced in the near term if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period are reduced. Refer to Note 2.18.

### c. Business combinations and intangible assets

Business combinations are accounted for using IFRS 3 (Revised), *Business Combinations*. IFRS 3 requires the Group to fair value identifiable intangible assets and contingent consideration to determine the net fair value of the acquiree's identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities.

External valuation experts conduct these valuations. Estimates are required to determine the value of contingent consideration, option arrangements, and intangible assets. These measurements are based on information available at the acquisition date and on management's reasonable expectations and assumptions. Refer to Notes 2.9 and 2.10.

### d. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Group's asset base. Periodic depreciation depends on estimates of each asset's expected useful life and residual value at the end of its life.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. Refer to Note 2.7.

### e. Impairment of Goodwill

Goodwill is tested for impairment annually and whenever there is an indication that the recoverable amount of a cash-generating unit (CGU) is less than its carrying amount.

For impairment testing, goodwill is allocated to the CGU or group of CGUs that benefits from the acquisition synergies and represents the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes.

The recoverable amount of CGUs is determined as the higher of value in use and fair value less costs to sell. Key assumptions in cash-flow projections include estimated long-term growth rates, weighted average cost of capital, and estimated operating margins. Refer to Note 2.9.

### Amendments to IAS 21

On August 15, 2023, the IASB issued amendments to IAS 21, *The Effects of Changes in Foreign Exchange Rates*, concerning lack of exchangeability. The amendments specify when a currency is exchangeable into another currency, when it is not, and how an entity determines the exchange rate when a currency is not exchangeable.

The amendments are effective for annual periods beginning on or after January 1, 2025, although early adoption is permitted. The Group evaluated the amendments and does not expect a material impact on its consolidated financial statements.

### IFRS 18 - Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements

On April 9, 2024, the IASB issued IFRS 18, *Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements*, which will replace IAS 1, *Presentation of Financial Statements*, from its effective date.

IFRS 18 introduces new requirements for information presented in the primary financial statements and disclosed in the notes, focusing on the statement of profit or loss. It introduces three categories for income and expenses: operating, investing, and financing.

IFRS 18 is effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2027, although early adoption is permitted. The Group has not yet evaluated the impact of the amendment.

### Amendments to IFRS 9 Financial Instruments and IFRS 7 Financial Instruments: Disclosures

On May 30, 2024, the IASB issued amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7 that:

- Clarify the classification of financial assets with environmental, social, and governance (ESG) and similar features.
- Address derecognition of financial liabilities settled through electronic payment systems.
- Introduce additional disclosure requirements concerning investments in equity instruments designated at fair value through other comprehensive income and financial instruments with contingent features.

The amendments are effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026, although early adoption is permitted. The Group has not yet evaluated their impact.

On December 18, 2024, the IASB issued further amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7 concerning:

- Factors considered in assessing the own-use requirements for contracts to buy and take delivery of nature-dependent renewable electricity.
- Hedge-accounting treatment for nature-dependent renewable electricity.
- Related disclosures.

These amendments are effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026, although early adoption is permitted. The Group evaluated the amendments and determined that they have no impact on its consolidated financial statements.
