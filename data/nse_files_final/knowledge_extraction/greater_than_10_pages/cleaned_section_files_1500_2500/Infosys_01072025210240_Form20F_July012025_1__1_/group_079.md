## Proposed acquisitions

On April 17, 2025, Infosys Singapore Pte Ltd., a wholly owned step-down subsidiary of Infosys Limited, entered into a definitive agreement to acquire 100% of the equity share capital of The Missing Link, a cybersecurity service provider headquartered in Australia, for consideration including earn-outs of up to AUD 98 million (approximately $62 million), excluding management incentives and retention bonuses, subject to customary closing adjustments. The acquisition was completed on April 30, 2025.

On April 17, 2025, Infosys Nova Holdings LLC, a wholly owned step-down subsidiary of Infosys Limited, entered into a definitive agreement to acquire 100% of the partnership interests of MRE Consulting Ltd., an energy consulting company headquartered in the United States, for consideration including earn-outs of up to $36 million, excluding management incentives and retention bonuses, subject to customary closing adjustments. To consummate the transaction, Infosys Nova Holdings LLC simultaneously incorporated Infosys Energy Consulting Services LLC. The acquisition was completed on April 30, 2025.

## 2.11 Revenue from operations

## Accounting policy

The Group derives revenues primarily from IT services comprising software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting and package implementation, licensing of software products and platforms across the Group’s core and digital offerings, collectively referred to as software-related services, and business process management services. Customer contracts may be on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe basis.

Revenue is recognized when a written contract has been approved by the parties, the parties are committed to performing their respective obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable. Revenue is recognized upon transfer of control of promised products or services to customers at an amount reflecting the consideration the Group expects to receive. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations in each contract and allocates the transaction price to them based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. When that evidence is unavailable, the Group generally estimates standalone selling prices using expected cost plus an appropriate margin.

Contracts may include variable consideration, including rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue when the uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue from time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services involve an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts whose performance obligations are satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because of the direct relationship between inputs and productivity. Progress is measured as costs or efforts incurred to date divided by estimated total costs or efforts. Transaction price and estimated total costs or efforts are monitored throughout the contract term, with changes recognized in net profit when estimates change or are revised. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.

Customer billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing and milestone-based progress billings. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

In software development, related services, and maintenance arrangements, software development and related services generally qualify as distinct performance obligations. Revenue is allocated to each performance obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. For software development and related services, performance obligations are satisfied as services are rendered because customers generally obtain control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. Where hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated to them based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the service period based on the contractual measure of progress.

Revenue from licenses providing the customer with a “right to use” the license is recognized when the license is made available to the customer. Revenue from licenses providing a “right to access” is recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When the standalone selling price for implementation cannot be determined, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. If the license requires substantial customization as part of implementation, the license and implementation fee is treated as a single performance obligation, and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services related to software product sales is recognized as the performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when the Group is the principal. The Group evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer, considering responsibility for fulfillment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors.

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price, or both, of a contract that is approved by the parties. A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for as a separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price. Otherwise, it is accounted for as termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract. A modification that does not create a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them.

Certain eligible, nonrecurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, are recognized as assets when they:

- Relate directly to the contract;
- Generate or enhance resources that will be used to satisfy future performance obligations; and
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to cost of sales over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored for impairment. Impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount of the capitalized costs.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its statement of comprehensive income.
