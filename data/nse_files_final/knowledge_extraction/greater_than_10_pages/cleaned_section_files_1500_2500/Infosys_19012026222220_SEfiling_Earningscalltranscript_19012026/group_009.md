## Salil Parekh

In financial services, Infosys is seeing discretionary spending begin to return. In energy and utilities, based on recent deals and AI work across its clients, Infosys expects the next financial year to show growth over the current financial year. The company is also monitoring other industries for a recovery in discretionary spending.

Management said that improving AI activity and potential economic and GDP growth in the United States could give clients greater ability to spend on technology.

On AI agents, Infosys is developing Topaz Fabric, a suite of purpose-built agents that works with interfaces from multiple native AI companies and supports horizontal and industry-specific client functions. Infosys also plans to integrate, implement, and expand agents developed by other companies, using its knowledge of clients’ technology landscapes and industries.

Infosys has built four small language models. Its Finacle product suite is expected to become more AI-oriented. Management said the company is not currently planning large-scale foundational model development and will focus on small language models and related capabilities.

Topaz also includes AI wrappers and orchestration modules intended to help clients switch between foundation models, switch between agents, and select agents.

Regarding the six areas of the AI services pool, Infosys is already undertaking work in several areas. At its Investor Day, the company plans to provide more detail on its work across industries, showcase its campus AI labs, and discuss the scale of the opportunity and potential growth dynamics.

Management said it expects to provide early indications on the CY 2026 budget during the April call, after completing the financial year.

Infosys reported a strong quarter, including good large deals and an increase in guidance. Its nine-month margin was 21%, after absorbing a 50-basis-point increase in sales costs and lower utilization associated with building capacity for future requirements.

Management reiterated that discretionary spending was beginning to return in financial services and that energy, utilities, and financial services were expected to look better in the next financial year than in the current year.
