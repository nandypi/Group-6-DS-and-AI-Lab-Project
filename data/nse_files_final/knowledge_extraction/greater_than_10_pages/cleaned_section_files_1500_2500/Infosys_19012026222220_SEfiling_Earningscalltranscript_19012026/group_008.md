## Moderator

Thank you very much. Next question is from the line of Keith Bachman from BMO Capital. Please go ahead.

## Keith Bachman

Could you speak about how you anticipate revenue per employee changing over the next couple of years? The question is intended to better understand how you see Infosys positioned in the industry, particularly its value proposition of managing agents instead of managing people.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

As with other technology waves, companies ahead of the curve may see increased productivity and retain some of the resulting premium, reflected in better pricing. Companies behind the curve in delivering productivity may see a decline in pricing.

Pricing models are also changing, with emerging approaches for pricing agents and the underlying platform. It is still too early to determine how these models will affect linearity or non-linearity.

## Keith Bachman

There have been press reports about the relationship with Daimler potentially moving away. Is the Q4 guidance reflecting any attrition in contracts or large contracts? Should investors consider any major contract attrition when looking toward FY'27?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Current contracts are valid through December 2026. Infosys does not currently see any contracts within that period expiring. The company does not provide client-specific or project-specific details beyond that.

## Moderator

Thank you very much. Next question is from the line of Gaurav Rateria from Morgan Stanley. Please go ahead.

## Gaurav Rateria

Regarding the deal renewal cycle, what has Infosys observed in renewals of larger deals using AI tools? Has there been higher-than-usual leakage?

## Salil Parekh

Large renewals and deals won from other companies, including through consolidation, generally involve significant client expectations for AI productivity benefits.

Infosys has modeled the productivity benefits currently being observed and built a path toward those benefits because these are typically three- to five-year deals. Client expectations over five years can differ significantly from current observations.

Infosys has been winning large deals at a strong pace. Across the first three quarters, the company had already exceeded the full-year total of the previous year. Q3 large deals were valued at $4.8 billion. The company has also won net-new deals, including NHS, and believes it is gaining market share through consolidation and other wins.

## Gaurav Rateria

Does Infosys have better visibility for CY'26 than it had for the previous year? The company entered the previous year after a significant fourth-quarter decline caused by specific issues, whereas the current guidance indicates a better fourth-quarter outlook. Does this provide a tailwind entering FY'27 compared with the entry into FY'26?

## Salil Parekh

The full-year guidance range provides good traction for Q4, supported by large deals that have been won and Q3 performance. Discretionary spending is returning in financial services. Energy, utilities, and financial services are expected to look better in the next financial year than in the current financial year. Infosys also sees good growth opportunities in six AI services value pools.

## Moderator

Thank you very much. Next question is from the line of Sandeep Shah from Equirus Securities. Please go ahead.

## Sandeep Shah

Is Infosys seeing the average size of AI-led deals increase materially year over year across sectors? If clients are becoming less concerned about modernizing legacy applications, could this lead to better discretionary spending on those services in CY'26 compared with the previous year?

## Salil Parekh

There are deals focused exclusively on AI, as well as deals in which AI is pervasive across every service being offered. As a result, it is difficult to assess deal size solely on the basis of AI. Overall, AI usage is increasing substantially across services, and AI-only deals are also progressing well.

Infosys is seeing legacy application modernization across sectors because AI can make modernization more efficient and improve client returns. Whether this leads to additional discretionary spending depends on industry conditions. Financial services is currently showing this trend, while developments in other industries will vary.

## Sandeep Shah

Has the number of deals below $50 million started increasing, and could this become a sustained trend? Also, should third-party costs as a percentage of revenue remain stable or decline beyond FY'26? Finally, will the INR 165 crore profit on property, plant and equipment create a headwind in the fourth quarter?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys does not externally share data on deals below $50 million. Overall wins are looking good, and the company is also seeing growth in work with smaller accounts.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

At the beginning of the year, Infosys expected third-party costs to be lower than the previous year because deals signed in the prior year were ramping up and additional deals were in the pipeline. The company did not yet have all the details required to provide guidance for the next year.

## Sandeep Shah

Could the 35–36 basis-point benefit from the INR 165 crore profit in the current quarter become a headwind in the next quarter because this line item may not recur?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Companies of this size regularly experience one-off items that create headwinds or tailwinds, either in revenue or costs. The property, plant and equipment item was largely or more than offset by an increase in variable pay.

## Moderator

Thank you very much. Next question is from the line of Jamie Friedman from Susquehanna International. Please go ahead.

## Jamie Friedman

Is the improvement in discretionary spending and financial services expected to expand into other verticals? Management also mentioned energy and resources.

How is Infosys thinking about the journey from AI to agentification? How has its thinking evolved toward agentification, and how does it view the productization of its AI initiatives across those two dimensions?
