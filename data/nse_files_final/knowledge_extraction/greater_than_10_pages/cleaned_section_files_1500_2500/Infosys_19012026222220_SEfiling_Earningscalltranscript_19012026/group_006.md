## Tailwinds of

- 40 basis points from currency movement.
- 40 basis points from Maximus, comprising primarily value-based selling, critical portfolios, and lean and automation.

## offset by

- 70 basis points from furloughs and lower working days.

The impact of higher variable pay was partly offset by one-off benefits during the quarter.

Consolidated cash and investments were $3.9 billion at the end of the quarter after returning $3 billion to shareholders through dividends and buybacks. Yield on the cash balance was 6.19%, and ROE stood at 32.8%.

Infosys signed 26 large deals during the quarter, including two mega deals:

## Financial services

Financial services grew approximately 5% over the last nine months, led by large deal wins and increased discretionary spending across banking, payments, mortgages, and asset and wealth management.

There is elevated interest in AI-led transformation, platform modernization, and vendor consolidation, with a shift from compliance spending toward business growth. Core transformation across financial-services sub-verticals is creating a strategic pipeline and opportunities for IP and platform partnerships.

Infosys is a preferred AI partner for 15 of its top 25 banking clients. Management said the increase in discretionary spending and recent deal wins position the vertical for better growth in FY'27.

## Manufacturing

Manufacturing is affected by tariff uncertainties, which are preventing clients from committing to long-term investments. Discretionary spending is under pressure and decision-making is slow. Industrial and aerospace are performing well, while the automotive sector remains challenged.

Clients are prioritizing cost discipline, consolidation, and efficiency. Infosys is supporting them through digital rationalization and AI-led productivity initiatives. The overall pipeline remains healthy, with focus on cost takeouts, infrastructure consolidation, and ERP modernization.

## EURS

EURS companies are increasingly allocating budgets toward AI infrastructure, data readiness, cloud, and software platforms. Demand for setting up GCCs is increasing across sectors, with most clients seeking systems integrators to complement their GCC strategies.

Infosys is a preferred AI partner for 15 of its top 25 clients. Management expects increased discretionary demand in utilities and energy to support growth acceleration in FY'27.

The utilities sector is seeing increased demand driven by investments in infrastructure and AI data centers. Energy-sector discretionary demand is focused on decarbonization and low-carbon solutions, alongside cost optimization and consolidation related to enterprise AI adoption.

## Retail and CPG

Retail and CPG clients continue to face uncertainty from tariff negotiations and changing geopolitical conditions. Clients are prioritizing cost takeouts and AI-led productivity deals, while discretionary spending remains soft except in SAP, DNA, testing, and AI-augmented services.

Infosys continues to use Topaz and its AI Next platform to deliver business value, with safeguards covering privacy, governance, and customer and employee experience.

## Communication

The communications sector continues to be affected by geopolitical uncertainty. Deal wins from prior quarters are supporting year-on-year growth acceleration.

Telecommunications companies are prioritizing AI automation and productivity improvements, while traditional IT spending remains under pressure. Clients are increasingly seeking systems-integrator partnerships to scale and innovate within complex ecosystems. There is a focus on outcome-based engagement models rather than traditional effort-based pricing.

## FY'26 guidance

Strong year-to-date performance and deal wins enabled Infosys to revise its FY'26 revenue-growth guidance upward to 3%–3.5%. The guidance excludes revenue from the Telstra joint venture because regulatory approvals remain pending.

Operating margin guidance remains at 20%–22%.

## Analyst questions

### FY'27 growth outlook and Q4 guidance

Management said the improved guidance reflects momentum and deal wins, but other sectors are recovering at a lower level. Lower working and calendar days in the quarter are also a headwind.

For FY'27, management identified stronger growth in Energy, Utilities, Resources and Services and Financial services, supported by large deal wins, AI activity, and partnerships with 15 of the top 25 clients. Management did not provide guidance for the overall business.

### Margin and pricing

A one-off benefit during the quarter was largely offset by higher variable pay. Management said pricing remains accretive and has increased due to factors including AI and Project Maximus. New-generation pricing, including pricing for AI projects, is part of Project Maximus, and management does not currently see AI projects creating a pricing headwind.

### North America

Management had previously expected third-party costs and third-party revenue to be lower than the prior year, based on the deal pipeline and projects under execution. The impact is across segments, although its effect may vary by segment.

### Enterprise budgets and AI activity

Management reported more discretionary work in Financial services, increased AI activity, and traction in six AI value-pool areas identified for faster growth. Partnerships with companies are also contributing to momentum. AI activity was described as higher than at the same time in the previous year.

### Healthcare

Healthcare generated approximately $44 million of incremental revenue during the quarter. The question of whether the NHS deal contributed to this quarter’s revenue was raised, but the supplied section does not include management’s response.
