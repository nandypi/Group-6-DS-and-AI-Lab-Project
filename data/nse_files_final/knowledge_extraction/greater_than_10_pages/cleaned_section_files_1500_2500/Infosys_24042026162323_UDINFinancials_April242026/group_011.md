## Information Other than the Financial Statements and Auditor's Report Thereon

The Company's Board of Directors is responsible for preparing the other information, which includes:

- Management Discussion and Analysis
- The Board's Report and its annexures
- Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report
- Corporate Governance information
- Shareholder information

The other information excludes the Consolidated Financial Statements, Standalone Financial Statements, and the auditor's reports thereon.

The auditor's opinion on the Consolidated Financial Statements does not cover the other information, and no assurance conclusion is expressed on it. As part of the audit, the auditor read the other information and considered whether it was materially inconsistent with the Consolidated Financial Statements, information obtained during the audit, or otherwise materially misstated.

The auditor reported that there was nothing to report regarding material misstatement of the other information.

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Consolidated Financial Statements

The respective Boards of Directors or Trustees of the entities included in the Group are responsible for:

- Preparing the Consolidated Financial Statements in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in India, including Ind AS specified under section 133 of the Act.
- Safeguarding the Group's assets.
- Preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities.
- Maintaining adequate internal financial controls relevant to the preparation and presentation of financial statements.
- Ensuring that the accounting records are accurate and complete and that the financial statements give a true and fair view and are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error.
- Assessing the ability of the respective entities to continue as a going concern and using the going-concern basis of accounting unless the respective Boards intend to liquidate the entities, cease operations, or have no realistic alternative.
- Overseeing the Group's financial reporting process.

## Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit of the Consolidated Financial Statements

The auditor's objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the Consolidated Financial Statements are free from material misstatement and to issue an auditor's report containing the auditor's opinion. Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but is not a guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with Standards on Auditing will always detect a material misstatement when it exists.

The auditor exercised professional judgment and maintained professional scepticism throughout the audit. The audit included:

- Identifying and assessing risks of material misstatement due to fraud or error, designing and performing responsive audit procedures, and obtaining audit evidence sufficient to support the auditor's opinion. The risk of not detecting a material misstatement resulting from fraud is higher than for one resulting from error.
- Obtaining an understanding of internal financial controls relevant to the audit and designing appropriate audit procedures. Under section 143(3)(i) of the Act, the auditor also reported on whether companies incorporated in India had adequate internal financial controls with reference to the Consolidated Financial Statements and whether those controls operated effectively.
- Evaluating the appropriateness of accounting policies, the reasonableness of accounting estimates, and related management disclosures.
- Evaluating management's use of the going-concern basis of accounting and whether a material uncertainty existed relating to events or conditions that could cast significant doubt on the Group's ability to continue as a going concern.
- Evaluating the related disclosures in the Consolidated Financial Statements and modifying the auditor's opinion if the disclosures were inadequate.
- Obtaining sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the financial information of entities within the Group to express an opinion on the Consolidated Financial Statements.
- Considering quantitative materiality and qualitative factors when planning the audit, evaluating audit results, and assessing the effect of identified misstatements on the Consolidated Financial Statements.

The auditor communicated with those charged with governance, including identified deficiencies in internal financial controls. The auditor also provided statements regarding compliance with relevant ethical requirements and independence safeguards.

From the matters communicated with those charged with governance, the auditor determined the matters of most significance in the audit to be key audit matters. Such matters are described in the auditor's report unless law or regulation prohibits communication or the adverse consequences of communication would reasonably be expected to outweigh the public interest benefits.

## Report on Other Legal and Regulatory Requirements

As required by Section 143(3) of the Act, based on the audit, the auditor reported that:

- All information and explanations necessary for the audit of the Consolidated Financial Statements were sought and obtained.
- Proper books of account and relevant records required by law for preparing the Consolidated Financial Statements were maintained by the Group.
- The Consolidated Balance Sheet, Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss including Other Comprehensive Income, Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity, and Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows agreed with the relevant books of account maintained for preparing the Consolidated Financial Statements.
- The Consolidated Financial Statements complied with the Ind AS specified under section 133 of the Act.
- Based on written representations received from the directors as of March 31, 2026, and reports from the statutory auditors of Indian subsidiary companies, none of the directors of Group companies incorporated in India was disqualified as of March 31, 2026, under section 164(2) of the Act.
- The auditor's separate report in Annexure A expressed an unmodified opinion on the adequacy and operating effectiveness of internal financial controls with reference to the Consolidated Financial Statements for the Company and its subsidiary companies incorporated in India.
- The remuneration paid by the Company to its directors during the year complied with section 197 of the Act.
- The Consolidated Financial Statements disclosed the impact of pending litigation on the Group's consolidated financial position. Refer to Note 2.24 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.
- The Group made provisions required under applicable law or accounting standards for material foreseeable losses. Refer to Note 2.16 to the Consolidated Financial Statements. The Group did not have any long-term derivative contracts.
- There was no delay in transferring amounts required to be transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund by the Company and its subsidiary companies incorporated in India.
- Management of the Company and its Indian subsidiaries represented that, to the best of their knowledge and belief, no funds had been advanced, loaned, or invested—whether from borrowed funds, share premium, or any other source—outside the Group through any person or entity, including a foreign entity, with the understanding that the intermediary would:
  - Directly or indirectly lend or invest in persons or entities identified by or on behalf of the Company or its subsidiaries; or
  - Provide any guarantee, security, or similar support on behalf of such ultimate beneficiaries.
