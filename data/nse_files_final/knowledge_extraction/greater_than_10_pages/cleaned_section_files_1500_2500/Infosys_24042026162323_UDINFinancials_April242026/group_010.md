## Chartered Accountants

## Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP

### Revenue recognition

Revenue recognition for the Group’s software products, platforms, digital offerings, and business process management services involves significant judgment in identifying distinct performance obligations and determining whether the Group acts as principal or agent.

The Group assesses the services promised in each contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. In certain integrated service arrangements, contracts include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as principal.

The Group evaluates whether it obtains control of specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include:

- Responsibility for fulfilling the promise to provide the specified goods or services.
- Inventory risk.
- Pricing discretion.
- Other factors relevant to determining control.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using either:

1. A straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
2. The percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and the Group’s costs to fulfil the contract are not even over the contract period because the services are generally discrete.

These judgments were identified as a key audit matter and required a higher extent of audit effort. The matter is referenced in Notes 1.5 and 2.18 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.

#### Auditor’s response

The auditors tested the effectiveness of controls relating to:

- Identification of distinct performance obligations.
- Determination of whether the Group acts as principal or agent.
- Determination of whether fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method.

For a sample of customer contracts, the auditors:

- Obtained and read contract documents, including master service agreements and other documents forming part of the agreements.
- Identified significant terms and deliverables.
- Assessed management’s conclusions regarding distinct performance obligations, principal-versus-agent status, and the revenue recognition method for fixed-price maintenance contracts.

### Revenue recognition - Fixed price contracts using the percentage of completion method

The estimate of total efforts or costs to complete fixed-price contracts measured using the percentage-of-completion method involves significant judgment. These estimates are assessed throughout the contract period using the latest available information.

Provisions for estimated losses on uncompleted contracts are recorded in the period in which the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

The estimate has high inherent uncertainty and requires consideration of:

- Contract progress.
- Efforts or costs incurred to date.
- Estimated efforts or costs required to complete the remaining contract performance obligations.

This matter required a high degree of auditor judgment and a higher extent of audit effort in evaluating the reasonableness of the total estimated revenue recognized on fixed-price contracts. It is referenced in Notes 1.5 and 2.18 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.

#### Auditor’s response

The auditors tested the estimates for consistency with the status of delivery milestones, customer acceptances, and customer sign-offs. They also assessed possible delays in achieving milestones that could require changes to estimated costs or efforts needed to complete the remaining performance obligations.
