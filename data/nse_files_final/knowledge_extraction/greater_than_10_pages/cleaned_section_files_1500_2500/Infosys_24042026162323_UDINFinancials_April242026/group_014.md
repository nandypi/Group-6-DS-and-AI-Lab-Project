## Deloitte Haskins &amp; Sells LLP

### Report on theAudit of theInterim Consolidated FinancialStatements

### Opinion

The auditors audited the interim consolidated financial statements of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries (collectively, the “Group”), comprising:

- The Consolidated Balance Sheet as at March 31, 2026.
- The Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income for the three months and year ended March 31, 2026.
- The Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity for the year ended March 31, 2026.
- The Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows for the year ended March 31, 2026.
- The related notes, including material accounting policies and other explanatory information.

In the auditors’ opinion, the interim consolidated financial statements give a true and fair view, in conformity with International Accounting Standard 34, “Interim Financial Reporting” (IAS 34), as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board, of:

- The consolidated state of affairs of the Group as at March 31, 2026.
- The consolidated profit and consolidated other comprehensive income for the three months and year ended March 31, 2026.
- The consolidated changes in equity and consolidated cash flows for the year ended March 31, 2026.

### Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the applicable Standards on Auditing. The auditors stated that they fulfilled their ethical responsibilities in accordance with the ICAI Code of Ethics and believed that the audit evidence obtained was sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their opinion.

### Key Audit Matters

Key audit matters were matters that, in the auditors’ professional judgment, were of most significance in the audit of the consolidated financial statements for the current period. These matters were addressed in the context of the audit as a whole, and the auditors did not provide a separate opinion on them.

#### 1. Revenue recognition

The Group’s contracts with customers include multiple products and services. Revenue is derived from:

- IT services, including software development and related services.
- Maintenance.
- Consulting and package implementation.
- Licensing of software products and platforms.
- Core and digital offerings.
- Business process management services.

The Group assesses the services promised in a contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. This requires significant judgment in determining the deliverables and whether the customer can benefit independently from them.

For certain integrated service arrangements, contracts include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded:

- Net of costs when the Group acts as an agent between the customer and the vendor.
- Gross when the Group acts as the principal.

The Group evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other indicators of control.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably:

- On a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
- Using the percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and the Group’s costs to fulfil the contract are not even throughout the contract period because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.

The method used to recognize maintenance revenue requires judgment based on the promises in the contract and the nature of the deliverables. Revenue recognition involving these judgments was identified as a key audit matter and required a higher extent of audit effort. Refer to Notes 1.5 and 2.16 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.

**Auditor’s procedures included:**

- Testing the effectiveness of controls relating to:
  - Identification of distinct performance obligations.
  - Determination of whether the Group acts as principal or agent.
  - Determination of whether fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Selecting a sample of customer contracts and:
  - Reading contract documents, including master service agreements and other documents forming part of the agreement.
  - Identifying significant terms and deliverables.
  - Assessing management’s conclusions regarding distinct performance obligations, principal-versus-agent classification, and the revenue-recognition method for fixed-price maintenance.

#### 2. Revenue recognition for fixed-price contracts using the percentage-of-completion method

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized using either a straight-line basis or the percentage-of-completion method, depending on the pattern of benefits and the nature of the services.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-time-frame contracts for which performance obligations are satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.

This method requires the Group to determine actual efforts or costs expended to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs required to complete the contract. Efforts or costs expended are used to measure progress toward completion because there is a direct relationship between input and productivity.

Estimating total efforts or costs requires significant judgment. Estimates are assessed throughout the contract period to reflect changes based on the latest available information. Provisions for estimated losses on uncompleted contracts are recorded in the period in which the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

**Auditor’s procedures included:**

- Testing controls relating to:
  - Recording efforts or costs incurred.
  - Estimating efforts or costs required to complete the remaining contract performance obligations.
  - Access and application controls over time-recording, allocation, and budgeting systems that prevent unauthorized changes to records of efforts incurred.
- Selecting a sample of fixed-price contracts measured using the percentage-of-completion method and:
  - Evaluating management’s ability to reasonably estimate progress toward satisfying performance obligations by comparing actual efforts or costs incurred with prior-year estimates budgeted for fulfilled performance obligations.
  - Comparing efforts or costs incurred with the Group’s estimates of efforts or costs incurred to date to identify significant variations and assess whether they were appropriately considered in estimating remaining costs or efforts.
  - Testing the consistency of estimates with delivery milestones, customer acceptances, and customer sign-offs to identify possible delays requiring changes in estimated costs or efforts to complete the remaining performance obligations.
