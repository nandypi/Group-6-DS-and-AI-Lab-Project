## Basis for Opinion

The audit of the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI). The auditors state that they are independent of the Group under the ICAI Code of Ethics and that they obtained sufficient and appropriate audit evidence to support their audit opinion.

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements

The Company’s Board of Directors is responsible for preparing and presenting Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements that give a true and fair view of the Group’s:

- Consolidated financial position
- Consolidated financial performance
- Consolidated other comprehensive income
- Consolidated changes in equity
- Consolidated cash flows

The financial statements are prepared in accordance with IAS 34 as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB).

The respective boards of directors of the entities included in the Group are responsible for:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records
- Safeguarding the Group’s assets
- Preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls
- Ensuring the accuracy and completeness of accounting records used to prepare the interim financial statements

The boards are also responsible for assessing each entity’s ability to continue as a going concern, making related disclosures where applicable, and using the going-concern basis of accounting unless they intend to liquidate the entity, cease operations, or have no realistic alternative but to do so.

The respective boards are responsible for overseeing the Group’s financial reporting process.

## Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit of the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements

The auditor’s objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance that the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor’s report containing the auditor’s opinion.

Misstatements are considered material if, individually or in aggregate, they could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of users based on the financial statements.

As part of the audit, the auditors exercise professional judgment and maintain professional scepticism. Their responsibilities include:

- Identifying and assessing the risks of material misstatement due to fraud or error, designing responsive audit procedures, and obtaining sufficient and appropriate audit evidence.
- Considering that the risk of failing to detect a material misstatement due to fraud is higher than for error because fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or management override of internal controls.
- Obtaining an understanding of internal financial controls relevant to the audit to design appropriate audit procedures, without expressing an opinion on the effectiveness of those controls.
- Evaluating the appropriateness of accounting policies and the reasonableness of accounting estimates and related disclosures made by management.
- Assessing the appropriateness of management’s use of the going-concern basis of accounting.
- Determining, based on audit evidence, whether a material uncertainty exists regarding events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the Group’s ability to continue as a going concern.
- Drawing attention in the auditor’s report to related disclosures when a material uncertainty exists, or modifying the audit opinion if those disclosures are inadequate.
- Recognizing that future events or conditions may cause the Group to cease to continue as a going concern.
- Evaluating whether the overall presentation, structure, and content of the financial statements represent the underlying transactions and events fairly.
- Obtaining sufficient and appropriate audit evidence regarding the financial information of entities within the Group to express an opinion on the consolidated financial statements.
- Directing, supervising, and performing the audit of the financial statements of entities included in the consolidated financial statements for which the auditors are independent auditors.

Audit materiality is the magnitude of misstatements that, individually or in aggregate, makes it probable that the economic decisions of a reasonably knowledgeable user of the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements may be influenced. The auditors evaluate materiality when planning and performing the audit and assessing the effect of identified misstatements.

The auditors communicate with those charged with governance of the Company and other entities included in the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements regarding:

- The planned scope and timing of the audit
- Significant audit findings
- Significant deficiencies in internal financial controls identified during the audit
- Compliance with relevant ethical requirements regarding independence
- Relationships and other matters that may reasonably be thought to bear on auditor independence, including related safeguards where applicable
