## Deloitte Haskins &amp; Sells LLP

### Key Audit Matter: Revenue Recognition

Revenue recognition from contracts with customers involves management judgment in:

1. Identifying distinct performance obligations.
2. Determining whether the Company is acting as a principal or an agent.
3. Determining whether fixed-price maintenance revenue should be recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method.

These judgments were identified as a key audit matter and required a higher extent of audit effort. Refer Notes 1.4 and 2.18 to the Standalone Financial Statements.

The Company provides software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings, as well as business process management services. It assesses the services promised in a contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identification of distinct performance obligations involves significant judgment regarding the deliverables and the customer's ability to benefit independently from those deliverables.

In certain integrated service arrangements, customer contracts include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded:

- Net of costs when the Company acts as an agent between the customer and the vendor.
- Gross when the Company is the principal in the transaction.

The Company evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. It considers whether it is primarily responsible for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably:

- On a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
- Using the percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits from the services and the Company's costs to fulfill the contract are not even over the contract period because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.

Other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts whose performance obligations are satisfied over time are recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.

The percentage-of-completion method requires the Company to determine actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs to be incurred. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress toward completion because there is a direct relationship between input and productivity.

The estimation of total efforts or costs involves significant judgment and is assessed throughout the contract period using the latest available information. Probable losses on uncompleted contracts are recorded in the period in which those losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs required to complete the contract.

The estimate of total efforts or costs to complete fixed-price contracts has high inherent uncertainty. It requires consideration of contract progress, efforts or costs incurred to date, and estimates of efforts or costs required to complete the remaining performance obligations. This required a high degree of auditor judgment and a higher extent of audit effort in evaluating the reasonableness of revenue recognized on fixed-price contracts.

### Auditor's Response

The auditors:

- Tested the effectiveness of controls relating to:
  - Identification of distinct performance obligations.
  - Determination of whether the Company acts as principal or agent.
  - Determination of whether fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method.
  - Recording of efforts or costs incurred and estimation of efforts or costs required to complete remaining contract performance obligations.
  - Access and application controls over time-recording, allocation, and budgeting systems that prevent unauthorized changes to recorded efforts.
- Selected samples of customer contracts, including master service agreements and other documents forming part of the agreements.
- Identified significant contract terms and deliverables to assess management's conclusions regarding performance obligations, principal-versus-agent status, and the revenue recognition method.
- Selected samples of fixed-price contracts measured using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Evaluated management's ability to estimate progress toward satisfying performance obligations by comparing actual efforts or costs incurred with prior-year estimates and budgeted efforts or costs for fulfilled performance obligations.
- Compared efforts or costs incurred with the Company's estimates to identify significant variations and assessed whether those variations were appropriately considered in estimating remaining costs or efforts.
- Tested the estimates for consistency with delivery milestones, customer acceptances, and customer sign-offs to identify possible delays requiring changes to estimated costs or efforts to complete remaining performance obligations.

## Information Other than the  Financial  Statements and Auditor's  Report Thereon

The Company's Board of Directors is responsible for the other information, comprising:

- Management Discussion and Analysis.
- Board's Report, including its annexures.
- Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report.
- Corporate Governance.
- Shareholder's Information.

The other information does not include the Consolidated Financial Statements, Standalone Financial Statements, or the auditor's report thereon.

The auditor's opinion on the Standalone Financial Statements does not cover the other information, and the auditor does not express any form of assurance conclusion on it.

In connection with the audit of the Standalone Financial Statements, the auditor's responsibility is to read the other information and consider whether it is materially inconsistent with the Standalone Financial Statements, knowledge obtained during the audit, or otherwise materially misstated.

The auditor reported that there was nothing to report regarding a material misstatement in the other information.

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Standalone  Financial Statements

The Company's Board of Directors is responsible under section 134(5) of the Act for preparing Standalone Financial Statements that give a true and fair view of the Company's financial position, financial performance, including other comprehensive income, changes in equity, and cash flows, in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles in India, including Ind AS specified under section 133 of the Act.

This responsibility includes:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records under the Act.
- Safeguarding the Company's assets.
- Preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls that operate effectively to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting records relevant to preparing and presenting Standalone Financial Statements free from material misstatement due to fraud or error.

In preparing the Standalone Financial Statements, management and the Board of Directors are responsible for assessing the Company's ability to continue as a going concern, making applicable going-concern disclosures, and using the going-concern basis of accounting unless the Board intends to liquidate the Company or cease operations, or has no realistic alternative but to do so.

The Board of Directors is also responsible for overseeing the Company's financial reporting process.
