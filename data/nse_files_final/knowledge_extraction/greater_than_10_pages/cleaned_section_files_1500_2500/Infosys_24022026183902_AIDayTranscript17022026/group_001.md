## Nandan Nilekani

### The AI transition

Technology transitions have occurred for centuries, including the printing press and telegraph. Over the last 60–70 years, the pace of change has accelerated through personal computers, cloud computing, generative AI, and agentic AI.

The progression has included mainframes, minicomputers, PCs, client-server systems, local area networks, web computing, mobile technology, enterprise applications, and big data. Each transition created different implications for technology companies and required different approaches to making technology globally available and integrating it with enterprise IT.

The current AI transition is occurring faster than earlier transitions. The internet took more than 10 years to reach 1 billion users, smartphones took 5 years, and AI is taking a couple of years. The existing ubiquity of the internet and smartphones helped distribute applications such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude quickly.

AI represents a more fundamental change in how businesses operate than previous transitions. Smartphones enabled applications to be used through a different front end, and cloud computing enabled applications to be moved from on-premises infrastructure to the cloud. AI requires changes to business processes, customer journeys, talent, operating models, and technology architecture.

AI-native architecture is one dimension of the transition, but businesses must also change how they operate. Talent will need to work on making AI function effectively, including orchestration, rather than focusing only on writing code. Enterprises also need operating models that can implement AI at scale across large workforces.

Technology has traditionally been deterministic: the same input produces the same output. AI systems are non-deterministic, with different outputs possible for the same prompt. Enterprises therefore need to achieve the robustness, reliability, and resilience associated with deterministic systems while using non-deterministic technology.

### Legacy modernization and technology debt

Modernization of legacy systems can no longer be deferred. Large enterprises may have mainframes from the 1960s, minicomputers from the 1980s, local area networks from the 2000s, and other systems operating together in silos. To take advantage of AI, enterprises need to address this accumulated technology debt.

Many large companies spend 60% to 80% of their IT budgets maintaining existing systems, leaving limited spending for new systems. They want to move from approximately 60%–70% maintenance and 30% new systems to approximately 30%–40% maintenance and 60%–70% new systems.

Legacy systems also create security risks because many were designed before online attacks became prevalent. Security breaches are increasing, with state and non-state actors using AI to improve their capabilities.

Data held in silos also limits the speed of innovation. Enterprises face strong demand for modernization, while AI is providing tools that may enable modernization to be performed more quickly and economically.

### Build-versus-buy and agentic interfaces

As AI becomes a larger part of technology spending, the balance of advantage may shift toward building applications rather than buying them. Building applications has become simpler, and enterprises may build new applications or replace purchased applications with internally built alternatives.

Foundational systems are expected to remain systems of record, while user interfaces increasingly become agentic. Agentic interfaces can hide underlying complexity and provide a simpler, user-oriented experience.

Enterprises may add agentic layers to existing applications without changing the underlying systems of record. These layers may combine purchased agents with internally built agents. Agents need to be composable within seamless customer journeys, requiring orchestration across internally developed and externally sourced agents.

### Pace of AI technology change

AI technology is changing rapidly. In 2023, foundation or frontier models had approximately 100 billion parameters; by 2026, models had reached approximately 1 trillion parameters. The number of agent networks increased from approximately 10–12 to approximately 60.

The United States has at least five frontier models, while China has approximately four or five major frontier models. India has also seen significant activity, including announcements concerning India-based sovereign models.

The pace of change creates a technology-selection challenge for businesses. Technology adopted today may fall behind newer technology tomorrow. Enterprises therefore need architectures that can adapt to rapid technology changes and account for the advantages and disadvantages of different approaches. Infosys has experience across 2,000 locations and different technology approaches.

### Enterprise deployment gap

AI technology is advancing faster than enterprises can deploy it. Model performance is increasing, but implementation progress is slower because enterprise deployment requires organizational change, business-process change, employee retraining, new approaches to non-deterministic systems, and changes to data structures that are currently siloed.

There is therefore a deployment gap between the capability of AI technology and the capacity of businesses to use it. The principal challenge is the speed at which companies can implement AI, rather than simply the availability of more capable products.
