So my sense is if you look at our current business situation, next year, we definitely see in Financial Services a strong growth, and we see in Energy and Utilities a strong growth compared to this year. We are already seeing signs of visibility. This is a function of AI leverage, macroeconomic conditions, and compression, which are somewhat intertwined.

## Jonathan Lee

Jonathan Lee from Guggenheim asked about the investment required to reskill and hire laterally for the AI opportunity, and the potential margin impact excluding currency benefits.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys intends to invest in building out its AI capabilities, including AI Topaz Fabric, platforms, tools, go-to-market activities, and training.

The company has also maintained a margin and cost-efficiency program. Its current view is to maintain margin guidance and invest the substantial savings from the margin program into scaling AI more rapidly.

Infosys also has the balance-sheet capacity to make appropriate acquisitions that fit its overall value framework, consistent with its past approach.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

During the first nine months of the year, Infosys maintained stable margins. This followed a 50-basis-point margin expansion in FY 2025. The company absorbed investments in partnerships, technology, training, sales, and marketing, including a 50-basis-point impact already reflected in the profit and loss statement.

## Pankaj Murarka

Pankaj Murarka from Renaissance asked about the 5.5% AI revenue contribution, the size of current AI opportunities and deals, and the timeframe for larger deals and accelerated adoption.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys reported that 5.5% of revenue comes from AI-related activity. AI is being incorporated across many areas of the business and is becoming part of almost every client discussion.

The company expects AI activity to continue growing and sees a long runway for adoption over the next several years. Infosys compared this with the digital cycle, during which digital revenue increased from approximately 20%–25% to 65%–70% over three to five years. The company has not determined whether AI adoption will reach a similar scale in 18 months or seven years, but stated that it is prepared for either pace.

## Pankaj Murarka

Murarka asked how the proposed approximately $300 billion of new opportunities over the next five to six years could affect Infosys' financial metrics over a five-year period.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

In a technology cycle, being ahead of the curve should lead to better growth, better revenue per employee (RPP), and better margins.

Infosys reported that RPP grew by 3% in both FY 2025 and FY 2026. Margins expanded by 50 basis points in the previous year and remained stable in the current year despite the investments discussed.

## Aditya Chandrasekar

Aditya Chandrasekar from UBS asked how pricing models are evolving as AI and Topaz move from pilot projects to enterprise-wide use cases. He also asked how to assess margins when projects require smaller teams supported by platforms, and how Infosys will utilize its existing fresher and entry-level workforce as projects become more specialized.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys' pricing models are evolving. Examples include outcome-based pricing and combinations of outcome-based pricing with agent-based or platform-based pricing.

There will not be one pricing model for every client. The model will depend on the client's context and how effectively Infosys can justify the value it creates.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys expects specialized talent to become more important while continuing to recruit college hires and freshers. These employees will be trained to work both with and without AI tools, understand when tools should be used, and assess code generated by models.

Utilization will remain an important metric, although its application may differ according to the specialization, scale, and size of the workforce.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

During the digital cycle, Infosys retrained its employee base as digital revenue increased from approximately 25% to more than two-thirds of total revenue. The company stated that it has a strong training culture and uses it to retrain and repurpose employees.

## Gaurav Rateria

Gaurav Rateria from Morgan Stanley asked whether the boundaries between software and services are becoming blurred as a result of build-versus-buy decisions, and what this could mean for the addressable market for service providers.

He also asked whether evolving pricing models could create greater non-linearity and provide additional capacity for investment to accelerate Infosys' AI journey.
