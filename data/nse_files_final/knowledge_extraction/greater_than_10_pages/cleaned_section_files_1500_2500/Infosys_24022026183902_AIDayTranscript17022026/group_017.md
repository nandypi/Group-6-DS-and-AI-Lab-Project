## Salil Parekh

AI could significantly expand the amount of work Infosys can undertake, particularly for activities at the edge of core systems of record. Some work may be more effectively built than bought, and different clients may require different solutions.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

If a larger proportion of revenue becomes enabled through platforms or agents, pricing could become somewhat nonlinear. These models are still at an early stage, and companies are testing different approaches.

## Surendra Goyal

Surendra Goyal of Citi asked whether Infosys could capture more of the value generated through client savings and productivity improvements, including whether AI would change the way that value is captured.

## Salil Parekh

Some client examples involve outcome-based arrangements. If Infosys can implement value-sharing models, it may capture more of the benefits.

Currently, enterprise expectations for model-driven benefits are developing faster than the operational reality within enterprises. Clients may expect those benefits to be reflected in pricing or cost, while Infosys is not yet able to fully realize them. Value capture may become more equitable as enterprise adoption becomes more aligned with the capabilities of the models.

Outcome-based arrangements could provide additional value capture, but this is not yet visible and is not an expectation for the coming year. Infosys is monitoring how pricing approaches develop.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Value capture can also occur indirectly. Infosys stated that it is the AI strategic partner for 15 of its top 25 clients across multiple sectors. This position is associated with having created significant value for clients. Infosys also described itself as a large player across a substantial part of the telecommunications sector, which it attributed to creating client value and becoming a strategic partner.

## Kunal Tayal

Kunal Tayal of Bank of America asked whether Infosys sees a larger opportunity among mid-market customers or customers in emerging markets, given its investments in small language models and proprietary solutions. He also asked whether less labour-intensive delivery models could improve project profitability in emerging markets.

## Salil Parekh

Small language models indicate the depth of Infosys’ capabilities and are useful today for limited data sets within large clients. Infosys is seeing benefits in areas such as alignment and code development, including through its own model for code development.

Infosys has not yet focused on the mid-market because the cost of sales is materially different and the economics need to be evaluated.

Infosys is considering growth markets, including the Middle East, but does not characterize them as emerging markets. Adoption within each geography will influence the applicability of these models, including their use with mid-market clients.

## Sandeep Shah

Sandeep Shah noted that after enterprise clients complete AI adoption, application modernization, data-layer creation, and cloud migration, investors may be concerned that terminal growth rates could decline because applications and data will have been modernized.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys does not view the post-AI environment as a static period in which all software work is complete. Even assuming a 10x productivity benefit for developers, the potential demand for software development could remain substantial. An example discussed at the event suggested that the amount of software demand could become 100 times larger in terms of the size of the opportunity.

In the post-AI environment, large enterprises may use agents across many areas, operate strong platforms such as Topaz Fabric, build new functions and features, and manage existing systems. New work is expected to continue as technology creates additional features, functions, and areas of enterprise activity.

## Kawaljeet Saluja

Kawaljeet Saluja of Kotak asked whether the AI agents deployed in client environments are developed internally by Infosys or provided by frontier model companies.

## Salil Parekh

AI agents are being developed by multiple groups:

- Infosys is building its own agents.
- Foundation-model companies are building broader agents.
- Third-party companies are developing agents.
- Public-cloud providers are developing and offering their own agents.
- Clients are also building agents.

Clients will select from this range of agents based on their requirements.

## Kawaljeet Saluja

Kawaljeet Saluja asked about the services revenue associated with customizing and configuring a third-party agent for a client environment compared with the amount captured by a frontier-model company or external agent provider.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys does not have detailed statistics that it can share on the revenue ratio between third-party agent costs and services revenue.

Beyond the cost of building the agent, significant work may be required to integrate it into the client environment, ensure that it works effectively, and address performance and security attributes. Infosys has examples of this work but does not have a publicly shareable statistic expressing the relationship as a fixed ratio. It expects to build a larger internal data set over the next few quarters and may later assess whether the information can be disclosed publicly.

## Shaan Chugh

Shaan Chugh of Capital Group asked whether Infosys expects net headcount to increase as AI services grow, considering fresher hiring, internal efficiencies, and natural attrition.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys added approximately 13,000 net employees during the first three quarters of the year. Infosys expects to continue adding headcount in the coming quarters.

The expected headcount trajectory reflects both macroeconomic conditions and the AI opportunity. Infosys expects the macro environment may improve and has a strong view of the AI opportunity set. Based on the approximately 13,000 net headcount increase during the first three quarters, Infosys expects headcount to continue increasing.
