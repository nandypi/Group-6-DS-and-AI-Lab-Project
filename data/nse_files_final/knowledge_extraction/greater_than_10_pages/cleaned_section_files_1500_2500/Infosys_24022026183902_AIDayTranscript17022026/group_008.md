## Anand Swaminathan

AI is no longer limited to experimentation for many telecommunications, media, and technology companies. These companies are considering AI as a core operating model for customer experience, engineering, network resilience, and operational efficiency.

The communications, media, and technology business spans semiconductor companies, OEM platforms, hyperscalers, and media and entertainment companies. Infosys identified several AI demand drivers:

- Telecom companies face limited growth in mobile consumer markets and weak B2B growth. They are using AI to reimagine B2B and B2C customer journeys and rethink go-to-market models.
- Infosys has a joint venture with Australian company Telstra. Infosys owns the majority stake, and the companies are jointly responsible for taking B2B non-connectivity solutions to the Australian market.
- Sovereign and sovereign cloud capabilities are an opportunity for telecom companies. Outside the United States, countries are looking to telecom companies to provide AI infrastructure, data residency, cyber resilience, and in-country AI operations.
- Telecom companies are seeking step-change productivity improvements and lower unit costs because traditional productivity factors are insufficient.
- Technology companies are making substantial investments in AI farms, but much of the spending remains focused on model building and model training within the technology sector. Infosys sees an opportunity to apply its industry domain knowledge to implement technology-company products and services across other industries.
- The race among technology companies to gain AI market share is creating opportunities in engineering spending and in new sales and partner channels.

Trust and scale are important to participating in the AI opportunity. The communications, media, and technology segment is concentrated, with a small number of large companies and a long tail of smaller companies. More than 60% of Infosys’s revenue in this segment comes from its top 15 clients.

Infosys described three broad areas where AI is being deployed and value is being created:

- **Customer experience:** Companies are rethinking B2B and B2C processes in an agentic AI environment to improve customer satisfaction and retention. In many cases, building platforms using an agentic AI framework is becoming preferable to purchasing new platforms, particularly where legacy technology is at end of life or end of support. Infosys reported an incremental improvement of approximately 30% in Net Promoter Score in many cases.
- **Network resilience and engineering reliability:** Infosys Smart Network Assurance, part of the Topaz suite of products, is being used with telecom companies globally to improve network resilience and significantly reduce outages.
- **Operations:** Infosys is applying Agentic AI through a human-plus-agent model to improve unit economics and operational efficiency for customers.

### Liberty Global

Liberty Global is a European broadband and mobile communications company operating across multiple European countries, with approximately 10 million subscribers using its entertainment and connectivity platform.

Infosys owns and operates Liberty Global’s entire hardware, software, and services stack. The engagement is priced on a per-subscriber basis, so Infosys’s fee changes with the subscriber count.

Using an Agentic AI framework, Infosys has sought to unlock value in Liberty Global’s software stack and reduce costs across the platform. The framework is also being used to improve customer experience. For example, subscribers can use natural language to speak to the television and request shows instead of searching with a traditional remote control or other device. Infosys also reported improvements in network resilience and employee experience.

## Jasmeet Singh

Leading manufacturers are embedding AI into products and workflows and using agentic execution to unlock value. Infosys’s recently published Manufacturing tech index reported that 75% of manufacturers embed AI into their enterprise strategy.

Infosys identified three major manufacturing opportunities:

1. Connected products, smarter operations, and as-a-service models.
2. AI-led modernization driven by rigid technology stacks.
3. Applying AI to the substantial data held in smart products and manufacturing operations.

AI is being applied across the manufacturing value chain, from design to service, as well as in horizontal functions such as finance, HR, and legal. In manufacturing operations, Infosys is using computer vision and AI for product-quality assessment at a leading industrial manufacturer. This resulted in a reported 10% increase in throughput.

### Rolls-Royce

Rolls-Royce manufactures and sells aircraft engines on a power-by-the-hour basis. Engines return to Rolls-Royce maintenance, repair, and overhaul facilities for maintenance and overhaul, and aircraft-on-ground situations can create revenue pressure for airlines and Rolls-Royce.

Infosys developed a multi-agent solution for reviewing and authoring repair procedures for each engine. Each engine has unique operating parameters. The solution reportedly delivered:

- 40% reduction in engineering effort.
- Increase in first-time-right rates from under 40% to 75%.
- A multimillion-pound revenue uplift for Rolls-Royce.

The process is manual, highly regulated, and safety-critical. The Agentic AI solution has been approved by EASA, the European Union Aviation Safety Agency, allowing it to be scaled across Rolls-Royce.

### GE Vernova

GE Vernova is described as a company with $40 billion in revenue and as a provider in power, wind, and electrification. Its stated aim is to electrify the world while decarbonizing it.

GE Vernova selected Infosys as its AI strategy partner, covering strategy through execution. Infosys reported that it had delivered more than 25 agents and multi-agent use cases for GE Vernova in production.
