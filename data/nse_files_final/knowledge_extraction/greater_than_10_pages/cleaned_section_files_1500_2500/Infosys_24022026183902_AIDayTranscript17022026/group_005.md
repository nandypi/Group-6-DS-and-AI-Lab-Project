# Strategic Pillars and AI Transformation Use Cases

## Enterprise Data and Business-Process Transformation

Infosys describes a framework to help customers harness and transform data through a uniform data fabric. The data fabric is intended to support intelligence by connecting semantics and ontology across processes that differ by region and business unit and are codified in systems of record and systems of experience.

Data governance is described as a further layer, including data fingerprinting and controls over data access. For one large industrial manufacturer, Infosys brought together 10 petabytes of data and created a semantic ontology to support supply-chain optimization of over 20% to 30%.

Infosys also describes reimagining business processes in large enterprises that rely on point-to-point solutions. Workflows are to be redesigned by domain and persona, combining human intuition with AI and agents. The workflows must be contextualized to the relevant industry, business, and region, with operationalization covering the workflow, technology, and change management.

Infosys has worked with Toyota Motors Europe on a supply-chain transformation process using its automobile industry asset and agentic playbook. The work examined personas including buyers, planners, and customer-service agents. In the drop-ship process, agent orchestration was intended to reduce manual work and improve inventory visibility.

## Modernization

Infosys identifies legacy modernization as a major challenge for large organizations because of technology debt, obsolete code, limited documentation, and a lack of subject-matter experts. Earlier modernization efforts often did not provide sufficient return on investment relative to the time required.

Infosys states that AI models powered by Topaz Fabric enable the transition of legacy data and processes to modern, cloud-based microservices architectures.

## Physical AI

Infosys describes physical AI as the movement of intelligence from digital workflows into physical objects. The applications discussed include:

- New-product introduction, with products designed or defined with AI integrated from the outset.
- Real-time intelligence moving from the cloud to the edge to support decision-making in vehicles, industrial operations, and infrastructure.
- Increasing use of autonomous systems across industries and operating areas.

Infosys identifies two acquisitions as relevant to this area:

- **InSemi:** Silicon design and validation capabilities.
- **in-tech:** Automobile-related capabilities.

## Trust and Governance

Infosys identifies trust and governance as important across the other strategic layers. The issues mentioned include AI hallucinations, model breaches, and governance requirements associated with new AI legislation such as the AI Act. Infosys describes trustworthy AI as an enterprise differentiator and states that it intends to build and monetize trust for customers.

## Customer Use Cases

### Nova Chemicals

Nova Chemicals is described as a large, asset-intensive petrochemical manufacturer operating in Canada and the United States. Infosys worked with the company on a smart-maintenance program.

The program brought together data from machinery, original-equipment-manufacturer manuals, maintenance manuals, historical data, and log data. A planner could use a natural-language-processing chatbot to identify which parts of industrial operations required maintenance.

Infosys also implemented agent-AI orchestration to connect operational-technology systems with information-technology systems. The resulting process supported work-order creation and planning, including movement from operational-technology systems to SAP or the company’s enterprise-resource-planning system. Infosys reported improvements in planning efficiency and asset utilization.

Microsoft was a partner, and the solution used the Azure technology stack.

### Hertz

Infosys describes work with Hertz, a mobility organization, to modernize its reservation, fleet-management, pricing, and related systems. The systems comprise approximately 3 million lines of code running on a Tandem computer.

The modernization effort uses the context of Hertz’s business processes, code, existing architecture, and target modular architecture to support migration. Infosys identifies this context as a critical component of the iLEAD approach to legacy modernization.
