## Summary of AI Day

### Salil Parekh

Infosys presented a comprehensive set of AI offerings organized around the Hexagon framework, including 30 offerings and 100 sub-offerings across six areas. The company also discussed augmented services and their impact.

Management described the AI opportunity as substantial. External analysts have quantified the potential expansion at $300 billion to $400 billion across the six areas. Infosys stated that it will provide further detail over the coming months and quarters on the value of these opportunities and how it intends to address them.

Large enterprise clients are working with Infosys on AI initiatives across the Hexagon. Management described these engagements as active implementations involving large delivery teams rather than theoretical opportunities.

Infosys discussed its Topaz Fabric capability, AI Next, internally developed AI agents, and the ability to integrate Infosys capabilities with client and third-party platforms.

The company highlighted its engineering talent, reskilling efforts, and innovation culture as capabilities relevant to AI services. Management stated that Infosys considers itself an innovation-oriented company rather than one focused solely on “break and fix” work.

Infosys also discussed its partner ecosystem, including a large partnership announced at the event. Management described the partnerships as joint activities combining Infosys's domain knowledge and talent with partner capabilities.

The company has established a go-to-market approach focused on meeting large clients to identify where the Hexagon offerings, 30 offerings, and 100 sub-offerings can be applied to create business impact.

## Questions and Answers

### AI opportunity and service compression

#### Ankur Rudra, J.P. Morgan

What is the incremental opportunity represented by the estimated $300 billion to $400 billion of new AI services, after accounting for compression in existing services caused by faster delivery and fewer required resources?

#### Salil Parekh

The $300 billion to $400 billion figure represents the expansion opportunity across six areas, based on external sources. Infosys has discussed compression in areas such as application development and infrastructure but has not quantified the compression figure for external use.

Management stated that the expansion opportunity currently appears larger than the compression opportunity.

### Competitive differentiation

#### Kumar Rakesh, BNP Paribas

What incremental capabilities give Infosys a stronger position in enterprise AI implementations, given that competitors also cite knowledge of client data estates and context and maintain partner ecosystems?

#### Salil Parekh

Infosys highlighted its Topaz Fabric platform, internally developed agents, and ability to integrate other agents. It also emphasized its identification of six growth areas and execution against them through employee reskilling, capability investments, and client-specific development.

Management stated that execution will be the key differentiator.

### Pipeline and TCV conversion timelines

#### Aditi Patil, ICICI Securities

Have pipeline-to-TCV conversion timelines improved because AI enables faster development of prototypes and working models and allows clients to assess returns on investment more quickly?

#### Jayesh Sanghrajka

Based on large deals signed in recent years and recent quarters, Infosys has not observed a significant reduction in deal timelines. Timelines have remained broadly similar. Management noted that future deal timelines remain to be seen.

### ERP migration and modernization

#### Sudheer Guntupalli, Kotak Mahindra Asset Management

Is the reported compression of ERP migration programs from years to weeks occurring broadly, or is it limited to specific use cases?

#### Salil Parekh

Infosys has not observed that specific level of compression for ERP migration. Management distinguished ERP migration from broader modernization, which involves moving from a legacy landscape to a more current landscape. Infosys has observed greater compression in the timeline and cost of broader modernization programs.

### GenAI adoption cycle and revenue compression

#### Vibhor Singhal, Nuvama

How does the current GenAI cycle compare with the earlier digital cycle in terms of revenue cannibalization, peak compression, and the point at which new GenAI revenue becomes positive?

#### Salil Parekh

Management described multiple factors affecting IT services revenue growth, including macroeconomic conditions and technology changes.

Infosys views macroeconomic conditions as improving in its major markets. In the United States, management cited potential effects from regulatory changes, tax reductions, and interest rates.

The technology cycle is developing differently across the six areas. Some process-related opportunities may develop quickly. Customer service, for example, is seeing significant activity, and Infosys is pursuing opportunities that are not currently part of its revenue base. Management stated that Infosys has the relevant technology, partnerships, and capabilities.

Modernization also represents an incremental opportunity where Infosys has relevant technology. Other areas may develop over longer timelines.

Management stated that service compression is visible but neither large nor insignificant, and that it is not accelerating at this stage. Data-related activity is developing more quickly than some other areas.
