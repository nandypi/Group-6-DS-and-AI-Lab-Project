## Salil Parekh

Infosys is seeing substantial interest and deep work across a broad range of AI projects, including specific knowledge processes, credit risk, software development, and customer service. In some cases, Infosys is working across an entire enterprise to transform its AI capabilities and position it as a leading AI enterprise in its industry.

The global environment remains uncertain. Some large markets continue to show growth, but inflation and constrained job creation remain factors. Other markets and industries face cost constraints. Financial Services clients are performing strongly, and Manufacturing also delivered good growth during the quarter. Infosys maintains a portfolio across multiple industries.

Europe has performed extremely well. Infosys has invested in several European countries, where companies have increasingly considered transformation and cost-efficiency services over the past two years. The U.S. remains a strong market. Infosys intends to continue growing and investing in both markets and other global markets, including through acquisitions.

Infosys's large-deal pipeline remains strong. Clients are focused on cost optimization, consolidation, automation, and using AI to improve efficiency. There is also interest in using AI for growth initiatives and AI transformation.

Financial Services and Manufacturing have good pipeline traction. The Retail pipeline is also strong and is being evaluated for conversion. Overall, the large-deal pipeline is focused on cost efficiency, automation, and related activities.

Infosys is focused on externally sharing its AI work. It tracks AI-related developments internally and will share further information externally when appropriate.

AI is creating a significant opportunity in legacy modernization. Without AI tools, modernization programs generally required longer time horizons. AI tools can shorten the time horizon and improve the client's return on investment. Infosys expects AI tools to be increasingly deployed in modernization programs as they mature. The company remains comfortable with its current AI strategy.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys's net employee addition for the quarter was 8,000. The company had guided for approximately 20,000 freshers during the year and had hired more than 12,000 freshers in the first half. These freshers were being onboarded.

Project Maximus continues to deliver. In the previous year, Infosys expanded margins by 50 basis points despite several headwinds, including compensation-related costs, higher variable pay, the impact of an acquisition, and the ramp-up of multiple mega deals signed in the prior year.

During the current quarter, margins expanded by 20 basis points sequentially. Project Maximus contributed 30 basis points of this expansion. Pricing provided a tailwind, which was offset by higher subcontracting costs and lower onsite utilization.

In the second half of the year, seasonally lower working days and furloughs are expected to be headwinds. Project Maximus has multiple tracks that may provide tailwinds, including pricing, lean operations, automation, and optimization of large portfolios and programs. As newly hired freshers are onboarded onto projects, the employee pyramid is expected to provide an additional benefit.

Changes in client contribution were described as marginal and attributable to projects ramping up and ramping down.

The current-quarter acquisition contribution was 20 basis points out of 2.2%. Two months of the acquisition had already been included in the previous quarter, and the acquisition contribution was already incorporated into guidance. Project Versent and the joint venture in Australia will be incorporated when they close. No additional contribution from the acquisition was expected to be added to the guidance run rate for the third or fourth quarter.
