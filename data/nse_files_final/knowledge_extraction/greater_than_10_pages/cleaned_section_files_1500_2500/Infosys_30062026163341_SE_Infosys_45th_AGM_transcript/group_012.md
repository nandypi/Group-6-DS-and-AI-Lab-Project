## Nandan M. Nilekani

Nandan M. Nilekani answered shareholder questions received through the web chat.

### Bonus shares

Infosys has issued multiple bonus shares since listing. The last bonus issue was in 2019. Management stated that there were no announcements regarding a new bonus issue as of the AGM.

### Strategic priorities and competitive differentiation

Infosys’s strategic priority is to help customers navigate the next phase of technology adoption and unlock value from AI. Management cited deep client relationships and delivery capabilities as key advantages.

The company’s differentiators include:

- The Infosys AI Suite, Topaz, and Fabric.
- Collaborations with AI disruptors.
- AI-first and AI-augmented services.
- Platforms designed to connect AI solutions to business outcomes.
- Expansion of sales and marketing across geographies to increase the total addressable market.
- Mergers and acquisitions where strategic fits are identified.
- Talent transformation to prepare employees for AI-related work.

### AI deployment opportunity

Management stated that the AI deployment gap creates significant opportunities for Infosys. It referred to approximately $300 billion of IT spend by 2030, although the exact wording in the transcript was unclear.

Management said enterprise implementation depends heavily on context, while tools act as accelerators. Infosys’s opportunity is to combine agentic software with clients’ traditional transaction systems and make AI useful and relevant to each client’s business.

### ESG, employees, and CSR

Infosys will continue to shape its ESG Vision 2030. The company had 328,000 employees as of the end of March.

Management highlighted CSR activities involving:

- Digital skilling.
- Healthcare.
- Environmental sustainability.
- Employee volunteering.
- Job creation.
- Healthcare innovation and interventions.
- Women’s empowerment.
- Infosys Springboard for digital learning.
- Cornea care programs.
- Lake rejuvenation.
- Agroforestry projects supporting water conservation and climate resilience.

## Salil Parekh

Salil Parekh answered questions on Infosys’s future plans, AI adoption, innovation, capital allocation, regional development, productivity, and margins.

### Future plans and FY2027 guidance

Infosys identified AI services as a major area of focus for financial year 2027 and beyond. Management also noted competitive intensity and the impact of productivity on revenues.

The company’s FY2027 revenue growth guidance, issued with the April results, was **1.5% to 3.5%**.

Over the long term, Infosys expects opportunities across six major AI services areas, including agent development, modernization, and data-related work. Management estimated the related addressable spend at approximately **$300 billion** entering 2030.

### AI adoption and operating metrics

Management provided the following AI-related metrics based on data discussed around Q3:

- AI services represented **5.5% of revenue** at that stage.
- **90% of employees** were AI aware.
- **90% of the 200 largest clients** had work related to AI, including work using Topaz and Fabric.
- Infosys was working on more than **4,800 AI projects**.
- Infosys had built more than **600 agents**.
- The company was scaling a team of forward-deployed engineers working closely with clients to deploy AI in client activities.
- Infosys had partnerships with foundation-model companies, tool companies, and compute companies across the AI ecosystem.
- Infosys had received approximately **16 leadership rankings** in analysts’ AI-related rankings.

### Innovation, research, and emerging technologies

Infosys has a Center for Emerging Technology and Solutions focused on incubating new technologies. Areas mentioned include:

- Artificial intelligence.
- Machine learning.
- Digital.
- Cloud.
- Quantum technologies.

The company builds capabilities by training employees in new technologies and deploying them for large clients.

Infosys has also established Infosys Living Labs, described as global innovation hubs involving clients and partners, and sometimes startups and academic institutions. Management cited joint AI work with a leading university in the UK and a leading university in the US.

Management stated that dividends and buybacks are distributed under the company’s capital return and capital allocation policy.

### Regional development and delivery model

Across India, Infosys has established innovation hubs, nearshore centers, digital design studios, partnerships, and AI-related global capability centers in cities where the company and its clients operate.

The company has also developed a hybrid work model that allows employees to work flexibly from different locations.

Management specifically referred to location centers in Ahmedabad, Hubli, Visakhapatnam, and Noida. The Ahmedabad center was described as supporting development in Gujarat and other locations.

### AI-driven productivity and margins

Management expects AI to generate productivity improvements that can create savings for clients. Clients may reinvest those savings in additional or new IT spending, potentially expanding the addressable market.

Infosys has undertaken an internal margin improvement and expansion project. Management stated that revenue per employee has increased over the past few years and that revenue has grown faster than headcount.

Margin performance cited in the transcript included:

- Operating margin improved by **50 basis points in financial year 2025**.
- Infosys maintained its margin in financial year 2026 despite a negative macroeconomic environment and additional investments in sales and marketing.
- FY2027 operating margin guidance was **20% to 22%**.
- Over the medium term, management’s stated objective is to improve margins from current levels through productivity benefits and related activities.
