## Rishi Basu

Thank you, Sai. The next question is from Avik Das from Business Standard.

## Avik Das

Avik Das asked about the GCC business, including whether mid-market GCCs that have not reached the desired maturity are carving out brownfield businesses or operations and transferring them to service providers. He also asked about the timing of wage-hike announcements and whether wage volatility could stabilize over the next 12 to 15 months, as well as the potential impact on employee morale.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys has a robust business with GCC clients. The company supports global companies both outside India and in India, including with GCC setup, scaling, build-operate-transfer models, and AI skilling.

Salil Parekh said he did not see a broad trend of mid-market GCCs carving out businesses for service providers, although there were some public examples. AI-driven GCCs are receiving increased attention from clients. Infosys has also had recent success with GCCs built for AI.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Wage decisions consider multiple factors, including:

- Company performance.
- Expectations for the next few quarters.
- Industry practice and expected industry growth.
- Wage actions taken by peers.
- Inflation in the relevant markets.
- Employee morale.

## Rishi Basu

The next question is from Sanjana from The Hindu BusinessLine.

## Sanjana B.

Sanjana asked how Infosys is recalibrating its strategy amid continuing uncertainty, whether it would take on large transformation programs such as legacy modernization that may initially dilute margins, and whether visa costs had become a headwind that offset cross-currency benefits from rupee depreciation.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys's AI approach is resonating with clients. The company's six AI service areas include AI strategy and engineering, legacy modernization, process work, and work related to trust. Infosys is pursuing this work in partnership with foundation-model companies, companies building tools on foundation models, and large technology and cloud providers.

The company expects a significant AI transformation in its work over the next several years. It is also focused on ensuring that clients receive available productivity benefits while retaining part of those benefits to support margin resilience. Salil Parekh described the first element as supporting growth and the second as supporting margins.

Infosys will take on large transformation programs when their economic profile is manageable. The company has a broad portfolio of activities and seeks to manage its commitments and internal objectives while continuing to undertake large programs.

Some legacy-modernization programs become self-funded. In one program for a transport company, Infosys is helping move an older technology environment to a microservices architecture. The estimated cost of the work is 60% lower, and the time required is also lower, than it would have been before the AI approach. Infosys is executing the program with a partner and has similar programs in its pipeline.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Visa costs have remained stable. Infosys has not filed H1B visas under the new regime, and its visa costs have not increased.

## Rishi Basu

The next question is from Uma Kannan from the Deccan Herald.

## Uma Kannan

Uma Kannan asked whether clients are asking Infosys to reduce expenses, including employee-related costs, whether current uncertainty is making it harder to retain existing business, and whether the company is seeing project ramp-downs.

She also asked how partnerships with AI companies, including the recently announced OpenAI partnership, are helping clients and whether projects are moving from pilots to production.

## Salil Parekh

AI is now part of every client discussion. Infosys distinguishes between:

- **AI-first services:** New work in the six AI service areas.
- **AI-augmented services:** Existing services in which Infosys has infused AI.

Some AI work is focused on growth rather than cost reduction. For a consumer-products company, Infosys built an application that customers could use while purchasing products. The application increased customer engagement and sales.

For cost-focused work, clients seek productivity improvements. Infosys uses its tools and Topaz Fabric to create these improvements. Salil Parekh said that growth and compression are both present, but growth currently exceeds compression. He cited 3.1% growth last year, with variations much higher, and said the forecast also included growth.

Infosys has projects already in production, including large-scale programs for transport and consumer-products clients. The company described approximately 12 projects at its AI Day, and these were large client projects rather than pilots.

Infosys announced its OpenAI partnership the day before the discussion and its Anthropic partnership a few weeks earlier. These partnerships provide access to foundation-model technology that Infosys can use with clients across its six AI service areas. Infosys also presented examples at its AI Day showing client activity and how partnerships could generate revenue and additional activity for the company.

## Rishi Basu

The next question is from Poulomi Chatterjee from The Financial Express.

## Poulomi Chatterjee

Poulomi Chatterjee asked about the quarterly decline in headcount, the composition of freshers being hired for new AI service lines, and how pricing has evolved so Infosys can capture value from AI-led services.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Headcount depends on volume growth, utilization, and the resulting number of people required to be hired laterally or through other channels. During the quarter, volumes were softer and utilization was lower, which contributed to lower headcount. On a full-year basis, Infosys added 5,000 people to its headcount.

The pricing environment remained stable. Most of the company's growth during the year was pricing-led because volumes were softer. Jayesh Sanghrajka said this was consistent with AI revenue coming at better pricing.
