## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Last year, Infosys began with guidance of 0%–3%. As visibility improved, the company either tightened the band or improved its guidance each quarter. The current guidance reflects the company’s view based on information available at the time.

Infosys stayed away from a European Manufacturing deal that did not meet its return expectations. The client is experiencing a challenging macro environment, resulting in ramp-downs that are included in the guidance.

The guidance also includes the expected impact of the onsite mix. The exit trajectory of the onsite mix is expected to affect the following year by 40–50 basis points. The resulting growth guidance is 1.5%–3.5%. Infosys may review the guidance if visibility improves during the year.

## Gaurav Rateria

Gaurav Rateria asked whether new AI services generate higher revenue productivity and better gross margins than the core business, and when wage increases would begin during the current financial year.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

AI projects generally receive better pricing, which results in better margins. However, they also have higher costs because AI talent currently commands a premium. The premium depends on how far ahead of the market benchmark Infosys is positioned. At benchmark levels, there may be no premium; being ahead of the curve can result in a premium.

Infosys stated that it had won $15 bn of deals, which it cited as evidence of its market positioning. The company also referred to its AI capabilities and client feedback presented during its AI Day. AI deal pricing and margins reflect this positioning.

The timing of wage increases has not been decided. Factors considered include attrition levels, the timing of the last wage increases, market conditions, and inflation.

## Sumeet Jain

Sumeet Jain asked whether the recent launch of Anthropic models had increased client demand for productivity and what types of productivity-related conversations Infosys was having with clients.

## Salil Parekh

The need for productivity remains broadly similar. Competitive intensity has increased in some areas, which is creating additional demand. In a small number of cases, client expectations are outside a range that Infosys believes it can address, and the company is not pursuing those opportunities.

Infosys has not seen a major step change in client demand during the last two months. Productivity expectations have changed over multiple quarters and years, but there has not been a sudden change during the latest two-month period.

Sumeet Jain also asked whether productivity levels in new AI-enabled deals were similar to those being passed on in the existing business, and whether higher revenue compression in the base business could accelerate as AI models improve.

Infosys did not provide a breakdown of revenue compression, gross growth, and net growth across its portfolio. The company stated that growth in AI services and other areas is helping manage compression in other parts of the business.

Sumeet Jain asked for quantification of the impacts from the European Manufacturing client ramp-down, increased competition, and the shift toward more offshore delivery.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

The European client is expected to have an impact of approximately 75 basis points to 1% on revenue growth. This reflects both the decision not to pursue a deal that did not meet return expectations and client ramp-downs during the year due to challenging macroeconomic conditions in that sector.

A reduction in the onsite mix is expected to have an impact of approximately 70 basis points. Of this, 40–50 basis points is already visible in the exit trajectory. Infosys expects further improvement in the onsite mix, which would also affect revenue growth.

## Jonathan Lee

Jonathan Lee asked why net new deals represented a relatively low percentage of total deals, including whether this reflected Infosys’ capabilities, AI-related pressure on demand, or other factors. He also asked about the expected level of net new deals for the year.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

For the full year, Infosys signed $15 bn of deals. Of the 96 deals signed, approximately 55% were net new. This represented approximately 28% year-on-year growth.

Jonathan Lee also asked what had occurred during the quarter relative to internal expectations and when deflationary impacts or lower revenue realization had begun to appear.

Infosys does not disclose internal targets or plans for net large deals and performance against those targets. The company described $3.2 bn as a strong performance. It observed slower decision-making in some areas during March but did not consider this to have had a significant impact on large-deal sign-ups.

## Vibhor Singhal

Vibhor Singhal asked where Infosys was in the AI-related revenue deflation or compression cycle. He compared the cycle with the previous digital cycle, in which revenue compression increased until reaching a trough before declining as incremental digital revenue grew.

He asked whether Infosys was already at the trough of the current revenue deflation cycle, whether further deflation could occur, or whether the worst was behind the company even though compression might continue at a slower rate.
