## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Financial year '26 performance reflected financial discipline and operational execution in a challenging business environment. Client spending was guarded, with greater focus on cost optimization engagements than growth-led transformation programs. AI-driven initiatives showed increasing momentum, particularly in productivity, automation, and platform-led modernization.

### FY26 and Q4 Highlights

1. FY26 revenue crossed $20 billion and grew 3.1% in constant-currency terms, within the upgraded guidance band given in January. Third-party costs decreased by 1% as a percentage of revenue, and onsite mix declined by 0.7%. Acquisitions contributed approximately 70 basis points to full-year growth.
2. Communications, Manufacturing, and Europe grew at more than twice the company average in FY26, led by the ramp-up of large deal wins. Financial Services and EURS also grew above the company average in constant-currency terms.
3. Full-year volumes were broadly flat. Growth was led by higher realization from Project Maximus.
4. Adjusted operating margin was stable at 21%. Benefits from currency and Project Maximus were reinvested in talent, AI, and sales and marketing.
5. Q4 revenue grew 4.1% year-on-year. Sequentially, revenue declined 1.3% in constant-currency terms because of seasonality and slower decision-making in March.
6. Q4 growth was broad-based across major geographies. Communications, EURS, and LS grew well above the company average year-on-year in constant-currency terms.
7. Q4 operating margin was 20.9%, down 0.3 percentage points sequentially, adjusted for the Labor Code impact in Q3.
8. Onsite mix declined to 22.8% from 23.1% in Q3.
9. Utilization excluding trainees was 83% in Q4 and 84.4% for FY26. Utilization including trainees was 81.1% for FY26, reflecting investment in future capacity.
10. Technology-enabled collections efforts reduced DSO, including unbilled revenue net of unearned revenue, to 78 days, the lowest level in seven years.
11. Reported EPS in INR grew 23.8% year-on-year in Q4 and 11% in FY26. EPS adjusted for income tax orders and the Labor Code grew 13.9% in Q4 and 12.1% for the full year in INR terms.
12. Free cash flow adjusted for the Labor Code and income tax refunds was $3.5 billion for FY26 and $882 million for Q4. Adjusted free cash flow as a percentage of net profit was 106% for FY26 and 111% for Q4.
13. FY26 large deal wins had total contract value (TCV) of $15 billion, with 55% net new. The large deal pipeline remained strong. The number of clients with more than $50 million in revenue increased by 3, clients with more than $100 million increased by 3, and clients with more than $400 million increased by 2 during FY26.
14. Year-end headcount exceeded 328,000. Voluntary attrition declined by 1.5 percentage points to 12.6% for the year.
15. Infosys onboarded more than 20,000 freshers in FY26 and expected to hire a similar number in FY27, subject to growth expectations and attrition trends.

## Headwinds of

- 50 basis points from past acquisitions because of additional amortization of intangible assets.
- 30 basis points from normalization of the previous quarter's one-off gain.
- 20 basis points from compensation-related costs, partly offset by lower variable pay.

These were partially offset by the following tailwinds:

- 40 basis points from currency.
- 30 basis points from Project Maximus, including value-based selling, lean and automation, and critical portfolio initiatives.

Q4 yield on cash and investments was 6.2%, compared with 6.7% for the year. Return on equity was 31.6%. Consolidated cash and investments were $4.5 billion after returning more than $4 billion to shareholders in FY26.

Infosys signed 19 large deals during Q4 with TCV of $3.2 billion:

| Vertical | Number of deals |
|---|---:|
| Financial Services | 5 |
| Manufacturing | 5 |
| Retail | 4 |
| Life Sciences | 2 |
| Communications | 2 |
| EURS | 1 |

By geography, 11 deals were signed in Europe, 5 in the Americas, and 3 in the Rest of the World.

In FY26, Infosys signed 96 large deals with TCV of $15 billion, including three mega deals. Net new deals represented 55% of FY26 large deal TCV.

The Q4 tax rate was lower because of the reversal of prior-year tax provisions following favorable tax orders. The expected effective tax rate for FY27 was 29% to 30%.

In line with the capital allocation policy, the board proposed a final dividend of INR 25 per share. If approved by shareholders, total FY26 dividend would be INR 48 per share, an increase of 11.6% over the previous year.

## Coming to verticals,

Financial Services grew 4.4% in FY26, above the company average, led by large deal ramp-ups and continued momentum in AI-led transformation, legacy modernization, and vendor consolidation. Market sentiment remained positive, with continued consumer spending across US banking, capital markets, and Europe. US budgets for CY26 were expected to grow. Infosys signed a large GCC deal for a regional US bank, described as an industry-first, AI-first GCC deal. Infosys was the strategic AI partner for 18 of the top 20 clients in this vertical. Large deal closures, new account openings, and the strong large deal pipeline were expected to support growth acceleration in FY27.

Manufacturing clients remained cautious amid softer demand, particularly in automotive and parts of Europe. Tariffs and the ongoing Middle East conflict contributed to uncertainty and delayed decision-making in some areas. Discretionary spending remained constrained, while clients prioritized cost optimization and operational resilience. The large deal pipeline included infrastructure outsourcing, application management services, and S/4HANA rollouts. Near-term and FY27 growth was expected to be affected by lower revenue from one large client.

Demand across EURS remained constructive, supported by a strong large deal pipeline. Clients continued to prioritize cost reduction and operational efficiency, driving vendor consolidation. Energy saw increased outsourcing and healthy deal momentum. Utilities demand was structurally higher, driven by grid constraints, renewable integration, and increasing electricity needs for data centers. Eighty percent of FY26 large deal TCV in EURS was net new, supporting expected growth acceleration in FY27.

In Retail, clients faced continued uncertainty from supply-chain disruptions, geopolitical conflict, and shifting trade policy. Consumer demand remained muted, and budgets were tightly controlled, with discretionary spending under pressure. Clients expected AI-led productivity to generate savings within similar budgets. Demand was expected to increase for AI-assisted legacy modernization. Topaz Fabric and AI Next platforms supported client ideation through the transition from concept to deployable solutions, with privacy, ethics, and control guardrails.

Communications growth in FY26 was led by large deal ramp-ups. The overall environment remained cautious amid macroeconomic uncertainty and client margin pressures. Budgets were flat to negative, affecting discretionary spending. Non-discretionary spending was selective and increasingly AI-led. Clients were shifting from Generative AI to Agentic AI and consolidating IT and BPM to reduce costs. Infosys saw increased AI deal activity in IT operations, software replacement, and mainframe migration.

As FY27 began, enterprise budgets remained measured and selective amid macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainties, higher interest rates, rapid technology shifts, and high competitive intensity.

FY27 growth guidance was 1.5% to 3.5% in constant-currency terms. The guidance included:

- Contribution from Stratus, which had closed earlier that week, but excluded the Versent joint venture and Optimum Healthcare acquisitions, which had not yet closed.
- A reduction of 0.75% to 1% because of lower revenue from one large European manufacturing client. The lower revenue reflected reduced client spending in a challenging macroeconomic environment and the decision not to pursue deals that were not aligned with return expectations.
- A further reduction in onsite mix of 0.75% to 1%.
- An assumption that third-party costs would remain at similar levels to FY26.

FY27 operating margin guidance was 20% to 22%. This assumed headwinds from wage increases, productivity pass-throughs, and AI investment, offset by Project Maximus initiatives.

The impact of Optimum Healthcare, Stratus, and Versent on operating margin was expected to be approximately 0.7% on a full-year annualized basis after closure.

## Moderator

The question-and-answer session began with a question from Yogesh Aggarwal of HSBC Securities regarding the factors supporting the lower and upper ends of FY27 growth guidance and the areas where the benefits of INR depreciation had been reinvested.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

At the lower end of the guidance range, Infosys assumed greater deterioration in the business environment. At the upper end, it assumed an improved environment, similar to the prior year.

Benefits from the rupee and Project Maximus were reinvested in the business, including sales and marketing costs, which increased by 40 basis points on a full-year basis, AI talent, and AI partnerships. These investments were absorbed within the year's margin.
