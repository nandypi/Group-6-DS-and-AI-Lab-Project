## 23.28.8 In relation to the physical Equity Shares:

If physical Equity Shares tendered by Eligible Shareholders are not accepted, the share certificate will be returned to the Eligible Shareholder by registered post, ordinary post, or courier at the Eligible Shareholder’s sole risk. The Company encourages Eligible Shareholders holding physical shares to dematerialize them.

### 23.28.9

If only a portion of the physical shares held by an Eligible Shareholder is accepted in the Buyback, the Company will issue a Letter of Confirmation (“LOC”), instead of a split share certificate, for the unaccepted Equity Shares. The LOC will be issued in accordance with SEBI Circular No. SEBI/HO/MIRSD/MIRSD_RTamb/P/CIR/2022/8 dated January 25, 2022, as amended, and will relate to the new consolidated share certificate for the unaccepted shares.

The LOC will be dispatched to the address registered with the RTA. The RTA will retain the original share certificate and mark it with the stamp “Letter of Confirmation Issued” on the face or reverse of the certificate for the excess physical shares.

The LOC will be valid for 120 days from its date of issuance. During this period, the Equity Shareholder must request their depository participant to dematerialize the physical Equity Shares. If the Equity Shareholder does not submit the dematerialization request within this period, the RTA will credit the physical Equity Shares to a separate demat account of the Company opened for this purpose.

The lien marked against unaccepted Equity Shares, if any, will be released. Unaccepted physical shares may otherwise be returned by registered post, ordinary post, or courier at the Eligible Shareholder’s sole risk. Eligible Shareholders should maintain their depository account until all Buyback formalities are completed.

### 23.28.10

The Stock Broker will issue a contract note for the Equity Shares accepted under the Buyback. The Company’s Broker will also issue a contract note to the Company for the Equity Shares accepted under the Buyback.

### 23.28.11

Equity Shareholders intending to participate in the Buyback should consult their respective stock brokers regarding any costs, applicable taxes, charges, and expenses, including brokerage, that may be levied for tendering Equity Shares in the Buyback as a secondary-market transaction.

The Manager to the Buyback and the Company will not bear or pay these costs, which will be incurred solely by the Eligible Shareholders.

### 23.28.12

The Equity Shares credited to the Company Demat Account and the Equity Shares bought back and accepted in physical form will be extinguished in accordance with the procedure prescribed in the Buyback Regulations.

## 23.29 Rejection Criteria

Equity Shares tendered by Eligible Shareholders may be rejected on the following grounds.

### Eligible Shareholders holding shares in dematerialized form

- The shareholder was not an Eligible Shareholder of the Company on the Record Date.
- A restraint order of a court or other competent authority exists in relation to the transfer, disposal, or sale of the Equity Shares.
- Loss of share certificates has been notified to the Company.
- The title to the Equity Shares is under dispute, is otherwise unclear, or another restraint subsists.

### Eligible Shareholders holding Equity Shares in physical form

- The documents specified in the Tender Form are not received by the Registrar by the close of business on November 26, 2025, by 5 p.m. IST.
- A share certificate of another company is enclosed instead of the Company’s share certificate.
- Share certificates stating a face value of ₹10 each were cancelled and are no longer valid. Equity Shares tendered using such certificates will be rejected.
- Transmission of the Equity Shares is incomplete, or the Equity Shares are not registered in the name of the Eligible Shareholder.
- The Registrar does not receive the physical Equity Share certificate.
- The signature on the Tender Form and Form SH-4 does not match the specimen signature recorded with the Company or Registrar.
- The shareholder was not an Eligible Shareholder of the Company on the Record Date.
- There is a name mismatch in the shareholder’s share certificate.
- The Eligible Shareholder has made a duplicate bid. Only the duplicated bid will be liable to rejection.
- A restraint order of a court or other competent authority exists in relation to the transfer, disposal, or sale of the Equity Shares.
- Loss of share certificates has been notified to the Company.
- The title to the Equity Shares is under dispute, is otherwise unclear, or another restraint subsists.

## A. INDIAN TAXATION

The income-tax considerations described in this section are based on the provisions of the Income-tax Act, 1961, and related regulations, judicial decisions, and administrative interpretations in force at the time. These provisions and interpretations may be changed or modified by subsequent legislative, regulatory, administrative, or judicial decisions, which could result in different income-tax implications.

Shareholders are required to consult their tax advisers regarding the applicable tax provisions, including the treatment that may be adopted by their respective tax officers and the appropriate course of action. The Company accepts no responsibility for the accuracy of such advice. The tax implications described below are indicative and for guidance purposes only.

### General

The basis of charge of Indian income-tax depends on the taxpayer’s residential status during a tax year. The Indian tax year runs from April 1 to March 31.

An Indian tax resident is liable to income-tax in India on worldwide income, subject to exemptions under the Income-tax Act, 1961 (the “Act”).

A person treated as a non-resident for Indian income-tax purposes is generally subject to tax in India only on:

- India-sourced income, including income accruing or arising, or deemed to accrue or arise, in India; and
- Income received by that person in India.

For shares of a company, the source of income depends on the “situs” of the shares. Based on judicial precedents, the situs of shares is generally where the company is incorporated and where its shares can be transferred.

Because the Company is incorporated in India, the Company’s shares should be deemed to be situated in India. Gains arising to a non-resident from the acceptance of such shares under the Buyback should therefore be taxable in India under the Act.

A non-resident shareholder may claim benefits under the Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (“DTAA”) between India and the country in which the shareholder is tax resident, subject to satisfying applicable conditions, including:

- Conditions under the relevant DTAA, if any, read with the relevant provisions of the Multilateral Convention to Implement Tax Treaty Related Measures to Prevent Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (“Multilateral Instrument” or “MLI”) as ratified by India with the shareholder’s country of tax residence;
- Non-application of the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (“GAAR”); and
- Providing and maintaining the information and documents prescribed under the Act.

The Act provides different income-tax rates for gains arising from the Buyback of shares, depending on the shareholder’s residential status, classification, and the nature of income earned. References to Equity Shares in the following classification refer to listed Equity Shares unless stated otherwise.

### Classification of Shareholders

Shareholders may be classified as follows:

- **Resident Shareholders**
  - Individuals, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), Associations of Persons (AOPs), and Bodies of Individuals (BOIs)
  - Companies
  - Other than companies

- **Non-Resident Shareholders**
  - Non-Resident Indians (NRIs)
  - Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs)
  - Companies
  - Other than companies
