## Overview and Notes to the Standalone Financial Statements

## 1. Overview

## 1.1 Company overview

Infosys Limited ("the Company" or Infosys) provides AI-first business consulting and technology services to enable organizations to unlock AI value at scale. The Company has over four decades of experience managing the systems and operations of global enterprises. Its services include business transformation supported by an AI-first value framework, domain expertise, and an AI-native partner ecosystem.

Infosys is a public limited company incorporated and domiciled in India. Its primary listings are on BSE Ltd. and the National Stock Exchange of India Limited. Its American Depositary Shares (ADS), representing equity shares, are listed on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE).

The standalone financial statements were approved for issue by the Company’s Board of Directors on April 23, 2026.

## 1.2 Basis of preparation of financial statements

The standalone financial statements are prepared in accordance with the Companies Act, 2013, guidelines issued by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), and Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS).

The financial statements use the historical cost convention and accrual basis, except for certain financial instruments measured at fair value and defined benefit liabilities or assets recognized at the present value of the defined benefit obligation less the fair value of plan assets. Ind AS is prescribed under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013, read with Rule 3 of the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015, and subsequent amendment rules.

Accounting policies have been applied consistently, except when a newly issued accounting standard is initially adopted or a revision to an existing standard requires a change in accounting policy. Material accounting policy information is discussed in the respective notes.

Year-end figures are taken from the source and rounded to the nearest digits. Consequently, figures reported for previous quarters may not always add up to the year-end figures reported in this statement.

## 1.3 Use of estimates and judgments

The preparation of financial statements in conformity with Ind AS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies, reported amounts of assets and liabilities, disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities, and reported revenues and expenses.

The accounting policies involving critical accounting estimates and complex or subjective judgments are disclosed in Note 1.4. Estimates, judgments, and assumptions may change from period to period, and actual results could differ from those estimates. Changes in estimates are reflected in the financial statements in the period in which they are made; material effects are disclosed in the notes to the standalone financial statements.

## 1.4 Critical accounting estimates and judgments

## a. Revenue recognition

The Company’s customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue recognition and measurement begin when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable.

The Company assesses the services promised in each contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying distinct performance obligations, determining the deliverables and whether the customer can benefit independently from them, and allocating the transaction price to those obligations involve significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and the Company’s costs to fulfill a fixed-price maintenance contract are uneven because the services are discrete rather than repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method. The method used depends on the promises in the contract and the nature of the deliverables.

The Company uses the percentage-of-completion method for other fixed-price contracts. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because they have a direct relationship with input and productivity. Estimated total efforts or costs are reassessed throughout the contract period using the latest available information.

Some integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Company acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Company assesses whether it controls the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer, considering factors such as:

- primary responsibility for fulfilling the promise;
- inventory risk; and
- pricing discretion.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts, when losses become probable, are recorded in the period in which they become probable.

## b. Income taxes

The Company’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. It also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is required to determine income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing whether deferred income tax assets are realizable, management considers whether some or all of the assets may not be realized. Ultimate realization depends on the generation of future taxable income during the periods in which temporary differences become deductible. Management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies.

Based on historical taxable income and projections for future taxable income over the periods in which deferred tax assets are deductible, management believes the Company will realize the benefits of the deductible differences. The amount of deferred income tax assets considered realizable could be reduced in the near term if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period are reduced. (Refer to Note 2.17.)

## c. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Company’s asset base. Periodic depreciation is determined using estimates of an asset’s expected useful life and residual value.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. (Refer to Note 2.1.)

## 2. Notes to the Standalone Financial Statements

## 2.1 Property, plant and equipment

## Accounting Policy

Property, plant and equipment are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment, if any. Costs directly attributable to acquisition are capitalized until the assets are ready for use as intended by management.

The Company depreciates property, plant and equipment over their estimated useful lives using the straight-line method. The estimated useful lives are:

| Asset | Estimated useful life |
|---|---|
| Building (1) | 22–25 years |
| Plant and machinery (1) | 5 years |
| Office equipment | 5 years |
| Computer equipment (1) | 3–5 years |
| Furniture and fixtures (1) | 5 years |
| Vehicles (1) | 5 years |
| Leasehold improvements | Lower of the useful life of the asset or the lease term |

(1) Based on technical evaluation, management believes these useful lives best represent the period over which the assets are expected to be used. Accordingly, the useful lives for these assets differ from those prescribed under Part C of Schedule II of the Companies Act, 2013.

Depreciation methods, useful lives, and residual values are reviewed periodically, including at each financial year-end. The useful lives are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events that may affect asset lives, including changes in technology.

Advances paid toward the acquisition of property, plant and equipment outstanding at each balance sheet date are classified as capital advances under other non-current assets. The cost of assets not ready for use before that date is disclosed as capital work-in-progress.

Subsequent expenditure relating to property, plant and equipment is capitalized only when future economic benefits are probable and the cost can be measured reliably. The cost and related accumulated depreciation are removed from the financial statements upon sale or retirement of the asset.
