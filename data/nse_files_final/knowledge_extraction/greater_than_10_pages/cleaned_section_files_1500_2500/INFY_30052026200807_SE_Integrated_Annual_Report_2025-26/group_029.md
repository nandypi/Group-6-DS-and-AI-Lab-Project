## Management's discussion and analysis

## Overview

Infosys provides AI-first business consulting and technology services. It has more than four decades of experience managing the systems and operations of global enterprises and uses an AI-first value framework, domain expertise, and an AI-native partner ecosystem to support clients' business transformation.

Its strategic objective is to build a sustainable and resilient organization, remain relevant to clients, create growth opportunities for employees, generate profitable returns for investors, and contribute to the communities in which it operates. Business risks and challenges are discussed in the Risk management report of the Integrated Annual Report.

## I. Industry structure and developments

Fiscal 2026 saw lower interest rates and stable economic sentiment, while policy changes and geopolitical uncertainties affected the technology services industry.

The industry experienced subdued growth, with technology-led growth continuing in some parts of the economy. AI-driven technology transformation changed discretionary spending patterns and increased pressure on traditional IT services.

Global GDP growth remained flat at 3.3% in CY 2025. During the same period, IT services grew by 4.6%, representing a contraction of 10 basis points. Client spending and investments were expected to continue shifting toward AI in the near term.

Businesses used enterprise AI to reimagine cost structures, improve resilience and agility, personalize customer and employee experiences, and launch new products and services.

For more information, refer to the Our business context section of the Integrated Annual Report.

## II. Opportunities and threats

## Our strategy

Infosys's clients and prospective clients are responding to opportunities created by advances in software and computing technology, particularly AI. They are reimagining core offerings, processes, and systems and positioning themselves for leadership in their industries.

Infosys's strategy is to help clients ideate, plan, and scale their AI transformation by building strong data foundations and robust cloud capabilities. The company also emphasizes safeguards for privacy, ethics, and controls through Responsible AI practices.

## Our strengths

Infosys has more than four decades of experience managing the systems and operations of global enterprises. It has invested in its own AI-first strategy and in strengthening its AI capabilities to support clients' AI transformation.

## Our competition

Infosys operates in an environment characterized by intense competition in traditional services, rapid technological change, and the emergence of specialized providers focused on niche areas.

The company has invested in proprietary intellectual property in software platforms and products, including:

- **Infosys Cobalt™:** Services, solutions, and platforms for enterprises' cloud journeys.
- **Infosys Topaz™:** AI-first services, solutions, and platforms using generative and agentic AI technology.
- **Infosys Aster™:** An AI-amplified marketing suite.

## III. Financial condition

Detailed schedules and notes are provided in the Standalone and Consolidated financial statements of the Integrated Annual Report.

## 1. Equity share capital

Infosys has one class of equity shares with a par value of ₹5 each. During the year, share capital changed due to:

- Buyback of 10,00,00,000 shares, resulting in a cash outflow of ₹18,000 crore, excluding transaction costs.
- Shares issued upon exercise of stock options.

## 2. Other equity

Retained earnings changed due to profit earned during the year, dividend payments, and the buyback of equity shares.

The company funded the buyback from free reserves, including securities premium, in accordance with Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013. As at March 31, 2026, it created a Capital Redemption Reserve of ₹50 crore, equal to the nominal value of the shares bought back, by appropriation from the general reserve in accordance with Section 69 of the Companies Act, 2013.

The increase in securities premium was mainly due to the exercise of stock options. The Group elected to present subsequent changes in the fair value of certain instruments in other comprehensive income.

The Special Economic Zone Re-Investment Reserve was created from the profit of the eligible SEZ unit under Section 10AA(1)(ii) of the Income-Tax Act, 1961. The reserve is to be used to acquire new plant and machinery for the company's business under Section 10AA(2) of the Income-Tax Act, 1961.

## 3. Property, plant and equipment

Additions to the gross block were mainly related to computer equipment and infrastructure.

## 4. Goodwill and other intangible assets

On a consolidated basis, the carrying value of goodwill was ₹12,117 crore as at March 31, 2026, compared with ₹10,106 crore as at March 31, 2025.

The increase was primarily attributable to:

- Goodwill of ₹444 crore recognized on the acquisition of MRE Consulting Ltd. and The Missing Link Security Pty. Ltd., The Missing Link Security Limited, and The Missing Link Automation Pty. Ltd., collectively referred to as “The Missing Link”.
- A translation difference of ₹1,567 crore arising from foreign currency fluctuations.

The consolidated carrying value of intangible assets was ₹2,825 crore as at March 31, 2026, compared with ₹2,766 crore as at March 31, 2025. During the year ended March 31, 2026, intangible assets of ₹297 crore, mainly customer-related intangibles, were added through acquisitions.

Refer to Note 2.4.2 of the Consolidated Financial Statements for further details.

## 5. Financial assets

### A. Investments

During the year, Infosys made additional standalone investments in subsidiaries for the acquisition of entities and operations and for expansion.

Infosys invests in the startup ecosystem through minority equity positions in startup companies and/or venture capital funds. These investments provide access to innovations that may be combined with its services and solutions for clients.

The company's investments comprise:

- Mutual fund units
- Tax-free bonds
- Non-convertible debentures
- Certificates of deposit
- Commercial paper
- Quoted bonds and securities issued by government and quasi-government organizations

Certificates of deposit and commercial paper are marketable securities issued for specified periods by banks, NBFCs, and eligible financial institutions with high credit ratings from domestic credit-rating agencies.

Government securities are liquid, marketable instruments issued across different tenures, backed by the Government, and carrying sovereign credit. Non-convertible debentures are debt instruments issued by government-aided institutions and financial institutions with high credit ratings.

Most investments are fair valued using Level 1 or Level 2 inputs. The company assesses counterparty risk using criteria including Tier-I capital, capital adequacy ratio, credit rating, profitability, non-performing asset levels, and deposit base. These risks are monitored regularly as part of the risk-management program.

### B. Trade receivables

Days Sales Outstanding in USD terms was 67 days for the year ended March 31, 2026, compared with 69 days in the previous year.

### C. Cash and cash equivalents

Cash and cash equivalents comprise deposits with banks that have high credit ratings assigned by international and domestic credit-rating agencies. The deposits can be withdrawn at any time without prior notice or penalty on principal. Ratings are monitored periodically.

### D. Loans

Loans are provided to subsidiaries according to business requirements. Infosys had no loans outstanding as at March 31, 2026.

### E. Other financial assets

Deposits with corporations represent restricted deposits used to settle employee-related obligations as they arise during the normal course of business.

Unbilled revenues are classified as financial assets when the right to consideration is unconditional and payment is due only after the passage of time.

Foreign-currency forward and options contracts are entered into to mitigate the risk of changes in exchange rates on foreign-currency exposures. The counterparties are generally banks.

## 6. Other assets

Unbilled revenues are classified as non-financial assets when the right to consideration depends on completion of contractual milestones.

Withholding taxes and other amounts represent credits that can be claimed against local taxes payable in various countries.

Deferred contract costs mainly comprise the costs of obtaining and fulfilling contracts, recorded in accordance with Ind AS 115, *Revenue from Contracts with Customers*.

## 7. Deferred tax assets / liabilities

Deferred tax assets primarily arise from temporary differences relating to property, plant and equipment, lease liabilities, allowances for trade receivables, credits related to branch profits, and compensated absences.

These assets are partially offset by deferred tax liabilities relating to the Special Economic Zone Re-Investment Reserve, branch profit tax, and intangibles from business combinations.

A deferred income tax asset is recognized to the extent that it is probable that future taxable profit will be available against which deductible temporary differences and tax losses can be utilized.
