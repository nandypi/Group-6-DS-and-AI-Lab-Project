## Selection and appointment of new directors

The Board delegates the screening and selection process to the Nomination and Remuneration Committee. The Committee presents a diverse slate of eligible candidates to the Board for approval under the criteria in the Nomination and Remuneration Policy. The Board then recommends the candidates to shareholders for approval.

## Succession planning

The Nomination and Remuneration Committee works with the Board on leadership succession planning for Board and senior management appointments. The Company seeks to maintain an appropriate balance of skills and experience while introducing new perspectives and maintaining experience and continuity. Promoting senior management internally also supports future leadership development.

## Training of Board members

New non-executive directors receive orientation on the Company’s culture, operations, values, commitments, organization structure, services, Group structure and subsidiaries, constitution, Board procedures, matters reserved for the Board, major risks, and risk management strategy.

Details of the familiarization program are available in the [training program document](https://www.infosys.com/investors/reports-filings/Documents/training-board-members2026.pdf).

Non-executive and independent directors are familiarized through engagements including periodic management strategy retreats. These retreats cover strategic planning, progress of strategic initiatives, risks to strategy execution, and the need for new strategic programs. They also enable interaction with senior leadership and discussion of competitive differentiation, execution plans, and implementation challenges.

### Geopolitical impact and preparedness

Board members received briefings on foreign trade policy, technical and AI competition, immigration, and international relations that may affect the business. Management also briefed the Board on the Company’s preparedness for evolving geopolitical conditions.

### Evolving Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) landscape

The Board reviewed the evolving global ESG landscape and its implications for the IT sector, including:

- AI-driven emissions growth
- Increasing exposure to extreme weather risks
- Expansion of green technology markets
- Changes in workforce skill requirements
- Sustainability solutions, inclusive hiring, employee development, and governance disclosures
- Rising expectations for transparent ESG reporting and verified outcomes

The training also covered regulatory developments relevant to readiness planning, including the EU’s simplified CSRD requirements, emerging climate disclosure regulations in California and New York, India’s phased climate finance taxonomy, and SEBI’s revised timelines for BRSR value-chain reporting.

The training highlighted the Board’s expanding role in ESG oversight, including strengthening governance structures, enhancing ESG competencies, improving ESG-related information flows, integrating sustainability into strategy and enterprise risk management, and aligning leadership remuneration with ESG performance metrics.

Each director is entitled to a training fee of US $5,000 per year. Support is provided to independent directors who attend educational programs in Board or corporate governance.

During the year, one independent director attended the NACD “AI in Action” event on artificial intelligence and Board decision-making. Another independent director participated in the Master Class® on Technology & Innovation Oversight — an AI Tools workshop covering frameworks for technology, operations, strategy, and governance.

## Effective corporate governance

The Company’s corporate governance framework includes the constitution, governance, and working of Board committees.

## Availability of information to Board members

The Board has unrestricted access to Company-related information, including employee-related information. Managers and representatives may be invited to Board meetings to provide additional insights on matters under discussion.

Information is provided continuously for review, input, and approval. Strategic and operational plans, quarterly and annual financial statements, acquisition proposals, important managerial decisions, material positive or negative developments, and statutory matters are presented to the Board or its committees as applicable. Committee recommendations are subsequently presented to the Board for approval.

Board materials are submitted with the agenda well in advance of meetings. Directors’ inputs and feedback are considered when preparing meeting agendas and documents, and directors may provide suggestions on strategic and operational matters.
