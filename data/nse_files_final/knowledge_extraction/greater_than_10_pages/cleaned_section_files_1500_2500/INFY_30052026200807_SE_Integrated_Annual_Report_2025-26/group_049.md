## Risk management report

Over the last fiscal year, businesses worldwide faced challenges from geopolitical shifts, economic uncertainties, supply chain disruptions, evolving regulations, advances in artificial intelligence, technology upheavals, and cybersecurity risks. These factors affected operations and decision-making.

The risk-related information in this section may not be exhaustive. It may contain forward-looking statements, and actual results may differ materially because of known or unknown risks and uncertainties. Readers are advised to refer to the detailed discussion of risk factors and related disclosures in the Company's regulatory filings.

Infosys' Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) function supports the achievement of the Company's strategic objectives by identifying, analyzing, assessing, mitigating, monitoring, and governing risks and potential threats. The ERM framework considers the interests of employees, customers, investors, regulatory bodies, partners, and the community.

Strategic decisions consider primary, secondary, consequential, and residual risks. The ERM framework supports resource allocation through qualitative and quantitative risk-impact assessments and prioritization based on the Company's risk appetite. Risk impact and likelihood are assessed using a four-point scale, and risks are categorized into four levels. Risks are mitigated in order of criticality: critical, high, medium, and low. Low impact is the default risk appetite for all risks. Preventive, detective, and corrective controls, together with governance mechanisms, are used to keep residual risk exposure within acceptable limits.

The ERM framework also identifies potential opportunities during risk assessments. It covers strategy and strategy execution, operational, and legal and compliance risks, each of which may have internal or external dimensions. Risk indicators are used to identify risks proactively, including their cumulative impact.

### Emerging risks

The ERM framework includes a process to identify, assess, plan for, and monitor risks emerging from the internal and external environment. The process includes horizon scanning and SWOT analysis covering political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental aspects. Emerging risks are monitored continuously and discussed periodically in risk councils and with the Risk Management Committee (RMC) of the Board.

| Risk category | Description |
|---|---|
| Strategy and strategy execution | Risks arising from the choices made in defining the Company's strategy and from unsuccessful strategy execution. Risks inherent to the industry and competitiveness are analyzed and mitigated through choices relating to target markets, market offerings, business model, and talent base. |
| Operational | Risks affecting policies, procedures, people, and systems, potentially impacting service delivery or operations or compromising core values or business practices. Examples include inefficient internal processes, human rights risks, fraud, disruption from natural calamities, climate change events, human conflicts, system failures, and cybersecurity attacks. |
| Legal and compliance | Risks threatening the Company's financial, organizational, or reputational standing through litigation, non-conformance with laws, regulatory or geopolitical developments, codes of conduct, and contractual non-compliance. |

## Integrated Enterprise Risk Management Framework

Infosys has adopted an integrated ERM framework implemented across the organization by the Risk Management Office. The framework incorporates practices based on COSO and ISO 31000 and is tailored to the Company's business requirements.

Risk management encompasses the first, second, and third lines of defense. It is embedded in core business processes and the day-to-day decision-making of every function, including development processes and project execution. Risk management responsibilities are included in the roles of senior management, middle management, and employees who engage with clients. Management at all levels is responsible for proactively managing risks. Financial incentives are linked to the achievement of strategic goals.

The first line of defense evaluates risks and opportunities before key business decisions are made. It works with corporate ERM through risk councils and sub-risk councils established across organizational units.

The second line of defense includes the Information Security Group (ISG), Data Privacy Office (DPO), Quality, Delivery Risk Management (DRM), Business Continuity, Legal, and Compliance functions. These functions work with ERM to identify, assess, report, and govern risks. Risks are primarily mitigated by the first line and business functions, while the second line tracks and governs them. Risks are reported to executive leadership through councils including the Operational Risk Council (ORC) and the Legal & Compliance Risk Council (LCRC). Key risks are presented to the Risk Management Committee (RMC) of the Board quarterly.

The third line of defense consists of internal audit, which periodically reviews risks and controls, assesses the effectiveness of the risk management process, and reports independently. ERM inputs on key risks are considered when preparing the internal audit plan.

The Company maintains a single hierarchical risk repository from L1 to L5, covering strategic to tactical risks. These risks are governed through the integrated ERM framework.

The Company periodically conducts an ERM process maturity assessment through an independent external agency. The maturity assessment was conducted in March 2024.

Risk management is embedded throughout the organization through forums for risk discussions and decision-making. Comprehensive awareness and training programs are conducted regularly for employees and contractors. Lex, the Company's in-house learning platform, provides risk management courses accessible worldwide. Periodic tabletop exercises are conducted in key areas to assess preparedness for high-impact, low-probability risks.
