## Independent Auditor's Report

To the Members of Infosys Limited

## Report on the Audit of the Standalone Financial Statements

## Opinion

The auditors audited the standalone financial statements of Infosys Limited for the year ended March 31, 2026. These comprise:

- Balance Sheet as at March 31, 2026
- Statement of Profit and Loss, including Other Comprehensive Income, for the year ended March 31, 2026
- Statement of Changes in Equity
- Statement of Cash Flows
- Notes to the financial statements, including material accounting policies and explanatory information

In the auditors’ opinion, the standalone financial statements give the information required under the Companies Act, 2013, and present a true and fair view of the Company’s financial position as at March 31, 2026, its profit, other comprehensive income, changes in equity, and cash flows for the year ended on that date. The statements conform to Indian Accounting Standards prescribed under section 133 of the Act and other generally accepted accounting principles in India.

## Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013. The auditors stated that they were independent of the Company under the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India’s Code of Ethics and applicable ethical requirements, and that they had fulfilled their other ethical responsibilities.

The auditors considered the audit evidence obtained to be sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their opinion.

## Key Audit Matters

Key audit matters were matters that, in the auditors’ professional judgment, were of most significance in the audit of the current-period standalone financial statements. These matters were considered in the context of the financial statements as a whole, and no separate opinion was provided on them.

### 1. Revenue recognition

Infosys’s customer contracts may include multiple products and services. Revenue sources include:

- Software development and related services
- Maintenance
- Consulting and package implementation
- Licensing of software products and platforms across core and digital offerings
- Business process management services

The Company assesses the services promised in each contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. This requires significant judgment concerning the deliverables and whether customers can benefit independently from them.

For integrated service arrangements involving subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue from third-party products or services is recorded:

- Net of costs when Infosys acts as an agent between the customer and vendor
- Gross when Infosys acts as the principal

The principal-versus-agent assessment considers whether Infosys obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors include responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other relevant considerations.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably:

- On a straight-line basis when services consist of an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period
- Using the percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of customer benefits and fulfilment costs is uneven because services are discrete and non-repetitive

Revenue recognition was identified as a key audit matter because it involves judgment in:

1. Identifying distinct performance obligations
2. Determining whether Infosys is acting as principal or agent
3. Selecting the appropriate method for recognizing fixed-price maintenance revenue

Refer to Notes 1.4 and 2.18 to the Standalone Financial Statements.

#### Auditor’s Response

The auditors’ procedures included:

- Testing the effectiveness of controls relating to the identification of distinct performance obligations, principal-versus-agent determinations, and the method used to recognize fixed-price maintenance revenue.
- Selecting customer contracts and reading master service agreements and other contract documents.
- Identifying significant contractual terms and deliverables.
- Assessing management’s conclusions regarding performance obligations, principal-versus-agent status, and the method used to recognize fixed-price maintenance revenue.

### 2. Revenue recognition — fixed-price contracts using the percentage-of-completion method

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized either on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method, depending on the nature and pattern of the services.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts in which performance obligations are satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.

Under this method, actual efforts or costs incurred to date are measured as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs required to complete the contract. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because of the direct relationship between inputs and productivity. Estimates of total efforts or costs are reassessed throughout the contract period to reflect the latest available information. Provisions for estimated losses, if any, on uncompleted contracts are recorded.

This was identified as a key audit matter because estimating total efforts or costs to complete fixed-price contracts involves significant judgment and inherent uncertainty. The assessment considers contract progress, efforts or costs incurred to date, and estimated efforts or costs required to complete the remaining performance obligations.

Refer to Notes 1.4 and 2.18 to the Standalone Financial Statements.

#### Auditor’s Response

The auditors’ procedures included:

- Testing controls over recording efforts or costs incurred and estimating efforts or costs required to complete remaining contract performance obligations.
- Testing access and application controls for time-recording, allocation, and budgeting systems to prevent unauthorized changes to recorded efforts.
- Selecting fixed-price contracts measured using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Evaluating management’s ability to estimate progress by comparing actual efforts or costs incurred with prior-year estimates and budgeted efforts or costs for fulfilled performance obligations.
- Comparing efforts or costs incurred with the Company’s estimates to identify significant variations and assessing whether the variations were appropriately considered in estimating remaining costs or efforts.
- Testing estimates against delivery milestones and customer acceptances or sign-offs to identify delays requiring changes to estimated costs or efforts for remaining performance obligations.
