## Overview and notes to the Consolidated Financial Statements

## 1. Overview

## 1.1 Company overview

Infosys Limited ("the Company" or Infosys) provides AI-first business consulting and technology services to enable organizations to unlock AI value at scale. The Group comprises Infosys, its subsidiaries, and controlled trusts.

The Company is a public limited company incorporated and domiciled in India. Its registered office is in Electronics City, Hosur Road, Bengaluru 560100, Karnataka, India. Infosys is primarily listed on BSE Ltd. and the National Stock Exchange of India Limited. Its American Depositary Shares (ADS), representing equity shares, are listed on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE).

The Group’s consolidated financial statements were approved for issue by the Company’s Board of Directors on April 23, 2026.

## 1.2 Basis of preparation of financial statements

The consolidated financial statements are prepared in accordance with the Companies Act, 2013, guidelines issued by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), and Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS), under the historical cost convention and on an accrual basis, except for certain financial instruments measured at fair value and defined benefit liabilities or assets recognized at the present value of the defined benefit obligation less the fair value of plan assets.

Ind AS are prescribed under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013, read with Rule 3 of the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015, and relevant amendment rules issued thereafter.

Accounting policies have been applied consistently, except when a newly issued accounting standard is initially adopted or a revision to an existing standard requires a change in accounting policy. Material accounting policy information used in preparing the audited consolidated financial statements is discussed in the respective notes.

Quarter and year-to-date figures are taken from source data and rounded to the nearest digits. Therefore, quarter figures may not always add up to the year-to-date figures reported in the statement.

## 1.3 Basis of consolidation

Infosys consolidates entities that it owns or controls. The consolidated financial statements comprise the financial statements of the Company, its controlled trusts, and its subsidiaries. Control exists when the parent has power over an entity, is exposed or has rights to variable returns from its involvement with the entity, and can affect those returns through its power.

Power is demonstrated through existing rights that provide the ability to direct relevant activities, which significantly affect an entity’s returns. Subsidiaries are consolidated from the date control commences until the date control ceases.

Group companies are consolidated on a line-by-line basis. Intra-group balances and transactions, including unrealized gains or losses, are eliminated on consolidation. The financial statements are prepared using uniform accounting policies across the Group.

Non-controlling interests represent the portion of the net profit or loss and net assets of subsidiaries that are not directly or indirectly owned or controlled by the Company. Refer to Note 2.25 for the list of subsidiaries and controlled trusts.

## 1.4 Use of estimates and judgments

The preparation of consolidated financial statements in conformity with Ind AS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies, reported amounts of assets and liabilities, disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities, and reported revenues and expenses.

The accounting policies requiring critical accounting estimates and complex or subjective judgments are disclosed in Note 1.5. Estimates, judgments, and assumptions may change from period to period, and actual results may differ from those estimates. Changes are reflected in the period in which they are made; material effects are disclosed in the notes to the consolidated financial statements.

## 1.5 Critical accounting estimates and judgments

### a. Revenue recognition

Customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is considered for recognition and measurement when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable.

The Group assesses the services promised in a contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying deliverables, determining whether the customer can benefit independently from them, and allocating the transaction price to distinct performance obligations involve significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven because services are discrete rather than repetitive, fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized using a percentage-of-completion method. The method depends on the contractual promises and the nature of the deliverables.

Other fixed-price contracts are accounted for using the percentage-of-completion method. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs. This input measure is used because efforts or costs have a direct relationship with productivity. Estimates of total efforts or costs are assessed throughout the contract period and updated using the latest available information.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Group assesses whether it controls the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer, considering factors such as responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other relevant factors.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded in the period when the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

### b. Income taxes

The Group’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. The Company also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is involved in determining income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, management considers whether some or all of the assets may not be realized. Ultimate realization depends on generating future taxable income during the periods when temporary differences become deductible. Management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies.

The amount of deferred income tax assets considered realizable could be reduced in the near term if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period are reduced. Refer to Note 2.17.

### c. Business combinations and intangible assets

Business combinations are accounted for under Ind AS 103, Business Combinations. The standard requires identifiable intangible assets and contingent consideration to be measured at fair value to determine the net fair value of the acquiree’s identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities.

External valuation experts conduct these valuations. Estimates are required to determine the value of contingent consideration, option arrangements, and intangible assets. Measurements are based on information available at the acquisition date and on expectations and assumptions considered reasonable by management. Refer to Notes 2.1 and 2.4.2.

### d. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Group’s asset base. Periodic depreciation is determined using estimates of an asset’s expected useful life and expected residual value at the end of its life.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. Refer to Note 2.2.

### e. Impairment of goodwill

Goodwill is tested for impairment annually and whenever there is an indication that the recoverable amount of a cash-generating unit (CGU) is less than its carrying amount.

For impairment testing, goodwill is allocated to the CGU or group of CGUs that benefits from the acquisition synergies and represents the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes.

The recoverable amount of CGUs is determined as the higher of value in use and fair value less costs to sell. Key assumptions in cash-flow projections include estimated long-term growth rates, weighted average cost of capital, and estimated operating margins. Refer to Note 2.4.1.
