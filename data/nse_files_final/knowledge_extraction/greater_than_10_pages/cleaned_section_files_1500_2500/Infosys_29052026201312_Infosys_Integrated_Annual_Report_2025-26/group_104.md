## Remaining performance obligation disclosure

The remaining performance obligation disclosure provides the aggregate transaction price yet to be recognized at the end of the reporting period and the expected timing of revenue recognition.

Applying the practical expedient under Ind AS 115, Infosys does not disclose remaining performance obligations for contracts where recognized revenue corresponds directly with the value delivered to the customer, typically time-and-material and unit-of-work-based contracts.

Remaining performance obligation estimates may change due to contract terminations, changes in contract scope, periodic revalidations, adjustments for revenue that has not materialized, and currency fluctuations.

As at March 31, 2026, the aggregate value of completely or partially unsatisfied performance obligations, excluding contracts meeting the criteria above, was ` 1,13,675 crore. Infosys expects to recognize approximately 50.1% within one year, 21.6% between one and two years, and the remainder thereafter.

As at March 31, 2025, the aggregate value of completely or partially unsatisfied performance obligations was ` 90,815 crore. Customers can generally terminate the contracts, typically subject to an enforceable termination penalty. Customers have generally not terminated contracts without cause.

## 2.19 Other income, net

### 2.19.1 Other income

#### Accounting policy

Other income primarily comprises interest income, dividend income, gains or losses on investments, and exchange gains or losses on forward and options contracts and the translation of foreign currency assets and liabilities.

Interest income is recognized using the effective interest method. Dividend income is recognized when the right to receive payment is established.

### 2.19.2 Foreign currency Accounting policy

#### Functional currency

The functional currency of Infosys is the Indian rupee. The financial statements are presented in Indian rupees, rounded off to crore. One crore equals ten million.

#### Transactions and translations

Foreign-currency-denominated monetary assets and liabilities are translated into the relevant functional currency at exchange rates in effect at the Balance Sheet date. Resulting gains or losses are recognized in the standalone Statement of Profit and Loss within exchange gains or losses on translation of assets and liabilities, net, except when deferred in Other Comprehensive Income as qualifying cash flow hedges.

Non-monetary foreign-currency assets and liabilities measured at fair value are translated at the exchange rate prevailing when fair value was determined. Those measured at historical cost are translated at the exchange rate prevailing on the transaction date. Related revenue and expenses are recognized using the same exchange rate.

Transaction gains or losses realized on settlement of foreign currency transactions are included in net profit for the period of settlement. Foreign-currency-denominated revenue, expenses, and cash-flow items are translated using the exchange rate in effect on the transaction date.

Other Comprehensive Income, net of taxes, includes translation differences on non-monetary financial assets measured at fair value at the reporting date, including equities classified as financial instruments measured at fair value through other comprehensive income (FVOCI).

#### Government grant

Government grants are recognized only when there is reasonable assurance that the attached conditions will be complied with and the grants will be received.

Grants related to assets are treated as deferred income and recognized in net profit in the Statement of Profit and Loss systematically over the useful life of the asset. Grants related to revenue are recognized systematically in net profit over the periods necessary to match them with the related costs.

## 2.20.1 Impact of Labour Codes

On November 21, 2025, the Government of India notified provisions of the Code on Wages, 2019; the Industrial Relations Code, 2020; the Code on Social Security, 2020; and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020. These Labour Codes consolidate 29 existing labour laws into a unified framework governing employee benefits during employment and post-employment.

The changes include a uniform definition of wages and enhanced leave benefits. Infosys assessed the financial implications, resulting in an increase of ` 1,146 crore in gratuity liability, defined benefit plan costs arising from past service cost relating to plan amendments, and compensated absences.

Because the impact of the new legislation was considered non-recurring, Infosys presented the incremental amount as “Impact of Labour Codes” under “Exceptional Item” in the standalone Statement of Profit and Loss for the year ended March 31, 2026. Infosys continues to monitor developments relating to the Labour Codes and will evaluate any effects on the measurement of employee benefit liabilities.

## 2.21 Employee benefits

### 2.21.1 Gratuity and pensions

Infosys provides gratuity through a defined benefit retirement plan covering eligible employees in India. The plan provides a lump-sum payment to vested employees upon retirement, death, incapacitation, or termination of employment, based on salary and tenure.

Infosys contributes gratuity liabilities to the Infosys Limited Employees’ Gratuity Fund Trust. Trustees administer the contributions, which are invested in a Life Insurance Corporation of India scheme as permitted by Indian law.

Infosys operates defined benefit pension plans in certain overseas jurisdictions in accordance with local laws. These plans are managed by third-party fund managers and provide periodic post-retirement payouts and/or lump-sum payments, along with death and disability benefits. Contributions are based on a percentage of salary that varies according to the employee’s age.

Liabilities for defined benefit plans are determined through actuarial valuations performed by an external actuary at each Balance Sheet date using the projected unit credit method. The plans expose Infosys to longevity risk, interest rate risk, and market risk.

Infosys recognizes the net obligation of a defined benefit plan as an asset or liability in the Balance Sheet. Re-measurement gains and losses on the net defined benefit liability or asset are recognized in Other Comprehensive Income and are not subsequently reclassified to profit or loss. The actual return on plan assets exceeding the yield calculated using the discount rate applied to the defined benefit obligation is recognized in Other Comprehensive Income. The effect of plan amendments is recognized in net profit in the Statement of Profit and Loss.
