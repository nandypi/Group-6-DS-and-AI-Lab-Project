## 3.  Other income and finance cost

Other income primarily includes income from investments; gains or losses on investments; foreign exchange gains or losses on forward and options contracts; foreign exchange gains or losses on translation of other assets and liabilities; and interest on income tax refunds.

Other income for fiscal 2026 includes pre-tax interest of ₹381 crore on income tax refunds arising from orders received under Sections 250 and 254 of the Income-tax Act, 1961. Other income for fiscal 2025 includes pre-tax interest of ₹327 crore on income tax refunds arising from orders received under Section 250 of the Income-tax Act, 1961.

The Company received dividends of ₹2,676 crore from subsidiaries during fiscal 2026 and ₹1,522 crore during fiscal 2025. These amounts are reflected in the Standalone Financial Statements. Other income also includes a profit of ₹165 crore on the sale of property, plant and equipment in fiscal 2026.

Interest income increased in fiscal 2026 compared with fiscal 2025, primarily due to an increase in the investible base. The Company uses foreign exchange forward and options contracts to hedge exposure to movements in foreign exchange rates. Finance cost relates to leases. Lease payments are discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease or, where that rate is not readily determinable, the incremental borrowing rates in the country of domicile of the leases.

Export revenues represented 96.9% of revenues, while domestic revenues represented 3.1%. Refer to the “Segmental profitability” section for further analysis of segment revenues.

## 4. Exceptional items

On November 21, 2025, the Government of India notified provisions of the Code on Wages, 2019; the Industrial Relations Code, 2020; the Code on Social Security, 2020; and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 (the “Labour Codes”). These codes consolidate 29 existing labour laws into a unified framework governing employee benefits during and after employment.

The Labour Codes introduced changes including a uniform definition of wages and enhanced leave-related benefits. The Group’s assessment of the financial implications resulted in increases in gratuity liability, defined benefit plan costs arising from past service cost, and compensated absences.

Accordingly, ₹1,146 crore in the Standalone Statement of Profit and Loss and ₹1,289 crore in the Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss were recognized for the year ended March 31, 2026. Because the impact was considered non-recurring, the incremental amount was presented as “Impact of Labour Codes” under “Exceptional Item.”

## 5. Provision for tax

The Company provided for tax liabilities in India and overseas.

The applicable Indian corporate statutory tax rate for the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025 was 25.17%. Section 115BAA, introduced by the Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019, allows a domestic company to exercise an option for a reduced corporate tax rate without claiming certain deductions, including the deduction for SEZ units under Section 10AA of the Income-tax Act, 1961. From fiscal 2025, the Company’s income-tax expense has been recognized by applying Section 115BAA without claiming the Section 10AA deduction for SEZ units.

| Income tax expense (₹ crore) | 9,546 | 9,873 | 10,521 | 10,858 |

The effective tax rate is influenced by differential tax rates, non-deductible expenses, exempt non-operating income, overseas taxes, tax reversals and provisions relating to prior periods, changes to tax regulations, and other tax deductions.

The effective tax rate in fiscal 2026 was lower primarily because of the reversal of tax provisions following receipt of orders giving effect to prior assessment years. This reduced the effective tax rate by 2.2%.

During fiscal 2026, the Company received orders under Sections 250 and 254 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, from the Indian income-tax authorities for assessment years 2013-14 and 2017-18 to 2021-22. The orders confirmed the Company’s position on the tax treatment of certain matters. As a result:

- Pre-tax interest income of ₹381 crore was recognized.
- Income-tax provisions aggregating ₹869 crore were reversed, with a corresponding credit to the Statement of Profit and Loss.
- Contingent liabilities were reduced by ₹86 crore following resolution of the disputes.

During fiscal 2025, the Company received orders under Section 250 of the Income-tax Act, 1961, from the Indian income-tax authorities for assessment years 2016-17 and 2019-20. The orders confirmed the Company’s position on the tax treatment of certain matters. As a result:

- Pre-tax interest income of ₹327 crore was recognized.
- Income-tax provisions aggregating ₹183 crore were reversed, with a corresponding credit to the Statement of Profit and Loss.
- Contingent liabilities were reduced by ₹1,068 crore following resolution of the disputes.

## 6.  Segmental profitability

The Company’s operations predominantly relate to providing end-to-end business solutions to enable clients to enhance business performance.

The Company’s business segments primarily comprise enterprises in Financial Services and Insurance; Manufacturing; Retail, Consumer Packaged Goods and Logistics; Energy, Utilities, Resources and Services; Communication, Telecom OEM and Media; Hi-Tech; Life Sciences and Healthcare; and all other segments.

“All other segments” represents operating segments comprising businesses in India, Japan, China, Infosys Public Services, and identified enterprises in public services. Segment information is discussed in Note 2.26 to the Consolidated Financial Statement in this Integrated Annual Report.

## 7. Liquidity

The Company’s principal sources of liquidity are cash and cash equivalents and cash flow generated from operations. The Company had no outstanding borrowings and stated that its working capital was sufficient for its business operations.

Growth was financed largely through cash generated from operations.

Operating cash flows decreased marginally in fiscal 2026 compared with fiscal 2025, primarily because fiscal 2025 included higher income-tax refunds and fiscal 2026 had higher working-capital outflows. This was partially offset by an increase in net profits adjusted for non-cash items.

Consolidated cash and investments of ₹43,075 crore comprise cash and cash equivalents and current and noncurrent investments, excluding investments in equity and preference shares and others.
