## Capital Allocation Policy

Effective from financial year 2025, the Company expects to continue its policy of returning approximately 85% of free cash flow cumulatively over a five-year period through a combination of semi-annual dividends, share buybacks, and/or special dividends, subject to applicable laws and requisite approvals. Under this policy, the Company expects to progressively increase its annual dividend per share, excluding any special dividend.

Free cash flow is defined as net cash provided by operating activities less capital expenditure, as reported in the Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows prepared under IFRS. Dividends and buybacks include applicable taxes.

On September 11, 2025, the Board approved a proposal to buy back fully paid-up equity shares with a face value of ` 5 each, aggregating up to ` 18,000 crore. Shareholders approved the proposal through postal ballot on November 4, 2025.

The buyback involved purchasing 10,00,00,000 equity shares, representing approximately 2.41% of the Company’s standalone paid-up equity share capital as of June 30, 2025, at ` 1,800 per share. The offer was made to eligible shareholders as of the November 14, 2025 record date on a proportionate basis through the tender offer route, including shareholders who became eligible through ADS cancellation.

The tender period was open from November 20 to November 26, 2025. The buyback was completed on December 4, 2025, with all shares extinguished. The cash outflow was ` 18,000 crore, excluding transaction costs, and was funded from free reserves, including securities premium, in accordance with Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013.

As required under Section 69 of the Companies Act, 2013, the Company created a Capital Redemption Reserve of ` 50 crore as of March 31, 2026, equivalent to the nominal value of the shares bought back, by appropriating funds from general reserves.

During the year ended March 31, 2026, the Company paid an interim dividend of ` 23 per equity share and announced a final dividend of ` 25 per share, subject to shareholder approval at the ensuing Annual General Meeting. Including the final dividend declared, the Company stated that it had returned approximately ` 55,523 crore, equivalent to 82.1% of free cash flow for fiscal 2025 and 2026, through dividends and buybacks.

The Capital Allocation Policy is available at the [Infosys Capital Allocation Policy](https://www.infosys.com/investors/corporate-governance/documents/capital-allocation-policy.pdf).

## Liquidity

The principal sources of liquidity are cash and cash equivalents, investments, and cash flow generated from operations. The Company stated that it remained debt-free and maintained sufficient cash for strategic and operational requirements.

As of March 31, 2026, working capital was ` 40,794 crore on a standalone basis and ` 51,167 crore on a consolidated basis.

Cash and investments were ` 28,662 crore on a standalone basis and ` 43,075 crore on a consolidated basis as of March 31, 2026, compared with ` 35,916 crore and ` 47,549 crore, respectively, as of March 31, 2025.

Cash and investments included deposits with banks having high credit ratings from international and domestic credit rating agencies. Liquid assets also included debt mutual fund units, tax-free bonds, Certificates of Deposit (CDs), Commercial Paper (CP), quoted bonds and securities issued by government and quasi-government organizations, and non-convertible debentures.

CDs and CPs represented marketable securities of banks, Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs), and eligible financial institutions for specified periods. Government securities were described as highly liquid and marketable instruments backed by the Government and carrying sovereign credit. Non-convertible debentures were issued by government-aided institutions and financial institutions with high credit ratings.

Investment counterparty risk was assessed using criteria including Tier-I capital, capital adequacy ratio, credit rating, profitability, Non-Performing Asset (NPA) levels, and deposit base of banks and financial institutions.

Details of these investments are disclosed under the “non-current and current investments” sections of the standalone and consolidated financial statements in the Integrated Annual Report.
