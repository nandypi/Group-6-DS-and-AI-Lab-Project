## Overview and notes to the Consolidated Financial Statements

## 1. Overview

## 1.1 Company overview

Infosys Limited ("the Company" or Infosys) provides AI-first business consulting and technology services to enable organizations to unlock AI value at scale. The Group’s strategy is to help clients ideate, plan, and execute their journey toward an AI-first future through its AI-first value framework, domain expertise, and AI-native partner ecosystem.

Infosys, together with its subsidiaries and controlled trusts, is referred to as "the Group."

The Company is a public limited company incorporated and domiciled in India. Its primary listings are on BSE Ltd. and the National Stock Exchange of India Limited. Its American Depositary Shares (ADS), representing equity shares, are listed on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE).

The Group’s consolidated financial statements were approved for issue by the Company’s Board of Directors on April 23, 2026.

## 1.2 Basis of preparation of financial statements

The consolidated financial statements are prepared in accordance with the Companies Act, 2013, guidelines issued by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), and Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS). They use the historical cost convention on an accrual basis, except for certain financial instruments measured at fair value and defined benefit liabilities or assets recognized at the present value of the defined benefit obligation less the fair value of plan assets.

Ind AS is prescribed under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013, read with Rule 3 of the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015, and subsequent amendment rules.

Accounting policies have been applied consistently, except when a newly issued accounting standard is initially adopted or a revision to an existing standard requires a change in accounting policy. Material accounting policy information is discussed in the respective notes to the audited consolidated financial statements.

Quarter and year-to-date figures are rounded to the nearest digits. As a result, quarter figures may not always add up to the year-to-date figures reported in the statement.

## 1.3 Basis of consolidation

Infosys consolidates entities that it owns or controls. The consolidated financial statements comprise the financial statements of the Company, its controlled trusts, and its subsidiaries.

Control exists when the parent has power over an entity, is exposed or has rights to variable returns from its involvement with the entity, and can use its power to affect those returns. Power is demonstrated through existing rights that provide the ability to direct relevant activities that significantly affect the entity’s returns. Subsidiaries are consolidated from the date control commences until the date control ceases.

Group companies’ financial statements are consolidated on a line-by-line basis. Intra-group balances and transactions, including unrealized gains or losses, are eliminated on consolidation. Uniform accounting policies are applied across the Group.

Non-controlling interests represent the portion of the net profit or loss and net assets of subsidiaries that is not directly or indirectly owned or controlled by the Company.

Refer to Note 2.25 for the list of subsidiaries and controlled trusts of the Company.

## 1.4 Use of estimates and judgments

Preparing the consolidated financial statements in conformity with Ind AS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies, reported amounts of assets and liabilities, disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities, and reported revenues and expenses.

The application of accounting policies involving critical accounting estimates and complex or subjective judgments is disclosed in Note 1.5. Estimates, judgments, and assumptions may change as circumstances change. Actual results could differ from these estimates. Changes are reflected in the period in which they are made, and material effects are disclosed in the notes to the consolidated financial statements.

## 1.5 Critical accounting estimates and judgments

## a. Revenue recognition

Customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is considered for recognition and measurement when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to their respective obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable.

The Group assesses the services promised in a contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying distinct performance obligations, determining deliverables and the customer’s ability to benefit independently from them, and allocating the transaction price to those obligations involve significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and the Group’s costs to fulfill the contract are uneven because the services are discrete rather than repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method. The method selected depends on the contractual promises and the nature of the deliverables.

The Group uses the percentage-of-completion method for other fixed-price contracts. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs. Efforts or costs incurred are used as a measure of progress because they have a direct relationship with productivity. Estimates of total efforts or costs are reviewed throughout the contract period and updated using the latest available information.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Group first assesses whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include primary responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other indicators of control.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded in the period in which the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs required to complete the contract.

## b. Income taxes

The Group’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States, although the Company also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is involved in determining income tax provisions, including the amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, management considers whether some or all of the assets will be realized. Ultimate realization depends on generating future taxable income during the periods in which temporary differences become deductible. Management considers the scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies.

Based on historical taxable income and projections for future taxable income over the periods in which deferred tax assets are deductible, management believes the Group will realize the benefits of those deductible differences. The amount considered realizable could be reduced in the near term if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. Refer to Note 2.17.

## c. Business combinations and intangible assets

Business combinations are accounted for using Ind AS 103, Business Combinations. The standard requires the Group to fair-value identifiable intangible assets and contingent consideration to determine the net fair value of the acquiree’s identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities.

External valuation experts conduct these valuations. Estimates are required to determine the value of contingent consideration, option arrangements, and intangible assets. These measurements are based on information available at the acquisition date and on expectations and assumptions considered reasonable by management. Refer to Notes 2.1 and 2.4.2.

## d. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Group’s asset base. Periodic depreciation is determined using estimates of an asset’s expected useful life and residual value at the end of its life.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. Refer to Note 2.2.

## e. Impairment of goodwill

Goodwill is tested for impairment annually and whenever there is an indication that the recoverable amount of a cash-generating unit (CGU) is less than its carrying amount.

For impairment testing, goodwill is allocated to the CGU or group of CGUs that benefits from the acquisition synergies and represents the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes.

The recoverable amount of CGUs is determined as the higher of value in use and fair value less costs to sell. Key assumptions in cash-flow projections include estimated long-term growth rates, weighted average cost of capital, and estimated operating margins. Refer to Note 2.4.1.
