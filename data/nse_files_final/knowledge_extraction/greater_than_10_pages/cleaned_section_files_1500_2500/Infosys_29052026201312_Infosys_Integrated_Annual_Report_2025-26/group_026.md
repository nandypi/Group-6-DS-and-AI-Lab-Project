## Opinion

Based on an examination of relevant records, information and explanations provided by management, and management representations, the company complied with the conditions of corporate governance stipulated in regulations 17 to 27 and clauses (b) to (i) of regulation 46(2), and paragraphs C and D of Schedule V of the Listing Regulations, for the year ended March 31, 2026.

This compliance statement is not an assurance of the company’s future viability or of the efficiency or effectiveness with which management conducted its affairs.

## Annexure 5 - Secretarial audit report for the financial year ended March 31, 2026 Form No. MR-3

The secretarial audit covered the period from April 1, 2025 to March 31, 2026. Based on verification of the company’s books, papers, minute books, forms, returns, and other records, and information provided during the audit, the auditors reported compliance with the following:

- Companies Act, 2013 and the rules made under it.
- Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 and the rules made under it.
- Depositories Act, 1996 and related regulations and bye-laws.
- Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 and related rules and regulations, to the extent applicable to foreign direct investment and overseas direct investment. External commercial borrowings were not applicable during the audit period.
- SEBI regulations and guidelines, including:
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011.
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015.
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018. Not applicable during the audit period.
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Share Based Employee Benefits and Sweat Equity) Regulations, 2021.
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Issue and Listing of Non-Convertible Securities) Regulations, 2021. Not applicable during the audit period.
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents) Regulations, 1993 and 2025, regarding the Companies Act and dealing with clients.
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Delisting of Equity Shares) Regulations, 2021. Not applicable during the audit period.
  - Securities and Exchange Board of India (Buy-back of Securities) Regulations, 2018.
- Secretarial Standards issued by The Institute of Company Secretaries of India.
- Securities and Exchange Board of India (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015, including amendments.
- Special Economic Zones Act, 2005 and the rules made under it.
- Software Technology Parks of India Rules and Regulations.

The auditors reported that the company had proper Board processes and compliance mechanisms in place and had complied with the applicable Acts, rules, regulations, guidelines and standards during the audit period.

## We further report that

- The Board of Directors was duly constituted with an appropriate balance of Executive, Non-Executive and Independent Directors. No changes took place in the Board composition during the audit period.
- Board meeting notices were provided to all directors, and agendas and detailed agenda notes were generally sent at least seven days in advance. Two meetings were convened at shorter notice, with the necessary approvals obtained under applicable provisions.
- A system existed for obtaining further information and clarifications on agenda items before meetings.
- All decisions at Board and Committee meetings were carried out unanimously and recorded in the relevant minutes.
- The company maintained systems and processes commensurate with its size and operations to monitor and ensure compliance with applicable laws, rules, regulations and guidelines.
- The company issued and allotted 8,74,856 equity shares of face value ₹5 each under the 2015 Stock Incentive Compensation Plan.
- The company issued and allotted 14,53,412 equity shares of face value ₹5 each under the Infosys Expanded Stock Ownership Program 2019.
- The company bought back 10,00,00,000 fully paid-up equity shares of face value ₹5 each for an aggregate consideration of ₹18,000 crore, at ₹1,800 per share.

The secretarial audit report states that maintenance of secretarial records and compliance with applicable laws are management’s responsibility. The audit was conducted on a test basis and did not include verification of the correctness and appropriateness of the company’s financial records and books of account. Management representations were obtained where required.

The report is not an assurance of the company’s future viability or of the efficacy or effectiveness with which management conducted its affairs.

## Annexure 6 - Annual report on CSR activities

### 1. Brief outline on CSR Policy of the Company

Infosys’s CSR policy focuses on sustainable development of society and the environment. Its CSR activities include:

- Education and skill development to enable sustained livelihoods.
- Healthcare, including preventive health.
- Environmental sustainability and ecological balance.
- Gender equality and empowerment of women.
- Promotion of national heritage, art and culture.
- Rural development.
- Disaster relief and rehabilitation.

Infosys undertakes CSR initiatives directly and through Infosys Foundation, which was established in 1996.

## CSR activities

Key CSR activities during the year included:

- Continued education-to-employment initiatives focused on skilling underserved youth through strategic partnerships and livelihood training programs.
- Scholarships for students pursuing undergraduate studies in engineering and other STEM fields, including nursing, pharmacy, biochemistry and biotechnology, at colleges accredited under the National Institutional Ranking Framework.
- The Infosys Springboard digital literacy program, providing students with core digital and life skills through a technology platform.
- Initiation of the Universal Cornea Care Mission at the L.V. Prasad Eye Institute, including model optimization, operationalization and validation, and development of a strategy for replication across India.
- Construction of a mother and child healthcare center at the Government Hospital, Kunigal.
- Tree plantation initiatives intended to promote environmental sustainability, maintain ecological balance and enhance farmer livelihoods and income.
- Co-implementation of the Gear for Gold program to build sustainable, inclusive and high-performing sports academies through support for coaching, infrastructure, sports science, governance and administration.
- Support for the operationalization of an advanced mother and child center at PGIMER Chandigarh through provision of medical equipment.
