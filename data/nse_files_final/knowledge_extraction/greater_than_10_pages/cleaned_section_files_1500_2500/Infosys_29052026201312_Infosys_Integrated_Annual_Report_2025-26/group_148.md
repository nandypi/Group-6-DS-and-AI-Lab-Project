For domestic defined benefit plans in India, the yield on government bonds is used as the discount rate because a market for high-quality corporate bonds is not developed. For most overseas defined benefit plans, the government bond rate adjusted for corporate spreads is used.

The average rate of increase in compensation levels is determined using the Company’s past compensation revision trends, inflation in the respective markets, and management’s estimate of future salary increases. The attrition rate is based on management’s estimate, considering the Company’s long-term employee turnover trend. Employee tenure reflects the long-term trend in employees’ average remaining service life and the average estimated term of the post-employment benefit obligation.

For domestic plans in India, assumptions regarding future mortality experience are based on published statistics from the Life Insurance Corporation of India. For overseas plans, mortality assumptions consider the latest life-expectancy statistics, plan experience, and other relevant data. The Group assesses these assumptions against its projected long-term growth plans and prevalent industry standards.

The Company contributes all ascertained gratuity liabilities to the Infosys Limited Employees’ Gratuity Fund Trust. Infosys BPM and EdgeVerve contribute to the Infosys BPM Employees’ Gratuity Fund Trust and EdgeVerve Systems Limited Employees Gratuity Fund Trust, respectively. As of March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025, contributions for gratuity were invested in a Life Insurance Corporation of India scheme as permitted by Indian law.

Overseas defined benefit plan assets were primarily invested in insurer-managed funds. Asset allocation is determined according to investment criteria prescribed under regulations applicable to pension funds and insurer managers. The insurers’ investments are diversified and provide guaranteed interest-rate arrangements.

These defined benefit plans expose the Group to the following actuarial risks:

- **Interest rate risk:** The defined benefit obligation is generally calculated using a discount rate based on government bond yields, or, in certain overseas jurisdictions, government bond yields adjusted for a corporate spread. A decline in bond yields would generally increase the defined benefit obligation.
- **Life expectancy and investment risk:** The pension fund offers a choice between a lifelong pension and a cash lump sum at retirement. The fund has defined rates for converting the lump sum to a pension, creating a risk that members live longer than implied by those rates or that pension assets do not achieve the investment return implied by the rates.
- **Asset volatility:** A proportion of pension fund assets is invested in equities. Although equities are expected to outperform corporate bonds over the long term, they create short-term volatility and risk. The pension fund insurer’s board is responsible for the investment strategy and equity allocation.

Sensitivity to significant actuarial assumptions is computed by varying one actuarial assumption used to value the defined benefit obligation while keeping all other actuarial assumptions constant. In practice, this scenario is not probable, and changes in some assumptions may be correlated.

The Group expects to contribute ` 540 crore to gratuity and ` 66 crore to pension during fiscal 2027.
