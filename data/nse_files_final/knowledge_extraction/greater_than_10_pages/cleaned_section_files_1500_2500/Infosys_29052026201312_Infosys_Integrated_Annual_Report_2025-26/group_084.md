## Overview and Notes to the Standalone Financial Statements

## 1. Overview

## 1.1 Company overview

Infosys Limited ("the Company" or Infosys) provides AI-first business consulting and technology services to enable organizations to unlock AI value at scale. The Company has over four decades of experience managing the systems and workings of global enterprises.

Infosys’s strategy is to serve as a navigator for clients as they ideate, plan, and execute their journey toward an AI-first future, using its AI-first value framework, domain expertise, and AI-native partner ecosystem.

The Company is a public limited company incorporated and domiciled in India. Its primary listings are on BSE Ltd. and the National Stock Exchange of India Limited. Its American Depositary Shares (ADSs), representing equity shares, are listed on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE).

The standalone financial statements were approved for issue by the Company’s Board of Directors on April 23, 2026.

## 1.2 Basis of preparation of financial statements

The standalone financial statements are prepared in accordance with the Companies Act, 2013, guidelines issued by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), and Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS).

The financial statements are prepared under the historical cost convention on an accrual basis, except for certain financial instruments measured at fair value and defined benefit liabilities or assets recognized at the present value of the defined benefit obligation less the fair value of plan assets.

Accounting policies have been applied consistently, except when a newly issued accounting standard is initially adopted or a revision to an existing accounting standard requires a change in accounting policy. Material accounting policy information is discussed in the respective notes.

Year-end figures are taken from the source and rounded to the nearest digits. Consequently, figures reported for previous quarters may not always add up to the year-end figures reported in the statement.

## 1.3 Use of estimates and judgments

Preparing financial statements in conformity with Ind AS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies and the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, contingent assets and liabilities, revenues, and expenses.

Critical accounting estimates and judgments are disclosed in Note 1.4. They may change from period to period, and actual results could differ from those estimates. Changes in estimates and judgments are reflected in the period in which they are made; material effects are disclosed in the notes to the standalone financial statements.

## 1.4 Critical accounting estimates and judgments

### a. Revenue recognition

Customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue recognition and measurement begin when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to their respective obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable.

The Company identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to them. This requires judgment regarding the deliverables and the customer’s ability to benefit independently from them.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and the Company’s costs to fulfill the contract are uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method.

For other fixed-price contracts, the Company uses the percentage-of-completion method. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs, because there is a direct relationship between inputs and productivity. Estimated total efforts or costs are reassessed throughout the contract period.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Company acts as an agent and gross when it acts as principal. The Company assesses control by considering responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.

### b. Income taxes

The Company’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. It also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is required to determine income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies. The amount considered realizable could be reduced if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. (Refer to Note 2.17.)

### c. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Company’s asset base. Periodic depreciation depends on estimates of expected useful lives and residual values.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. These estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. (Refer to Note 2.1.)

## 2. Notes to the Standalone Financial Statements

## 2.1 Property, plant and equipment

## Accounting Policy

Property, plant and equipment are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment, if any. Costs directly attributable to acquisition are capitalized until the assets are ready for intended use. Depreciation is calculated using the straight-line method over estimated useful lives.

The estimated useful lives of assets are as follows:

| Asset category | Estimated useful life |
|---|---|
| Building (1) | 22–25 years |
| Plant and machinery (1) | 5 years |
| Office equipment | 5 years |
| Computer equipment (1) | 3–5 years |
| Furniture and fixtures (1) | 5 years |
| Vehicles (1) | 5 years |
| Leasehold improvements | Lower of the asset’s useful life or the lease term |

(1) Based on technical evaluation, management believes these useful lives best represent the period over which the assets are expected to be used. Accordingly, the useful lives for these assets differ from those prescribed under Part C of Schedule II of the Companies Act, 2013.

Depreciation methods, useful lives, and residual values are reviewed periodically, including at each financial year-end.

Advances paid toward the acquisition of property, plant and equipment that remain outstanding at each Balance Sheet date are classified as capital advances under other non-current assets. The cost of assets not ready for use by that date is disclosed as capital work-in-progress.

Subsequent expenditure on property, plant and equipment is capitalized only when future economic benefits are probable and the cost can be measured reliably. The cost and related accumulated depreciation are removed from the financial statements when an asset is sold or retired.
