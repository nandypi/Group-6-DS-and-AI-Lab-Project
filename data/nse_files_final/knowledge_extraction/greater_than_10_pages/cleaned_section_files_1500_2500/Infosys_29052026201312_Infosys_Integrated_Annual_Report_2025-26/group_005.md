## Performance overview

- Free cash flow: ₹33,097 crore.
- Free cash flow conversion: 112.3% of net profit.
- Adjusted operating margin: 21.0%.
- Consolidated cash and investments: ₹43,075 crore.
- Return on equity: 31.6%, an improvement of 2.6% over the last fiscal year.
- Large deal total contract value: US$14.9 billion, with 55% net new business.
- Clients with more than US$50 million in revenue: 88, an increase of 3 clients year over year.
- Share buyback completed: ₹18,000 crore at ₹1,800 per share.

### ESG and workforce metrics

- 84% of office space was certified with the highest green building rating.
- 81.8% of India operations were powered by renewable energy.
- Carbon offset programs benefited more than 300,000 rural families.
- Infosys was carbon neutral for 7 consecutive years across Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions.
- Approximately 14 million saplings were planted through the agroforestry program.
- 84% of the workforce was enabled on AI.
- Infosys was recognized as one of the 2026 World's Most Ethical Companies by Ethisphere for the sixth consecutive year.
- Infosys was recognized as a Global Top Employer 2026 for the sixth consecutive year.

## Chairman's message

### The age of orchestration

Nandan M. Nilekani, Chairman, stated that the adoption of generative AI is reshaping software development and enterprise work. Infosys intends to adopt coding tools, improve productivity, and continue work across the software development life cycle, including solution testing and validation, architecture design for speed, scale, and resilience, cybersecurity, data protection, and governance.

Continuous learning and talent transformation have been core to Infosys for several decades. The company plans to prepare its talent for the AI era and redistribute productivity gains toward new accounts and offerings.

AI is increasing customer urgency around legacy modernization, eliminating data silos, addressing AI-identified cybersecurity vulnerabilities, and reducing technical debt. The preference to build rather than buy software is identified as another source of opportunity.

The integration of models and agents with existing deterministic enterprise systems is described as a significant opportunity. Existing transaction systems remain relevant because of their scalability and reliability, while newer capabilities can provide intelligence, simplified user experiences, and automation.

Supply chains are continuing to change, and AI regulation is being developed in real time across multiple jurisdictions. Enterprises often stall when moving from AI pilots to scaled deployment because of legacy architecture, organizational inertia, and changing regulatory requirements. Closing this deployment gap requires redesigning business processes, customer journeys, and operating models rather than simply adding AI to existing structures.

Technology built for an earlier era has created substantial technical debt. These systems are increasingly expensive to maintain and can constrain the agility required for AI adoption. Enterprise AI outcomes depend on organizational readiness, data integrity, governance, and the pace of decision-making.

The World Economic Forum projects that by 2030, 92 million jobs will be displaced and 170 million created, representing a net gain of 78 million jobs. Capturing this gain requires attention to affected people, desired outcomes, and the interaction between technology and human judgment.

Enterprise AI modernization is complicated by differences in each company's legacy systems, data, and undocumented dependencies. Brownfield modernization is described as more difficult than greenfield development.

The future enterprise will require firms with long-term client relationships, a track record of execution, and the ability to combine legacy systems with new AI capabilities. Trusted partners can provide optionality among AI tools, help orchestrate complex technology environments, preserve client control over context, and commit to business outcomes.
