## Salient features of our Enterprise Risk Management program

Our ERM program adopts methods to identify risks, evaluate potential impact, and promote risk awareness across the organization.

## Highlights of fiscal 2026

During fiscal 2026, risk management initiatives focused on service delivery, transparent stakeholder communication, social responsibility, employee safety and health, and strengthening risk culture.

The Risk Office assessed, monitored, and reported on risks related to geopolitical scenarios; economic uncertainty; inflation; artificial intelligence, technology disruption, and innovation; talent availability; cybersecurity; data protection and privacy; ESG; contractual liabilities; and complex and evolving regulatory requirements.

The key and emerging risks and the Company’s mitigation approaches are described below.

| Key / Emerging risks | Impact on Company | Mitigation / Opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| Geopolitical, macroeconomic or health events | Revenues are concentrated in a few geographies and client industries, with a large proportion dependent on a limited number of clients. Unfavorable geopolitical, economic, or health events may cause currency volatility and reduce client spending on technology products and services, affecting demand, growth, and profitability. Government policy changes, including tariffs and trade restrictions, may be difficult to predict and assess. Impacted capitals: Financial, Social & Relationship, and Human. | Broad-based growth to reduce concentration in any single region, client, or industry; monitoring of geopolitical and macroeconomic scenarios; additional controls to improve operational agility; currency hedging; business continuity and resilience measures in affected regions; hybrid working; diversified supply chains; and acquisitions to expand into new areas or regions. Opportunity: clients are seeking cost-takeout projects and vendor consolidation. |

## Risk management report

| Key / Emerging risks | Impact on Company | Mitigation / Opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| Artificial Intelligence (AI) risks | Failure to transform existing services using AI or scale new AI-first offerings could affect win rates, market share, revenue, and profits. Impacted capitals: Financial and Intellectual. | Differentiation through innovation and AI-enabled industry solutions; continued investment in identifying, incubating, developing, and launching AI-first offerings; proactive operational risk mitigation; an AI talent transformation strategy; and development of deep AI engineering expertise. |
| Global Capability Centers (GCCs) risks | Customers may insource services or establish or expand GCCs, reducing Infosys’ addressable market share and affecting growth and profitability. Impacted capitals: Financial and Intellectual. | Collaboration and co-creation for GCC requirements; a GCC practice supporting assisted GCC build, build-operate-transfer, captive carveouts, and other engagement models; and an AI-first model to transform GCCs into innovation hubs. |
| Technology disruption and innovation | Failure to identify, invest in, incubate, and operationalize services aligned with changes in AI, client preferences, and market expectations could disrupt Infosys’ value proposition and reduce customer relevance, revenue, and profitability. The speed and nature of technological change make trends difficult to predict. Impacted capitals: Financial, Human, and Intellectual. | An executive-led innovation council; joint innovation showcases with clients, industry forums, alliances, partners, and academia; investments in research and development; reskilling in newer technologies and methodologies; large-deal investments to improve innovation, productivity, and industry expertise; and alliances or partnerships with emerging technology firms. Opportunity: identify, develop, and deploy offerings using next-generation technologies. |
| Talent supply constraints | As of March 31, 2026, Infosys had 3,28,594 employees globally. The Company depends on skilled technology, AI, and management talent. Difficulty attracting, hiring, engaging, motivating, reskilling, and retaining talent; managing leadership succession; reshaping the AI-driven talent model; protecting human rights; or evolving the hybrid work model could affect reputation, project staffing, execution of large and complex programs, and cost optimization. Impacted capitals: Financial, Human, Intellectual, and Manufactured. | Recruitment and onboarding improvements; employee engagement and support initiatives; retention and recognition for key and tenured employees; career and leadership development programs; a hybrid operating model balancing client requirements, employee preferences, legal requirements, and information security risks; redesigned career architecture; and a future-ready workforce supported by global learning infrastructure. |
| Cybersecurity | Cyber threats evolve rapidly. Failure to adapt threat detection and prevention measures could allow attacks to breach information networks or solutions and product offerings. Failure to protect sensitive and confidential stakeholder information in accordance with applicable laws and contractual obligations could adversely affect operations and client satisfaction and result in significant regulatory penalties. Impacted capitals: Financial, Human, Intellectual, and Manufactured. | A cybersecurity strategy, framework, processes, policies, and controls; executive and Board oversight; continued investment in cybersecurity technologies; collaboration with cyber-intelligence and forensic consultants; periodic tabletop exercises; an up-to-date cybersecurity crisis plan; cybersecurity awareness training with consequence management; security-by-design training; encryption; data backup and recovery; and a Cyber Risk Assessment Framework aligned with ISO 31000, ISO 27001, and ISO 27005. Opportunity: provide cybersecurity services to customers. |
| Data protection and privacy | Failure to protect personal and sensitive stakeholder information in accordance with applicable laws could affect operations or result in significant regulatory penalties. Impacted capitals: Financial, Human, and Intellectual. | A data privacy framework, policies, processes, and controls; employee training on privacy by design and data protection and privacy awareness, including geographic nuances; executive and Board oversight; and continuous monitoring of the regulatory landscape. |
| Cost inflation / Inability to improve margin | Failure to operate effectively with sustainable cost levers could adversely affect long-term profitability. Impacted capital: Financial. | Effective operations with sustainable cost-optimization and margin-improvement levers, together with automation and a planned capital expenditure program focused on technology adoption and efficiency. |
| Environment, Social, Governance (ESG) ambition | Reputation, access to capital, and longer-term financial stability could be affected if Infosys does not meet its stated ESG 2030 Vision goals. Climate change risks are increasingly reflected as strategic, physical, transitional, market, and compliance risks. Inadequate management could affect reputation and profitability. ESG expectations may continue to change because of stakeholder expectations and regulatory requirements. | ESG 2030 ambitions and an execution roadmap with dedicated owners; Board-level oversight through a dedicated ESG Committee; periodic risk assessments; alignment of ESG ambitions with strategy; and regular regulatory monitoring. Opportunity: provide climate change-related solutions and services to customers. For more details, see the [Infosys ESG Vision 2030](https://www.infosys.com/about/esg/esg-vision-2030.html). |
