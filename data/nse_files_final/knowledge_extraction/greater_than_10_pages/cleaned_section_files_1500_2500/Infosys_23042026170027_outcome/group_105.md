## Credit risk

Credit risk refers to the risk of default by a counterparty resulting in financial loss. The maximum exposure to credit risk at the reporting date was primarily:

- Trade receivables: ₹30,337 crore at March 31, 2026, compared with ₹26,413 crore at March 31, 2025.
- Unbilled revenue: ₹13,706 crore at March 31, 2026, compared with ₹11,988 crore at March 31, 2025.

Trade receivables and unbilled revenue are typically unsecured and are derived from customers primarily located in the United States of America and Europe.

The Company manages credit risk through credit approvals, credit limits, and continuous monitoring of customer creditworthiness. It uses the expected credit loss model to assess allowances and a provision matrix to calculate expected credit loss allowances for trade receivables and unbilled revenue. The matrix considers available credit reports and other relevant credit information.

Credit risk is influenced mainly by the individual characteristics of customers and the concentration of exposure among the largest customers. Customer exposure is diversified, with no single customer contributing more than 10% of outstanding trade receivables and unbilled revenue.

Credit risk on cash and cash equivalents is limited because the Company generally invests in deposits with highly rated banks. Ratings are monitored periodically, using the latest available credit ratings as at the date of approval of the financial statements.

The Company’s investments primarily include mutual fund units, target maturity fund units, tax-free bonds, certificates of deposit, commercial paper, treasury bills, government securities, non-convertible debentures, and quoted bonds issued by government and quasi-government organizations.

The Company considers counterparty risk using criteria including Tier I Capital, Capital Adequacy Ratio, credit rating, profitability, non-performing asset levels, and deposit base of banks and financial institutions. These risks are monitored regularly under the Group’s risk management program.

## 2.12 EQUITY

## Ordinary Shares

Ordinary shares are classified as equity share capital. Incremental costs directly attributable to issuing new ordinary shares and share options, and to buybacks, are recognized as a deduction from equity, net of related tax effects.

## Capital redemption reserve

In accordance with Section 69 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013, the Company creates a capital redemption reserve equal to the nominal value of shares bought back, appropriated from general reserve or retained earnings.

## Retained earnings

Retained earnings represent the Company’s accumulated earnings.

## Securities premium

Amounts received in excess of the par value of equity shares are classified as securities premium. The securities premium account has been used for bonus issues and share buybacks.

## Share options outstanding account

The share options outstanding account records the fair value of equity-settled share-based payment transactions with employees. Amounts recorded in this account are transferred to securities premium when stock options are exercised and to general reserve when stock options are not exercised by employees.

## Special Economic Zone Re-investment reserve

The Special Economic Zone Re-investment reserve was created from the profits of an eligible SEZ unit under Section 10AA(1)(ii) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The reserve is to be used for acquiring new plant and machinery for the Company’s business in accordance with Section 10AA(2) of the Income Tax Act, 1961.
