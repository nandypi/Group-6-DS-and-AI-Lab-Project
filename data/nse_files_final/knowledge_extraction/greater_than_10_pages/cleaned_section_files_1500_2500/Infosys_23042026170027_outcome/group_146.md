## Deloitte Haskins &amp; Sells LLP

### Key Audit Matters

#### Revenue recognition — Fixed-price contracts using the percentage-of-completion method

The recognition of maintenance revenue requires judgment based on the promises in the contract and the nature of the deliverables. Certain customer contracts require management to determine:

1. The distinct performance obligations.
2. Whether the Group is acting as a principal or an agent.
3. Whether fixed-price maintenance revenue should be recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method.

Revenue recognition involving these judgments was identified as a key audit matter and required a higher extent of audit effort. Refer to Notes 1.5 and 2.18 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.

#### Estimation of total efforts or costs to complete fixed-price contracts

The percentage-of-completion method requires the Group to determine actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of the estimated total efforts or costs required to complete the contract. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because there is a direct relationship between input and productivity.

The estimated total efforts or costs are assessed throughout the contract period to reflect changes based on the latest available information. Provisions for estimated losses on uncompleted contracts are recorded in the period in which those losses become probable, based on the estimated efforts or costs required to complete the contract.

The estimation of total efforts or costs to complete fixed-price contracts was identified as a key audit matter because it involves significant judgment and high inherent uncertainty. The assessment considers:

- Contract progress.
- Efforts or costs incurred to date.
- Estimated efforts or costs required to complete the remaining performance obligations over the contract term.

The matter required a high degree of auditor judgment and a higher extent of audit effort to evaluate the reasonableness of the total estimated amount of revenue recognized on fixed-price contracts. Refer to Notes 1.5 and 2.18 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.

The principal audit procedures performed included:

- Evaluating management’s ability to reasonably estimate progress toward satisfying performance obligations by comparing actual efforts or costs incurred with prior-year estimates or budgeted costs for fulfilled performance obligations.
- Comparing efforts or costs incurred with the Group’s estimates of efforts or costs incurred to date to identify significant variations and evaluating whether those variations were appropriately considered when estimating remaining costs or efforts required to complete the contract.
- Testing whether estimates were consistent with delivery milestones and customer acceptances or sign-offs to identify possible delays requiring changes to estimated costs or efforts for completing the remaining performance obligations.

## Information Other than the Financial Statements and Auditor’s Report Thereon

The Company’s Board of Directors is responsible for preparing the other information, which comprises the Management Discussion and Analysis, Board’s Report and its annexures, Business Responsibility and Sustainability Report, Corporate Governance, and Shareholder’s Information. It does not include the consolidated financial statements, standalone financial statements, or the auditor’s reports thereon.

The auditor’s opinion on the Consolidated Financial Statements does not cover the other information, and no assurance conclusion is expressed on it.

In connection with the audit of the Consolidated Financial Statements, the auditor’s responsibility is to read the other information and consider whether it is materially inconsistent with the Consolidated Financial Statements, the auditor’s knowledge obtained during the audit, or otherwise appears to be materially misstated.

The auditor reported that there was nothing to report regarding material misstatements in the other information.

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Consolidated Financial Statements

The Company’s Board of Directors is responsible under Section 134(5) of the Act for preparing and presenting Consolidated Financial Statements that give a true and fair view of the Group’s:

- Consolidated financial position.
- Consolidated financial performance, including other comprehensive income.
- Consolidated changes in equity.
- Consolidated cash flows.

The financial statements are prepared in accordance with accounting principles generally accepted in India, including Ind AS specified under Section 133 of the Act.

The respective Boards of Directors or Trustees of entities included in the Group are responsible for:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records in accordance with the Act.
- Safeguarding the Group’s assets.
- Preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls that operate effectively to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting records relevant to preparing and presenting financial statements free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error.

In preparing the Consolidated Financial Statements, the respective Boards of Directors or Trustees are responsible for assessing the ability of the relevant entities to continue as a going concern, disclosing applicable going-concern matters, and using the going-concern basis of accounting unless they intend to liquidate the entities, cease operations, or have no realistic alternative but to do so.

The respective Boards of Directors or Trustees are also responsible for overseeing the Group’s financial reporting process.

## Auditor’s Responsibilities for the Audit of the Consolidated Financial Statements

The auditor’s objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance that the Consolidated Financial Statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor’s report containing the auditor’s opinion.

Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but is not a guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with Standards on Auditing will always detect a material misstatement when it exists. Misstatements may arise from fraud or error and are considered material if, individually or in aggregate, they could reasonably be expected to influence users’ economic decisions based on the Consolidated Financial Statements.

As part of the audit, the auditor exercises professional judgment and maintains professional skepticism throughout the audit. The auditor also:

- Identifies and assesses the risks of material misstatement of the Consolidated Financial Statements, whether due to fraud or error; designs and performs procedures responsive to those risks; and obtains sufficient and appropriate audit evidence to support the opinion.
- Recognizes that the risk of failing to detect a material misstatement resulting from fraud is higher than for one resulting from error because fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or management override of internal control.
- Obtains an understanding of internal financial controls relevant to the audit to design appropriate audit procedures.
- Expresses an opinion under Section 143(3)(i) of the Act on whether the Company and its subsidiary companies incorporated in India have adequate internal financial controls with reference to the Consolidated Financial Statements and whether those controls operated effectively.
- Evaluates the appropriateness of accounting policies, the reasonableness of accounting estimates, and related disclosures made by management.
- Concludes on the appropriateness of management’s use of the going-concern basis of accounting and whether, based on the audit evidence obtained, a material uncertainty exists relating to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the Group’s ability to continue as a going concern.
- Draws attention in the auditor’s report to related disclosures when a material uncertainty exists, or modifies the opinion if those disclosures are inadequate.
- Bases going-concern conclusions on audit evidence obtained up to the date of the auditor’s report, while recognizing that future events or conditions may cause the Group to cease to continue as a going concern.
- Evaluates the overall presentation, structure, and content of the Consolidated Financial Statements, including disclosures, and whether they represent the underlying transactions and events in a manner that achieves fair presentation.
- Obtains sufficient and appropriate audit evidence regarding the financial information of entities within the Group to express an opinion on the Consolidated Financial Statements.
