## Trade Receivables and Contract Balances

The timing of revenue recognition, billings, and cash collections results in receivables, unbilled revenue, and unearned revenue on the Group’s Consolidated Balance Sheet. Amounts are billed as work progresses under agreed contractual terms, either at periodic intervals, such as monthly or quarterly, or upon achievement of contractual milestones.

Receivables are unconditional rights to consideration. Unbilled revenue from time-and-material contracts and fixed-price maintenance contracts is classified as a financial asset when the right to consideration is unconditional and payment is due only after the passage of time.

For other fixed-price contracts, invoicing is based on contractual milestones. Because the timing of revenue recognition differs from the timing of invoicing, unbilled revenue for these contracts is classified as a non-financial contract asset when the right to consideration depends on completion of contractual milestones.

Invoicing in excess of earnings is classified as unearned revenue. Trade receivables and unbilled revenue are presented net of impairment in the consolidated balance sheet.

During the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025, the Company recognized revenue of ₹6,608 crore and ₹5,669 crore, respectively, from opening unearned revenue as of April 1, 2025 and April 1, 2024.

During the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025, ₹4,839 crore and ₹4,896 crore, respectively, of unbilled revenue relating to other fixed-price and fixed-time-frame contracts as of April 1, 2025 and April 1, 2024 was reclassified to trade receivables upon billing customers after completion of milestones.

## Remaining performance obligation disclosure

The remaining performance obligation disclosure presents the aggregate transaction price yet to be recognized as of the reporting date and when the Group expects to recognize these amounts as revenue.

Applying the practical expedient under IFRS 15, the Group has not disclosed remaining performance obligations for contracts where recognized revenue corresponds directly with the value to the customer of performance completed to date. These typically include time-and-material contracts and unit-of-work-based contracts.

Remaining performance obligation estimates are subject to change because of factors including contract terminations, changes in contract scope, periodic revalidations, adjustments for revenue that has not materialized, and currency fluctuations.

The aggregate value of completely or partially unsatisfied performance obligations as of March 31, 2026, excluding contracts meeting the criteria above, was ₹130,017 crore. The Group expected to recognize approximately 49.7% within the following year and the remainder thereafter.

The aggregate value of completely or partially unsatisfied performance obligations as of March 31, 2025 was ₹104,785 crore. Contracts can generally be terminated by customers and typically include an enforceable termination penalty payable by the customer. Customers generally have not terminated contracts without cause.

## 2.18 Equity

### Ordinary Shares

Ordinary shares are classified as equity. Incremental costs directly attributable to issuing new ordinary shares or share options, and to buybacks, are recognized as a deduction from equity, net of related tax effects.

### Treasury Shares

When an entity within the Group purchases the Company’s ordinary shares, the consideration paid, including directly attributable incremental costs, is presented as a deduction from total equity until the shares are cancelled, sold, or reissued.

When treasury shares are subsequently sold or reissued, the amount received is recognized as an increase in equity, and the resulting surplus or deficit is transferred to or from share premium.

### Share premium

Amounts received in excess of the par value of equity shares are classified as share premium. Share-based compensation recognized in net profit in the consolidated statement of comprehensive income is also credited to share premium.

Amounts from the share premium account have been used for bonus issues and share buybacks.

### Other Reserve

The Special Economic Zone Re-investment Reserve was created from the profit of an eligible SEZ unit under Section 10AA(1)(ii) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The reserve is to be used to acquire new plant and machinery for the Company’s business under Section 10AA(2) of the Income Tax Act, 1961.

### Capital Redemption Reserve

Under Section 69 of the Companies Act, 2013, the Company creates a capital redemption reserve equal to the nominal value of shares bought back, appropriated from general reserves or retained earnings.

### Cash flow hedge reserve

When a derivative is designated as a cash flow hedging instrument, the effective portion of changes in its fair value is recognized in other comprehensive income and accumulated in the cash flow hedge reserve.

The cumulative gain or loss previously recognized in the cash flow hedge reserve is transferred to net profit in the interim consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income when the related forecast transaction occurs.

### Other components of equity

Other components of equity include currency translation; remeasurement of net defined benefit liabilities or assets; fair value changes of equity instruments measured through other comprehensive income; and changes in the fair value of investments, net of taxes.

### 2.18.1 Voting

Each holder of equity shares is entitled to one vote per share. Equity shares represented by American Depositary Shares (ADSs) carry similar voting and dividend rights as other equity shares. Each ADS represents one underlying equity share.

### 2.18.2 Liquidation

In the event of liquidation, shareholders are entitled to receive the remaining assets of the Company after distribution of all preferential amounts. No such preferential amounts currently exist, other than amounts held by irrevocable controlled trusts.

Amounts distributed to shareholders will be in proportion to the number of equity shares held. For irrevocable controlled trusts, the corpus will be settled in favor of the beneficiaries.

### 2.18.3 Share options

Holders of options issued under the Company’s share option plans have no voting, dividend, or liquidation rights.

### 2.18.4 Share capital and share premium

The Company has one class of shares, referred to as equity shares, with a par value of ₹5 each. Controlled trusts held 8,650,911 shares as of March 31, 2026 and 9,655,927 shares as of March 31, 2025.

### 2.18.5 Capital allocation policy

Effective fiscal 2025, the Company expects to continue its policy of returning approximately 85% of cumulative free cash flow over a five-year period through a combination of semi-annual dividends, share buybacks, and/or special dividends, subject to applicable laws and requisite approvals.

Under this policy, the Company expects to progressively increase its annual dividend per share, excluding any special dividend.

Free cash flow is defined as net cash provided by operating activities less capital expenditure, as reported in the consolidated statement of cash flows prepared under IFRS. Dividends and buybacks include applicable taxes.

## Buyback completed in December 2025

At its meeting on September 11, 2025, the Board approved a proposal to buy back fully paid-up equity shares with a face value of ₹5 each for ₹18,000 crore, subject to shareholder approval by postal ballot.

Shareholders approved the proposal through e-voting by postal ballot, with results declared on November 6, 2025. The buyback comprised 10,00,00,000 equity shares, approximately 2.41% of the Company’s total paid-up equity share capital as of June 30, 2025 on a standalone basis, at a price of ₹1,800 per equity share.

The buyback was offered to eligible equity shareholders, including shareholders who became equity shareholders by cancelling ADSs and withdrawing the underlying equity shares, as of the record date of November 14, 2025. It was conducted on a proportionate basis through the tender offer route.

The tender period began on November 20, 2025 and ended on November 26, 2025. The Company completed the buyback procedures on December 4, 2025. All 10,00,00,000 equity shares were bought back and extinguished.

The buyback resulted in a cash outflow of ₹18,000 crore, excluding transaction costs. It was funded from free reserves, including securities premium, under Section 68 of the Companies Act, 2013.

Under Section 69 of the Companies Act, 2013, the Company created a capital redemption reserve of ₹50 crore as of March 31, 2026, equal to the nominal value of the shares bought back and appropriated from the general reserve.

The Company’s capital management objective is to safeguard its ability to continue as a going concern and maintain an optimal capital structure. To maintain or achieve this structure, the Company may adjust dividend payments, return capital to shareholders, issue new shares, or buy back issued shares.

As of March 31, 2026, the Company had one class of equity shares and no debt. Consequently, there were no externally imposed capital requirements.
