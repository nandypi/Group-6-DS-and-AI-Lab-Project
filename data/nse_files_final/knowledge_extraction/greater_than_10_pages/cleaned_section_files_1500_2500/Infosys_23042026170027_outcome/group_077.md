## 2.15.2 Significant clients

No client individually accounted for more than 10% of revenues for the three months and years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025, respectively.

## Accounting Policy

The Group derives revenues primarily from IT services comprising software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting and package implementation, licensing of software products and platforms across the Group's core and digital offerings (together called “software related services”), and business process management services. Contracts with customers are entered into on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-time-frame basis.

Revenues from customer contracts are recognized and measured when the contract has been approved in writing by the parties, the parties are committed to perform their respective obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable. Revenue is recognized upon transfer of control of promised products or services (“performance obligations”) to customers, in an amount that reflects the consideration the Group has received or expects to receive in exchange for those products or services. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group assesses the services promised in a contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. The transaction price is allocated to each distinct performance obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. In the absence of such evidence, the primary method used to estimate standalone selling price is expected cost plus a margin, based on the estimated cost of satisfying the performance obligation and an appropriate margin for similar services.

Contracts may include variable consideration, including rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue recognized will not occur when the related uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue from time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed. Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably either on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period, or using a percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and costs is uneven because the services are generally discrete and non-repetitive.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-time-frame contracts where performance obligations are satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Efforts or costs expended are used to measure progress toward completion because there is a direct relationship between input and productivity. Progress is measured as the ratio of costs or efforts incurred to date, representing work performed, to estimated total costs or efforts.

Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are continuously monitored over the contract term. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates change or are revised. Revenues and estimated total costs or efforts are subject to revision as the contract progresses. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded in the period in which the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

Billing schedules agreed with customers include periodic performance-based billing and/or milestone-based progress billings. Revenues in excess of billing are classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenues is classified as contract liabilities, referred to as unearned revenues.

For software development and related services and maintenance services, arrangements generally meet the criteria for considering software development and related services as distinct performance obligations. Revenue is allocated to each performance obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. Where standalone selling prices cannot be determined, the Group uses the expected cost-plus-margin approach. For software development and related services, performance obligations are satisfied as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements that may be subject to specific accounting guidance, such as leasing guidance. These contracts are accounted for in accordance with that guidance. When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated to them based on relative standalone selling prices. Where standalone selling prices cannot be determined, the Group uses the expected cost-plus-margin approach. When the arrangement is considered a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period and progress is measured based on the promise in the contract.

Revenue from licenses where the customer obtains a “right to use” the licenses is recognized when the license is made available to the customer. Revenue from licenses where the customer obtains a “right to access” is recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally have three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When implementation services are provided with the licensing arrangement and the license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated to each performance obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. Where standalone selling prices for implementation cannot be determined, the Group uses the expected cost-plus-margin approach.

When a license is substantially customized as part of the implementation service, the entire arrangement fee for the license and implementation is treated as a single performance obligation, and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services arising from the sale of software products is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the period in which the services are rendered.

Some integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent between the customer and vendor, and gross when the Group is the principal. The Group evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other indicators of control.

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price, or both, of a contract that is approved by the parties. A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for as a separate contract if the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price. If they are not so priced, it is accounted for as termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract. If the modification does not result in a distinct performance obligation, it is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract, meaning costs that would not have been incurred if the contract had not been obtained, are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them.

Certain eligible, nonrecurring costs, such as set-up, transition, or transformation costs, that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as an asset when they:

- Relate directly to the contract;
- Generate or enhance resources that will be used to satisfy performance obligations in the future; and
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to cost of sales over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services to the customer. Capitalized costs are monitored regularly for impairment. Impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount of the capitalized costs.

The Group presents revenues net of indirect taxes in its interim Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income.
