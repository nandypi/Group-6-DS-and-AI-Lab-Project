## 1.4 Use of estimates and judgments

The preparation of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements in conformity with IFRS requires Management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies; reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses; and disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities.

Critical accounting estimates and judgments are disclosed in Note 1.5 and may change from period to period. Actual results could differ from these estimates. Changes in estimates and judgments are reflected in the period in which the changes are made, with material effects disclosed in the notes.

### a. Revenue recognition

The Group’s contracts with customers may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is considered for recognition and measurement when:

- the contract has been approved in writing by the parties;
- the parties are committed to performing their respective obligations; and
- the contract is legally enforceable.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to those obligations. Determining distinct deliverables and assessing whether customers can benefit independently from them involves significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and the Group’s fulfillment costs are uneven because the services are discrete rather than repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method.

For other fixed-price contracts, the Group uses the percentage-of-completion method. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs. Estimates of total efforts or costs are assessed throughout the contract period and updated using the latest available information.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded:

- net of costs when the Group acts as an agent; and
- gross when the Group acts as principal.

The Group assesses whether it controls the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other relevant factors.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when those losses become probable.

### b. Income taxes

The Group’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. The Group also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is required to determine income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, Management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax planning strategies. The ultimate realization of deferred tax assets depends on the generation of future taxable income during the periods in which temporary differences become deductible.

Based on historical taxable income and projections for future taxable income, Management believes the Group will realize the benefits of deductible temporary differences. The amount considered realizable could be reduced if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. Refer to Note 2.12.

### c. Business combinations and intangible assets

Business combinations are accounted for under IFRS 3 (Revised), *Business Combinations*. IFRS 3 requires identifiable intangible assets and contingent consideration to be fair-valued to determine the net fair value of the acquiree’s identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities.

External valuation experts conduct these valuations. Estimates are required to determine the value of contingent consideration, option arrangements, and intangible assets. Measurements are based on information available at the acquisition date and on assumptions considered reasonable by Management. Refer to Notes 2.10 and 2.9.2.

### d. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Group’s asset base. Periodic depreciation depends on estimates of the expected useful life and residual value of each asset.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. Refer to Note 2.7.

### e. Impairment of Goodwill

Goodwill is tested for impairment annually and whenever there is an indication that the recoverable amount of a cash-generating unit (CGU) is less than its carrying amount.

Goodwill is allocated to the CGU or group of CGUs that benefits from acquisition synergies and represents the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes.

The recoverable amount of CGUs is determined as the higher of value in use and fair value less costs to sell. Key assumptions in cash flow projections include estimated long-term growth rates, weighted average cost of capital, and estimated operating margins. Refer to Note 2.9.1.

### IFRS 18 - Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements

On April 9, 2024, the IASB issued IFRS 18, *Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements*, which will replace IAS 1, *Presentation of Financial Statements*, from its effective date.

IFRS 18 introduces new requirements for information presented in the primary financial statements and disclosed in the notes. It focuses on the statement of profit or loss and introduces three categories for income and expenses: operating, investing, and financing.

IFRS 18 is effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2027. Early adoption is permitted. The Group is evaluating the impact of the standard.

### Amendments to IFRS 9 Financial Instruments and IFRS 7 Financial Instruments: Disclosures

On May 30, 2024, the IASB issued amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7 that clarify:

- classification of financial assets with environmental, social, and corporate governance (ESG) and similar features;
- derecognition of financial liabilities settled through electronic payment systems; and
- additional disclosure requirements for investments in equity instruments designated at fair value through other comprehensive income and financial instruments with contingent features.

The amendments are effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026. Early adoption is permitted. The Group evaluated the amendments and determined that they have no impact on its consolidated financial statements.

On December 18, 2024, the IASB issued further amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7 relating to:

- factors considered in assessing the own-use requirements for contracts to buy and take delivery of nature-dependent renewable electricity;
- hedge accounting treatment for nature-dependent renewable electricity; and
- related disclosures.

These amendments are effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026. Early adoption is permitted. The Group evaluated the amendments and determined that they have no impact on its consolidated financial statements.
