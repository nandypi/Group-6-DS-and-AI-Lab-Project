Income tax expense for the three months ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025 includes reversals, net of provisions, of ₹882 crore and ₹117 crore, respectively. Income tax expense for the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025 includes reversals, net of provisions, of ₹877 crore and provisions, net of reversals, of ₹132 crore, respectively. These provisions and reversals relating to prior periods primarily arose from the adjudication of disputed matters following the filing of tax returns and completion of assessments across various jurisdictions.

During the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026, the Company received orders under Sections 250 and 254 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, from the Income Tax Authorities in India for assessment year 2013–14 and assessment years 2017–18 to 2021–22. The orders confirmed the Company’s position regarding the tax treatment of certain matters. As a result, pre-tax interest income of ₹381 crore was recognized, income tax provisions aggregating ₹869 crore were reversed with a corresponding credit to the Statement of Profit and Loss, and contingent liabilities were reduced by ₹86 crore.

During the quarter ended March 31, 2025, the Company received orders under Section 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, from the Income Tax Authorities in India for assessment years 2016–17 and 2019–20. The orders confirmed the Company’s position regarding the tax treatment of certain matters. As a result, pre-tax interest income of ₹327 crore was recognized, income tax provisions aggregating ₹183 crore were reversed with a corresponding credit to the Statement of Profit and Loss, and contingent liabilities were reduced by ₹1,068 crore.

Deferred income tax for the three months and years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025 substantially relates to the origination and reversal of temporary differences.

The Company’s Advanced Pricing Arrangement (APA) with the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) for US branch income tax expired in March 2021. The Company has applied for renewal of the APA. US taxable income is currently based on the Company’s best estimate determined using the expected value method.

## Accounting policy

The Group derives revenue primarily from IT services, including software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting and package implementation, licensing of software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings, and business process management services. Contracts with customers are entered into on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe basis.

Revenue from customer contracts is recognized and measured when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable. Revenue is recognized upon transfer of control of promised products or services, referred to as performance obligations, in an amount reflecting the consideration the Group expects to receive. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to each obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. When this evidence is unavailable, the Group primarily uses the expected cost-plus-margin approach.

Contracts may include variable consideration, including rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that a significant reversal of cumulative revenue recognized will not occur when the uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue from time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed. Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably either on a straight-line basis, when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period, or using a percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven because the services are discrete rather than repetitive.

Revenue from other fixed-price and fixed-timeframe contracts whose performance obligations are satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress toward completion. Progress is measured as costs or efforts incurred to date, representing work performed, divided by estimated total costs or efforts. Transaction price and estimated costs or efforts are monitored throughout the contract term, and changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period of change. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.

Customer billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as contract liabilities, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development, related services, and maintenance arrangements, software development and related services generally qualify as distinct performance obligations. Revenue is allocated to each performance obligation based on its relative standalone selling price. Where the standalone selling price cannot be determined, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. Performance obligations for software development and related services are satisfied as services are rendered because customers generally obtain control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, such as leasing guidance. Where hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. Where standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is considered a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period and progress is measured based on the promise in the contract.

Revenue from licenses where the customer obtains a “right to use” the licenses is recognized when the licenses are made available to the customer. Revenue from licenses where the customer obtains a “right to access” is recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When licensing and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on their relative standalone selling prices. Where the standalone selling price for implementation is unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the license requires substantial customization as part of implementation, the license and implementation fee is treated as a single performance obligation and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services related to software product sales is recognized as the performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when the Group acts as principal. The Group evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer, considering responsibility for fulfillment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors.

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price, or both, of a contract approved by the parties. A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for as a separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price. If they are not priced at their standalone selling price, the modification is accounted for as termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract. A modification that does not create a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them.

Certain eligible, nonrecurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as an asset when they:

- Relate directly to the contract.
- Generate or enhance resources used by the Group to satisfy future performance obligations.
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to expenses over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are regularly monitored for impairment. Impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount of the capitalized costs.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss.
