## TO THE MEMBERS OF INFOSYS LIMITED

# Report on the Audit of the Standalone Financial Statements

## Opinion

The auditors audited the standalone financial statements of Infosys Limited (the “Company”), comprising:

- The Balance Sheet as at March 31, 2026
- The Statement of Profit and Loss, including Other Comprehensive Income
- The Statement of Changes in Equity
- The Statement of Cash Flows for the year ended March 31, 2026
- The notes to the financial statements, including a summary of material accounting policies and other explanatory information

In the auditors’ opinion, the standalone financial statements provide the information required by the Companies Act, 2013, and give a true and fair view, in conformity with the Indian Accounting Standards prescribed under Section 133 of the Act and other accounting principles generally accepted in India, of:

- The state of affairs of the Company as at March 31, 2026
- Its profit and other comprehensive income for the year ended on that date
- Changes in equity for the year ended on that date
- Its cash flows for the year ended on that date

## Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under Section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013.

The auditors stated that they were independent of the Company in accordance with the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India and had fulfilled their other ethical responsibilities under the Act, its related rules, and the ICAI Code of Ethics.

The auditors considered the audit evidence obtained to be sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their audit opinion.

## Key Audit Matters

Key audit matters were those matters that, in the auditors’ professional judgment, were of most significance in the audit of the standalone financial statements for the current period. These matters were addressed in the context of the audit of the standalone financial statements as a whole and in forming the audit opinion. The auditors did not provide a separate opinion on these matters.

### 1. Revenue Recognition

The Company’s customer contracts include multiple products and services. Revenue is derived from:

- Software development and related services
- Maintenance
- Consulting and package implementation
- Licensing of software products and platforms across the Company’s core and digital offerings
- Business process management services

The Company assesses the services promised in each contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying distinct performance obligations, determining the deliverables, and assessing whether customers can benefit independently from those deliverables involve significant judgment.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Company acts as an agent and gross when the Company acts as the principal. The Company first evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer.

The auditors identified revenue recognition as a key audit matter because it involves management judgment regarding:

1. Identification of distinct performance obligations
2. Determination of whether the Company acts as principal or agent
3. Whether fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method

The audit procedures included:

- Testing the effectiveness of controls relating to the identification of distinct performance obligations, principal-versus-agent assessments, and the method used to recognize fixed-price maintenance revenue.
- Selecting samples of customer contracts and reading the relevant contract documents, including master service agreements and other documents forming part of the agreements.
- Assessing management’s conclusions regarding the identification of distinct performance obligations and whether the Company acted as principal or agent.
- Comparing costs incurred to date with estimated costs to identify significant variations and evaluating whether those variations were appropriately considered in estimating remaining costs or efforts.
- Testing estimates for consistency with contract delivery milestones and customer acceptances or sign-offs.
- Assessing whether delays in achieving milestones required changes to estimated costs or efforts to complete the remaining performance obligations.

### 2. Revenue Recognition for Fixed-Price Contracts Using the Percentage-of-Completion Method

Certain customer contracts involve management judgment in identifying distinct performance obligations, determining whether the Company acts as principal or agent, and determining whether fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method.

Revenue recognition involving these judgments was identified as a key audit matter because it required a higher extent of audit effort. The relevant disclosures are referenced in Notes 1.4 and 2.18 to the Standalone Financial Statements.

Provisions for estimated losses, if any, on uncompleted contracts are recorded in the period in which the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

The auditors identified estimates of total efforts or costs to complete fixed-price contracts measured using the percentage-of-completion method as a key audit matter because estimating total efforts or costs involves significant judgment and is assessed throughout the contract period using the latest available information.

The estimate has a high level of inherent uncertainty and requires consideration of:

- Contract progress
- Efforts or costs incurred to date
- Estimated efforts or costs required to complete the remaining contract performance obligations over the contract term

The auditors evaluated the reasonableness of the total estimated amount of revenue recognized on fixed-price contracts, including by:

- Comparing costs incurred to date with estimates to identify significant variations.
- Evaluating whether identified variations were appropriately considered in estimating remaining costs or efforts to complete the contract.
- Testing the estimate for consistency with delivery milestones and customer acceptances or sign-offs.
- Identifying possible delays in achieving milestones that could require changes to estimated costs or efforts to complete the remaining performance obligations.

Refer to Notes 1.4 and 2.18 to the Standalone Financial Statements.
