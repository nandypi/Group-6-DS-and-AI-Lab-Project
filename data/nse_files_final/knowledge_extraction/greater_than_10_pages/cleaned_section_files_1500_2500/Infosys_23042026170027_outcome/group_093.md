## 1.2 Basis of preparation of financial statements

These standalone financial statements are prepared in accordance with the Companies Act, 2013 (the “Act”), guidelines issued by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), and Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) prescribed under Section 133 of the Act, read with Rule 3 of the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015 and subsequent amendment rules.

The financial statements use the historical cost convention and accrual basis, except for certain financial instruments measured at fair value and defined benefit liabilities or assets recognized at the present value of the defined benefit obligation less the fair value of plan assets.

Accounting policies have been applied consistently, except when a newly issued accounting standard is initially adopted or a revision to an existing standard requires a change in accounting policy. Material accounting policy information used in preparing the audited standalone financial statements is discussed in the respective notes.

Year-to-date figures are taken from the source and rounded to the nearest digits. Consequently, figures reported for previous quarters may not always add up to the year-to-date figures reported in this statement.

## 1.3 Use of estimates and judgments

Preparing financial statements in conformity with Ind AS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies, reported amounts of assets and liabilities, disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities at the reporting date, and reported revenues and expenses during the period.

The accounting policies requiring critical accounting estimates and complex or subjective judgments are disclosed in Note 1.4. Estimates, judgments, and assumptions may change from period to period, and actual results could differ from those estimates. Changes in estimates are made when management becomes aware of changed circumstances and are reflected in the period in which they occur. Material effects are disclosed in the notes to the standalone financial statements.

## 1.4 Critical accounting estimates and judgments

### a. Revenue recognition

The Company's customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is considered for recognition and measurement when:

- the contract has been approved in writing by the parties;
- the parties are committed to performing their respective obligations; and
- the contract is legally enforceable.

The Company assesses the services promised in a contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying distinct performance obligations, determining the deliverables and whether the customer can benefit independently from them, and allocating the transaction price to those performance obligations involve significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits from the services and the Company's costs to fulfill the contract is not even over the contract period because the services are discrete rather than repetitive, fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method. Selecting the revenue recognition method requires judgment based on the contractual promises and nature of the deliverables.

For other fixed-price contracts, the Company uses the percentage-of-completion method. This requires determining actual efforts or costs expended to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs to be incurred. Efforts or costs expended measure progress toward completion because there is a direct relationship between input and productivity. Estimated total efforts or costs involve significant judgment and are reassessed throughout the contract period using the latest available information.

Some integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Company acts as an agent and gross when it acts as principal. The Company first evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before their transfer to the customer. Factors considered include whether the Company is primarily responsible for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other indicators of control.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts, if any, are recorded in the period in which the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

### b. Income taxes

The Company's two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. The Company also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is involved in determining income tax provisions, including the amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, management considers whether some or all of those assets will be realized. Ultimate realization depends on generating future taxable income during the periods in which temporary differences become deductible. Management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax planning strategies.

Based on historical taxable income and projections for future taxable income during the periods in which deferred income tax assets are deductible, management believes the Company will realize the benefits of the deductible differences. The realizable amount could be reduced in the near term if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. Refer to Note 2.17.

### c. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Company's asset base. Periodic depreciation is determined using estimates of an asset's expected useful life and residual value at the end of its life.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. Refer to Note 2.1.

## 2. Notes to the Standalone Financial Statements

## 2.1 PROPERTY, PLANT AND EQUIPMENT

### Accounting Policy

Property, plant and equipment are stated at cost, less accumulated depreciation and impairment, if any. Costs directly attributable to acquisition are capitalized until the assets are ready for use as intended by management.

The Company depreciates property, plant and equipment over their estimated useful lives using the straight-line method.

The estimated useful lives of assets are as follows:

| Asset | Estimated useful life |
|---|---|
| Building (1) | 22–25 years |
| Plant and machinery (1) | 5 years |
| Office equipment | 5 years |
| Computer equipment (1) | 3–5 years |
| Furniture and fixtures (1) | 5 years |
| Vehicles (1) | 5 years |
| Leasehold improvements | Lower of the useful life of the asset or the lease term |
