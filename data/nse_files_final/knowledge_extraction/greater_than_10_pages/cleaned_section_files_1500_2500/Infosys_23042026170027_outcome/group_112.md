## 2.18 REVENUE FROM OPERATIONS

## Accounting Policy

The Company derives revenues primarily from IT services, including software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting, package implementation, and licensing of software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings. Customer contracts may be based on time and materials, units of work, fixed prices, or fixed timeframes.

Revenue is recognized when:

- The contract has been approved in writing by the parties.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- The contract is legally enforceable.
- Control of the promised products or services has transferred to the customer.
- The transaction price can be determined, subject to collectability.

When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Company identifies the distinct performance obligations in each customer contract and allocates the transaction price to each obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. If this evidence is unavailable, the Company estimates standalone selling price using expected cost plus an appropriate margin based on similar services.

Contracts may include variable consideration such as rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing the amount will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue when the uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue recognition by contract type is as follows:

- Revenue from time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services consist of an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized using a percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of customer benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.
- Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts whose performance obligations are satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Progress toward completion is measured using costs or efforts incurred to date compared with estimated total costs or efforts, reflecting the direct relationship between inputs and productivity.
- Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are monitored throughout the contract. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates change.
- Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.

Customer billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue recognized in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development, related services, and maintenance services, the Company generally treats software development and related services as distinct performance obligations when the revenue recognition criteria are met. The transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices, using the expected cost-plus-margin approach when standalone selling prices cannot be determined. For software development and related services, performance obligations are satisfied as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure service contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. Where hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated between them based on relative standalone selling prices. If standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. Where the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period based on the measure of progress specified in the contract.

Revenue from licenses is recognized as follows:

- Licenses providing the customer with a right to use are recognized when the license is made available to the customer.
- Licenses providing the customer with a right to access are recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When the license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on their relative standalone selling prices. When the standalone selling price for implementation is unavailable, it is estimated using expected cost plus margin. If the license requires substantial customization as part of implementation, the license and implementation are treated as a single performance obligation, and the arrangement fee is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services related to software product sales is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

For integrated services arrangements involving subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Company acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Company assesses whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include primary responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other relevant factors.

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price of a contract, or both, approved by the parties. A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for as:

- A separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price.
- A termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract when the additional services are not priced at their standalone selling price.

A modification that does not create a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Company expects to recover them. Eligible nonrecurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as assets when they:

- Relate directly to the contract.
- Generate or enhance Company resources used to satisfy future performance obligations.
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to expenses over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored for impairment, and impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount.

The Company presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Statement of Profit and Loss.

## Products & platforms

The Company derives revenue from the sale of products and platforms, including Infosys Applied AI, which applies artificial intelligence and machine learning.

The percentage of revenue from fixed-price contracts was 58% for each of the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025.

## Trade receivables and Contract Balances

The timing of revenue recognition, billing, and cash collection results in trade receivables, unbilled revenue, and unearned revenue on the Company's Balance Sheet. Amounts are billed as work progresses under agreed contractual terms, either periodically, such as monthly or quarterly, or upon achievement of contractual milestones.

Trade receivables represent unconditional rights to consideration. Unbilled revenue from time-and-material contracts and fixed-price maintenance contracts is classified as a financial asset when the right to consideration is unconditional and payment is due only after the passage of time.

For other fixed-price contracts, invoicing is based on contractual milestones. Because revenue recognition may occur at a different time from invoicing, unbilled revenue for these contracts is classified as a non-financial contract asset when the right to consideration depends on completion of contractual milestones.

Invoicing in excess of earnings is classified as unearned revenue. Trade receivables and unbilled revenue are presented net of impairment in the Balance Sheet.

During the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025, the Company recognized revenue of ₹5,276 crore and ₹4,404 crore, respectively, arising from opening unearned revenue balances as of April 1, 2025 and April 1, 2024, respectively.

During the years ended March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025, ₹4,413 crore and ₹4,448 crore, respectively, of unbilled revenue related to other fixed-price and fixed-timeframe contracts as of April 1, 2025 and April 1, 2024 was reclassified to trade receivables upon billing customers after completion of milestones.
