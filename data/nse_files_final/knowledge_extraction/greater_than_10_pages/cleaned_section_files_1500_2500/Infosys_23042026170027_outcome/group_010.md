## Basis for Opinion

The audit of the Statement was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under Section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013. The auditor was independent of the Company under the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI) Code of Ethics and other applicable ethical requirements.

The auditor stated that the audit evidence obtained was sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for the audit opinion.

## Management's and Board of Directors' Responsibilities for the Statement

The Statement, including the standalone financial results, is the responsibility of the Company's Board of Directors and was approved by them for issuance. It was compiled from the related audited interim condensed standalone financial statements for the three months and year ended March 31, 2026.

The Board's responsibilities include:

- Preparing and presenting standalone financial results that give a true and fair view of net profit, other comprehensive income, and other financial information.
- Applying the recognition and measurement principles under Ind AS 34, prescribed under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013, and complying with Regulation 33 of the LODR Regulations.
- Maintaining adequate accounting records to safeguard assets and prevent and detect fraud and other irregularities.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting records and prevent material misstatement due to fraud or error.
- Assessing the Company's ability to continue as a going concern, making relevant disclosures, and using the going-concern basis of accounting unless liquidation or cessation of operations is intended or there is no realistic alternative.
- Overseeing the Company's financial reporting process.

## Auditor's Responsibilities for audit of the Standalone Financial Results for the quarter and year ended March 31, 2026.

The auditor's objectives were to obtain reasonable assurance that the Statement as a whole was free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report containing the opinion.

Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but does not guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing will always detect a material misstatement. Misstatements are considered material if they could reasonably be expected to influence users' economic decisions based on the standalone financial results.

As part of the audit, the auditor exercised professional judgment and maintained professional skepticism. The audit procedures included:

- Identifying and assessing risks of material misstatement due to fraud or error, designing responsive audit procedures, and obtaining sufficient and appropriate audit evidence.
- Recognizing that the risk of failing to detect a fraud-related misstatement is higher than for an error-related misstatement because fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or management override of internal control.
- Obtaining an understanding of internal control relevant to the audit to design appropriate procedures, without expressing an opinion on the effectiveness of those controls.
- Evaluating the appropriateness of accounting policies and the reasonableness of accounting estimates made by the Board of Directors.
- Evaluating the appropriateness and reasonableness of disclosures made by the Board of Directors under Regulation 33 of the LODR Regulations.
- Assessing the appropriateness of the Board's use of the going-concern basis of accounting and whether audit evidence indicates a material uncertainty related to events or conditions that could cast significant doubt on the Company's ability to continue as a going concern.

If a material uncertainty exists, the auditor is required to draw attention to the related disclosures in the Statement or modify the opinion if the disclosures are inadequate. The auditor's conclusions are based on evidence obtained up to the date of the auditor's report; future events or conditions may cause the Company to cease to continue as a going concern.
