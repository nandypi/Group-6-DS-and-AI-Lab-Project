## Accounting Policy

The Group derives revenue primarily from IT services, including software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting and package implementation, licensing of software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings, and business process management services. Customer contracts may be based on time and materials, unit of work, fixed price, or a fixed timeframe.

Revenue is recognized when the customer contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable. Revenue is recognized upon transfer of control of promised products or services to customers, at the amount of consideration the Group expects to receive. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. When that evidence is unavailable, the Group estimates standalone selling price using expected cost plus an appropriate margin.

Contracts may include variable consideration such as rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue.

Revenue from time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed. Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services consist of an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. It is recognized using a percentage-of-completion method when the services are discrete and the pattern of benefits and fulfillment costs is not even over the contract period.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because of the direct relationship between input and productivity. Progress is measured as costs or efforts incurred to date divided by estimated total costs or efforts. Transaction price and estimated total costs or efforts are monitored continuously, with revisions recognized in net profit when estimates change. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.

Customer billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development and related services and maintenance arrangements, software development and related services generally represent distinct performance obligations. Revenue is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. Software development and related services are recognized as services are rendered because customers generally obtain control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure service contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period based on the contractual promise and related measure of progress.

Revenue from licenses providing a customer with a right to use the license is recognized when the license is made available to the customer. Revenue from licenses providing a right to access is recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include a license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When the license is substantially customized as part of implementation, the license and implementation are treated as a single performance obligation and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

When contracts include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as agent and gross when it acts as principal. The Group assesses control of the specified goods or services by considering responsibility for fulfillment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors.

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price of a contract approved by the parties. A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for as a separate contract when the additional services are priced at standalone selling price. Otherwise, it is accounted for as termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract. A modification that does not create a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract using a cumulative catch-up adjustment.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them. Eligible nonrecurring setup, transition, or transformation costs that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as assets when they relate directly to the contract, generate or enhance resources used to satisfy future performance obligations, and are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to cost of sales over the contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored for impairment, with impairment losses recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its interim Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income.
