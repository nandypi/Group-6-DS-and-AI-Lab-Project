The Group assesses defined benefit plan assumptions in accordance with its projected long-term growth plans and prevalent industry standards.

For domestic defined benefit plans in India, the yield on government bonds is used as the discount rate because a developed market for high-quality corporate bonds is not available. For most overseas defined benefit plans, the government bond rate adjusted for corporate spreads is used because a developed market for high-quality corporate bonds is not available.

The average rate of increase in compensation levels is determined using the Company’s past compensation revision trends, inflation in the respective markets, and management’s estimate of future salary increases. The attrition rate is based on management’s estimate using the Company’s long-term employee turnover trend. Employee tenure reflects the long-term trend in employees’ average remaining service life and the estimated term of post-employment benefit obligations.

For domestic defined benefit plans in India, future mortality assumptions are based on published statistics from the Life Insurance Corporation of India. For overseas defined benefit plans, mortality assumptions consider the latest life expectancy statistics, plan experience, and other relevant data.

The Company contributes all ascertained gratuity liabilities to the Infosys Limited Employees’ Gratuity Fund Trust. Infosys BPM and EdgeVerve contribute to the Infosys BPM Employees’ Gratuity Fund Trust and EdgeVerve Systems Limited Employees Gratuity Fund Trust, respectively. As at March 31, 2026 and March 31, 2025, trustees administered contributions made to these trusts. Gratuity contributions are invested in a Life Insurance Corporation of India scheme as permitted by Indian law.

Overseas defined benefit plan assets were primarily invested in insurer-managed funds. Asset allocation is determined using investment criteria prescribed under regulations applicable to pension funds and insurer managers. The insurers’ investments are diversified and provide guaranteed interest rate arrangements.

### Actuarial risks

The defined benefit plans expose the Group to the following actuarial risks:

- **Interest rate risk:** The present value of the defined benefit obligation is generally calculated using a discount rate based on government bond yields. In certain overseas jurisdictions, the rate is based on government bond yields adjusted for a corporate spread. If bond yields fall, the defined benefit obligation generally increases.
- **Life expectancy and investment risk:** The pension fund offers members a choice between a lifelong pension and a cash lump sum upon retirement. The fund has defined rates for converting the lump sum into a pension. There is a risk that members live longer than implied by these conversion rates or that pension assets do not achieve the investment return implied by the rates.
- **Asset volatility:** A proportion of the pension fund is held in equities, which are expected to outperform corporate bonds over the long term but create short-term volatility and risk. The insurer’s pension fund board is responsible for the investment strategy and equity allocation.

### Sensitivity of significant assumptions used for valuation of defined benefit obligation

| Impact from | Gratuity: 1% increase/decrease | Pension: 0.5% point increase/decrease |
|---|---:|---:|
| Discount rate | 205 | 70 |

Sensitivity to significant actuarial assumptions is computed by varying one assumption used to value the defined benefit obligation while keeping all other actuarial assumptions constant. In practice, this is not probable, and changes in some assumptions may be correlated.

The Group expects to contribute ₹540 crore to gratuity and ₹66 crore to pension during fiscal 2027.
