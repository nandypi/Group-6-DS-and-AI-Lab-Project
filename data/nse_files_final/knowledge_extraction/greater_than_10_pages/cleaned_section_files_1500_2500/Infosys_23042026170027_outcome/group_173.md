## 2.18 REVENUE FROM OPERATIONS

## Accounting policy

The Group derives revenues primarily from IT services, including software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting and package implementation, licensing of software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings, and business process management services. Customer contracts may be on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe basis.

Revenue is recognized when a customer contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable. Revenue is recognized when control of promised products or services transfers to the customer, at an amount reflecting the consideration the Group expects to receive. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to them based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged when an item is sold separately is the best evidence of standalone selling price. When that evidence is unavailable, the Group generally estimates standalone selling price using expected cost plus an appropriate margin.

Contracts may include variable consideration, such as rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue when the uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue under time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed. Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably either on a straight-line basis, when services involve an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period, or using a percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts with performance obligations satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because of the direct relationship between inputs and productivity. Progress is measured as the ratio of costs or efforts incurred to date to estimated total costs or efforts. Transaction price and estimated total costs or efforts are monitored throughout the contract term, with changes recognized in net profit when estimates change or are revised. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.

Customer billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development and related services and maintenance services, the Group generally treats software development and related services as distinct performance obligations. Revenue is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices, using an expected cost-plus-margin approach when standalone selling prices cannot be determined. For software development and related services, performance obligations are satisfied as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure service contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period based on the measure of progress specified by the contract.

Revenue from licenses providing the customer with a right to use the licenses is recognized when the licenses are made available to the customer. Revenue from licenses providing a right to access is recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include a license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When the license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on their relative standalone selling prices. When the standalone selling price of implementation is unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. If the license must be substantially customized as part of implementation, the license and implementation fee are treated as a single performance obligation, and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

In certain integrated services arrangements, customer contracts include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as principal. The Group assesses control of the specified goods or services, including primary responsibility for fulfillment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors.

A contract modification is a change in scope or price, or both, approved by the parties. A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for as a separate contract when the additional services are priced at standalone selling price. Otherwise, it is accounted for as termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract. A modification that does not add a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract using a cumulative catch-up adjustment.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them. Eligible nonrecurring setup, transition, or transformation costs that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as assets when they relate directly to the contract, generate or enhance resources used to satisfy future performance obligations, and are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to expenses over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored for impairment, and impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss.

## Products & platforms

The Group derives revenue from products and platforms including Finacle core banking solution, Edge Suite of products, Panaya platform, Stater digital platform, and Infosys McCamish insurance platform.
