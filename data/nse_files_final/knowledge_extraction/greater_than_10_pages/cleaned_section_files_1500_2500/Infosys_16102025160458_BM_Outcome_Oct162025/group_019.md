## NOTES

1. The information was extracted from the audited condensed consolidated balance sheet and statement of comprehensive income for the quarter ended September 30, 2025, taken on record at the Board meeting held on October 16, 2025.
2. Other income is net of finance cost.
3. Quarter and six-month figures are rounded to the nearest digits. Accordingly, quarter figures added to the previous quarter’s figures may not always equal the six-month figures reported in the statement.

## Opinion

The interim condensed consolidated financial statements of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries comprise:

- The consolidated balance sheet as at September 30, 2025.
- The condensed consolidated statement of comprehensive income for the three months and six months ended September 30, 2025.
- The condensed consolidated statement of changes in equity for the six months ended September 30, 2025.
- The condensed consolidated statement of cash flows for the six months ended September 30, 2025.
- Notes to the financial statements, including material accounting policies and explanatory information.

In the auditor’s opinion, the interim condensed consolidated financial statements present, in conformity with International Accounting Standard 34, “Interim Financial Reporting,” as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board, the consolidated state of affairs of the Group as at September 30, 2025, its consolidated profit and total comprehensive income for the three months and six months ended on that date, and its consolidated changes in equity and cash flows for the six months ended on that date.

## Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India. The auditors stated that they were independent of the Group in accordance with the ICAI Code of Ethics and had fulfilled their other ethical responsibilities.

The auditors considered the audit evidence obtained to be sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their opinion.

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements

The Company’s Board of Directors is responsible for the preparation and presentation of interim condensed consolidated financial statements that provide a true and fair view of the Group’s consolidated financial position, financial performance, total comprehensive income, changes in equity, and cash flows in accordance with IAS 34.

The respective boards of directors of the entities included in the Group are responsible for:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records.
- Safeguarding the Group’s assets.
- Preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls that operate effectively to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting records.
- Assessing the ability of the respective entities to continue as going concerns.
- Disclosing applicable going-concern matters.
- Using the going-concern basis of accounting unless the entities intend to liquidate, cease operations, or have no realistic alternative but to do so.
- Overseeing the Group’s financial reporting process.

## Statements

The auditors’ objective was to obtain reasonable assurance that the interim condensed consolidated financial statements as a whole were free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor’s report containing their opinion.

The audit included:

- Identifying and assessing risks of material misstatement and performing responsive audit procedures.
- Obtaining audit evidence sufficient and appropriate to support the audit opinion.
- Understanding internal financial controls relevant to the audit, without expressing an opinion on their effectiveness.
- Evaluating the appropriateness of accounting policies and the reasonableness of accounting estimates and related disclosures.
- Assessing the appropriateness of management’s use of the going-concern basis of accounting.
- Considering whether events or conditions created a material uncertainty that could cast significant doubt on the Group’s ability to continue as a going concern.
- Evaluating the overall presentation, structure, content, and disclosures of the financial statements.
- Obtaining sufficient appropriate audit evidence regarding the financial information of entities within the Group.

Materiality refers to the magnitude of misstatements that, individually or in aggregate, could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of a reasonably knowledgeable user of the financial statements. The auditors considered quantitative materiality and qualitative factors when planning the audit, determining its scope, evaluating audit results, and assessing identified misstatements.

The auditors communicated with those charged with governance regarding the planned scope and timing of the audit, significant audit findings, and any significant deficiencies in internal financial controls identified during the audit. They also communicated compliance with relevant ethical requirements regarding independence and any relationships or other matters relevant to their independence.
