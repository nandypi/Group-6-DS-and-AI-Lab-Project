Depreciation methods, useful lives, and residual values are reviewed periodically, including at each financial year end. Useful lives are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology.

Advances paid toward the acquisition of property, plant and equipment outstanding at each balance sheet date, and the cost of assets not ready for use before that date, are disclosed as **capital work-in-progress**.

Subsequent expenditures relating to property, plant and equipment are capitalized only when it is probable that future economic benefits will flow to the Group and the cost can be measured reliably. The cost and related accumulated depreciation are removed from the financial statements upon sale or retirement of the asset.

## Impairment

Property, plant and equipment are evaluated for recoverability whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. For impairment testing, the recoverable amount—the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use—is determined on an individual asset basis unless the asset does not generate cash flows that are largely independent of those from other assets. In those cases, the recoverable amount is determined for the cash-generating unit (CGU) to which the asset belongs.

An impairment loss recognized in net profit in the interim condensed consolidated statement of comprehensive income is measured as the excess of the asset’s carrying value over its estimated recoverable amount. An impairment loss is reversed in net profit if there has been a change in the estimates used to determine the recoverable amount. The asset’s carrying amount is increased to its revised recoverable amount, provided that it does not exceed the carrying amount that would have been determined, net of accumulated depreciation, had no impairment loss been recognized in prior years.
