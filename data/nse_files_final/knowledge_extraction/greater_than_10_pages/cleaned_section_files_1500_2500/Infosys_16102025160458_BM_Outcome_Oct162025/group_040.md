## Financial Statements

The Company's Board of Directors is responsible for the preparation and presentation of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements, which provide a true and fair view of the Group's consolidated financial position, consolidated financial performance, consolidated total comprehensive income, consolidated changes in equity, and consolidated cash flows in accordance with IAS 34 as issued by the IASB.

The respective Boards of Directors of the entities included in the Group are responsible for:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting records relevant to the preparation and presentation of the respective interim financial statements.
- Assessing the ability of the respective entities to continue as a going concern.
- Disclosing, where applicable, matters related to going concern.
- Using the going-concern basis of accounting unless the respective Boards intend to liquidate the entities, cease operations, or have no realistic alternative but to do so.

The financial statements are intended to be free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error.

## Statements

The auditors' objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance that the interim condensed consolidated financial statements as a whole are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report that includes their opinion.

Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but does not guarantee that an audit will detect every material misstatement. Misstatements are considered material if, individually or in aggregate, they could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of users based on the interim condensed consolidated financial statements.

As part of an audit in accordance with the applicable auditing standards, the auditors exercise professional judgment and maintain professional scepticism. Their procedures include:

- Identifying and assessing the risks of material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error.
- Designing and performing audit procedures responsive to those risks.
- Obtaining sufficient and appropriate audit evidence to support the audit opinion.
- Considering that the risk of failing to detect a material misstatement resulting from fraud is higher than that resulting from error because fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or management override of internal control.
- Obtaining an understanding of internal financial controls relevant to the audit to design appropriate audit procedures. This work is not performed to express an opinion on the effectiveness of those controls.
- Evaluating the appropriateness of accounting policies and the reasonableness of accounting estimates and related disclosures.
- Evaluating the appropriateness of management's use of the going-concern basis of accounting and whether events or conditions create significant doubt about the Group's ability to continue as a going concern.
- Drawing attention in the auditor's report to related disclosures when a material uncertainty about going concern exists, or modifying the opinion if those disclosures are inadequate.
- Recognizing that conclusions are based on audit evidence obtained up to the date of the auditor's report and that future events or conditions may cause the Group to cease to continue as a going concern.
- Evaluating the overall presentation, structure, and content of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements, including whether they represent the underlying transactions and events fairly.
- Obtaining sufficient and appropriate audit evidence regarding the financial information of entities within the Group.

The auditors are responsible for the direction, supervision, and performance of the audit of the financial statements of the entities included in the Group for which they are independent auditors.

## Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP

Audit materiality is the magnitude of misstatements in the interim condensed consolidated financial statements that, individually or in aggregate, makes it probable that the economic decisions of a reasonably knowledgeable user will be influenced. Materiality is considered when:

1. Planning the scope of audit work and evaluating its results.
2. Evaluating the effect of identified misstatements on the interim condensed consolidated financial statements.

The auditors communicate with those charged with governance regarding, among other matters, the planned scope and timing of the audit, significant audit findings, and any significant deficiencies in internal financial controls identified during the audit.

The auditors also provide those charged with governance with a statement confirming compliance with relevant ethical requirements regarding independence. They communicate all relationships and other matters that may reasonably be thought to bear on their independence and, where applicable, related safeguards.

The interim condensed consolidated financial statements were prepared under IFRS in Indian Rupee for the three months and six months ended September 30, 2025. The auditor's report is dated October 16, 2025, and identifies Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP as the chartered accountant firm.
