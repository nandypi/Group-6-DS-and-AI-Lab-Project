## 1.3 Basis of consolidation

Infosys consolidates entities that it owns or controls. The interim condensed consolidated financial statements comprise the financial statements of the Company, its controlled trusts, and its subsidiaries.

Control exists when the parent has power over an entity, is exposed or has rights to variable returns from its involvement with the entity, and has the ability to affect those returns through its power over the entity. Power is demonstrated through existing rights that provide the ability to direct relevant activities that significantly affect the entity’s returns. Subsidiaries are consolidated from the date control commences until the date control ceases.

Group companies are consolidated on a line-by-line basis. Intra-group balances and transactions, including unrealized gains and losses, are eliminated upon consolidation. The financial statements are prepared using uniform accounting policies across the Group. Non-controlling interests represent the portion of net profit or loss and net assets of subsidiaries that is not directly or indirectly owned or controlled by the Company.

## 1.4 Use of estimates and judgments

Preparing the interim condensed consolidated financial statements in conformity with Ind AS requires Management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies; reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses; and disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities.

Critical accounting estimates involving complex and subjective judgments are disclosed in Note 1.5. Estimates may change as circumstances change, and actual results could differ from those estimates. Changes in estimates and judgments are recognized in the period in which they are made. Material effects are disclosed in the notes to the interim condensed consolidated financial statements.

### a. Revenue recognition

The Group’s customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is considered for recognition and measurement when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to those obligations. Determining deliverables, whether customers can benefit independently from those deliverables, and the allocation of transaction price involves significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven because services are discrete rather than repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method.

The Group uses the percentage-of-completion method for other fixed-price contracts. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs, because there is a direct relationship between input and productivity. Estimates of total efforts or costs are reassessed throughout the contract period using the latest available information.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Group assesses control of the specified goods or services, primary responsibility for fulfillment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors in making this determination.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded in the period in which the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

### b. Income taxes

The Group’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. The Company also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is required to determine income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, Management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies. The amount of deferred income tax assets considered realizable could be reduced if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. See Note 2.15.

### c. Business combinations and intangible assets

Business combinations are accounted for using Ind AS 103, Business Combinations. Identifiable intangible assets and contingent consideration are measured at fair value to determine the net fair value of the acquiree’s identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities.

External valuation experts conduct these valuations. Estimates are required to determine the value of contingent consideration, option arrangements, and intangible assets. The measurements are based on information available at the acquisition date and Management’s reasonable expectations and assumptions. See Notes 2.1 and 2.3.

### d. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Group’s asset base. Periodic depreciation is based on estimates of expected useful lives and residual values.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. See Note 2.2.

### e. Impairment of Goodwill

Goodwill is tested for impairment annually and whenever there is an indication that the recoverable amount of a cash-generating unit (CGU) is less than its carrying amount.

For impairment testing, goodwill is allocated to the CGU or group of CGUs that benefit from acquisition synergies and represent the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes.

The recoverable amount of CGUs is based on the higher of value-in-use and fair value less costs to sell. Key assumptions in cash-flow projections include estimated long-term growth rates, weighted average cost of capital, and estimated operating margins. See Note 2.3.

## 2.1  BUSINESS COMBINATIONS

## Accounting policy

Business combinations are accounted for using the acquisition method under Ind AS 103, Business Combinations.

The purchase price is measured at the fair value of assets transferred, equity instruments issued, and liabilities incurred or assumed on the date control transfers to the Group. It includes the fair value of contingent consideration.

Identifiable assets acquired and liabilities and contingent liabilities assumed are initially measured at fair value on the acquisition date. Contingent consideration is remeasured at fair value at each reporting date, with changes recognized in the interim condensed Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss.

Non-controlling interests are initially measured either at fair value or at the non-controlling interests’ proportionate share of the acquiree’s identifiable net assets. The measurement basis is selected separately for each acquisition. After acquisition, the carrying amount of non-controlling interests includes the initial amount recognized and their share of subsequent changes in the subsidiaries’ equity.

Business combinations between entities under common control are accounted for at the carrying value of assets acquired and liabilities assumed in the Group’s consolidated financial statements.

Options issued by the Group over non-controlling interests in subsidiaries are accounted for as financial liabilities and initially recognized at the estimated present value of the gross obligations. They are subsequently measured at fair value to reflect the amount payable when the option becomes exercisable. If an option expires unexercised, the liability is derecognized.

## Acquisition

During the six months ended September 30, 2025, the Group completed two business combinations by acquiring 100% partnership interests or voting interests in the following entities:

1. **MRE Consulting Ltd.** — Acquired on April 30, 2025. The company is headquartered in Texas, U.S., and provides energy and business consulting services. The acquisition is expected to add capabilities in trading and risk management, particularly in the energy sector.

2. **The Missing Link** — Comprises The Missing Link Security Pty. Ltd., The Missing Link Security Limited, and The Missing Link Automation Pty. Ltd. Acquired on April 30, 2025, the companies are headquartered in Australia and provide cybersecurity services. The acquisition is expected to strengthen the Group’s cybersecurity capabilities and Australian market presence.
