## a. Revenue recognition

The Group’s contracts with customers may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is considered for recognition and measurement when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their respective obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations based on the deliverables and the customer’s ability to benefit independently from them. Identifying performance obligations and allocating the transaction price requires significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and costs is uneven because services are discrete rather than repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method.

For other fixed-price contracts, the Group uses the percentage-of-completion method. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs, where there is a direct relationship between input and productivity. Estimates of total efforts or costs are reassessed throughout the contract period.

For integrated service arrangements involving subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as principal. The assessment considers whether the Group controls the specified goods or services before transfer, including responsibility for fulfillment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs required to complete the contract.

## b. Income taxes

The Group’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. The Group also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is involved in determining income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, Management considers scheduled reversals of deferred tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax planning strategies. The amount considered realizable could be reduced if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. (Refer to note 2.12.)

## c. Business combinations and intangible assets

Business combinations are accounted for under IFRS 3 (Revised), *Business Combinations*. Identifiable intangible assets and contingent consideration are fair-valued to determine the net fair value of the acquiree’s identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities. External valuation experts conduct these valuations.

The valuation of contingent consideration, option arrangements, and intangible assets requires estimates based on information available at the acquisition date and assumptions considered reasonable by Management. (Refer to notes 2.10 and 2.9.2.)

## d. Property, plant and equipment

Periodic depreciation is determined using estimates of an asset’s expected useful life and residual value. Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end.

Useful lives are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. (Refer to note 2.7.)

## e. Impairment of Goodwill

Goodwill is tested for impairment annually and whenever there is an indication that the recoverable amount of a cash-generating unit (CGU) is less than its carrying amount.

Goodwill is allocated to the CGU or group of CGUs that benefits from acquisition synergies and represents the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes.

The recoverable amount of CGUs is based on the higher of value in use and fair value less costs to sell. Key assumptions in cash flow projections include estimated long-term growth rates, weighted average cost of capital, and estimated operating margins. (Refer to note 2.9.1.)

## IFRS 18 - Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements

On April 9, 2024, the IASB issued IFRS 18, *Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements*, which will replace IAS 1, *Presentation of Financial Statements*, from its effective date.

IFRS 18 introduces new requirements for information presented in the primary financial statements and disclosed in the notes, with a focus on the statement of profit or loss. It introduces operating, investing, and financing categories for income and expenses.

IFRS 18 is effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2027; early adoption is permitted. The Group is yet to evaluate the impact of the amendment.

## Amendments to IFRS 9 Financial Instruments and IFRS 7 Financial Instruments: Disclosures

On May 30, 2024, the IASB issued amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7. The amendments clarify the classification of financial assets with environmental, social, and governance (ESG) and similar features, address derecognition of financial liabilities settled through electronic payment systems, and introduce additional disclosure requirements for equity instruments designated at fair value through other comprehensive income and financial instruments with contingent features.

The amendments are effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026; early adoption is permitted. The Group is yet to evaluate their impact.

On December 18, 2024, the IASB issued further amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7 concerning:

- Own-use requirements for contracts to buy and take delivery of nature-dependent renewable electricity.
- Hedge accounting treatment for nature-dependent renewable electricity.
- Related disclosures.

These amendments are effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026; early adoption is permitted. The Group has evaluated the amendments and determined that they have no impact on its consolidated financial statements.
