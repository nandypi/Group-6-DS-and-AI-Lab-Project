## 2.15.2 Significant clients

No client individually accounted for more than 10% of revenues for the three months and six months ended September 30, 2025 and September 30, 2024.

## Accounting Policy

The Group derives revenues primarily from:

- IT services, including software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting, package implementation, and licensing of software products and platforms across its core and digital offerings, collectively referred to as software-related services.
- Business process management services.

Customer contracts may be based on time and materials, units of work, fixed prices, or fixed time frames.

Revenue is recognized when:

- The contract has been approved in writing and is legally enforceable.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- Control of the promised products or services has transferred to the customer.
- The amount recognized reflects the consideration the Group expects to receive.

When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to each obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged when an item is sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. When that evidence is unavailable, the Group generally estimates standalone selling price using expected cost plus an appropriate margin.

Contracts may include variable consideration, including rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue.

Revenue recognition by contract type is as follows:

- Time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts: Revenue is recognized as the related services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance contracts: Revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services consist of an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When services are discrete and the pattern of benefits and costs is uneven, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method.
- Other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts satisfied over time: Revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Progress is measured using costs or efforts incurred to date relative to estimated total costs or efforts.
- Incomplete contracts: Provisions for estimated losses are recorded when such losses become probable.

Transaction price and estimated total costs or efforts are monitored throughout the contract term. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates are revised.

Customer billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development and related services, performance obligations are generally satisfied as services are rendered because the customer obtains control of the work as it progresses. For software development and maintenance arrangements, the transaction price is allocated to each performance obligation based on relative standalone selling prices.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements subject to other accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices are unavailable, the Group uses the expected cost-plus-margin approach. When the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period based on the contractual promise and related measure of progress.

Revenue from software licenses is recognized as follows:

- Right-to-use licenses: Revenue is recognized when the license is made available to the customer.
- Right-to-access licenses: Revenue is recognized over the access period.

Software product arrangements generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When the standalone selling price for implementation is unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used.

When a license must be substantially customized as part of implementation, the license and implementation are treated as a single performance obligation, and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services related to software products is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

In integrated services arrangements involving subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Group assesses whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other relevant factors.

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price, or both, of a contract approved by the parties. A modification that adds distinct performance obligations is accounted for:

- As a separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price.
- As termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract when the additional services are not priced at their standalone selling price.

A modification that does not add a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them.

Eligible nonrecurring costs, such as set-up, transition, or transformation costs, that do not represent separate performance obligations are recognized as assets when they:

- Relate directly to the contract.
- Generate or enhance resources that will be used to satisfy future performance obligations.
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to cost of sales over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored for impairment. Impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its interim condensed Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income.
