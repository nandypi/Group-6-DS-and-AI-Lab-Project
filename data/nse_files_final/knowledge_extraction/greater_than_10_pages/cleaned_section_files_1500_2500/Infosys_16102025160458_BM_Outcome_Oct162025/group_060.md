## Report on the Audit of the Interim Condensed Standalone Financial Statements

## Opinion

The auditor audited the interim condensed standalone financial statements of Infosys Limited, comprising:

- the Condensed Balance Sheet as at September 30, 2025;
- the Condensed Statement of Profit and Loss, including Other Comprehensive Income, for the three and six months ended September 30, 2025;
- the Condensed Statement of Changes in Equity;
- the Condensed Statement of Cash Flows for the six months ended September 30, 2025; and
- the related notes, including material accounting policies and explanatory information.

In the auditor’s opinion, the interim condensed standalone financial statements give a true and fair view, in accordance with section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013, relevant rules, and generally accepted accounting principles in India, of:

- the state of affairs of the Company as at September 30, 2025;
- its profit and total comprehensive income for the three and six months ended September 30, 2025;
- changes in equity; and
- its cash flows for the six months ended September 30, 2025.

## Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013.

The auditor stated that it was independent of the Company in accordance with the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India and had fulfilled its other ethical responsibilities. The auditor considered the audit evidence obtained to be sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for the audit opinion.

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Interim Condensed Standalone Financial Statements

The Company’s Board of Directors is responsible for the preparation and presentation of the interim condensed standalone financial statements in accordance with Ind AS 34 and other generally accepted accounting principles in India.

These responsibilities include:

- maintaining adequate accounting records;
- safeguarding the Company’s assets;
- preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities;
- selecting and applying appropriate and prudent accounting policies;
- making reasonable accounting estimates;
- designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls; and
- ensuring that the financial statements are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error.

The Board of Directors is also responsible for assessing the Company’s ability to continue as a going concern, making relevant disclosures, using the going-concern basis of accounting where appropriate, and overseeing the Company’s financial reporting process.

## Auditor's Responsibilities for the Audit of the Interim Condensed Standalone Financial Statements

The auditor’s objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the interim condensed standalone financial statements are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor’s report containing the auditor’s opinion.

The auditor’s responsibilities include:

- identifying and assessing the risks of material misstatement;
- designing and performing audit procedures responsive to those risks;
- obtaining sufficient and appropriate audit evidence;
- obtaining an understanding of relevant internal financial controls for designing audit procedures;
- evaluating the appropriateness of accounting policies and the reasonableness of accounting estimates and related disclosures;
- concluding on the appropriateness of management’s use of the going-concern basis of accounting;
- determining whether a material uncertainty exists relating to events or conditions that may cast significant doubt on the Company’s ability to continue as a going concern; and
- evaluating the overall presentation, structure, content, and disclosures of the interim condensed standalone financial statements.

The auditor noted that reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but is not a guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing will always detect a material misstatement.

Materiality is the magnitude of misstatements that, individually or in aggregate, could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of users of the interim condensed standalone financial statements. The auditor considered both quantitative materiality and qualitative factors when planning the audit, evaluating its results, and assessing identified misstatements.
