## Accounting Policy:

The Group derives revenues primarily from IT services comprising software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting and package implementation, licensing of software products and platforms across the Group’s core and digital offerings, together referred to as “software-related services,” and business process management services.

Contracts with customers are entered into on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe basis.

Revenue from customer contracts is recognized when:

- The contract has been approved in writing by the parties.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- The contract is legally enforceable.
- Control of the promised products or services has transferred to the customer.
- The amount recognized reflects the consideration the Group expects to receive.

When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations in each contract and allocates the transaction price based on the relative standalone selling price. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. If this evidence is unavailable, the Group primarily estimates standalone selling price using expected cost plus an appropriate margin.

Contracts may include variable consideration such as rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue.

Revenue recognition methods include:

- Time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts: recognized as the related services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance contracts: recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services consist of repetitive acts over a specified period, or using a percentage-of-completion method when the services are discrete and the pattern of benefits and costs is uneven.
- Other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts satisfied over time: recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Software development and related services: recognized as services are rendered because customers generally obtain control of the work as it progresses.
- Right-to-use software licenses: recognized when the license is made available to the customer.
- Right-to-access software licenses: recognized over the access period.
- Annual Technical Services: recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.
- Client training, support, and other services related to software products: recognized as the performance obligations are satisfied.

For percentage-of-completion contracts, progress is measured using the ratio of costs or efforts incurred to estimated total costs or efforts. Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are continuously monitored. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates are revised. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.

Billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. If standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period based on the contractual promise and the related measure of progress.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services. When license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. If the standalone selling price for implementation is unavailable, the expected cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the license is substantially customized as part of implementation, the license and implementation fee are treated as a single performance obligation, and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed.

Contracts may include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Group evaluates whether it controls the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer, considering factors including responsibility for fulfillment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other relevant factors.

A contract modification is a change in contract scope, price, or both, approved by the parties. A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for as:

- A separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price.
- A termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract when the additional services are not priced at their standalone selling price.

A modification that does not create a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them.

Eligible nonrecurring costs, such as setup, transition, transformation, or similar costs that do not represent separate performance obligations, are recognized as an asset when they:

- Relate directly to the contract.
- Generate or enhance resources that will be used to satisfy future performance obligations.
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to cost of sales over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored regularly for impairment. Impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its interim Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income.
