## Provident fund

Eligible employees of Infosys receive benefits from a provident fund classified as a defined benefit plan. The employee and the Company make monthly contributions equal to a specified percentage of the covered employee’s salary. A portion of the Company’s contribution is made to the Infosys Limited Employees’ Provident Fund Trust, which invests in designated instruments permitted under Indian law. The remaining portion is contributed to the government-administered pension fund.

The annual interest rate payable to beneficiaries by the trust is administered by the Government of India. The Company is obligated to make good any shortfall between the return on the trust’s investments and the notified interest rate.

In respect of Indian subsidiaries, eligible employees receive benefits from provident funds classified as defined contribution plans. The employees and respective companies make monthly contributions equal to a specified percentage of the covered employee’s salary. Contributions are deposited in a government-administered provident fund, and the companies have no further obligation beyond their monthly contributions.

## Superannuation

Certain employees of Infosys, Infosys BPM, and EdgeVerve participate in a defined contribution plan. The Group has no further obligations beyond its monthly contributions, which are periodically contributed to a trust fund whose corpus is invested with the Life Insurance Corporation of India.

## Compensated absences

The Group’s compensated absences policy includes accumulating and non-accumulating absences. The expected cost of accumulating compensated absences is determined by an external actuary at each Balance Sheet date using the projected unit credit method. The valuation reflects the additional amount expected to be paid or availed as a result of unused entitlement accumulated at the Balance Sheet date.

Expense for non-accumulating compensated absences is recognized in the period in which the absences occur.

## 2.18.1 Impact of Labour Codes

On November 21, 2025, the Government of India notified provisions of the Code on Wages, 2019, the Industrial Relations Code, 2020, the Code on Social Security, 2020, and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020 (collectively, the “Labour Codes”). The Labour Codes consolidate 29 existing labour laws into a unified framework governing employee benefits during employment and post-employment.

The Labour Codes introduce changes including a uniform definition of wages and enhanced leave benefits. The Group assessed the financial implications of these changes, resulting in an increase in gratuity liability arising from past service cost and an increase in leave liability of ₹1,289 crore.

Because the impact of the new legislation is non-recurring, the Group presented this incremental amount as “Impact of Labour Codes” under “Exceptional Item” in the Condensed Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss for the three and nine months ended December 31, 2025. The Group continues to monitor developments relating to the Labour Codes and will evaluate any impact on the measurement of employee benefit liabilities.

## 2.19 Leases

### The Group as a lessee

The Group’s lease asset classes primarily consist of land, buildings, vehicles, and computers. The Group assesses whether a contract contains a lease at contract inception. A contract is or contains a lease if it conveys the right to control the use of an identified asset for a period of time in exchange for consideration.

To assess control over the use of an identified asset, the Group considers whether:

1. The contract involves the use of an identified asset.
2. The Group obtains substantially all of the economic benefits from use of the asset during the lease period.
3. The Group has the right to direct the use of the asset.

At the lease commencement date, the Group recognizes a right-of-use asset (ROU) and a corresponding lease liability for all lease arrangements in which it is a lessee, except leases with a term of 12 months or less and low-value leases. For short-term and low-value leases, lease payments are recognized as an operating expense on a straight-line basis over the lease term.

The Group determines the lease term as the non-cancellable period of the lease, adjusted for options to extend or terminate the lease when exercise of those options is reasonably certain. The assessment is performed on a lease-by-lease basis and considers factors including significant leasehold improvements, termination costs, the importance of the underlying asset to operations, its location, and the availability of suitable alternatives. The lease term is reassessed in future periods to reflect current economic circumstances.

Certain lease arrangements include options to extend or terminate the lease before the end of the lease term. These options are included in the measurement of ROU assets and lease liabilities when exercise is reasonably certain.

ROU assets are initially recognized at cost, comprising the initial lease liability adjusted for lease payments made at or before commencement, initial direct costs, and lease incentives. They are subsequently measured at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment losses.

ROU assets are depreciated from the commencement date on a straight-line basis over the shorter of the lease term and the useful life of the underlying asset.

ROU assets are evaluated for recoverability when events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. For impairment testing, the recoverable amount—the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use—is determined on an individual asset basis unless the asset does not generate cash flows that are largely independent of those from other assets. In such cases, the recoverable amount is determined for the Cash Generating Unit (CGU) to which the asset belongs.

Lease liabilities are initially measured at amortized cost at the present value of future lease payments. Lease payments are discounted using the interest rate implicit in the lease or, if that rate cannot be readily determined, the incremental borrowing rates in the country of domicile of the leases.

Lease liabilities are remeasured with a corresponding adjustment to the related ROU asset when the Group changes its assessment of whether it will exercise an extension or termination option.

Lease liabilities and ROU assets are presented separately in the Balance Sheet, and lease payments are classified as financing cash flows.

### The Group as a lessor

Leases for which the Group is a lessor are classified as finance or operating leases. A lease is classified as a finance lease when its terms transfer substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to the lessee. All other leases are classified as operating leases.

When the Group is an intermediate lessor, it accounts for its interests in the head lease and the sublease separately. The sublease is classified as a finance or operating lease by reference to the ROU asset arising from the head lease.

For operating leases, rental income is recognized on a straight-line basis over the relevant lease term.
