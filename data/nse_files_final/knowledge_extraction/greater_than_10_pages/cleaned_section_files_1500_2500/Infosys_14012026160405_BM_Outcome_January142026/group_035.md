## 2.16 Revenue from Operations

### Accounting Policy

The Group derives revenues primarily from IT services comprising software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting and package implementation, licensing of software products and platforms across the Group's core and digital offerings (together called “software-related services”), and business process management services.

Contracts with customers are entered into on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe basis.

Revenue from customer contracts is recognized and measured when:

- The contract has been approved in writing by the parties.
- The parties are committed to performing their respective obligations.
- The contract is legally enforceable.

Revenue is recognized upon transfer of control of promised products or services (“performance obligations”) to customers, at an amount that reflects the consideration the Group has received or expects to receive. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies the distinct performance obligations in each contract and allocates the transaction price to each obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. The price regularly charged for an item when sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. When that evidence is unavailable, the Group estimates standalone selling price using the expected-cost-plus-margin approach, based on the estimated cost of satisfying the performance obligation and an appropriate margin for similar services.

Contracts may include variable consideration, including rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue when the related uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue on time-and-material and unit-of-work contracts is recognized as the related services are performed.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably:

- On a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
- Using a percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits from the services and the Group’s costs to fulfill the contract are uneven because the services are generally discrete and non-repetitive.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts in which performance obligations are satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method. Efforts or costs expended are used to measure progress toward completion because there is a direct relationship between input and productivity. Progress is measured as the ratio of costs or efforts incurred to date, representing work performed, to estimated total costs or efforts.

Transaction price and estimated total costs or efforts are continuously monitored over the contract term. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates change or are revised. Revenues and estimated total costs or efforts are subject to revision as the contract progresses. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

Customer billing schedules include periodic performance-based billing and/or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

In software development and related services and maintenance arrangements, the Group generally considers software development and related services to be distinct performance obligations. Revenue is allocated to each performance obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. When standalone selling prices cannot be determined, the expected-cost-plus-margin approach is used. For software development and related services, performance obligations are satisfied as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure service contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, such as leasing guidance. These contracts are accounted for under the applicable specific guidance. When hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated on a relative standalone selling price basis. When standalone selling prices are unavailable, the expected-cost-plus-margin approach is used. When the arrangement is considered a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period and progress is measured based on the promise in the contract.

Revenue from licenses is recognized as follows:

- Licenses providing the customer a “right to use” are recognized when the license is made available to the customer.
- Licenses providing the customer a “right to access” are recognized over the access period.

Arrangements to deliver software products generally contain three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. When the standalone selling price for implementation is unavailable, the expected-cost-plus-margin approach is used.

When a license requires substantial customization as part of implementation, the license and implementation arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation. Revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services arising from software product sales is recognized as the performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the period in which the services are rendered.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent between the customer and vendor, and gross when the Group is the principal. The Group evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other relevant factors.

A contract modification is a change in the scope or price, or both, of a contract approved by the parties. A modification that adds distinct performance obligations is accounted for:

- As a separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price.
- As termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract when the additional services are not priced at their standalone selling price.

When a modification does not create a distinct performance obligation, it is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract, meaning costs that would not have been incurred if the contract had not been obtained, are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them.

Certain eligible, nonrecurring costs, such as setup, transition, or transformation costs, that do not represent a separate performance obligation are recognized as an asset when they:

- Relate directly to the contract.
- Generate or enhance resources that the Group will use to satisfy future performance obligations.
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to cost of sales over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are regularly monitored for impairment. Impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount of the capitalized costs.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its interim Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income.
