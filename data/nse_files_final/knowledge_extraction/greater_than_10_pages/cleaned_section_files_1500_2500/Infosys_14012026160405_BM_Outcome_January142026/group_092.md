## Fair value hierarchy

Level 1 comprises quoted prices, unadjusted, in active markets for identical assets or liabilities.

Level 2 comprises inputs other than quoted prices included within Level 1 that are observable for the asset or liability, either directly, such as prices, or indirectly, such as inputs derived from prices.

Level 3 comprises inputs for assets or liabilities that are not based on observable market data and are therefore unobservable.

A one-percentage-point change in the unobservable inputs used in the fair valuation of Level 3 assets and liabilities does not have a significant impact on their value.

The majority of the Group’s investments are fair valued using Level 1 or Level 2 inputs. These investments primarily include mutual fund units, target maturity fund units, tax-free bonds, certificates of deposit, commercial paper, treasury bills, government securities, non-convertible debentures, and quoted bonds issued by government and quasi-government organizations.

The Group assesses counterparty risk before investing using criteria including Tier I capital, capital adequacy ratio, credit rating, profitability, non-performing asset levels, and deposit base of banks and financial institutions. These risks are monitored regularly under the Group’s risk management program.

## 2.11 EQUITY

### Ordinary Shares

Ordinary shares are classified as equity share capital. Incremental costs directly attributable to issuing new ordinary shares, share options, and buybacks are recognized as a deduction from equity, net of related tax effects.

### Treasury Shares

When an entity within the Group purchases the Company’s ordinary shares, the consideration paid, including directly attributable incremental costs, is presented as a deduction from total equity until the shares are cancelled, sold, or reissued.

When treasury shares are subsequently sold or reissued, the amount received is recognized as an increase in equity. The resulting surplus or deficit is transferred to or from securities premium.

### Capital Redemption Reserve

In accordance with Section 69 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013, the Company creates a capital redemption reserve equal to the nominal value of shares bought back, appropriated from general reserve or retained earnings.

### Securities premium

The amount received in excess of the par value of equity shares is classified as securities premium. Amounts have been used for bonus issues and share buybacks from the share premium account.

### Share options outstanding account

The share options outstanding account records the fair value of equity-settled, share-based payment transactions with employees.

Amounts recorded in the share options outstanding account are transferred to securities premium when stock options are exercised and to general reserve when stock options are not exercised by employees.
