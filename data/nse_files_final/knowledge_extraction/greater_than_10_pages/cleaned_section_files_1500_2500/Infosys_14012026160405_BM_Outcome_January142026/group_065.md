## 1.3 Use of estimates and judgments

Preparation of the interim condensed standalone financial statements under Ind AS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies; reported asset and liability amounts; disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities; and reported revenues and expenses.

Critical accounting estimates and judgments are disclosed in Note 1.4. They may change as circumstances change. Changes are reflected in the period in which they are made, with material effects disclosed in the notes.

## 1.4 Critical accounting estimates and judgments

### a. Revenue recognition

The Company's customer contracts may include multiple products and services. Revenue is considered for recognition and measurement when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable.

The Company identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to those obligations. This involves judgment regarding the deliverables and the customer's ability to benefit independently from them.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven because services are discrete rather than repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method.

For other fixed-price contracts, the Company uses the percentage-of-completion method. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs. Estimates of total efforts or costs are reassessed throughout the contract period.

For integrated services arrangements involving subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, third-party revenue is recorded net of costs when the Company acts as an agent and gross when it acts as principal. The Company assesses control of the specified goods or services, considering responsibility for fulfillment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.

### b. Income taxes

The Company's two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. It also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is involved in determining income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies. The realizable amount of deferred income tax assets could be reduced if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. (Refer to Note 2.16.)

### c. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Company's asset base. Periodic depreciation depends on estimates of expected useful lives and residual values. These are determined when assets are acquired and reviewed periodically, including at each financial year end, based on historical experience and anticipated future events such as technological changes. (Refer to Note 2.1.)

## 2. Notes to the Interim Condensed Standalone Financial Statements

## 2.1 PROPERTY, PLANT AND EQUIPMENT

### Accounting Policy

Property, plant and equipment are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment, if any. Directly attributable acquisition costs are capitalized until the assets are ready for use. Property, plant and equipment are depreciated over their estimated useful lives using the straight-line method.

The estimated useful lives of assets are as follows:

| Asset | Estimated useful life |
|---|---|
| Building (1) | 22–25 years |
| Plant and machinery (1) | 5 years |
| Office equipment | 5 years |
| Computer equipment (1) | 3–5 years |
| Furniture and fixtures (1) | 5 years |
| Vehicles (1) | 5 years |
| Leasehold improvements (1) | Lower of useful life of the asset or lease term |

(1) Based on technical evaluation, management believes that these useful lives best represent the periods over which it expects to use the assets. Accordingly, the useful lives for these assets differ from those prescribed under Part C of Schedule II of the Companies Act, 2013.

Depreciation methods, useful lives, and residual values are reviewed periodically, including at each financial year end.

Advances paid for the acquisition of property, plant and equipment that remain outstanding at each balance sheet date are classified as capital advances under other non-current assets. The cost of assets not ready for use at that date is disclosed as capital work-in-progress.

Subsequent expenditure is capitalized when future economic benefits are probable and the cost can be measured reliably. The cost and related accumulated depreciation are removed from the financial statements upon sale or retirement of the asset.

### Impairment

Property, plant and equipment are evaluated for recoverability when events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. The recoverable amount, being the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use, is determined on an individual asset basis unless the asset does not generate largely independent cash flows. In that case, recoverability is assessed for the relevant cash-generating unit (CGU).

An impairment loss recognized in the interim condensed Statement of Profit and Loss equals the amount by which the carrying value exceeds the estimated recoverable amount. An impairment loss is reversed when estimates used to determine recoverable amount change, subject to the asset's carrying amount not exceeding the amount that would have been determined, net of accumulated depreciation, had no impairment loss been recognized in prior years.
