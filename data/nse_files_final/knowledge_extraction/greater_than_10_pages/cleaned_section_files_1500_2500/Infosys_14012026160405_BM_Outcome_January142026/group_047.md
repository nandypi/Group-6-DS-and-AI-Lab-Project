## 2.6 Provisions and other contingencies

### 2.6.1 Provisions

A provision is recognized when, as a result of a past event, the Group has a present legal or constructive obligation that is reasonably estimable and it is probable that an outflow of economic benefits will be required to settle the obligation. Provisions are determined by discounting expected future cash flows at a pre-tax rate reflecting current market assessments of the time value of money and risks specific to the liability.

The Group recognizes a reimbursement asset only when it is virtually certain that reimbursement will be received if the Group settles the obligation.

A contingent liability is:

- A possible obligation arising from past events whose existence will be confirmed only by the occurrence or non-occurrence of one or more uncertain future events not wholly within the Group’s control; or
- A present obligation arising from past events that is not recognized because an outflow of resources embodying economic benefits is not probable or the amount cannot be measured with sufficient reliability.

### a. Post sales client support

The Group provides clients with fixed-period post-sales support on fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts. Costs associated with these services are accrued when the related revenues are recorded and included in cost of sales. The Group estimates these costs based on historical experience and periodically reviews the estimates for material changes in assumptions and likelihood of occurrence.

### b. Onerous contracts

Provisions for onerous contracts are recognized when the expected benefits to be derived from a contract are lower than the unavoidable costs of meeting future obligations.

Estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded in the period in which the losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract. The provision is measured at the present value of the lower of:

- The expected cost of terminating the contract; and
- The expected net cost of continuing with the contract.

Before establishing a provision, the Group recognizes any impairment loss on assets associated with the contract.

## McCamish Cybersecurity incident

In November 2023, certain systems of Infosys McCamish Systems LLC (“McCamish”), a subsidiary of Infosys BPM Limited, which is wholly owned by Infosys Limited, were encrypted by ransomware. This resulted in the non-availability of certain applications and systems.

McCamish initiated its incident response and engaged cybersecurity and other specialists to investigate, respond to the incident, and remediate and restore affected applications and systems. By December 31, 2023, McCamish had substantially remediated and restored the affected applications and systems with assistance from external specialists.

A third-party cybersecurity firm conducted investigative analysis to determine whether, and to what extent, company or customer data had been subject to unauthorized access or exfiltration. McCamish also engaged a third-party eDiscovery vendor to assess the extent and nature of the data. McCamish and the eDiscovery vendor identified corporate customers and individuals whose information was subject to unauthorized access and exfiltration. McCamish processes personal data on behalf of its corporate customers.

Between March 6, 2024 and July 25, 2024, six actions were filed against McCamish in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Georgia. The actions arose from the cybersecurity incident initially disclosed on November 3, 2023. The actions were consolidated, and a consolidated class action complaint was filed on November 7, 2024, purportedly on behalf of all persons residing in the United States whose personally identifiable information was compromised in the incident, including those who received notice of the incident.

On December 20, 2024, the Court granted the parties’ joint motion to stay proceedings while they pursued mediation. On March 13, 2025, McCamish and the plaintiffs engaged in mediation, resulting in an in-principle agreement for a proposed settlement of the class action lawsuits against McCamish and seven class action lawsuits arising from the incident that had been filed against McCamish’s customers.

Under the settlement terms, McCamish agreed to pay $17.5 million, approximately ₹150 crore, into a fund to settle these matters. On December 18, 2025, the Court granted final approval of the settlement. If the settlement is not appealed within 30 days, it will become effective and resolve all allegations made in the class action lawsuits without admission of liability.

During the three months ended March 31, 2025, McCamish recorded an accrual of $17.5 million, approximately ₹150 crore, for the settlement and recognized an insurance reimbursement receivable of $17 million, approximately ₹145 crore. The receivable was offset against the settlement expense of $17.5 million, approximately ₹150 crore, in the Statement of Comprehensive Income.

McCamish may incur additional costs, including costs arising from indemnities or damages and claims. The amount of these additional costs was indeterminable at that time.

## Government Investigation

The U.S. Department of Justice (“DOJ”) is conducting an investigation into how the Company classified certain H-1B visa-recipient employees working for one of its clients in immigration documents filed with certain U.S. government authorities.

The Company is engaged in discussions with the DOJ regarding the ongoing investigation and continues its own inquiry. At this stage, the Company is unable to predict the outcome, including whether it could have a material adverse effect on the Company’s business and results of operations.

## Others

Apart from the matters described above, the Group is subject to legal proceedings and claims arising in the ordinary course of business. Management reasonably expects that these actions, when ultimately concluded and determined, may not have a material adverse effect on the Group’s results of operations or financial condition.

## 2.7 Property, plant and equipment

## Accounting Policy

Property, plant and equipment are stated at cost, less accumulated depreciation and impairment, if any. Costs directly attributable to acquisition are capitalized until the assets are ready for use as intended by management.

Periodic depreciation is determined using estimates of an asset’s expected useful life and residual value at the end of its life. The Group depreciates property, plant and equipment over their estimated useful lives using the straight-line method.

| Asset | Estimated useful life |
|---|---|
| Building | 22–25 years |
| Plant and machinery (1) | 5 years |
| Computer equipment | 3–5 years |
| Furniture and fixtures | 5 years |
| Vehicles | 5 years |
| Leasehold improvements | Lower of the asset’s useful life or the lease term |
