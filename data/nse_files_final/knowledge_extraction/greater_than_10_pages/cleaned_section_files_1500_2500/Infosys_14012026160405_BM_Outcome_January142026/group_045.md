## 2.3.3 Derecognition of financial instruments

The Group derecognizes a financial asset when the contractual rights to its cash flows expire, or when it transfers the financial asset and the transfer qualifies for derecognition under IFRS 9.

A financial liability, or part of a financial liability, is derecognized from the Group’s balance sheet when the obligation specified in the contract is discharged, cancelled, or expires.

## 2.3.4 Fair value of financial instruments

The Group determines the fair value of financial instruments using methods and assumptions based on market conditions and risks existing at each reporting date. Methods include discounted cash flow analysis, quoted market prices, option pricing models, market multiples, and dealer quotes.

Fair-value measurement methods provide a general approximation of value, which may not be realized.

For financial assets and liabilities maturing within one year from the balance sheet date and not carried at fair value, carrying amounts approximate fair value because of their short maturities.

## 2.3.5 Impairment

The Group recognizes loss allowances using the expected credit loss (ECL) model for financial assets and unbilled revenue that are not fair valued through profit or loss.

- For trade receivables and unbilled revenues with no significant financing component, the loss allowance equals lifetime ECL.
- For all other financial assets, expected credit losses equal 12-month ECL unless credit risk has increased significantly since initial recognition, in which case lifetime ECL is recognized.

The Group determines credit loss allowances using historical loss experience adjusted for current and estimated future economic conditions, including conditions relating to the industries in which the Group operates and the countries where it conducts business.

The ECL or reversal required to adjust the loss allowance at the reporting date is recognized as an impairment loss or gain in the condensed consolidated statement of comprehensive income.
