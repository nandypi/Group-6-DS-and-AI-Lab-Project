## Basis for Opinion

The audit of the Statement was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under Section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013. The auditors stated that they were independent of the Company in accordance with the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI) Code of Ethics and had fulfilled their other ethical responsibilities under the applicable legal and ethical requirements.

## Management's and Board of Directors' Responsibilities for the Statement

The Statement, including the standalone financial results, was the responsibility of the Company's Board of Directors and was approved by them for issuance. It was compiled from the related audited interim condensed standalone financial statements for the three months and six months ended September 30, 2025.

The Board's responsibilities included:

- Preparing and presenting the standalone financial results, comprehensive income, and other financial information in accordance with the recognition and measurement principles of Ind AS 34, Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013, applicable rules, generally accepted accounting principles in India, and Regulation 33 of the LODR Regulations.
- Maintaining adequate accounting records to safeguard the Company's assets and prevent and detect fraud and other irregularities.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls that operate effectively to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting records and the preparation of financial statements that are free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error.
- Assessing the Company's ability to continue as a going concern.
- Disclosing, where applicable, matters related to going concern.
- Using the going-concern basis of accounting unless the Board intends to liquidate the Company, cease operations, or has no realistic alternative.
- Overseeing the Company's financial reporting process.

## Auditor's Responsibilities for audit of the Standalone Financial Results for the quarter and half year ended September 30, 2025

The auditors' objectives were to obtain reasonable assurance about whether the Statement as a whole was free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report containing their opinion.

As part of the audit, the auditors:

- Identified and assessed the risks of material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and designed audit procedures responsive to those risks.
- Obtained sufficient and appropriate audit evidence to provide a basis for their opinion.
- Recognized that the risk of failing to detect a material misstatement resulting from fraud is higher than for one resulting from error because fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or the override of internal control.
- Obtained an understanding of internal control relevant to the audit to design appropriate audit procedures, without expressing an opinion on the effectiveness of those controls.
- Evaluated the appropriateness of accounting policies and the reasonableness of accounting estimates made by the Board of Directors.
- Evaluated the appropriateness and reasonableness of disclosures made under Regulation 33 of the LODR Regulations.
- Concluded on the appropriateness of the Board of Directors' use of the going-concern basis of accounting.
- Assessed whether audit evidence indicated the existence of a material uncertainty related to events or conditions that could cast significant doubt on the Company's ability to continue as a going concern.
- Evaluated the overall presentation, structure, content, and disclosures of the Statement, including whether it represented the underlying transactions and events.

If a material uncertainty related to going concern existed, the auditors were required to draw attention in their report to the related disclosures or modify their opinion if those disclosures were inadequate. Their conclusions were based on audit evidence obtained up to the date of the auditor's report; future events or conditions could cause the Company to cease to continue as a going concern.

Materiality was described as the magnitude of misstatements, individually or in aggregate, that could reasonably be expected to influence the economic decisions of a reasonably knowledgeable user of the Statement. The auditors considered quantitative materiality and qualitative factors when planning the audit, evaluating audit results, and assessing identified misstatements.

The auditors communicated with those charged with governance regarding the planned scope and timing of the audit, significant audit findings, and significant deficiencies in internal control identified during the audit. They also provided a statement confirming compliance with relevant independence requirements and communicated relationships or other matters that could reasonably be thought to bear on their independence, together with related safeguards where applicable.

## Reporton theAuditof theInterim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements

## Opinion

The auditors audited the interim condensed consolidated financial statements of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries, collectively referred to as the Group. These statements comprised:

- The condensed consolidated balance sheet as at September 30, 2025.
- The condensed consolidated statement of comprehensive income for the three months and six months ended September 30, 2025.
- The condensed consolidated statement of changes in equity.
- The condensed consolidated statement of cash flows for the six months ended September 30, 2025.
- Notes to the financial statements, including a summary of material accounting policies and other explanatory information.

In the auditors' opinion, the interim condensed consolidated financial statements gave a true and fair view, in conformity with International Accounting Standard 34, "Interim Financial Reporting" (IAS 34), as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board, of:

- The consolidated state of affairs of the Group as at September 30, 2025.
- The consolidated profit and consolidated total comprehensive income for the three months and six months ended September 30, 2025.
- The consolidated changes in equity.
- The consolidated cash flows for the six months ended September 30, 2025.

## Basisfor Opinion

The audit of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing issued by the ICAI. The auditors stated that they were independent of the Group in accordance with the ICAI Code of Ethics and had fulfilled their other ethical responsibilities. They stated that the audit evidence obtained was sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their audit opinion.

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Interim Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements

The respective Boards of Directors of the entities included in the Group were responsible for preparing interim condensed consolidated financial statements that gave a true and fair view of the Group's consolidated financial position, consolidated financial performance, consolidated total comprehensive income, changes in equity, and cash flows in accordance with IAS 34.

Their responsibilities included:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records to safeguard Group assets and prevent and detect fraud and other irregularities.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls that operated effectively to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting records.
- Preparing financial statements that were free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error.
- Assessing the ability of the respective entities to continue as going concerns.
- Disclosing, where applicable, matters related to going concern.
- Using the going-concern basis of accounting unless the respective Boards intended to liquidate their entities, cease operations, or had no realistic alternative.
- Overseeing the Group's financial reporting process.
