## Independent Auditor's Report

To The Members of Infosys Limited

## Report on the Audit of the Consolidated Financial Statements

## Opinion

We have audited the consolidated financial statements of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries, collectively referred to as the Group. The consolidated financial statements comprise:

- The Consolidated Balance Sheet as at March 31, 2026.
- The Consolidated Statement of Profit and Loss, including Other Comprehensive Income, for the year ended March 31, 2026.
- The Consolidated Statement of Changes in Equity.
- The Consolidated Statement of Cash Flows.
- Notes to the financial statements, including material accounting policies and other explanatory information.

In the auditors’ opinion, the consolidated financial statements:

- Provide the information required by the Companies Act, 2013.
- Give a true and fair view of the consolidated state of affairs of the Group as at March 31, 2026.
- Present the Group’s consolidated profit, other comprehensive income, changes in equity, and cash flows for the year ended March 31, 2026.
- Conform with the Indian Accounting Standards prescribed under Section 133 of the Act and other generally accepted accounting principles in India.

## Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under Section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013.

The auditors were independent of the Group in accordance with the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India, together with the ethical requirements relevant to the audit under the Act and related rules. The auditors stated that they had fulfilled their other ethical responsibilities and believed that the audit evidence obtained was sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their opinion.

## Key Audit Matters

Key audit matters were those matters that, in the auditors’ professional judgment, were of most significance in the audit of the consolidated financial statements for the current period. These matters were addressed in the context of the audit of the consolidated financial statements as a whole, and the auditors did not provide separate opinions on them.

### 1. Revenue recognition

The Group’s customer contracts include multiple products and services. Revenue is derived from:

- Software development and related services.
- Maintenance.
- Consulting and package implementation.
- Licensing of software products and platforms across the Group’s core and digital offerings.
- Business process management services.

The Group assesses the services promised in each contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying distinct performance obligations, deliverables, and whether customers can benefit independently from those deliverables involves significant judgment.

Certain integrated service arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when the Group is the principal. The Group evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include:

- Whether the Group is primarily responsible for fulfilling the promise.
- Inventory risk.
- Pricing discretion.
- Other relevant factors.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using either:

1. A straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
2. The percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of customer benefits and the Group’s fulfilment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.

The selection of the revenue-recognition method requires judgment based on the contractual promises and the nature of the deliverables.

Revenue recognition was identified as a key audit matter because it involves management judgment regarding:

- Identification of distinct performance obligations.
- Determination of whether the Group acts as principal or agent.
- Determination of whether fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method.

Refer to Notes 1.5 and 2.18 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.

#### Auditor’s Response

The principal audit procedures included:

- Testing the effectiveness of controls relating to:
  - Identification of distinct performance obligations.
  - Determination of whether the Group acts as principal or agent.
  - Determination of the appropriate method for recognizing fixed-price maintenance revenue.
- Selecting a sample of customer contracts.
- Reading contract documents, including master service agreements and other documents forming part of the agreements.
- Identifying significant contractual terms and deliverables.
- Assessing management’s conclusions regarding distinct performance obligations, principal-versus-agent determination, and the revenue-recognition method for fixed-price maintenance contracts.

### 2. Revenue recognition — Fixed-price contracts using the percentage-of-completion method

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably either:

1. On a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
2. Using the percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and fulfilment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts, where performance obligations are satisfied over time, is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.

Under this method, the Group determines actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs required to complete the contract. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because of the direct relationship between input and productivity.

Estimating total efforts or costs requires significant judgment and is reassessed throughout the contract period based on the latest available information. The estimate of total efforts or costs required to complete fixed-price contracts was identified as a key audit matter because it involves significant judgment and high inherent uncertainty. The assessment considers:

- Contract progress.
- Efforts or costs incurred to date.
- Estimated efforts or costs required to complete the remaining performance obligations.

The matter required a high degree of auditor judgment and greater audit effort to evaluate the reasonableness of revenue recognized on fixed-price contracts.

Refer to Notes 1.5 and 2.18 to the Consolidated Financial Statements.

#### Auditor’s Response

The principal audit procedures included:

- Testing controls over:
  - Recording efforts or costs incurred.
  - Estimating efforts or costs required to complete remaining contract performance obligations.
  - Access and application controls for time recording, allocation, and budgeting systems that prevent unauthorized changes to recorded efforts incurred.
- Selecting a sample of fixed-price customer contracts measured using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Evaluating management’s ability to estimate progress by comparing actual efforts or costs incurred with prior-year estimates or budgeted efforts and costs for fulfilled performance obligations.
- Comparing efforts or costs incurred with the Group’s estimates to identify significant variations and assessing whether those variations were appropriately considered in estimating remaining costs or efforts.
- Testing estimates against delivery milestones, customer acceptances, and customer sign-offs to identify possible delays requiring changes to estimated costs or efforts for the remaining performance obligations.
