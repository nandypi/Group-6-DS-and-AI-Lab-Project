## Risk management report

Over the last fiscal year, businesses worldwide faced challenges related to geopolitical shifts, economic uncertainties, supply chain disruptions, evolving regulations, advances in artificial intelligence, technology upheavals, and cybersecurity risks. Infosys’ enterprise risk management processes were used to identify and address emerging threats and support resilience and decision-making.

*Deepak Bhalla, EVP – Chief Risk Officer*

The risk-related information in this section may not be exhaustive. It includes forward-looking statements, and actual results may differ materially because of known or unknown risks and uncertainties. Readers are directed to the detailed discussion of risk factors and related disclosures in the Company’s regulatory filings.

Infosys’ Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) function supports the achievement of strategic objectives by identifying, analyzing, assessing, mitigating, monitoring, and governing risks and potential threats. The ERM framework considers the Company’s values, culture, and commitments to employees, customers, investors, regulators, partners, and the community.

Strategic decisions consider primary, secondary, consequential, and residual risks. The ERM function supports resource allocation through qualitative and quantitative risk-impact assessments and prioritization based on the Company’s risk appetite. Risk impact and likelihood are assessed using a four-point scale, and risks are categorized accordingly. Risks are mitigated in order of criticality: critical, high, medium, and low.

Low impact is the default risk appetite for all risks. Impact definitions, preventive, detective, and corrective controls, and governance mechanisms are used to manage risks and keep residual risk exposures within acceptable limits.

The ERM framework also identifies potential opportunities during risk assessments. It covers strategy and strategy execution, operational, legal, and compliance risks, each of which may have internal or external dimensions. Appropriate risk indicators are used to identify risks proactively, including their cumulative impact.

### Emerging risks

Infosys’ emerging-risk process identifies, assesses, plans for, and monitors risks arising from the internal and external environment. It includes horizon scanning and SWOT analysis covering political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental aspects. Emerging risks are monitored continuously and discussed periodically in risk councils and with the Risk Management Committee (RMC) of the Board.

### Risk categories

| Risk category | Description |
|---|---|
| Strategy and strategy execution | Risks arising from the choices made in defining the Company’s strategy and from the successful execution of that strategy. Industry and competitiveness risks are analyzed and mitigated through choices related to target markets, market offerings, the business model, and the talent base. |
| Operational | Risks affecting policies, procedures, people, and systems, potentially impacting service delivery or operations or compromising core values and business practices. Examples include internal process inefficiencies, human rights, fraud, business disruptions caused by natural calamities, climate change events, human conflicts, system failures, and cybersecurity attacks. |
| Legal and compliance | Risks arising from threats to financial, organizational, or reputational standing resulting from litigation, non-conformance with laws, regulatory or geopolitical developments, codes of conduct, and contractual compliance. |

## Integrated Enterprise Risk Management Framework

Infosys has adopted an integrated ERM framework implemented across the organization by the Risk Management Office. The framework incorporates practices based on COSO and ISO 31000 and is tailored to the Company’s business requirements.

Risk management covers the first, second, and third lines of defence. It is embedded in core business processes, day-to-day decision-making, development processes, and project execution. Risk management responsibilities are included in the roles of senior management, middle management, and employees who engage with clients. Management at all levels is responsible for proactively managing risks associated with strategic goals. Financial incentives are linked to the achievement of strategic goals.

The first line of defence evaluates risks and opportunities before key business decisions are made. It works with corporate ERM through risk councils and sub-risk councils established across organizational units.

The second line of defence includes functions such as the Information Security Group (ISG), Data Privacy Office (DPO), Quality, Delivery Risk Management (DRM), Business Continuity, Legal, and Compliance. These functions work with ERM to identify, assess, report, and govern risks. Risks are primarily mitigated by the first line and tracked and governed by the second line. They are reported to executive leadership through councils including the Operational Risk Council (ORC) and the Legal & Compliance Risk Council (LCRC). Key risks are also presented quarterly to the RMC of the Board.

The third line of defence is internal audit, which periodically reviews risks and controls to assess the effectiveness of the risk management process and reports independently. ERM inputs on key risks are considered when preparing the audit plan.

Infosys maintains a single hierarchical risk repository from L1 to L5, covering strategic to tactical risks. These risks are governed through the integrated ERM framework.

The Company periodically conducts an independent external assessment of ERM process maturity. The maturity assessment was conducted in March 2024.

Risk management is supported by forums for risk discussions and decision-making at multiple levels. Awareness and training programs covering applicable business risks are conducted regularly for employees and contractors. Lex, Infosys’ in-house learning platform, provides risk management courses accessible globally. Periodic tabletop exercises are conducted in key areas to assess preparedness for high-impact, low-probability risks.
