## Independent Auditor's Report

To The Members Of Infosys Limited

## Report on the Audit of the Standalone Financial Statements

## Opinion

The auditors audited the standalone financial statements of Infosys Limited for the year ended March 31, 2026. These statements comprise:

- Balance Sheet as at March 31, 2026
- Statement of Profit and Loss, including Other Comprehensive Income
- Statement of Changes in Equity
- Statement of Cash Flows
- Notes to the financial statements, including material accounting policies and explanatory information

In the auditors' opinion, the standalone financial statements provide the information required under the Companies Act, 2013, and give a true and fair view, in conformity with the Indian Accounting Standards prescribed under Section 133 of the Act and other generally accepted accounting principles in India, of:

- The Company's state of affairs as at March 31, 2026
- Its profit and other comprehensive income for the year ended on that date
- Changes in equity for the year ended on that date
- Its cash flows for the year ended on that date

## Basis for Opinion

The audit was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing specified under Section 143(10) of the Companies Act, 2013.

The auditors stated that they were independent of the Company in accordance with the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India and had fulfilled their other ethical responsibilities. They considered the audit evidence obtained to be sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their opinion.

## Key Audit Matters

Key audit matters were matters that, in the auditors' professional judgment, were of most significance in the audit of the standalone financial statements for the current period. These matters were addressed in the context of the standalone financial statements as a whole, and no separate opinion was provided on them.

### 1. Revenue recognition

Infosys's customer contracts may include multiple products and services. Revenue is derived from:

- Software development and related services
- Maintenance
- Consulting and package implementation
- Licensing of software products and platforms across core and digital offerings
- Business process management services

The Company assesses the services promised in each contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. This involves significant judgment regarding the deliverables and whether the customer can benefit independently from them.

For integrated services arrangements involving subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue is recorded:

- Net of costs when the Company acts as an agent between the customer and the vendor
- Gross when the Company is the principal in the transaction

The Company assesses whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. Factors considered include:

- Responsibility for fulfilling the promise to provide the specified goods or services
- Inventory risk
- Pricing discretion
- Other relevant factors

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using either:

- A straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period
- The percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits from the services and the Company's fulfilment costs is uneven because the services are generally discrete and non-repetitive

The method used to recognize fixed-price maintenance revenue requires judgment based on the contractual promises and the nature of the deliverables.

Revenue recognition was identified as a key audit matter because it involves management judgment in:

1. Identifying distinct performance obligations
2. Determining whether the Company is acting as a principal or an agent
3. Determining whether fixed-price maintenance revenue should be recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method

Refer to Notes 1.4 and 2.18 to the Standalone Financial Statements.

#### Auditor's Response

The principal audit procedures included:

- Testing the effectiveness of controls relating to:
  - Identification of distinct performance obligations
  - Determination of whether the Company is acting as a principal or an agent
  - Determination of the recognition method for fixed-price maintenance revenue
- Selecting samples of customer contracts
- Obtaining and reading contract documents, including master service agreements and other documents forming part of the agreements
- Identifying significant contractual terms and deliverables
- Assessing management's conclusions regarding performance obligations, principal-versus-agent determinations, and the revenue recognition method

### 2. Revenue recognition - Fixed price contracts using the percentage of completion method

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably either:

1. On a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period; or
2. Using the percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits from the services and the Company's fulfilment costs is uneven because the services are generally discrete and non-repetitive.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts, where performance obligations are satisfied over time, is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.

The percentage-of-completion method requires the Company to determine actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of the estimated total efforts or costs required to complete the contract. Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress because there is a direct relationship between input and productivity.

Estimating total efforts or costs requires significant judgment and is reassessed throughout the contract period based on the latest available information. The source text also states that provisions for estimated losses on uncompleted contracts are recorded, but the sentence is incomplete in the supplied section.

The estimate of total efforts or costs required to complete fixed-price contracts was identified as a key audit matter because:

- The estimate involves significant judgment
- The estimate has high inherent uncertainty
- The estimate requires consideration of contract progress
- The estimate requires consideration of efforts or costs incurred to date
- The estimate requires estimation of efforts or costs needed to complete the remaining performance obligations

This matter required a high degree of auditor judgment and increased audit effort to evaluate the reasonableness of revenue recognized on fixed-price contracts.

Refer to Notes 1.4 and 2.18 to the Standalone Financial Statements.

#### Auditor's Response

The principal audit procedures included:

- Testing controls relating to:
  - Recording efforts or costs incurred
  - Estimating efforts or costs required to complete remaining contract performance obligations
  - Access and application controls for time recording, allocation, and budgeting systems
- Assessing controls designed to prevent unauthorized changes to recorded efforts incurred
- Selecting samples of fixed-price contracts measured using the percentage-of-completion method
- Evaluating management's ability to estimate progress by comparing actual efforts or costs incurred with prior-year estimates or budgeted efforts and costs for fulfilled performance obligations
- Comparing efforts or costs incurred with the Company's estimates to identify significant variations
- Evaluating whether significant variations were appropriately considered in estimating remaining costs or efforts to complete the contracts
- Testing estimates for consistency with delivery milestones and customer acceptances or sign-offs
- Identifying possible delays in achieving milestones that could require changes to estimated costs or efforts needed to complete the remaining performance obligations
