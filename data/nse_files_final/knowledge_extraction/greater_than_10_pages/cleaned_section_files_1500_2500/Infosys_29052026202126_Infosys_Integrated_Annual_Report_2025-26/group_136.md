## Credit risk

Credit risk refers to the risk of counterparty default resulting in a financial loss. Maximum exposure to credit risk at the reporting date was primarily from trade receivables and unbilled revenues:

- Trade receivables: ₹35,234 crore at March 31, 2026, compared with ₹31,158 crore at March 31, 2025.
- Unbilled revenues: ₹17,221 crore at March 31, 2026, compared with ₹15,083 crore at March 31, 2025.

Trade receivables and unbilled revenues are typically unsecured and primarily relate to customers located in the United States of America and Europe. Credit risk is managed through credit approvals, credit limits, and continuous monitoring of customer creditworthiness. The Group uses an expected credit loss model and a provision matrix for trade receivables and unbilled revenues, incorporating available credit reports and related credit information.

The Group’s exposure is influenced mainly by individual customer characteristics and concentration among its largest customers. No single customer contributed more than 10% of outstanding trade receivables and unbilled revenues.

Credit risk on cash and cash equivalents is limited because the Group generally invests in deposits with banks carrying high ratings from international and domestic credit-rating agencies. Ratings are monitored periodically, using the latest available ratings at the date of approval of the Consolidated financial statements.

The Group’s investments primarily include mutual fund units, target maturity fund units, tax-free bonds, certificates of deposit, commercial paper, treasury bills, government securities, non-convertible debentures, and quoted bonds issued by government and quasi-government organizations. Counterparty risk is assessed using criteria including Tier I Capital, Capital Adequacy Ratio, credit rating, profitability, non-performing asset levels, and deposit base. These risks are monitored regularly under the Group’s risk management program.

## Liquidity risk

Liquidity risk is the risk that the Group will be unable to settle or meet its obligations on time.

The Group’s principal sources of liquidity are cash and cash equivalents and cash generated from operations. The Group has no outstanding borrowings and believes its working capital is sufficient to meet current requirements.

At March 31, 2026, working capital was ₹51,167 crore, including cash and cash equivalents of ₹22,201 crore and current investments of ₹12,950 crore. At March 31, 2025, working capital was ₹54,249 crore, including cash and cash equivalents of ₹24,455 crore and current investments of ₹12,482 crore.

## 2.12 Equity

## Ordinary shares

Ordinary shares are classified as equity share capital. Incremental costs directly attributable to issuing new ordinary shares, share options, and buybacks are recognized as a deduction from equity, net of related tax effects.

## Treasury shares

When an entity within the Group purchases the Company’s ordinary shares, the consideration paid, including directly attributable incremental costs, is presented as a deduction from total equity until the shares are cancelled, sold, or reissued. When treasury shares are subsequently sold or reissued, the amount received is recognized as an increase in equity, and the resulting surplus or deficit is transferred to or from securities premium.
