The depositary is not responsible if it determines that it is unlawful or impractical to make a distribution available to ADS holders. The company is not obligated to register ADSs, shares, rights, or other securities under the Securities Act, or to take other action to permit their distribution to ADS holders. Accordingly, ADS holders may not receive distributions made on the company’s shares, or any value for those distributions, if the company or the depositary determines that making them available is illegal or impracticable.

## Deposit, Withdrawal and Cancellation

## How are ADSs issued?

The depositary will deliver ADSs when equity shares, or evidence of rights to receive equity shares, are deposited with the custodian by an ADS holder or broker. After receiving payment of its fees and expenses, applicable taxes and charges, and any required certifications under the deposit agreement, the depositary will register and deliver the requested number of ADSs to the entitled person or persons.

## How do ADS holders cancel an American Depositary Share?

ADS holders may surrender their ADSs at the depositary’s corporate trust office or provide appropriate instructions to their broker. After payment of applicable fees, expenses, taxes, and charges, the depositary will deliver the equity shares and other deposited securities underlying the ADSs to the holder or a designated person at the custodian’s office. At the holder’s request, and at the holder’s risk and expense, the depositary may deliver the deposited securities at its corporate trust office to the extent permitted by law.

## How do ADS holders interchange between Certificated ADSs and Uncertificated ADSs?

An ADS holder may surrender an ADR to the depositary to exchange the related certificated ADSs for uncertificated ADSs. The depositary will cancel the ADR and provide a statement confirming ownership of the uncertificated ADSs. Conversely, upon receiving proper instructions from a holder of uncertificated ADSs, the depositary will execute and deliver an ADR evidencing those ADSs.

## How do you vote?

ADS holders may instruct the depositary to vote the equity shares or other deposited securities underlying their ADSs at meetings where they are entitled to vote under applicable law, the company’s memorandum and articles of association, and the provisions governing the deposited securities. Alternatively, an ADS holder may withdraw the underlying equity shares and exercise voting rights directly, although the holder may not receive notice of a meeting sufficiently in advance to do so.

If the company timely requests voting instructions, the depositary will notify ADS holders of an upcoming meeting, including a postal ballot, and arrange to deliver the company’s voting materials. The materials will include:

- Information contained in the meeting notice or solicitation of consents or proxies.
- A statement that ADS holders of record on the ADS record date may instruct the depositary on how to exercise voting rights associated with their ADSs, subject to applicable law and governing documents.
- Instructions for submitting voting directions, including notification of those directions to the company’s chairman or a designated director, who may attend the meeting and vote the deposited securities as instructed.
- Information on how instructions may be given or deemed given if no instructions are received, including the possibility of a discretionary proxy to a person designated by the company.

Voting instructions may cover only ADSs representing an integral number of equity shares or other deposited securities. For instructions to be valid, the depositary must receive them in writing by the specified deadline. Subject to applicable law and the company’s memorandum and articles of association, the depositary will try to vote, or cause the deposited securities to be voted, as instructed.

If the company timely requests voting instructions but receives no instructions for deposited securities represented by an ADS holder’s ADSs, the depositary will deem the holder to have instructed it to give a discretionary proxy to a person designated by the company. Subject to receiving required legal opinions and representations, the depositary will give that discretionary proxy. No instruction will be deemed given, and no discretionary proxy will be given, if the company informs the depositary that it does not want the proxy given, substantial opposition exists, or the matter would adversely affect the rights of equity holders.

Under Indian law, equity-share voting is by show of hands unless a poll is demanded by a shareholder or shareholders present in person or by proxy who either:

- Hold at least 10% of the total shares entitled to vote on the resolution; or
- Hold shares on which an aggregate sum of at least ₹5,00,000, or a higher prescribed amount, has been paid up.

Every company whose equity shares are listed on a recognized stock exchange, and every company with at least 1,000 shareholders, must provide shareholders with electronic voting facilities for resolutions proposed at general meetings. A proxy, including a body corporate represented by an authorized representative, may vote on a resolution.

If the depositary receives instructions from ADS holders to demand a poll, it may, subject to receiving required legal opinions and representations, request the company’s chairman or a designated person to demand a poll. The chairman or designated person will endeavor to do so.

The company cannot assure ADS holders that they will receive voting materials in time to instruct the depositary to vote the underlying equity shares.

The depositary and its agents are not responsible for failing to carry out voting instructions or for the manner in which voting instructions are carried out. ADS holders may therefore be unable to exercise their voting rights and may have no recourse if the underlying equity shares are not voted as requested.
