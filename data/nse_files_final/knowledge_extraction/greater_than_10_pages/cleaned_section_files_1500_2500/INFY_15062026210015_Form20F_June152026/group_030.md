## Results for Fiscal 2026 compared to Fiscal 2025

During fiscal 2026, the Company continued Project Maximus, a margin expansion program structured across five pillars:

- **Value-Based Selling:** Identifying opportunities to align pricing with the value clients receive from the Company’s services.
- **Efficient Pyramid:** Reducing effort costs through improved utilization, reduced subcontracting, and improvements in role and location mix.
- **Lean, Automation & Gen AI:** Combining productivity and process improvements using automation technologies, including generative AI.
- **Improving Key Portfolios:** Improving margins on large client engagements and service lines.
- **Optimization of Indirect Costs:** Using savings from Project Maximus to invest in strategic areas such as talent, AI, and sales and marketing.

## Non-IFRS measure

The Company uses IFRS and non-IFRS financial measures for financial and operational decision-making, including period-to-period performance evaluation and comparisons with competitors. Revenue growth in constant currency and adjusted operating margin are used as non-IFRS measures alongside reported IFRS financial information. These measures should not be considered in isolation or as substitutes for the most directly comparable IFRS measures.

Reported-currency revenue growth includes the effect of currency fluctuations. Constant-currency revenue growth excludes these effects by converting current-period revenues in local currencies into U.S. dollars using prior-period exchange rates and comparing the result with prior-period reported revenues.

Adjusted operating margin excludes the effect of provisions arising from the Labour Codes notified by the Government of India on November 21, 2025, relating to gratuity liability, defined benefit plan past service costs from plan amendments, and compensated absences. The reconciliation to reported IFRS measures is provided in the section on Operating profit.

## Revenues

The Company generates revenue principally from services provided on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-timeframe basis. Many client contracts, including fixed-price and fixed-timeframe contracts, may be terminated by clients with or without cause on short notice periods of 0 to 90 days. Revenue is generally collected as contractual milestones are completed, so terminated contracts are subject to collection for the portion completed through termination.

The Company monitors contract progress and change orders, including its and its clients’ ability to perform, extraordinary conditions that could lead to termination, and historical client performance. For fixed-price and fixed-timeframe projects, the Company bears the risk of cost overruns and inflation. Operating results may therefore be adversely affected by inaccurate estimates of completion costs and dates, including wage inflation and currency exchange rates. Project completion estimates are revised periodically; such revisions have not to date had a material adverse effect on operating results or financial condition.

The Company experiences periodic pricing pressure. Clients may seek volume discounts, fixed-price or fixed-timeframe arrangements, or reduced rates. The Company seeks to use fixed-price arrangements where project specifications are complete.

| Revenues (dollars in millions) | 20,158 | 19,277 | 881 | 4.6% |

Revenue growth in fiscal 2026 was primarily attributable to increased realization from role mix, improved pricing and productivity, incremental revenue from acquired companies, and favorable foreign currency translation.

Revenues in reported currency terms were $20,158 million in fiscal 2026, representing growth of 4.6% compared with fiscal 2025. Revenues in constant-currency terms grew by 3.1% compared with fiscal 2025.

The U.S. dollar depreciated during fiscal 2026 by 4.7% against the United Kingdom pound sterling, 8.4% against the euro, and 1.5% against the Australian dollar.

Overall segment profitability decreased primarily because of lower utilization and investments in talent, AI, sales, and marketing. These effects were partially offset by increased realization from pricing and productivity, improved onsite mix, and foreign currency translation.

Energy, Utilities, Resources and Services profitability decreased mainly because of acquisition-related effects and higher subcontractor costs.

Life Sciences profitability decreased mainly because of ramp-up and transition costs for large deals and declining revenues. Large deals typically have lower margins during the initial period because of transition costs and a higher onsite mix.

### Onsite and offshore delivery

The Company generally assumes full project management responsibility for its projects. Projects are divided into components executed simultaneously at client sites and at Development Centers outside India (“onsite”), and at Global Development Centers in India (“offshore”).

Onsite work generally has higher billing rates and higher compensation and other expenses. It typically generates higher revenue per capita but lower gross margins as a percentage of revenue than work performed at the Company’s facilities in India. Changes in delivery mix therefore affect total revenue, cost of sales, and gross profit.

|---|---:|---:|

| Offshore effort | 76.8% | 76.1% |

### Revenue by service and contract type

Software services, including BPM services, represented 95.2% and 95.3% of total revenues in fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2025, respectively. Products and platforms represented 4.8% and 4.7% of total revenues, respectively.

Fixed-price contracts represented 54% of revenues in both fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2025.

### Employee utilization

Employee utilization for IT services is defined as billed person-months as a proportion of available person-months, excluding sales, administrative, and support personnel. Utilization is managed by monitoring project requirements and timetables. Staffing levels vary according to project size, complexity, duration, and requirements.

Unexpected termination of a significant project may reduce utilization. Technology professionals are also not utilized while enrolled in training programs, particularly new-employee training.
