## Audit and Annual Report

Under the Indian Companies Act, a company must file its financial statements with the Registrar of Companies within 30 days of the AGM.

Under the Listing Regulations, copies of the annual report sent to shareholders, together with the AGM notice, must be submitted to the stock exchanges on which the company’s shares are listed and published on the company’s website no later than the date on which dispatch to shareholders begins.

At least 21 days before the AGM, a listed company must:

- Distribute soft copies of the full annual report to shareholders who have registered their email addresses.
- Distribute physical copies to shareholders who have not registered their email addresses and to shareholders who request physical copies.
- File an annual return containing a list of shareholders and other company information within 60 days after the conclusion of the AGM.

## Reduction of Share Capital

Under the Indian Companies Act, a company may reduce its share capital by special resolution and approval of the court or tribunal of the state in which its registered office is situated, provided that the reduction is authorized by the company’s articles of association. These provisions do not apply to a company share buyback.

## Company Acquisition of Equity Shares

A company may acquire its own equity shares without court or National Company Law Tribunal approval if it complies with the prescribed rules, regulations, and conditions of the Indian Companies Act.

Public companies listed on recognized stock exchanges in India must also comply with the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Buy-back of Securities) Regulations, 2018 (the “Buyback Regulations”). As a public company listed on two recognized stock exchanges in India, the company must comply with the relevant provisions of both the Indian Companies Act and the Buyback Regulations.

The Buyback Regulations provide two methods for buying back shares:

- Tender Offer method
- Open Market method through book building or the stock exchange

For ADS holders to participate in a tender-offer buyback, they must withdraw the equity shares underlying their ADSs before the record date fixed for the buyback and become holders of the equity shares on that record date.

An open-market buyback must be conducted through the stock exchanges in India. There can be no assurance that shares offered by an ADS investor in a company buyback will be accepted.

Conversion of ADSs into underlying equity shares and reconversion of those shares into ADSs is subject to applicable limits on permissible foreign shareholding. Indian law currently permits foreign investment of up to 100% of the company’s share capital.

ADS holder participation in a buyback is also subject to applicable requirements under:

- The Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, and related rules and regulations
- The Income Tax Act, 1961, and related rules and regulations
- The Depository Receipts Scheme, 2014
- The framework for issue of Depositary Receipts dated October 10, 2019, read with related SEBI circulars dated November 28, 2019, October 1, 2020, and December 18, 2020
- Any required approvals from relevant authorities, including the Reserve Bank of India under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999

## Liquidation Rights

Under the Indian Companies Act and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, the following payments have priority over payments to equity shareholders:

- Payments to employees
- Taxes
- Payments to secured and unsecured lenders
- Payments to holders of shares entitled by their terms to preferential repayment over equity shares

In a winding-up, equity shareholders are entitled to repayment of the paid-up capital, or the amount credited as paid-up, after the priority payments described above have been made. Any surplus assets are then distributed to equity shareholders in proportion to their shareholdings.

## Redemption of Equity Shares

Subject to the share buyback provisions described under “Company Acquisition of Equity Shares,” equity shares are not redeemable under the Indian Companies Act.

## Discriminatory Provisions in Articles

The company’s Articles of Association contain no provisions discriminating against or in favor of an existing or prospective holder of the company’s securities because that shareholder owns a substantial number of shares. There are also no restrictions on the company’s securities imposed by another class of securities or by contracts.

## Alteration of Shareholder Rights

Under the Indian Companies Act, and subject to the company’s memorandum or articles of association, the rights of a class of shareholders may be altered or varied by either:

1. Written consent from holders of at least three-fourths of the issued shares of that class; or
2. A special resolution passed at a separate meeting of holders of the issued shares of that class.

If the variation by one class of shareholders affects the rights of another class, the consent of three-fourths of that other class is also required.

If the articles of association contain no relevant provision, an alteration or variation is permitted provided it is not prohibited by the terms of issue of the shares of that class.

## Limitations on the Rights to Own Securities

The subscription, purchase, and sale of shares of an Indian company are governed by Indian laws that restrict the issuance of shares to non-residents and the subsequent transfer of shares by or to non-residents.

Non-residents may invest in the securities of Indian companies subject to conditions prescribed by the RBI. A non-resident may not acquire shares of a listed company on a stock exchange unless the non-resident is:

- Registered with SEBI as a foreign portfolio investor (FPI)
- An individual resident outside India who is a citizen of India (NRI)
- An individual resident outside India registered as an Overseas Citizen of India cardholder under the Citizenship Act, 1955 (OCI)
- A person resident outside India investing under the foreign direct investment (FDI) route who has acquired control of the company in accordance with the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeover) Regulations, 2011 (the “Takeover Code”) and continues to hold that control

Subject to certain conditions, FDI in most industry sectors does not require prior approval from the Government of India or the RBI if foreign ownership does not exceed specified industry-specific thresholds. Conditions include minimum pricing requirements, compliance with the Takeover Code, and ownership restrictions based on the type of foreign investor.

Purchases of ADSs by foreign investors are treated as direct foreign investment in the equity issued by Indian companies. Indian law currently permits foreign investment of up to 100% of the company’s share capital.

Under the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Amendment Rules, 2020, issued on April 22, 2020, prior Government approval is required for foreign investments made on or after April 22, 2020, including subsequent transfers of ownership, from countries sharing a land border with India. This requirement also applies when the beneficial owner of the foreign investment, either at the time of investment or after a change in ownership, is situated in or is a citizen of a country sharing a land border with India.

NRIs and OCIs may purchase or sell equity instruments of a listed Indian company on a recognized Indian stock exchange on a repatriation basis through a branch designated by an authorized dealer.

An NRI or OCI may purchase up to 5% of:

- The total paid-up equity capital of an Indian company on a fully diluted basis; or
- The paid-up value of each series of debentures, preference shares, or share warrants issued by an Indian company

The combined holdings of all NRIs and OCIs may not exceed 10% of the total paid-up equity capital on a fully diluted basis or 10% of the paid-up value of each series of debentures, preference shares, or share warrants. This 10% ceiling may be increased to 24% if approved by a special resolution at a general meeting of shareholders.

NRIs may also make FDI in Indian companies under the FDI route described above.

Overseas corporate bodies controlled by NRIs (OCBs) were previously permitted to invest on favorable terms under the Portfolio Investment Regulations. The RBI no longer recognizes OCBs as an eligible investment vehicle under various foreign exchange routes and schemes.

Transfers of the company’s shares or convertible debentures by sale or gift between two non-residents, other than NRIs, OCIs, or former OCBs, are not subject to RBI approval or pricing restrictions. However, where FDI in the relevant sector requires prior government approval, approval from the Government of India is required for transfers between two non-residents. The information provided states that FDI in the information technology sector does not require prior government approval.
