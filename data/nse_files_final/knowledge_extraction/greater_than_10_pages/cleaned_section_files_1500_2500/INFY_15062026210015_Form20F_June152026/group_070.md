## Revenue Recognition - Refer to Notes 1.5(a) and 2.11 to the consolidated financial statements

## Critical Audit Matter Description

The Group's contracts with customers include multiple products and services. The Group derives revenue from:

- Software development and related services
- Maintenance
- Consulting and package implementation
- Licensing of software products and platforms across the Group's core and digital offerings
- Business process management services

The Group assesses the services promised in a contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying distinct performance obligations, including the deliverables and whether the customer can benefit independently from them, involves significant judgment.

In certain integrated services arrangements, contracts include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded:

- Net of costs when the Group acts as an agent between the customer and the vendor
- Gross when the Group is the principal in the transaction

The Group first evaluates whether it obtains control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer. It considers whether it is primarily responsible for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors to determine whether it controls the products or services and is acting as a principal or an agent.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using either:

1. A straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
2. The percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of customer benefits and the Group's contract-fulfillment costs is uneven because the services are generally discrete and non-repetitive.

The method used to recognize maintenance revenue requires judgment based on the promises in the contract and the nature of the deliverables.

Revenue recognition involving judgments about distinct performance obligations, principal-versus-agent assessments, and the method used for fixed-price maintenance revenue was identified as a critical audit matter and required a higher extent of audit effort.

## How the Critical Audit Matter Was Addressed in the Audit

Audit procedures related to the identification of distinct performance obligations, the determination of whether the Group was acting as a principal or an agent, and the method used to recognize fixed-price maintenance revenue included:

- Testing the effectiveness of controls relating to:
  - Identification of distinct performance obligations
  - Determination of whether the Group was acting as a principal or an agent
  - Determination of whether fixed-price maintenance revenue for certain contracts was recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method
- Selecting a sample of customer contracts and:
  - Obtaining and reading contract documents, including master service agreements and other documents forming part of the agreements
  - Identifying significant terms and deliverables to assess management's conclusions regarding:
    - Identification of distinct performance obligations
    - Whether the Group was acting as a principal or an agent
    - Whether fixed-price maintenance revenue was recognized on a straight-line basis or using the percentage-of-completion method

## Revenue recognition - Fixed price contracts using the percentage of completion method - Refer to Notes 1.5(a) and 2.11 to the consolidated financial statements

## Critical Audit Matter Description

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably using either:

1. A straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period.
2. The percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits from services rendered to the customer and the Group's costs to fulfill the contract is uneven because the services are generally discrete and non-repetitive.

Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts, where performance obligations are satisfied over time, is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.

Under the percentage-of-completion method, the Group determines actual efforts or costs expended to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs to be incurred. Efforts or costs expended are used to measure progress toward completion because there is a direct relationship between input and productivity.

Estimating total efforts or costs involves significant judgment and is assessed throughout the contract period to reflect changes based on the latest available information. Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts, if any, are recorded in the period in which those losses become probable based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

The estimate of total efforts or costs to complete fixed-price contracts measured using the percentage-of-completion method was identified as a critical audit matter. The estimate has high inherent uncertainty and requires consideration of:

- Contract progress
- Efforts or costs incurred to date
- Estimated efforts or costs required to complete the remaining contract performance obligations over the contract term

This matter required a high degree of auditor judgment and a higher extent of audit effort to evaluate the reasonableness of the total estimated revenue recognized on fixed-price contracts.

## How the Critical Audit Matter Was Addressed in the Audit

Audit procedures related to estimates of total expected costs or efforts to complete fixed-price contracts included:

- Testing the effectiveness of controls relating to:
  - Recording efforts or costs incurred
  - Estimating efforts or costs required to complete the remaining contract performance obligations
  - Access and application controls for time-recording, allocation, and budgeting systems that prevent unauthorized changes to recorded efforts incurred
- Selecting a sample of fixed-price customer contracts measured using the percentage-of-completion method and:
  - Evaluating management's ability to reasonably estimate progress toward satisfying performance obligations by comparing actual efforts or costs incurred with prior-year estimates for performance obligations that had been fulfilled
  - Comparing efforts or costs incurred with the Group's estimates of efforts or costs incurred to date to identify significant variations and evaluate whether those variations were appropriately considered in estimating remaining costs or efforts to complete the contract
  - Testing the estimates for consistency with delivery milestones and customer acceptances and sign-offs to identify possible delays in achieving milestones that would require changes to estimated costs or efforts to complete the remaining performance obligations
