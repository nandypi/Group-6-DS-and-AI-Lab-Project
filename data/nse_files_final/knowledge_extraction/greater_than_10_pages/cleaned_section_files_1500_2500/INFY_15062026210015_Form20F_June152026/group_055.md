## Maintenance of Minimum Public Shareholding ('MPS') as a Condition for Continuous Listing

The Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957 were amended on June 4, 2010, to require all listed companies in India to maintain minimum public shareholding of 25%.

For this purpose, “public shareholding” means equity shares held by the public and includes shares underlying depository receipts if:

1. The holder of the depository receipts has the right to issue voting instructions; and
2. The depository receipts are listed on an international exchange in accordance with the Depository Receipt Scheme, 2014.

Equity shares held by a trust established to implement employee benefit schemes under regulations framed by SEBI are excluded from public shareholding.

Existing listed companies with public shareholding below 25% may meet the prescribed threshold through the following methods:

- Sale of shares held by promoters or the promoter group in the open market, subject to applicable conditions:
  - Promoters or the promoter group may sell up to 2% of the listed entity’s total paid-up equity share capital, subject to five times the average monthly trading volume of the shares, in each financial year until the applicable MPS compliance due date under the SCRR.
  - Promoters or the promoter group may sell up to 5% of the listed entity’s paid-up capital during a financial year, provided public holding reaches 25% after the sale. The sale may occur in a single tranche or multiple tranches over a period not exceeding 12 months. The shares sold may not exceed the listed entity’s trading volume during the preceding 12 months from the announcement date.
- Allotment of eligible securities through a qualified institutional placement under Chapter VI of the SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018.
- Issuance of shares to the public through a prospectus.
- Offers for sale of promoter or promoter-group shares to the public through a prospectus.
- Sale of promoter-held shares through the stock exchange mechanism, or secondary market, under SEBI circular no. SEBI/HO/MRD/MRD-PoD-3/P/CIR/2023/10 dated January 10, 2023.
- Rights issues to public shareholders, with promoter and promoter-group shareholders forgoing their entitlement to equity shares arising from the issue, whether present or future.
- Bonus issues to public shareholders, with promoter and promoter-group shareholders forgoing their entitlement to equity shares arising from the issue, whether present or future.
- Increase in public holding through the exercise of options and allotment of shares under an employee stock option scheme, subject to a maximum of 2% of the listed entity’s paid-up equity share capital.
- Transfer of promoter or promoter-group shares to an exchange-traded fund managed by a SEBI-registered mutual fund, subject to a maximum of 5% of the listed entity’s paid-up equity share capital.
- Any other method approved by SEBI on a case-by-case basis.

As of March 31, 2026, Infosys’ public shareholding was approximately 85.38%.

## Voting Rights of Deposited Equity Shares Represented by ADSs

Under Indian law, voting on equity shares is conducted by show of hands unless a poll is demanded by:

- A member or members present in person or by proxy holding at least 10% of the total shares entitled to vote on the resolution; or
- Members holding shares on which an aggregate amount of at least ₹5,00,000, or such higher amount as may be prescribed, has been paid up.

Every company whose equity shares are listed on a recognized stock exchange, and every company with at least 1,000 members, must provide members with electronic voting facilities for resolutions considered at a general meeting. The Listing Regulations require an e-voting facility for all shareholder resolutions in accordance with the Indian Companies Act, 2013.

After receiving notice of a general meeting or a solicitation of consents or proxies from holders of shares or other deposited securities, the Depositary will, as soon as practicable, establish a record date for determining which holders are entitled to provide voting instructions.

The Depositary will then send ADS holders a notice stating:

1. The information contained in the meeting notice and solicitation materials.
2. That each holder as of the Depositary’s record date may instruct the Depositary on the exercise of voting rights, if any, relating to the deposited securities represented by the holder’s ADRs.
3. How voting instructions may be provided, including instructions to give a discretionary proxy to a person designated by Infosys.
4. That, under the agreement with the Depositary, if the Depositary does not receive instructions from a holder, the holder will be deemed to have instructed the Depositary to give a discretionary proxy to a person designated by Infosys, subject to certain conditions.

ADS holders may instruct the Depositary how to exercise voting rights for the shares underlying their ADSs. For instructions to be valid, the Depositary must receive them by the specified date.

The Depositary will, to the extent practicable and in accordance with the deposit agreement, Indian law, and Infosys’ Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association, vote or arrange for its agents to vote the shares or other deposited securities according to ADS holders’ instructions. The Depositary will vote only according to an ADS holder’s instructions and will not exercise voting discretion itself.

Neither the Depositary nor its agents are responsible for:

- Failure to carry out voting instructions.
- The manner in which a vote is cast.
- The effect of any vote.

There is no guarantee that ADS holders will receive voting materials in time to instruct the Depositary. ADS holders, or persons holding ADSs through brokers, dealers or other third parties, may not have the opportunity to exercise voting rights.

## Insider Trading Regulations

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015 cover listed and proposed-to-be-listed securities. They prohibit trading and the communication or procurement of unpublished price-sensitive information in violation of the regulations.

Promoters, members of the promoter group, key managerial personnel and directors must disclose their holdings of company securities within seven days of appointment or of becoming a promoter or promoter-group member.

Every promoter, promoter-group member, designated person and director must disclose to the company securities acquired or disposed of within two trading days of a transaction if the traded value, individually or in a series of transactions during a calendar quarter, exceeds ₹1,000,000 or another prescribed threshold.

The company must notify the stock exchanges where its securities are listed within two trading days after receiving the disclosure or becoming aware of the relevant information.

A listed company may also require other connected persons or classes of connected persons to disclose their holdings and trading in company securities, in a form and at a frequency determined by the company, to monitor compliance with the Insider Trading Regulations.

## CURRENCY EXCHANGE CONTROLS

## General

The subscription, purchase and sale of shares of an Indian company are governed by Indian laws that restrict the issuance of shares to non-residents and the transfer of shares by or to non-residents. These restrictions have been relaxed in recent years.

The applicable framework includes various forms of investment and the restrictions applicable to each, including Indian-law requirements relating to the issuance of ADSs.
