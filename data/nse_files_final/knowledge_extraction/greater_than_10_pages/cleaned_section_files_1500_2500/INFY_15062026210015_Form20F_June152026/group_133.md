## Industrial Espionage

Infosys requires employees to respect competitors’ rights and comply with applicable laws. Employees must not appropriate or unlawfully use information, materials, products, intellectual property, or proprietary or confidential information belonging to suppliers, customers, business partners, competitors, or other parties.

## Governmental Relations

Employees dealing with government clients must follow the principles in Section B of the Code of Conduct, “Ethics in Our Business Activities,” and the Company’s Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption Policy, particularly regarding gifts and entertainment.

Employees must not attempt to influence government employees outside the agreed contractual arrangement. Employment opportunities for former government officials may not be discussed without prior guidance and approval from the Office of Integrity and Compliance. Discussions regarding contracts with businesses in which a government official or employee holds a significant interest also require prior approval.

## Selecting Suppliers

Infosys selects significant suppliers and enters into significant supplier agreements through a competitive bid process where possible. The Company also maintains a Supplier Code of Conduct.

## E. RECORDS, DISCLOSURES AND AUDITS

### Maintaining Accurate Records

Accurate financial transactions and records are critical to the Company’s operations and shareholder trust. As a listed company, Infosys is required to maintain accurate financial reporting and appropriate internal controls and procedures.

Employees involved in financial reporting or accounting must understand and follow applicable accounting and financial reporting principles, standards, laws, rules, regulations, and Company policies and procedures. Senior officers are expected to ensure that internal controls and procedures in their business areas are in place, understood, and followed.

All employees must ensure that business records and reports are honest, accurate, complete, and reliable.

### Ensuring Accurate Public Disclosures

Infosys is committed to full, fair, accurate, timely, and clear disclosures in reports and documents submitted to regulators and in other public communications. Employees must comply with the Company’s disclosure controls and procedures and internal control over financial reporting.

### Auditors

Employees must cooperate in good faith and in accordance with law with outside auditors. They must not mislead auditors regarding financial records, processes, controls, procedures, or other matters under review.

### Interacting with Regulators

Infosys must fully and truthfully cooperate with examinations or requests for information from regulators or law enforcement agencies. Contact with law enforcement agencies or regulators must be coordinated through the Legal Department.

### Records on Legal Hold

A legal hold may be placed on records in connection with litigation or government investigations. A legal hold prevents the destruction of documents that may be required for those matters. Employees must comply with Legal Department instructions while a legal hold remains in effect. The hold remains effective unless released in writing by the Legal Department.

### Record Retention

Company records must be maintained in accordance with the Document Retention and Archival Policy.

### Do Not

- Enter information into Company records that hides the true nature of a financial or non-financial transaction or result.
- Establish an undisclosed or unrecorded fund, account, asset, or liability for an improper purpose.
- Enter into a transaction or agreement that could affect the accurate and timely recording of revenues or expenses.

## F. ADMINISTERING OUR CODE

The Office of Integrity and Compliance administers the Code and reports to the General Counsel and the Audit Committee of the Company.

### Investigations

Infosys investigates potential legal or Code violations. Investigations are conducted confidentially, fairly, and respectfully. Employees must maintain strict confidentiality, cooperate with investigations, and provide accurate information when requested.

If the Company determines that a violation has occurred, it will take remedial action commensurate with the nature of the violation. Employees are encouraged to report potential violations in good faith and are protected from retaliation. Employees who are the subject of an external criminal investigation must promptly inform their manager unless prohibited by law.

### Amendments/Modifications to Our Code

The Board of Directors is responsible for approving and issuing the Code. The Code was first effective on April 10, 2003, with revisions through July 12, 2019; October 13, 2021; October 13, 2022; July 18, 2024; and April 23, 2026.

The Code is reviewed annually to determine whether revisions are required because of changes in law, regulations, the Company’s business, or the business environment. Changes to the Code require approval by the Board of Directors.

### Acknowledgement

Employees and members of the Board of Directors must acknowledge that they have read and understood the Code. Independent directors may also be required to acknowledge acceptance of the Code for Independent Directors.

Failure to read the Code, sign an acknowledgement, or certify online does not exempt anyone from the obligation to comply with it.

### Waivers

A waiver of the Code requires prior written approval from the Office of Integrity and Compliance or, in certain circumstances, the Board of Directors or a committee of the Board. Waivers will be disclosed promptly when required by applicable law.

### Disciplinary Actions

Infosys may take appropriate disciplinary action for violations of the Code. Corrective action may include a verbal or written warning, suspension with or without pay, loss or reduction of a bonus or stock options, or termination of employment.

Disciplinary action depends on the nature, severity, and frequency of the violation and may vary according to local law. Violations of applicable laws or regulations may expose the individual and the Company to civil damages and criminal penalties.

Corrective action may be taken if an individual:

- Violates the Code, Company policies and procedures, or applicable laws.
- Directs others to violate the Code, Company policies and procedures, or applicable laws.
- Knows of a violation or potential violation and fails to report it.
- Fails to effectively monitor the actions of people they manage.
- Fails to cooperate in a Company audit or investigation.
- Fails to participate in required training.
- Retaliates against someone for reporting a concern in good faith or participating in an investigation.
- Discloses information learned during an internal investigation.

## SPEAK UP

Employees may report concerns to their manager, skip-level manager, Human Resources, or the Company’s Helpline. Concerns that may be reported include:

- Inaccurate financial records.
- Accounting and auditing irregularities.
- Bribery, corruption, or illegal payments.
- Criminal conduct or violations of law.
- Discrimination and harassment.
- Conflicts of interest.

Reports may also be made through the Company’s whistleblower channels or to the Chief Compliance Officer. If a concern involves reaching the Chief Compliance Officer, it may be reported to the Audit Committee of Infosys.

Concerns about fellow employees’ behavior may also be raised through the Company’s designated employee reporting channel. Grievances relating to sexual harassment may be raised through the designated sexual-harassment grievance channel.

The Company’s Whistleblower Policy is available on the Infosys intranet and website.
