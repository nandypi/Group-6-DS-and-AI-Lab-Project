## Voting Rights

At any general meeting, the Company provides shareholders with an e-voting facility for resolutions proposed at the AGM. Voting by ballot is also available at the AGM. Members who have not already voted through remote e-voting may vote at the AGM.

The Chairperson has a casting vote in the event of a tie. Any shareholder entitled to attend and vote at a company meeting may appoint a proxy. The proxy instrument must be delivered to the Company at least 48 hours before the meeting. Unless the Articles of Association provide otherwise, a proxy may vote only on a poll.

A corporate shareholder may appoint an authorized representative to vote on its behalf through remote e-voting or at the general meeting. An authorized representative may also appoint a proxy.

Under the SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015, as amended in 2018, remote e-voting must be provided to all shareholders for all shareholder resolutions in accordance with the Indian Companies Act.

Ordinary resolutions may be passed by a simple majority at a general meeting for which the required notice has been given. Special resolutions, including amendments to the Articles of Association, waivers of pre-emptive rights for issuing new shares, and reductions of share capital, require votes in favor to be at least three times the votes against.

Companies listed on a recognized stock exchange with at least 1,000 members must provide electronic voting facilities for resolutions at general meetings. The Company's equity shares are listed on a recognized stock exchange, and the Company has more than 1,000 members. Accordingly, the Company may pass the relevant resolutions through electronic voting.

Two-thirds of the non-independent directors on the Board must retire by rotation each year. One-third of those directors whose office has been held for the longest period must retire. Retiring directors are eligible for reappointment.

Executive directors are appointed by shareholders for a maximum term of five years and are eligible for reappointment after completing their term. An independent director may hold office for up to two consecutive five-year terms and may be reappointed after a three-year cooling-off period, provided the individual is not appointed by or associated with the Company in any capacity during that period.

Cumulative voting for the election of directors is not recognized under applicable Indian law. The Companies Act treats the number of shares held as the number of votes available. A shareholder may cast all votes for, all votes against, or votes partly for and partly against each resolution.

## Register of Shareholders; Record Dates; Transfer of Shares

The Company maintains its register of shareholders electronically through National Securities Depository Limited and Central Depository Services (India) Limited.

To determine shareholders entitled to specified rights, such as dividends or rights issues, the Company may close the register of shareholders for a specified period of up to 30 days at a time. The date on which the closure begins is the record date. The Indian Companies Act requires at least seven days' prior public notice before closure, unless SEBI specifies a shorter period.

The register may not be closed for more than 30 consecutive days at any one time or for more than 45 days in a year. The Listing Regulations require at least 30 days between two record dates.

Following the Depositories Act, 1996, and the repeal of Section 22A of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956, the equity shares of a public company are freely transferable, subject to Section 58 of the Indian Companies Act and Regulation 40 of the Listing Regulations.

Under Section 58(2) of the Indian Companies Act, securities or other interests of a member are freely transferable, except where a contract or arrangement between two or more persons concerning the transfer of securities may be enforced as a contract. If the Board refuses to register a transfer, the shareholder seeking to transfer the shares may appeal to the National Company Law Tribunal.

Effective April 1, 2019, except for transmission or transposition of securities, requests to transfer securities could not be processed unless the securities were held in dematerialized form with a depository. Following subsequent amendments, transmission and transposition of securities held in physical or dematerialized form may also be effected only in dematerialized form.

Under a SEBI circular dated January 25, 2022, listed companies must issue securities only in dematerialized form when processing the following service requests:

1. Issue of duplicate securities certificates.
2. Claims from an unclaimed suspense account.
3. Renewal or exchange of securities certificates.
4. Endorsement.
5. Subdivision or splitting of securities certificates.
6. Consolidation of securities certificates or folios.
7. Transmission.
8. Transposition.

If a share transfer contravenes the Indian Companies Act, the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956, the Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992, related regulations, or other Indian laws, the National Company Law Tribunal may direct a company or depository to remedy the contravention and rectify the relevant registers and records.

The registrar and share transfer agent for the Company's equity shares is KFin Technologies Limited, Hyderabad, India.

## Disclosure of Ownership Interest

Section 89 of the Indian Companies Act requires registered holders who do not hold beneficial interests in shares of Indian companies to declare specified details to the company, including the nature of the holder's interest and details of the beneficial owner.

Beneficial interest includes, directly or indirectly through a contract, arrangement, or otherwise, the right or entitlement of a person, alone or together with another person, to:

1. Exercise or cause to be exercised any or all rights attached to the share.
2. Receive or participate in any dividend or other distribution relating to the share.

A person who fails to make the required declaration within 30 days may be liable for a fine of up to ₹50,000. For a continuing failure, an additional fine of ₹200 per day may apply, subject to a maximum of ₹500,000.

Failure to comply with Section 89 does not affect the Company's obligation to pay dividends to the registered holder of the relevant shares.

It is unclear under Indian law whether Section 89 applies to holders of the Company's ADSs. Investors who exchange ADSs for the underlying equity shares will be subject to Section 89. ADS holders may also be required to comply with notification and disclosure obligations under the Deposit Agreement among the holders, the Company, and the Depositary.

## Declaration of Significant beneficial ownership in the Company

Section 90 of the Indian Companies Act requires individual shareholders, including persons resident outside India, who alone or together with others, or through one or more persons or trusts, hold a beneficial interest of at least 10% in a company's shares, or have the right to exercise or actually exercise significant influence or control over the company, to declare the nature of their interest to the company.

The Companies (Significant Beneficial Ownership) Rules, 2018, as amended, prescribe thresholds and rules for identifying a significant beneficial owner and require filings and disclosures when relevant details change.

A person who fails to submit the required disclosures is subject to a minimum fine of ₹50,000, which may extend to ₹200,000. For a continuing failure, an additional fine of up to ₹1,000 per day may apply after the first day of continuing non-compliance.
