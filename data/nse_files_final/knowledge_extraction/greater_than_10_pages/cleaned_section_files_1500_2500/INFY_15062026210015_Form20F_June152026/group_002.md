## Currency of presentation and certain defined terms

In this Annual Report on Form 20-F:

- “U.S.” or “United States” means the United States of America, its territories, and its possessions.
- “India” means the Republic of India.
- “$,” “dollars,” and “U.S. dollars” mean the legal currency of the United States.
- “₹,” “Rupees,” and “Indian rupees” mean the legal currency of India.
- Consolidated financial statements are presented in U.S. dollars and prepared in accordance with International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) issued by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB).
- A reference to a fiscal year means the fiscal year ended March 31 of that year.
- “We,” “us,” “our,” “Infosys,” “Company,” and “Group” mean Infosys Limited and its consolidated subsidiaries, unless the context indicates otherwise.
- “IT services” excludes business process management services and the products and platforms business.

Unless otherwise stated, translations from Indian rupees to U.S. dollars use the fixing rate in Mumbai on business days for cable transfers in Indian rupees, as published by the Foreign Exchange Dealers’ Association of India (FEDAI). On March 31, 2026, the exchange rate was ₹94.84 per $1.00. No representation is made that Indian rupee amounts could be converted into U.S. dollars at that rate or any other rate. Differences between table totals and the sums of listed amounts are due to rounding.

## Risk Factors

Investing in Infosys’s ADSs involves a high degree of risk. Risks and uncertainties could adversely affect the company’s business, financial condition, results of operations, prospects, and the market price of its equity shares and ADSs. Risks not currently known to Infosys, or not currently considered material, could also have an adverse effect.

Infosys’s revenues are difficult to predict and may vary significantly between periods. Results may fall below market expectations, investor expectations, or company guidance. Period-to-period comparisons should not be relied upon as an indication of future performance.

The rapid growth of artificial intelligence is reshaping the technology industry and client expectations. Frequent releases of new AI models, products, and features are compressing innovation cycles, increasing customer expectations, and intensifying competition. These developments may increase operational, execution, and investment risks. Long-term success will depend on the ability to capitalize on AI opportunities while managing associated risks.

## I.     Risks related to the markets in which we and our clients operate

- Spending on technology products and services by clients and prospective clients may fluctuate because of technological advances, geopolitical changes, economic factors, regulatory conditions, tariffs, trade restrictions, and fiscal or monetary policies.
- An economic slowdown or other factors may affect the economic health of the United States, United Kingdom, European Union, Australia, or industries from which Infosys derives revenue.
- Changes in immigration laws, their interpretation or enforcement, increased visa fees, or visa delays could increase compliance costs, disrupt business, prevent Infosys from bidding for or fulfilling projects, or result in enforcement actions or penalties.
- Government or regulatory sanctions against clients in key operating geographies could limit Infosys’s ability to grow those relationships and increase exposure to penalties or consequential sanctions.
- A significant portion of revenue depends on a limited number of clients. The loss of a major client or large contract could significantly affect the business.
- Client financial stability may be affected by demand and supply challenges, currency fluctuations, regulatory sanctions, tariffs, trade restrictions, geopolitical conflicts, and other macroeconomic conditions. These factors could adversely affect Infosys’s ability to recover fees for services rendered.
- Intense competition in the technology-services market could affect win rates and pricing, reduce market share, and decrease revenue and profits.
