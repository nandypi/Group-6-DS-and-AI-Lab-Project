## Consolidation and Subdivision of Shares

The Indian Companies Act, 2013 permits a company to split or combine the par value of its shares at a general meeting if authorized by its Articles of Association and approved by its shareholders. Shareholders on record on a fixed record date are entitled to receive the split or combination.

## Pre-emptive Rights and Issue of Additional Shares

The Indian Companies Act, 2013 gives shareholders the right to subscribe to new shares in proportion to their existing shareholdings when a company makes a further issue of shares, unless otherwise determined by a special resolution.

In a pre-emptive issuance, the company must first offer the new shares to shareholders on a fixed record date. The offer must include:

- The right of shareholders on record to renounce the shares offered in favor of another person.
- The number of shares offered and an offer period of at least 15 days and not more than 30 days from the date of offer.

If the offer is not accepted, it is deemed to have been declined. The Board may then distribute the shares not purchased by pre-emptive-rights holders in a manner that is not disadvantageous to the shareholders or the company.

## Meetings of Shareholders

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013, each company must hold an annual general meeting (AGM) each year within 15 months of the previous AGM or within six months of the end of the previous fiscal year, whichever is earlier.

Because the Company is among the top 100 listed entities in India by market capitalization, Regulation 44(5) of the SEBI Listing Regulations requires it to hold its AGM within five months from the close of each financial year. In certain circumstances, the Registrar of Companies may grant a three-month extension.

The AGM is generally convened by the Company Secretary pursuant to a Board resolution. The Board may also convene an extraordinary general meeting (EGM) when necessary or at the request of shareholders holding at least 10% of the paid-up capital carrying voting rights to transact special business.

Written notice setting out the agenda must be given at least 21 days before the general meeting to shareholders on record, excluding the days of mailing and the meeting date. The EGM must be held at a place in India. The AGM must be held at the registered office or another place within the city where the registered office is located.

The Ministry of Corporate Affairs, through General Circular No. 03/2025 dated September 22, 2025, extended the facility for companies to conduct AGMs and EGMs through video conference or other audio-visual means until further orders.

## Voting Rights

The Company provides shareholders with remote e-voting facilities for resolutions proposed at general meetings. Voting by ballot is also available at the AGM, and members who have not already voted through remote e-voting may vote at the AGM.

The Chairperson has a casting vote in the event of a tie. A shareholder entitled to attend and vote may appoint a proxy. The proxy instrument must be delivered to the Company at least 48 hours before the meeting. Unless the Articles of Association provide otherwise, a proxy may vote only on a poll.

A corporate shareholder may appoint an authorized representative to vote through remote e-voting or at the general meeting. An authorized representative may also appoint a proxy.

Under the SEBI Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements Regulations, 2015, and subsequent amendments, remote e-voting must be provided to all shareholders for shareholders’ resolutions in accordance with the Indian Companies Act, 2013.

Ordinary resolutions may be passed by a simple majority at a general meeting for which the required notice has been given. Special resolutions, including amendments to the Articles of Association, waiver of pre-emptive rights for issuing new shares, and reduction of share capital, require votes in favor to be at least three times the votes cast against by members entitled to vote.

The following matters are required to be voted on by postal ballot:

- Alteration of the objects clause of the memorandum.
- Alteration of the Articles of Association to convert the Company into a private company.
- Change in the registered office outside the local limits of a city, town, or village.
- Change in the objects for which money was raised from the public through a prospectus, where any amount remains unutilized.
- Issue of shares with differential voting, dividend, or other rights under Section 43(a)(ii) of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.
- Variation in rights attached to a class of shares, debentures, or other securities under Section 48 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.
- Buyback of shares.
- Election of a director under Section 151 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.
- Sale of the whole or substantially the whole of an undertaking under Section 180(1)(a) of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.
- Loans, guarantees, or security exceeding the limit specified under Section 186(3) of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.

A company required to provide electronic voting at general meetings may pass these resolutions through electronic voting instead of postal ballot. Companies with equity shares listed on a recognized stock exchange and at least 1,000 members must provide electronic voting facilities. The Company’s equity shares are listed on a recognized stock exchange, and it has more than 1,000 members; therefore, it is eligible to pass the above-mentioned resolutions by electronic vote.

## Register of Shareholders, Record Dates, Transfer of Shares

The Company maintains its register of shareholders in electronic form through National Securities Depository Limited and Central Depository Services (India) Limited.

To determine shareholder entitlement to rights such as dividends or rights issues, the Company may close the register of shareholders for a specified period not exceeding 30 days at one time. The date on which the closure period begins is the record date.

The Listing Regulations require at least three working days’ prior notice, excluding the date of intimation and the record date, before such closure. The register may not be closed for more than 30 consecutive days at one time or more than 45 days in a year. There must also be at least five working days between two record dates.

Following the Depositories Act, 1996, and the repeal of Section 22A of the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956, the equity shares of a public company are freely transferable, subject to Section 58 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013, and Regulation 40 of the Listing Regulations.

Under Section 58(2) of the Indian Companies Act, 2013, securities and other member interests are freely transferable, except where a contract or arrangement between two or more persons regarding the transfer of securities may be enforced as a contract. If the Board refuses to register a transfer, the shareholder seeking to transfer the shares may appeal to the National Company Law Tribunal.

Under Section 59(4) of the Indian Companies Act, 2013, if a share transfer contravenes the Indian Companies Act, 2013, the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956, the Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992, regulations issued under those laws, or other Indian laws, the National Company Law Tribunal may direct a company or depository to correct the contravention and rectify the registers and records of members or beneficial owners.

The Company’s registrar and share transfer agent for its equity shares is KFin Technologies Limited.

SEBI clarified through an amendment notification dated June 8, 2018, and an additional clarification dated May 27, 2019, that, effective April 1, 2019, requests to transfer securities generally cannot be processed unless the securities are held in dematerialized form with a depository, except for transmission or transposition.

A SEBI notification dated January 24, 2022, removed this exception. Transmission or transposition of securities held in physical or dematerialized form can therefore be effected only in dematerialized form.

Under the SEBI Master Circular for Registrars to an Issue and Share Transfer Agents dated May 7, 2024, listed companies must issue securities only in dematerialized form when processing the following service requests:

- Issue of duplicate securities certificates.
- Claims from an unclaimed suspense account.
- Renewal or exchange of securities certificates.
- Endorsement.
- Subdivision or splitting of securities certificates.
- Consolidation of securities certificates or folios.
- Transmission.
- Transposition.
