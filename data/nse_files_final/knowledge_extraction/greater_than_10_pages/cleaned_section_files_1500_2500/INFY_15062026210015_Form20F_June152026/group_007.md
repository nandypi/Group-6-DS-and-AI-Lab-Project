Our growth strategy relies on expanding operations around the world. The costs of entering and establishing operations in new markets, and expanding those operations, may be higher than expected. We may face significant competition in these regions, and an inability to manage expansion and related growth could adversely affect our business, results of operations, and financial condition.

We may face competition in other countries from companies with greater experience operating in those countries or internationally. We may also face difficulties integrating new facilities in different countries into our existing operations. Our organizational structures, processes, and culture may not be sufficiently agile and adaptive to execute our strategy.

## Wage pressures and the hiring of employees and sub-contractors either outside or in India may prevent us from sustaining our competitive advantage and may reduce our profits.

Wage costs in India have historically been significantly lower than wage costs in the United States and Europe for comparably skilled professionals. However, wages in India have increased due to inflation, competition for skilled resources among IT services companies, economic growth, competition from traditional industries, and hiring by multinational companies establishing offshore operations and global capability centers in India. Higher levels of offshoring may further increase demand for talent, including management-level employees and skilled professionals.

Wages globally have also increased because of shortages of skilled resources and inflation. Prolonged conflicts, tariff changes, trade restrictions, and political or macroeconomic uncertainties may increase inflation in key geographies. Higher visa-processing costs and increased wages for visa-dependent workers in certain geographies may further affect our cost structure.

Changes to employment laws in the countries where we operate, including the introduction of the Labour Codes in India, may increase employee compensation or social-security benefits and reduce profitability.

We may need to increase compensation more rapidly to attract and retain employees with emerging-technology skills. Alternatively, we may recruit in lower-labor-cost jurisdictions. Demand for advanced AI skills has grown with increasing AI adoption, resulting in intense competition among global companies for trained AI professionals and potentially higher talent costs.

We have issued, and may in the future issue, incentive compensation plans, including stock-based compensation plans, to employees and management. A decline in our share price could affect the total cost of employee rewards and benefits and potentially increase attrition.

Higher compensation may increase operating costs and reduce profitability. In some years, we may delay or not provide wage increases because of adverse market conditions while competitors continue to provide them. This could increase attrition and reduce our ability to hire highly skilled technology professionals. If employees leave, replacement costs may increase because we may need to offer higher salaries to new hires. Differences between new-hire and tenured-employee compensation may negatively affect employee satisfaction.

Employee productivity and delivery milestones may also be adversely affected because new employees require time to scale up. This may increase fulfillment costs, including potential client penalties.

## We are investing substantial cash in creating and maintaining physical and technological infrastructure, and our profitability could be reduced if our business does not grow proportionately.

As of March 31, 2026, we had contractual commitments of $141 million for capital expenditures, including commitments related to expanding or constructing facilities.

Expansion of existing facilities and construction of new facilities may involve cost overruns or project delays. We will continue investing in facilities to meet growth requirements, which may increase fixed costs. Investments in new facilities, including facilities in Tier 2 cities across India, support our hybrid working model and may increase facilities costs. If business and revenue growth are not proportionate to these investments, profitability may be adversely affected.

Changes in business and operating models, including continued employee participation in hybrid work, may reduce use of physical infrastructure. Continuing to incur operating costs to maintain these facilities could adversely affect profitability.

Conversely, business conditions may lead us not to expand or invest in certain areas. This could affect our ability to meet commitments to federal or state governments and may affect our reputation and relationships with those governments.

## Currency fluctuations and changes in interest rates may affect the results of our operations and yield on cash balances.

Our functional currency is the Indian rupee, and the majority of our expenses are denominated in Indian rupees and U.S. dollars.

We generate most of our revenues in foreign currencies, including the U.S. dollar, Euro, Australian dollar, and United Kingdom pound sterling, through sales in the United States and elsewhere. We also obtain products and services from overseas suppliers in various currencies.

Foreign-exchange volatility may result in client demands that we bear the associated impact. We hold a substantial majority of our cash funds in Indian rupees. We expect that most revenues will continue to be generated in foreign currencies, particularly the U.S. dollar, Euro, Australian dollar, and United Kingdom pound sterling. A significant portion of expenses, including personnel costs and capital and operating expenditures, will continue to be denominated in Indian rupees and U.S. dollars.

Changes in exchange rates could adversely affect revenue, other income, cost of sales, gross margin, net income, business, results of operations, and financial condition. During fiscal 2026, every percentage-point depreciation or appreciation in the exchange rate between the Indian rupee and the U.S. dollar affected incremental operating margins by approximately 0.44%.

We use derivative financial instruments, including foreign-exchange forward and option contracts, to mitigate foreign-exchange exposure. These instruments may not adequately protect us from foreign-currency risks.

Unanticipated or significant intra-quarter currency movements may cause losses and adversely affect profits and results of operations. Currency-market volatility may also make it difficult to hedge foreign-currency exposures effectively.

Changes in Reserve Bank of India policies may limit our ability to hedge foreign-currency exposures adequately. Full or increased capital-account convertibility, if introduced, could increase exchange-rate volatility between the Indian rupee and foreign currencies.

Most of our investments are in India-based assets and are exposed to the country’s interest-rate environment, which depends substantially on RBI monetary policy. Interest-rate cuts could reduce interest income and affect profitability.

Adverse currency movements arising from macroeconomic issues or geopolitical conflicts may adversely affect profitability.

## IV.    Risks related to our employee workforce

## Our success depends largely upon our highly skilled technology professionals and our ability to hire, attract, motivate, retain and train these personnel.

Our ability to execute projects, particularly large, complex, and integrated programs; maintain client relationships; and acquire new clients depends substantially on attracting, hiring, training, motivating, and retaining skilled technology professionals, project managers, and other mid-level professionals. If we cannot hire, motivate, and retain personnel, our ability to bid for projects, obtain new deals, execute large deals, develop and sell new service offerings or software platforms, and expand could be impaired, potentially reducing revenues.

The availability of science and technology professionals in certain current or prospective operating geographies may be inadequate to meet demand. This may also affect efforts to localize the workforce. Travel or immigration restrictions and higher wage costs may further limit the ability to hire or deploy personnel needed to compete for and provide services to clients.

Worldwide competition for skilled professionals, including increased hiring by technology companies in India, may affect our ability to hire and retain sufficient skilled and experienced technology professionals. Workforce attrition may be volatile in India and across other locations.

Changing technology, industry needs, and demographics increase the need for differently skilled and more diverse talent. Digital services require skills in creative design, data science, statistical analysis, AI, and machine learning. These talent pools may differ from traditional recruitment sources and may cost more. Failure to hire, retain, or redeploy professionals as technology changes could adversely affect our ability to bid for and obtain new deals and could harm our business.

Our hybrid working model may make it difficult to integrate new employees into our corporate culture. We also hire and train fresh college graduates each year. If we cannot train and deploy them on client projects in a timely and effective manner, utilization, client satisfaction, and profitability could be adversely affected.

AI-led disruption is reshaping the traditional talent model. Historically, IT services companies relied on hiring entry-level employees and scaling their capabilities through training. AI has increased demand for employees who can contextualize software within client business environments, review and curate AI-generated code, and explain business value to clients. This may alter talent acquisition and retention models, requiring us to retain a significant percentage of these employees. The speed and scale of this shift may depend on the rate of enterprise AI adoption.

If we cannot increase employee compensation adequately, or if we reduce compensation or variable pay, attrition may increase. A significant decline in our share price could also increase attrition among employees whose compensation includes shares and could increase hiring costs.

Failure to attract, engage, retain, train, and deploy rebadged employees could affect profitability and project deliverables. Rebadged employees, together with negotiated agreements with or without work councils, may limit our ability to redeploy employees or modify employment contracts.

Attrition and mobility restrictions increase dependence on subcontractors to deliver committed client services, which may affect profitability.

We hire significant numbers of fresh college graduates and lateral employees to meet business needs. We invest substantial effort, money, and resources in training associates, particularly at entry level, for client engagements. Competency and talent quality are essential to service delivery. Increasing demand for specialized AI skills may raise training and talent-development costs.

Despite training and enablement efforts, some associates may fail to meet required competency or skill standards. Exits of such associates, even after following applicable legal processes, may attract media attention and scrutiny from authorities, potentially affecting our brand and reputation.
