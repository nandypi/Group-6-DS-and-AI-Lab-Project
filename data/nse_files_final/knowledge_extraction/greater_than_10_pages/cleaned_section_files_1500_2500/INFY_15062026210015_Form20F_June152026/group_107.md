## Dividends

Under the Indian Companies Act, the Board recommends dividends, which must then be approved by shareholders at a general meeting. The Board is not obliged to recommend a dividend.

At the annual general meeting (AGM), shareholders may declare a dividend in accordance with their rights and interests in the company’s profits. A dividend cannot exceed the amount recommended by the Board. Dividends are generally declared per equity share and paid in cash in proportion to the paid-up value of the shares within 30 days of shareholder approval.

The Board may declare and pay interim dividends without shareholder approval, subject to the conditions specified in the Indian Companies Act. The Company must inform the stock exchanges on which its equity shares and ADSs are listed of the dividend declared per equity share and the record date or book-closure date used to determine shareholder eligibility. Dividends may be paid only in cash to registered shareholders as of the record date, including by cheque, warrant, or electronic payment.

Unpaid or unclaimed dividends remaining after 30 days from the declaration date must be transferred to a special bank account at an approved bank. The Company transfers such amounts within seven days after the 30-day period expires. Amounts unclaimed for seven years after transfer are transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund (IEPF).

Shares for which dividends have not been paid or claimed for seven consecutive years or more are also transferred to the IEPF Authority, together with any benefits accruing on those shares. Shareholders may claim back the unclaimed dividend and corresponding shares from the IEPF under the procedure prescribed in the IEPF Rules. No claim lies against the Company for these amounts or shares.

Dividends may be paid out of:

- Profits for the year in which the dividend is declared, after providing for depreciation.
- Undistributed profits from previous fiscal years, after providing for depreciation.
- Accumulated profits transferred to free reserves, where profits are inadequate or absent and the applicable conditions are met.

Before declaring a dividend, a company may transfer an appropriate percentage of its profits to reserves.

Where profits are inadequate or absent, a dividend may be declared from accumulated profits transferred to free reserves subject to the following conditions:

- The dividend rate cannot exceed the average rate declared in the three immediately preceding years. This condition does not apply if the company did not declare dividends during those three financial years.
- The amount drawn from accumulated profits transferred to reserves cannot exceed 10% of the company’s paid-up capital plus free reserves, based on the latest audited financial statements. The amount drawn must first be used to offset losses incurred in the fiscal year in which the dividend is declared.
- After the withdrawal, reserves cannot fall below 15% of the company’s paid-up capital, based on the latest audited financial statements.
- Previous losses and depreciation not provided for in prior years must be set off against the current year’s profit before a dividend is declared.

## Bonus Shares

The Indian Companies Act permits a company to distribute free reserves to shareholders as bonus shares. Bonus shares may also be issued by capitalizing the securities premium account or the capital redemption reserve account. Reserves created through asset revaluation cannot be capitalized for this purpose.

Bonus shares are distributed to shareholders in the proportion recommended by the Board. Shareholders recorded on a fixed record date are entitled to receive the bonus shares. Once announced, the decision to issue bonus shares cannot be withdrawn.

Any bonus issue is subject to applicable Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) guidelines. The company must:

- Be authorized by its articles of association.
- Reserve equity shares of the same class for holders of outstanding compulsorily convertible debt instruments, if any, in proportion to the convertible portion.
- Issue the bonus shares from free reserves, the securities premium account, or the capital redemption reserve account, where those amounts are built from genuine profits or share premium collected in cash.
- Make any partly paid shares fully paid before issuing bonus shares.
- Not be in default on interest or principal relating to fixed deposits, interest on existing debentures, or principal due on redemption of debentures.
- Not declare bonus shares in lieu of dividends.
- Have sufficient reason to believe that it is not in default on statutory employee-related dues, including provident fund contributions, gratuity, and bonus payments.

The bonus issue must be implemented within 15 days of Board approval where shareholder approval is not required. If shareholder approval is required to capitalize profits or reserves, the issue must be implemented within two months of Board approval. An announced bonus issue cannot be withdrawn.

## Consolidation and Subdivision of Shares

The Indian Companies Act permits a company to split or combine the par value of its shares at a general meeting, if authorized by its articles of association and approved by shareholders. Shareholders recorded on a fixed record date are entitled to receive the resulting split or combination.

## Pre-emptive Rights and Issue of Additional Shares

When a company issues additional shares, shareholders generally have the right to subscribe in proportion to their existing shareholdings, unless otherwise determined by a special resolution of a general meeting.

For a pre-emptive share issue, the company must first offer the new shares to shareholders recorded on a fixed record date. The offer must include:

1. The right of shareholders to renounce the offered shares in favor of another person.
2. The number of shares offered and an offer period of at least 15 days, or any shorter prescribed period, and no more than 30 days from the date of the offer.

If shareholders do not accept the offer, it is deemed to have been declined. The Board may then distribute the remaining shares in a manner that is not disadvantageous to the shareholders or the Company.

## Meetings of Shareholders

The Company must convene an AGM each year within 15 months of the previous AGM or within six months of the end of the previous fiscal year, whichever is earlier. The Registrar of Companies may grant a three-month extension in certain circumstances.

The AGM is generally convened by the Company Secretary pursuant to a Board resolution. Ordinary AGM business includes:

1. Consideration of the financial statements and the reports of the Board and auditors.
2. Declaration of any dividend.
3. Appointment of directors in place of those retiring.
4. Appointment of auditors and fixing of their remuneration.

All other business is treated as special business.

The Board may convene an extraordinary general meeting (EGM) when necessary or at the request of shareholders holding at least 10% of the Company’s paid-up capital carrying voting rights. An EGM must be held at a place within India.

Written notice stating the agenda must be provided at least 21 days before a general meeting to shareholders on record, excluding the mailing date and the meeting date. An AGM must be held at the Company’s registered office or another location within the city where the registered office is located.

The Indian Companies Act permits certain business to be conducted by postal ballot together with electronic voting. The following resolutions must be voted on only by postal ballot:

- Alteration of the objects clause of the memorandum and articles of association.
- Alteration of the articles of association to convert the Company into a private company.
- Change of the registered office outside the local limits of a city, town, or village.
- Change in the objects for which money was raised from the public through a prospectus while any amount remains unutilized.
- Issue of shares with differential voting, dividend, or other rights under Section 43(a)(ii) of the Indian Companies Act.
- Variation of rights attached to a class of shares, debentures, or other securities under Section 48 of the Indian Companies Act.
- Buy-back of shares.
- Election of a director under Section 151 of the Indian Companies Act.
- Sale of the whole or substantially the whole of an undertaking under Section 180(1)(a) of the Indian Companies Act.
- Loans, guarantees, or security exceeding the limit specified under Section 186(3) of the Indian Companies Act.
