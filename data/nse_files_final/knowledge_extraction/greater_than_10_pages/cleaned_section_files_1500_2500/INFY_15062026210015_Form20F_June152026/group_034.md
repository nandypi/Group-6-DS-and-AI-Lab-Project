## Income tax expense

Profits earned from providing software development and other services outside India are subject to tax in the countries where the work is performed. Most taxes paid outside India can be claimed as a credit against tax liability in India.

As a resident company in India under the Income Tax Act, 1961, Infosys is required to pay tax in India on global income under Section 5 of the Act. These taxes are reflected as domestic taxes. Revenue geography disclosures in Note 2.11 of Item 18 of the Annual Report on Form 20-F are based on customer domicile and do not represent the locations where delivery or revenue-related efforts occur. Domestic taxable income is not limited to income generated from the India geography; therefore, domestic and foreign income taxes will not necessarily correlate with the proportion of revenue generated from India and other geographic segments.

The applicable Indian corporate statutory tax rate was 25.17% for both fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2025. Section 115BAA, introduced by the Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2019, allows a domestic company to elect a reduced corporate tax rate without claiming certain deductions, including the deduction for SEZ units under Section 10AA of the Income Tax Act. From fiscal 2025, Infosys has recognized income tax expense by applying Section 115BAA without claiming the Section 10AA deduction for SEZ units.

Infosys previously benefited from Indian tax incentives for exports of services from SEZ units registered under the Special Economic Zones Act, 2005. Eligible SEZ units that began providing services on or after April 1, 2005, could claim a 100% income tax deduction on profits or gains from exported services for the first five years, followed by a 50% deduction for the next five years. Up to 50% of such profits or gains could also be deducted for a further five years, subject to creating and using a Special Economic Zone Re-Investment Reserve to acquire new plant and machinery. Unutilized reserves may become taxable after the applicable period, increasing the effective tax cost.

For fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2025, income tax expense was recognized under Section 115BAA without claiming the Section 10AA deduction for SEZ units. See Note 2.18, Income Taxes, under Item 18 of the Annual Report on Form 20-F for reconciliation of the income tax provision to the amount computed using the statutory income tax rate on income before income taxes.

The effective tax rate is influenced by differential tax rates, non-deductible expenses, exempt non-operating income, overseas taxes, tax reversals and provisions relating to prior periods, changes in tax regulations, and other tax deductions.

The effective tax rate in fiscal 2026 was lower primarily because of the reversal of tax provisions following receipt of orders relating to prior assessment years. Fiscal 2026 income tax expense included reversals, net of provisions, of $93 million. These comprised reversal of $152 million of provisions made in earlier periods, offset by an additional $59 million provision relating to prior periods.

During fiscal 2026, Infosys received orders under Sections 250 and 254 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, from the Indian Income Tax Authorities for assessment years 2013–14 and 2017–18 to 2021–22. The orders confirmed the Company’s position on the tax treatment of certain matters. As a result, pre-tax interest income of $41 million, together with the corresponding tax impact, was recognized. An income tax provision of $93 million was reversed, with a corresponding credit to the Statement of Comprehensive Income. Contingent liabilities relating to these matters were reduced by $9 million.

Fiscal 2025 income tax expense included provisions, net of reversals, of $16 million. These comprised an additional provision of $86 million relating to prior periods, offset by reversal of $70 million of provisions made in earlier periods.

During fiscal 2025, Infosys received orders under Section 250 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, from the Indian Income Tax Authorities for assessment years 2016–17 and 2019–20. The orders confirmed the Company’s position on the tax treatment of certain contentious matters. As a result, pre-tax interest income of $38 million, together with the corresponding tax impact, was recognized. An income tax provision of $21 million was reversed, with a corresponding credit to the Statement of Comprehensive Income. Contingent liabilities relating to these matters were reduced by $125 million.

The provisions and reversals relating to prior periods primarily resulted from adjudication of disputed matters following the filing of tax returns and completion of assessments across various jurisdictions.

As of March 31, 2026, claims against the Group not acknowledged as debts from the Income Tax Authorities amounted to $207 million (₹1,964 crore). As of March 31, 2025, these claims amounted to $226 million (₹1,933 crore).

Amounts paid to statutory authorities against tax claims were $273 million (₹2,594 crore) as of March 31, 2026, and $491 million (₹4,199 crore) as of March 31, 2025. The amount paid against tax claims decreased in fiscal 2026 primarily because of an income tax refund received.

The claims primarily represent demands arising from assessment proceedings under the Income Tax Act, 1961. They relate to issues including disallowance of expenditure on software treated as capital in nature and payments to Associated Enterprises treated as subject to withholding tax, among other matters. These matters are pending before various Income Tax Authorities. Management and its tax advisors expect the Company’s position to be upheld on ultimate resolution and do not expect a material adverse effect on the Group’s financial position and results of operations.

The Company’s Advanced Pricing Arrangement (APA) with the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) for U.S. branch income tax expired in March 2021. The Company has applied for renewal, and U.S. taxable income is currently based on the Company’s best estimate using the expected value method.
