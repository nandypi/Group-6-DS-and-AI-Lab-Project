Under Section 402.04 of the NYSE Listed Company Manual, actively operating companies listed on the NYSE are required to solicit proxies for shareholder meetings. Section 105 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013 prohibits companies incorporated under that Act from soliciting proxies. Because Infosys is prohibited from soliciting proxies under Indian law, it does not meet the NYSE proxy solicitation requirement. Infosys provides written notices of all shareholder meetings to shareholders and files those notices with the SEC.

Under its listing agreements with Indian stock exchanges, Infosys maintains a Stakeholders Relationship Committee, which is not required under the NYSE Listed Company Manual.

## Item 16J. Insider trading policies

In May 2015, SEBI introduced the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015, replacing the SEBI (Insider Trading) Regulations, 1992.

Infosys has adopted insider trading policies and procedures to regulate, monitor, and report trading by all insiders, including Designated Persons and Immediate Relatives of Designated Persons, as defined in its Insider Trading Policy and Code. The policies are intended to promote compliance with the SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015, as amended, and applicable U.S. securities laws.

On April 23, 2026, the board adopted a revised Code of Conduct for Prohibition of Insider Trading, filed as an exhibit to the Annual Report on Form 20-F.

## Item 16K. Cybersecurity

Infosys’s cybersecurity strategy is designed to ensure business continuity by minimizing disruptions and continually improving its security compliance posture. The strategy includes continuous threat-landscape monitoring, management of cyber events, security controls and processes, secure-by-design principles, Responsible Artificial Intelligence (RAI), cyber-resilient systems, and cybersecurity governance.

### Risk management and strategy

Infosys maintains an integrated Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) framework implemented across the organization by the risk management office. The framework was developed with reference to the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations (COSO) and the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) 31000 and is tailored to Infosys’s business requirements.

The ERM framework covers strategic, operational, legal, and compliance risks. It identifies, analyzes, assesses, mitigates, monitors, and governs risks, vulnerabilities, and potential threats to the company’s strategic objectives. Risks are identified through top-down and bottom-up approaches and recorded in a multi-layer risk register.

Infosys’s ERM framework defines organization-wide risk assessment guidelines and an eight-layer governance structure covering risk categories. Risks identified across functions, including cybersecurity risks, are presented to the appropriate councils. Critical and cross-functional risks can be escalated to the next level of the ERM governance structure. ERM also helps define guidelines for the incident management process. Cybersecurity is monitored as a key enterprise risk, with periodic updates on risks and mitigations provided under the governance structure.

Infosys Cyber Risk Management (CRM) is designed to identify, analyze, prioritize, treat, and monitor cyber risks and vulnerabilities across the enterprise. Its cyber risk assessment framework aligns with ISO 31000, ISO 27001, and ISO 27005. CRM is integrated with the ERM framework to support consistent risk management and cross-functional analysis and reporting to management.

CRM safeguards information, information-processing assets, and facilities of Infosys and its subsidiaries to support business continuity. The vulnerability management program uses threat-based prioritization, vulnerability-aging analysis, and continuous tracking to guide remediation.

Infosys’s third-party security risk management program addresses security risks and vulnerabilities throughout supplier engagement. It includes governance processes, continuous monitoring of security metrics, threat-intelligence tracking, and periodic risk assessments.

Multiple committees and sub-councils, from the board level to departmental and functional levels, oversee and monitor cyber risks across the organization.

Infosys periodically collaborates with third-party security consultants on cybersecurity maturity assessments, gap analyses, security-control and process evaluations, and tabletop exercises. The consultants provide recommendations on identified risks. Observations are reviewed with senior management to determine appropriate actions.
