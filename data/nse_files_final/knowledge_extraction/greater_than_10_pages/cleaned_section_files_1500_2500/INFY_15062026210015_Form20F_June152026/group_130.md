## Gifts and Entertainment

In connection with certain holidays and other occasions, gifts of nominal value may be given to customers, government officials, and other business-related parties. Gifts and entertainment must comply with applicable regulations, Company policies, C-LIFE values, and the Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption Policy.

When offering gifts or entertainment:

- They must not be intended to obtain or retain business, influence a business decision, or gain an improper business advantage.
- They must be lawful in the country where offered and permitted under the client's policies.
- They must constitute a bona fide promotion or goodwill expenditure.
- They must not be provided in cash.
- Gifts must be of nominal value, both individually and in aggregate.
- They must be accurately recorded in the Company's books and records.
- The Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption Policy must be followed.

Acceptance of gifts is not encouraged. The Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption Policy should be consulted before accepting gifts or entertainment.

## Charitable Contributions

Infosys considers charitable contributions and donations part of its corporate social responsibility. Typical areas of support include education and research, social welfare, disaster relief, and similar social causes.

Before making a charitable contribution on behalf of Infosys:

- The recipient must be a registered, tax-paying, recognized organization.
- The contribution must be permissible under applicable local laws.
- The contribution must be made without a demand for or expectation of a business return.
- Beneficiaries must not be related to Infosys directors or executive officers.
- Contributions must not be made in cash or to an individual's private account.
- Contributions and donations must be fairly and accurately recorded in Infosys' books of account.
- The Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption Policy must be followed.

Infosys maintains a diligence process for charitable contributions.

## Transacting with Third Parties

Infosys seeks, to the extent reasonably practicable, to interact directly with government officials. If a third party must interface with government authorities on behalf of Infosys, the Company should:

- Verify the third party's credentials and reputation before engagement.
- Execute a formal contract containing appropriate requirements to comply with applicable anti-corruption and local laws.
- Ensure the engagement follows legal requirements and internal processes and guidelines.
- Provide the third party with a copy of the Supplier Code of Conduct.

Infosys and individual directors, officers, or employees may be liable for a payment made by a third-party agent if Infosys makes a payment or transfers another item of value to the agent knowing that it will be given to a government official.

Fees, commissions, and other remuneration paid to intermediaries or third-party agents must be reasonable, bona fide, and commensurate with the functions and services performed. Related expenses must be tracked and fairly and accurately recorded in Infosys' books of account.

## Red Flags While Transacting with Third Parties

- A background check reveals a flawed background or poor reputation.
- The transaction involves a country known for corrupt payments.
- An agent is suggested by a government official.
- An agent objects to anti-corruption compliance requirements.
- An agent has a personal or business relationship with a government official.
- The contract contains unusual terms or payment arrangements, including cash payment, payment in another country's currency, or payment to a financial institution outside the country where the contract is performed.
- The agent requests that its identity be kept confidential.
- The commission exceeds the prevailing rate or must be paid in cash.
- There is an indication that facilitation payments are required to obtain or advance business.
- The agent requests false invoices or other false documentation.
- Payment is requested in a third country or in another party's name.

## Trading in Company Shares

Infosys is a publicly listed company, and its shares and American Depository Receipts may be traded by the public. The price of Infosys securities may fluctuate based on publicly available information about the Company's activities.

Unpublished Price Sensitive Information (UPSI), also known as material nonpublic information, is information that:

1. Is not in the public domain; and
2. An investor would find useful when deciding whether to trade in the Company's securities.

UPSI may include information about financial results, dividends, changes in capital structure, mergers, de-mergers, acquisitions, delisting, disposal or expansion of the business, and changes in key managerial personnel. This list is not exhaustive.

A person who possesses UPSI and uses it to buy or sell Company securities before the information is made public may be subject to penalties under insider trading laws.

Employees, consultants, and vendors may access UPSI in the course of their work. Under applicable securities laws, a person possessing UPSI must not trade in Company securities or disclose the information to others who may trade. Insider trading may result in imprisonment, fines, and private lawsuits.

Individuals must:

- Avoid disclosing UPSI to anyone outside the Company, including family and friends.
- Avoid trading in Infosys securities, or the securities of any company involved with Infosys, while possessing UPSI.
- Comply with the Company's quarterly trading blackout period, during which designated persons under the Infosys Internal Code of Conduct of Prohibition of Insider Trading must not trade in Company securities.
- Follow applicable pre-clearance procedures and trade only when the trading window is open.

## Prohibition Against Short Selling of Company Securities

No Company director, officer, employee, or third-party agent may, directly or indirectly, sell an equity security, including derivatives, of the Company if they:

1. Do not own the security sold; or
2. Own the security but do not deliver it against the sale within the applicable settlement cycle.

## Conflict of Interest

An employee may serve as a director and majority shareholder of a spouse's online food-delivery company when the start-up is not in the same business as Infosys, based on the facts described. The employee must not use Infosys time, property, or other resources to help the spouse's company.

## Understanding Regulated Trade Restrictions

### Export Control and Import Regulations

Many countries control where products, services, software, and technical knowledge may be exported. An export can occur when these items are transferred to another country or to a foreign citizen or representative of another country, regardless of where that person is located. It can also occur when a foreign citizen receives access to controlled information or software through a network, visual inspection, or telephone conversation.

Infosys may encounter export-controlled items or data while developing software, working with clients, or reselling third-party products.

The United States maintains strict export controls. U.S. export regulations can apply to exports from the United States and to exports from other countries when products contain U.S.-origin components or technology. Other countries, including countries in Europe, also maintain export-control regulations.

During customer engagements:

- The Company's and customer's export-control obligations should be considered at an early stage.
- Infosys' export-control policies should be reviewed.
- The Office of Integrity & Compliance should be consulted early because export rules are complex.

Infosys imports software and other intangible assets. Applicable import laws, regulations, and requirements must be followed when transferring goods and controlled software across borders.

### Export Control Regulations

Software created in the United States may remain subject to U.S. export regulations even if it is duplicated and packaged abroad. In some circumstances, an oral presentation containing technical data to foreign nationals in the United States may constitute a controlled export.

### Anti-Boycott Laws

Infosys employees may receive requests to boycott certain countries, companies, or other entities. Boycott activity may include refusing to do business with certain groups or providing information about boycotted entities.

Infosys must not cooperate with a boycott that is not initiated by the U.S. or Indian governments, as this may constitute an illegal foreign boycott. Such requests may appear in master service agreements, invoices, statements of work, or other larger documents.
