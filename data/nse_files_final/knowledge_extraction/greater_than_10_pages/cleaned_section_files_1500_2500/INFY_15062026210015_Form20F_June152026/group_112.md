# Takeover Code and Change in Control

Under the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011 (the “Takeover Code”), an acquirer that, together with persons acting in concert, acquires shares or voting rights resulting in aggregate ownership of 5% or more in a listed Indian company must disclose the aggregate shareholding and voting rights to the company and the relevant stock exchanges within two working days of the acquisition or receipt of intimation of allotment.

A person that, together with persons acting in concert, holds shares or voting rights representing 5% or more must disclose the number of shares or voting rights held and any change exceeding 2% of the company’s total shareholding or voting rights. The disclosure is required within two working days of receiving intimation of allotment or of acquiring or disposing of shares or voting rights. In the case of a sale, disclosure is required even if the acquirer’s shareholding, together with that of persons acting in concert, falls below 5%.

For financial year 2020–2021, a promoter was permitted to acquire more than 5% and up to 10% of the voting rights in a target company if the acquisition occurred pursuant to a preferential issue of equity shares by the target company.

An acquisition that entitles the acquirer and persons acting in concert to exercise 25% or more of the voting rights in, or control over, the target company triggers a requirement to make an open offer for at least 26% of the target company’s total shares. The offer price must be determined under the Takeover Code. The acquirer must make a public announcement on the date on which it agrees to acquire the shares, voting rights, or control.

If an open offer reduces the target company’s public shareholding below the minimum level specified in the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Rules, 1957 (the “SCRR”) and the Listing Regulations, the acquirer must take the steps permitted under SEBI circular SEBI/HO/CFD/PoD2/P/CIR/2023/18 dated February 3, 2023. This circular superseded SEBI circular SEBI/HO/CFD/CMD/CIR/P/43/2018 dated February 22, 2018.

Following amendments to the Takeover Code dated December 6, 2021, an acquirer may make an offer to delist the company if the intention to do so is declared when making the public announcement of an open offer and the detailed public statement. For an indirect acquisition that is not deemed a direct acquisition under regulation 5(2) of the Takeover Code, the intention to delist is required to be declared initially only in the detailed public statement.

If the target company does not get delisted through the delisting offer, but the offer causes the acquirer’s shareholding to exceed the maximum permissible non-public shareholding threshold, the acquirer may make a further delisting attempt under the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Delisting of Equity Shares) Regulations, 2009 during the 12 months following completion of the open offer, provided the acquirer continues to exceed the threshold.

If the further delisting attempt fails, the acquirer must ensure compliance with the target company’s minimum public-shareholding requirement under the SCRR within 12 months after the end of that period. Subject to these requirements, an acquirer whose shareholding exceeds the maximum permissible non-public shareholding following an open offer is not eligible to make a voluntary delisting offer under the Delisting Regulations until 12 months have elapsed from completion of the offer period.

As Infosys is listed in India, the Takeover Code applies to Infosys and to any person acquiring its equity shares or voting rights, including through ADSs.

Infosys ADSs entitle holders to exercise voting rights in respect of the deposited equity shares. Accordingly, acquisitions of ADSs are subject to the Takeover Code. An ADS holder, together with persons acting in concert, would be required to make an open offer for at least 26% of the company’s shares if the shares underlying the ADSs represent 25% or more of the company’s shares or voting rights.

Under the Listing Regulations, Infosys has entered into listing agreements with each Indian stock exchange on which its equity shares are listed. Infosys must report to the stock exchanges disclosures made to the company under the Takeover Code.

The Articles of Association do not contain provisions that would delay, defer, or prevent a change in control of the company and that would operate only in connection with a merger, acquisition, or corporate restructuring involving Infosys.
