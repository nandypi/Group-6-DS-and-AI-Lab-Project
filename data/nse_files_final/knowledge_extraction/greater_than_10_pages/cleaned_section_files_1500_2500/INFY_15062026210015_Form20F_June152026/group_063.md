# Ownership of ADSs

For U.S. federal income tax purposes, holders of ADSs will generally be treated as holders of the equity shares represented by those ADSs.

## Dividends

Subject to the passive foreign investment company rules, the gross amount of cash or other property distributed with respect to ADSs or equity shares, before Indian withholding taxes, will generally be included in a U.S. holder’s income as ordinary dividend income when received. For a U.S. holder of ADSs, the receipt date generally should be the date the distribution is received by the depositary.

A distribution is generally treated as a dividend to the extent it is made from the company’s current or accumulated earnings and profits, as determined under U.S. federal income tax principles. The company does not intend to determine earnings and profits under those principles. Accordingly, U.S. holders should generally expect distributions to be treated as dividends.

Dividends generally will not qualify for the dividends-received deduction available to corporate U.S. holders. To the extent a distribution exceeds the company’s current and accumulated earnings and profits, the excess will first be treated as a tax-free return of capital, to the extent of the U.S. holder’s tax basis in the equity shares or ADSs, and thereafter as capital gain.

Subject to limitations, dividends paid to non-corporate U.S. holders, including individuals, may qualify for a reduced tax rate if the company is treated as a qualified foreign corporation. A qualified foreign corporation generally includes a foreign corporation whose shares, or potentially its ADSs under legislative history, are readily tradable on an established U.S. securities market, or that is eligible for benefits under a comprehensive income tax treaty with the United States.

A corporation is not a qualified foreign corporation if it is or was a passive foreign investment company during the taxable year in which the dividend is paid or the preceding taxable year.

The ADSs are traded on the NYSE. However, because there are no specific statutory provisions addressing ADSs, there is no assurance that the company qualifies as a qualified foreign corporation solely because of its NYSE listing, and it is unclear whether the company’s shares will be considered readily tradable for this purpose. The company may nevertheless be eligible for benefits under the applicable treaty.

U.S. holders should consult their own tax advisors regarding the treatment of dividends and eligibility for reduced tax rates.

## Indian Withholding Taxes and Foreign Tax Credits

Subject to conditions and limitations, Indian withholding taxes imposed on distributions paid to a U.S. holder with respect to ADSs or equity shares should generally be eligible for credit against the holder’s U.S. federal income tax liability.

Alternatively, a U.S. holder may claim a deduction for the withholding tax, but only for a year in which the holder does not claim a U.S. foreign tax credit for any foreign income taxes. The overall limitation on creditable foreign taxes is calculated separately for specific classes of income. Distributions on ADSs or ordinary shares generally will be treated as foreign-source income for purposes of calculating the allowable U.S. foreign tax credit.

The rules governing U.S. foreign tax credits are complex, and each U.S. holder should consult a tax advisor regarding the availability of credits under the holder’s circumstances.

## Dividends Paid in Indian Rupees

If a dividend is paid in Indian rupees, the amount included in a U.S. holder’s income will be the U.S. dollar value of the rupee payment, determined using the spot exchange rate between Indian rupees and U.S. dollars on the date the dividend is included in income, regardless of whether the payment is actually converted into U.S. dollars.

Generally, any gain or loss from currency exchange fluctuations between the dividend payment date and the date the payment is converted into U.S. dollars will be treated as U.S.-source ordinary income or loss.

# Tax Treatment of a Buyback

An exchange of equity shares for cash by a U.S. holder pursuant to a buyback will be a taxable transaction for U.S. federal income tax purposes. Depending on the holder’s circumstances, the tendering U.S. holder will be treated either as recognizing gain or loss from the disposition of the equity shares or as receiving a distribution from the company.

Under Section 302 of the Internal Revenue Code, a tendering U.S. holder will recognize gain or loss on the exchange of equity shares for cash if the exchange:

- Results in a “substantially disproportionate” redemption with respect to the U.S. holder; or
- Is “not essentially equivalent to a dividend” with respect to the U.S. holder.
