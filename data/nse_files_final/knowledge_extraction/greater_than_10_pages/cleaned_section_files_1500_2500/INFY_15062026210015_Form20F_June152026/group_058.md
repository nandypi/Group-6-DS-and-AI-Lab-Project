A single FPI, including its investor group, may purchase equity shares of an Indian company only below 10% of the company’s total paid-up equity capital on a fully diluted basis.

An investment by a person resident outside India through equity instruments in a listed Indian company is categorized as foreign portfolio investment if it is less than 10% of the post-issue paid-up equity share capital, on a fully diluted basis, or less than 10% of the paid-up value of each series of equity instruments. Investments by entities not registered with SEBI as FPIs may also qualify as foreign portfolio investments if they remain within the prescribed limits.

Subject to applicable Indian laws, regulations, guidelines, and approvals under Regulation 21 of the FPI Regulations, an FPI may issue, subscribe to, or otherwise deal in offshore derivative instruments only if:

1. The instruments are issued by persons registered as Category I FPIs.
2. The instruments are issued only to persons eligible for registration as Category I FPIs.
3. Applicable know-your-client requirements are met.
4. Other conditions prescribed by SEBI from time to time are satisfied.

Transfers of offshore derivative instruments must be made only to persons meeting these requirements. Prior consent of the issuing FPI is required, except where the transferees have been pre-approved by the FPI. Category II FPIs, and persons categorized as Category II FPIs, cannot deal in offshore derivative instruments.

The Non-Debt Rules permit FPIs to purchase equity instruments of listed Indian companies on recognized Indian stock exchanges, through public offers, or through private placements, subject to applicable individual and aggregate limits and conditions.

FPI securities transactions must generally be conducted through stock brokers registered with SEBI. Exceptions include:

- Transactions in Government securities and other securities under RBI jurisdiction, as specified by RBI.
- Sales in response to an acquirer’s offer under the SEBI (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011.
- Sales in response to an offer under the SEBI (Delisting of Equity Shares) Regulations, 2009.
- Sales under the SEBI (Buy-back of Securities) Regulations, 2018.
- Divestments in response to offers by Indian companies under the applicable ADR/GDR overseas disinvestment guidelines.
- Bids for, or acquisitions of, securities offered for disinvestment by the Central Government or a State Government.
- Transactions under an agreement with a merchant banker for market making or subscription to an unsubscribed portion of an issue under Chapter IX of the SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018.
- Transactions in corporate bonds.
- Transactions on the electronic book provider platform of recognized stock exchanges.
- Transactions to receive, hold, and sell unlisted securities under Regulation 20(2) of the FPI Regulations.
- Transactions in unlisted securities received through involuntary corporate actions, including approved mergers or demergers, or implementation of resolution plans under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, or applicable debt-resolution guidelines. Such unlisted holdings are treated as foreign direct investment.
- Transfers of rights entitlements.
- Purchases or sales of illiquid, suspended, or delisted securities.
- Transactions between registered FPIs with a multi-investment-manager structure, the same beneficial owner, and a common Permanent Account Number.
- Other transactions specified by SEBI.

Under the Non-Debt Rules, the total holding of each FPI or investor group must remain below 10% of an Indian company’s total paid-up equity capital on a fully diluted basis, or below 10% of the paid-up value of each series of debentures, preference shares, or share warrants issued by an Indian company.

An FPI that breaches the 10% limit may divest its holdings within five trading days from the settlement date of the trades causing the breach. If it does not divest, the entire investment of the FPI and its investor group in the company is treated as foreign direct investment, and the FPI and its investor group cannot make further portfolio investments in that company. Divestment and reclassification are subject to any further conditions specified by SEBI and RBI.

FPIs investing only in Government securities, subject to conditions specified by SEBI, are excluded from the investor group.

Effective April 1, 2020, the aggregate limit for FPI investment is the applicable sectoral cap under the foreign direct investment route.

Before March 31, 2020, companies could choose a lower aggregate limit of 24%, 49%, or 74%, with approval from their board of directors and shareholders through a special resolution. A company that had reduced its aggregate limit could subsequently increase it to 49%, 74%, the applicable sectoral cap, or the statutory ceiling, as applicable, with the required approvals. Once increased to a higher threshold, the aggregate limit could not be reduced to a lower threshold. For an Indian company operating in a sector where FDI is prohibited, the aggregate limit is 24%.

Aggregate FPI investment of up to 49% of paid-up capital on a fully diluted basis, or the applicable sectoral or statutory cap, whichever is lower, is not subject to Government approval or sectoral-condition compliance if the investment does not result in:

- Transfer of ownership and control of the resident Indian company from resident Indian citizens; or
- Transfer of ownership or control to persons resident outside India.

Other investments by persons resident outside India are subject to Government approval and sectoral conditions under the Non-Debt Rules.

Section 9 of the Income Tax Act was amended by the Finance Act, 2017 to clarify that Explanation 5 does not apply to an asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in an FII referred to in Section 115AD(a) for assessment years beginning on or after April 1, 2012, but before April 1, 2015. The amendment applies retrospectively from April 1, 2012, beginning with assessment year 2012–13.

Section 9 was further amended to clarify that Explanation 5 does not apply to an asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in a Category I or Category II FPI under the SEBI (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2014. This amendment applies retrospectively from April 1, 2015, beginning with assessment year 2015–16.

The Finance Act 2020, dated March 27, 2020, modified the proviso to Explanation 5. The amendments apply from April 1, 2020, beginning with assessment year 2020–21.

Explanation 5 shall not apply to an asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in an FII referred to in Section 115AD(a) for an assessment year beginning on or after April 1, 2012, but before April 1, 2015. Further, Explanation 5 shall not apply to an asset or capital asset held by a non-resident through direct or indirect investment in a Category I or Category II FPI under the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Foreign Portfolio Investors) Regulations, 2014, made under the Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992 (15 of 1992).
