## Our inability to execute contracts and/or amendments with clients on a timely basis can impact our revenues and profits, causing fluctuations in our reported results.

Infosys generally enters into contracts with clients before beginning work. However, it may occasionally begin client projects before contracts or amendments to previously executed contracts are completed. This may affect the company’s ability to recognize revenue for work performed in a given period and may affect margins when costs and revenue are accounted for in different periods. In rare cases of contract abandonment, Infosys may be unable to recover costs incurred before contract execution.

## VI. Risks related to our operations

## Maintaining a hybrid working model may continue to expose us to various risks.

Infosys continues to operate under a hybrid work model for employees and service providers. As the company hires, trains, and deploys talent remotely, it may face the following risks:

- Infosys has adopted a hybrid hiring process in India, where the majority of its employees are hired, while virtual hiring continues in locations outside India. Virtual hiring may make it more difficult to assess candidates effectively because of fake profiles, fraudulent mechanisms used to respond to interview questions, limited in-person interaction, and technical infrastructure issues. This could result in the hiring of unqualified or unsuitable candidates, service delivery disruptions, and business-operation impacts. Increased fraud risks may delay background verification, affecting timely talent deployment, client satisfaction, and reputation.
- Difficulty training employees effectively in a hybrid model because of limited physical training sessions and personal interaction could affect talent deployment, business requirements, service delivery, productivity, client sentiment, and costs.
- Employees working remotely over the long term may be more difficult to integrate into the company’s corporate culture and social networks, potentially affecting engagement and retention of high-performing employees.
- A large number of employees working remotely may increase exposure to cybersecurity and data-privacy breaches, which could disrupt services and operations and affect revenue, profitability, and reputation.
- Continued remote working creates a risk that some employees may work for multiple employers simultaneously, or “moonlight,” without required prior approvals. This could result in conflicts of interest, confidentiality breaches, and reduced productivity.
- Employee preferences for long-term remote work, together with client expectations that employees return to offices, could affect attrition, client satisfaction, and the company’s ability to grow profitably if not managed adequately.

If employees change locations while working remotely without informing Infosys, the company’s ability to comply with local tax laws and other regulatory requirements may be affected.

## Our reputation could be at risk and we may be liable to our clients or to regulators for damages caused by unauthorized disclosure of confidential information and sensitive data.

Infosys depends on information-technology networks and systems to process, transmit, host, and securely store electronic information and to communicate among its locations worldwide and with clients, suppliers, and partners. The company often collects and stores sensitive or confidential client data. Security breaches, employee misappropriation, unauthorized access, and human or technological error could result in unauthorized disclosure of sensitive data.

The theft, unauthorized use, or publication of Infosys’s or its clients’ confidential or proprietary business information could adversely affect the company’s reputation and competitive position. Failures in networks or computer systems used by Infosys or its clients could result in substantial-damages claims and significant reputational harm. Many client agreements do not limit Infosys’s potential liability for breaches of confidentiality.

As a global service provider serving clients across industries, Infosys manages, uses, collects, and stores sensitive data subject to multiple regulatory regimes, including U.S. federal and state laws governing personal financial and health data and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). These laws and regulations are increasing in complexity and number and change frequently. Their scope and coverage may involve stakeholders beyond the geography in which Infosys operates, increasing compliance risk and cost.

If any person, including an employee, negligently disregards or intentionally breaches established controls, or otherwise mismanages or misappropriates Infosys or client data, the company could face litigation, monetary damages, regulatory enforcement actions, fines, and criminal prosecution in one or more jurisdictions, in addition to reputational damage. Monetary damages may not be subject to contractual liability limits or exclusions of consequential or indirect damages and could be significant.

Infosys’s liability insurance, including cyber insurance, may not be sufficient in type or amount to cover claims related to security breaches, cyberattacks, and other related incidents. Some contracts involve projects critical to clients’ operations and provide benefits that may be difficult to quantify. Failures in a client’s system or security breaches, regardless of Infosys’s responsibility, could result in substantial-damages claims, significant defense expenses, or large settlement payments. Unauthorized access to or disclosure of data in Infosys’s possession or control, or failure to comply with applicable laws and regulations, could also result in civil or criminal enforcement actions, penalties, and lawsuits by clients, clients’ customers, or other parties.

Data-protection laws and expectations continue to evolve in ways that may restrict Infosys’s access to, use of, or disclosure of sensitive data, require increased expenditures, or prevent the company from offering certain services.

The hybrid working model creates a relatively higher potential for confidential data exposure. Infosys seeks to strengthen and automate controls and educate employees about data security and best practices; however, non-material data breaches have occurred, and future material or non-material breaches cannot be completely ruled out. Such breaches could adversely affect the company’s brand, financial condition, and results of operations.

## Our reputation could be at risk and we may be liable to our clients for damages caused by cybersecurity incidents.

AI-led cyberattacks represent a new frontier in digital threats. Artificial intelligence can be used to identify vulnerabilities, create convincing phishing messages, and adapt attacks in real time to bypass security measures. The increasing sophistication and complexity of cyberattacks, cybercrime, and cyber insecurity have made the global cyber-threat landscape highly volatile.

Infosys and its third-party service providers have been, and may in the future be, targets of cybersecurity attacks. The company may be unable to adapt threat-detection and prevention measures quickly enough to detect or prevent new, modified, or evolving threats to its solutions or product offerings.

Potential causes of cybersecurity breaches and other information-security incidents include:

- Insider threats.
- Hackers and other state or non-state actors seeking to harm Infosys or its clients, including government clients and clients in sensitive industries such as financial services, insurance, energy, utilities, and healthcare.
- Technological or human error and inadvertent actions by employees and contractors.
- Malware, ransomware, viruses, worms, and similar threats, including infections spreading between environments.
- Increased threat and attack surfaces resulting from the hybrid work model and the emergence of the metaverse.
- Vulnerabilities in security-automation systems.
- Unintended data leakage and security vulnerabilities arising from the use of AI.
- Vulnerabilities in the supply chain, including the software supply chain.
- Malicious use of AI to industrialize tailored, high-volume, high-impact cyberattacks, potentially overwhelming organizations and preventing effective operations.
