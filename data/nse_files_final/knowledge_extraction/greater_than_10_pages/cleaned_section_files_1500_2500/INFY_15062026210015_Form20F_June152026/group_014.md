# Intellectual Property and Open-Source Software Risks

The company cannot assure that its services and solutions do not infringe or misappropriate third-party intellectual property rights. Under its hybrid work model, employees may reuse intellectual property from different clients despite safeguards and policies designed to prevent such reuse. Related claims could damage the company’s reputation, result in substantial costs, restrict its ability to offer certain services or solutions, and require significant resources over an extended period.

In most contracts, the company indemnifies clients for expenses and liabilities arising from claimed infringement or misappropriation of third-party intellectual property rights. In some cases, these indemnities could exceed the revenue received from the client.

If the company cannot secure the right to continue offering an affected service or solution on reasonable terms, or cannot substitute alternative technology, its results of operations could be materially adversely affected. The risk of infringement and misappropriation claims may increase as the company expands its industry software solutions and platforms and develops and licenses software to multiple clients.

The company also relies on third-party software for some services and solutions. If it loses the ability to use that software, including because the software is found to infringe third-party rights, it may need to obtain substitute software or alternative technology. An inability to replace the software in a timely or cost-effective manner could adversely affect results of operations.

The company incorporates open-source technology into its services, proprietary products, and platforms. Open-source licenses may require component code, or software into which components are integrated, to be made freely accessible under open-source terms. Security vulnerabilities in open-source software may expose products to risks and result in financial claims.

Although the company takes measures to comply with open-source terms and assess known security vulnerabilities, unknown vulnerabilities or third-party claims could require disclosure of proprietary source code or other confidential product information, or make that information freely accessible under open-source terms. These outcomes could adversely affect the company’s competitive position, business operations, financial condition, and relationships with clients.
