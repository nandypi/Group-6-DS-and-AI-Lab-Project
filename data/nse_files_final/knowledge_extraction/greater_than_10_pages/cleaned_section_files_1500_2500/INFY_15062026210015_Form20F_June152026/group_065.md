## Item 12. Description of Securities Other Than Equity Securities

The fees and charges payable by holders of Infosys ADSs include:

- A fee not exceeding U.S.$0.05 per ADS for each issuance of ADSs, including issuances resulting from distributions of shares, share splits, and rights distributions.
- A fee not exceeding U.S.$0.05 per ADS for each surrender of ADSs in exchange for the underlying deposited securities.
- A fee not exceeding U.S.$0.01 per ADS for each cash distribution.
- A fee not exceeding U.S.$0.01 per ADS for each stock dividend or other free or bonus distribution of the underlying deposited securities distributed in the form of ADSs.
- An annual fee not exceeding U.S.$0.02 per ADS for the operation and maintenance of the ADS program.
- A fee for distributing deposited securities under the deposit agreement. This fee equals the fee for the execution and delivery of ADSs described above that would have been charged if the securities had instead been deposited with the depositary.

Under the deposit agreement, the depositary may also charge each registered holder for:

- Taxes and other governmental charges incurred by the depositary or custodian on an ADS or an equity share underlying an ADS.
- Transfer or registration fees for registering or transferring deposited securities, including fees charged by a central securities depository where applicable.
- Cable, telex, facsimile transmission, and delivery expenses.
- Customary expenses incurred in converting foreign currency, including expenses related to foreign-exchange controls and other regulatory requirements.
- Fees and expenses incurred in complying with regulatory requirements applicable to ADSs or the underlying deposited securities.
- Fees and expenses incurred by the depositary or its agents in servicing the ADSs or underlying deposited securities, selling securities, delivering underlying deposited securities, or complying with applicable laws, rules, and regulations.

For cash distributions, fees are generally deducted from the cash distributed. Other fees may be collected from ADS holders in a manner determined by the depositary, including for ADSs registered in investors’ names and ADSs held in brokerage and custodian accounts through DTC. For non-cash distributions, such as stock dividends, the depositary charges the applicable ADS record-date holder at the time of distribution. For ADSs registered in the investor’s name, the depositary sends invoices to the applicable record-date ADS holders.

If ADS holders or beneficial owners are responsible for taxes or other governmental charges, the depositary, custodian, or Infosys may withhold or deduct those amounts from distributions relating to deposited securities. They may also sell deposited securities for the account of the holder or beneficial owner and apply the distributions and sale proceeds toward the taxes, including applicable interest and penalties. The holder or beneficial owner remains liable for any deficiency.

## Fees and other payments made by the depositary

During fiscal 2026, the depositary incurred approximately $227,413 in expenses related to the ADS program, including payments to proxy-processing firms for mailing notices, proxy cards, and other interim communications to ADS holders or their brokers, including dividend reinvestment and transfer fees.

For the year ended March 31, 2026, the depositary paid Infosys approximately $5.04 million, after withholding tax. Infosys may use these funds for investor relations activities related to the ADR program, including annual reports and Form 20-F filings, listing fees, roadshows, investor targeting, peer analysis, perception studies, postage for annual and interim reports and other communications to ADR holders, broker conferences, and analyst events.

Under certain circumstances, including termination of the ADS program or removal of the depositary, Infosys may be required to repay a portion of amounts reimbursed in prior periods.

## Item 15. Controls and Procedures

## DISCLOSURE CONTROLS AND PROCEDURES

As of March 31, 2026, management, with the participation of the CEO and CFO, evaluated the effectiveness of Infosys’s disclosure controls and procedures under Rules 13a-15(e) and 15d-15(e) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, as amended.

Disclosure controls and procedures are designed to ensure that information required in filings and submissions under the Exchange Act is recorded, processed, summarized, and reported within the periods specified by SEC rules and forms. They also provide for material information to be accumulated and communicated to management, including the CEO and CFO, to support timely disclosure decisions.

Management recognizes that controls and procedures, regardless of how well designed and operated, can provide only reasonable assurance that their objectives will be met.

Based on the evaluation, the CEO and CFO concluded that Infosys’s disclosure controls and procedures were effective as of March 31, 2026. They concluded that required information was recorded, processed, summarized, and reported within the periods specified by SEC rules and forms, and that material information relating to Infosys and its consolidated subsidiaries was accumulated and communicated to management as appropriate.

## Management's Annual Report on Internal Control over Financial Reporting

Management is responsible for establishing and maintaining adequate internal control over financial reporting under Rules 13a-15(f) and 15d-15(f) of the Exchange Act.

Internal control over financial reporting is designed to provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of financial reporting and the preparation of financial statements for external purposes in accordance with International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB). The controls include policies and procedures that:

- Maintain records that accurately and fairly reflect transactions and dispositions of assets in reasonable detail.
- Provide reasonable assurance that transactions are recorded as necessary to prepare financial statements in accordance with IFRS and that receipts and expenditures are made only with authorization from management and directors.
- Provide reasonable assurance regarding the prevention or timely detection of unauthorized acquisition, use, or disposition of assets that could materially affect the financial statements.

Because of inherent limitations, internal control over financial reporting may not prevent or detect misstatements. Evaluations of effectiveness in future periods are also subject to the risk that controls may become inadequate because of changing conditions or that compliance with policies and procedures may deteriorate.

Management assessed the effectiveness of internal control over financial reporting as of March 31, 2026, using the Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013) issued by the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission (COSO). Management concluded that Infosys’s internal control over financial reporting was effective as of March 31, 2026.

Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP, Infosys’s independent registered public accounting firm, audited the consolidated financial statements included in this Annual Report on Form 20-F and issued a report on the effectiveness of internal control over financial reporting as of March 31, 2026.

## Opinion on Internal Control over Financial Reporting

Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP audited the internal control over financial reporting of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries as of March 31, 2026, based on the COSO Internal Control—Integrated Framework (2013).
