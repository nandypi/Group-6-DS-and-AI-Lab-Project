## Our clients may operate in sectors that are adversely impacted by climate change, which could consequently impact our business and reputation.

There is increased concern and awareness of climate-change risks across industry sectors in which our clients operate. Current or future clients in affected sectors may need to transition from climate-impacting solutions to climate-friendly solutions, creating transition or investment risks for their businesses. For example, clients in mining and oil-exploration businesses may face severe financial stress because of investments in climate-friendly solutions. This could impede Infosys's growth due to its exposure to such sectors.

Societal pressure or adverse media publicity against companies using or implementing climate-impacting solutions could also affect Infosys's reputation if it is a significant service provider to those clients.

In certain geographies where clients have begun transitioning to sustainable solutions, regulatory or regime changes may cause clients to abandon or temporarily hold those investments, potentially affecting growth and profitability in related service lines.

## Changes in immigration laws, their interpretation or enforcement, increase in visa fees, or delays in getting visas in the jurisdictions in which we operate, could result in increased compliance costs, business disruptions, inability to bid for or fulfill projects, enforcement actions or penalties, any of which could adversely affect our business, results of operations and financial condition

A significant portion of Infosys's employees in the United States, the United Kingdom, the EU, and other countries and regions rely on work visas and permits. This exposes the business to changes in immigration laws, their interpretation and enforcement. These legal requirements are numerous, frequently changing, and sometimes conflict with one another. The United States and certain other jurisdictions have adopted protectionist interpretations of these laws and increased regulatory scrutiny and enforcement.

On September 19, 2025, the President of the United States signed a presidential proclamation imposing a $100,000 fee on new H-1B visa applications, subject to certain exclusions. The U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) also issued a pronouncement restricting immigrants' ability to file for a Green Card while residing in the United States, except in extraordinary circumstances.

Changes in legal requirements or their interpretation, increased visa fees, regulatory scrutiny and enforcement may result in higher compliance costs, business disruptions, enforcement actions, sanctions, fines on Infosys or its employees, higher visa-application rejection rates, loss of access to visas, and increases in prevailing wages for the visa-dependent workforce. Visa delays and adverse revisions to immigration laws and regulations have caused, and may continue to cause, delays, staffing shortages, additional costs, and an inability to bid for or fulfill client projects.

The U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) is conducting an investigation into how Infosys classified certain H-1B visa-recipient employees in immigration documents filed with certain U.S. government authorities. Infosys is engaged in discussions with the DOJ and continues its own inquiry. At this stage, Infosys is unable to predict the outcome, including whether it could have a material adverse effect on its business and results of operations.

## Our clients may be the subject of economic or other sanctions by governments and regulators in key geographies that we operate in, limiting our ability to grow these relationships, and risking increased penalties and exposure of our business to consequential sanctions.

Infosys engages with clients operating in various geographies and sectors, including conflict zones and highly regulated sectors. Sanctions may be imposed on clients, their investors, or key managerial personnel before or during an engagement.

Infosys takes reasonable precautions to determine whether a potential client is on a sanctions list. However, its ability to screen clients and avoid contracts with sanctioned entities or personnel is limited and depends largely on publicly available information and third-party databases. If a client becomes subject to sanctions during an engagement, the relationship could expose Infosys to consequential sanctions, administrative action, or loss of government contracts or engagements.

## A large part of our revenues is dependent on a limited number of our clients, and the loss of any one of our major clients or large contracts could significantly impact our business.

Infosys has historically earned, and expects to continue earning, a significant portion of its revenues from a limited number of clients and large contracts. In fiscal 2026, its five largest clients accounted for 12.9% of total revenues, while its ten largest clients accounted for approximately 20.5%.

The volume of work performed for individual clients may vary from year to year. A major client in one year may not provide the same level of revenues in a subsequent year. Clients may reduce or discontinue work for reasons including:

- changing their outsourcing strategy by moving work in-house;
- establishing Global Capability Centers (GCCs);
- replacing existing software with packaged software supported by the licensor;
- reducing technology spending;
- shortening contract durations because of geopolitical, economic, or competitive conditions; or
- adopting new AI solutions that reduce or eliminate the need for certain existing services.

If Infosys loses a major client or large contract, or if a major client significantly reduces its business volume, revenues and profitability could be adversely affected.

Clients' financial stability may be affected by demand and supply challenges, currency fluctuations, regulatory sanctions, changes in tariffs or trade restrictions, geopolitical conflicts, and other macroeconomic conditions. These factors may reduce clients' ability to pay vendors on time and may result in credit-rating downgrades or reduced access to funding.

A client's credit-rating downgrade could adversely affect Infosys's credit terms with that client, cash flow, and results of operations. Although Infosys evaluates client credit risks before onboarding, sudden changes in clients' financial health may adversely affect its ability to recover fees for services rendered. An adverse change in India's debt rating or Infosys's credit rating could also negatively affect its business and profitability.

## Outbreaks of contagious diseases, viruses or pandemics, could disrupt our business, financial condition, and results of operations.

Outbreaks of contagious diseases, viruses, or pandemics, together with related mitigation efforts, have disrupted and may in the future disrupt Infosys's business. The impact of a future outbreak would depend on uncertain factors, including the availability of effective vaccines, regulatory actions affecting Infosys and its clients, changes in consumer behavior or spending patterns, and effects on third-party providers.

A pandemic or similar public-health threat could also heighten other risks described in the “Risk Factors” section.

## We may not be able to provide end-to-end business solutions for our clients, which could lead to clients discontinuing their work with us, which in turn could harm our business.

Infosys has been expanding the breadth of solutions and services offered in client engagements, including:

- software applications;
- automation solutions;
- digital design and analytics services;
- engineering services;
- cloud-related services;
- application development and maintenance;
- consulting;
- business process management;
- systems integration; and
- security and infrastructure management.

If Infosys fails to anticipate and develop offerings using emerging technologies, or fails to enhance existing offerings in response to rapid changes in technology and target industries, its business, financial condition, and results of operations may be adversely affected. Relevant technologies include AI-based automation, generative AI, cloud, robotic process automation, the Internet of Things (IoT), and autonomous vehicles.

An inability to provide solutions in areas of client interest may lead clients to insource work, establish GCCs, or shift work to competitors.

The broader service portfolio may result in larger and more complex client projects. These projects require closer relationships with clients and potentially with other technology-service providers and vendors, as well as a more thorough understanding of client operations. If Infosys cannot establish these relationships or understand client operations adequately, its offerings may not meet client needs, jeopardizing engagements and negatively affecting revenues and financial condition.

Large projects often involve multiple components, engagements, or stages. Clients may choose not to retain Infosys for additional stages, or may cancel or delay planned engagements, for reasons unrelated to service quality and outside Infosys's control. These reasons may include clients' business or financial conditions, the broader economy, cost pressures arising from tariffs or trade restrictions, the introduction of tariffs or taxes on IT services, and geopolitical conflicts in Eastern Europe and the Middle East.

Terminations, cancellations, or delays can make project-resource planning difficult and negatively affect profitability. Geopolitical conflicts in Eastern Europe and the Middle East may also affect Infosys's ability to service global clients in certain geographies, potentially resulting in client dissatisfaction, loss of future business, contract terminations, and litigation.

Business owners at client organizations are increasingly making or influencing technology-related buying decisions. If Infosys cannot maintain or establish relationships with new buying centers, or cannot articulate the value of its technology services to these functions, revenues may be adversely affected.
