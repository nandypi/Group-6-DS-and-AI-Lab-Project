## Taxation for buyback of equity shares

- All domestic companies were subject to buyback tax at 23.296%, including a 12% surcharge and 4% Health and Education cess.
- Shareholders were eligible for an income tax exemption under section 10(34A) of the Income Tax Act, 1961.
- A non-resident shareholder’s obligation to pay tax on gains, if any, in the country where the shareholder is tax resident was separate from the company’s buyback tax obligation. Shareholders were advised to consult their own tax advisors.

Pursuant to the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024, section 115QA of the Income Tax Act, 1961 no longer applies to buybacks undertaken on or after October 1, 2024. The corresponding shareholder exemption under section 10(34A) also ceased to apply.

For buybacks undertaken on or after October 1, 2024:

- Consideration received by shareholders is treated as dividend income under section 2(22)(f) and taxed as “Income from other sources”.
- The income is subject to withholding tax at applicable rates under the Income Tax Act, 1961 and applicable tax treaties, together with the Multilateral Instrument where applicable.
- Non-resident shareholders may need to provide tax forms, including a Tax Residency Certificate and electronic Form 10F.
- For computing capital gains on shares bought back, consideration received is deemed to be nil under section 46A of the Income Tax Act, 1961, resulting in a capital loss.
- The capital loss is eligible for set-off and carry-forward under section 74 of the Income Tax Act, 1961.

With effect from April 1, 2026, buyback proceeds are taxed as capital gains. Promoters are subject to additional income tax on capital gains arising from a buyback. The total tax payable by a promoter consists of regular capital gains tax plus the prescribed additional tax.

### Effective Tax Rates for Promoters

Promoter being a domestic company with listed shares: aggregate effective tax rate of approximately 22%.

| Capital Gains | Normal Rate | Additional Tax |
|---|---:|---:|
| Short-term Capital Gains | 20% | 2% |
| Long-term Capital Gains | 12.5% | 9.5% |

Promoter other than a domestic company: aggregate effective tax rate of approximately 30%.

| Capital Gains | Normal Rate | Additional Tax |
|---|---:|---:|
| Short-term Capital Gains | 20% | 10% |
| Long-term Capital Gains | 12.5% | 17.5% |

### Effective Tax Rates for Non-Promoters

| Capital Gains | Normal Rate | Additional Tax |
|---|---:|---|
| Short-term Capital Gains | 20% | No Additional Tax |
| Long-term Capital Gains | 12.5% | No Additional Tax |

For purposes of this section:

- For a company whose shares are listed on a recognized stock exchange in India, “promoter” has the meaning assigned under regulation 2(k) of the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Buy-Back of Securities) Regulations, 2018, made under the Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992.
- In any other case, “promoter” means:
  - A promoter as defined in section 2(69) of the Companies Act, 2013; or
  - A person who holds, directly or indirectly, more than 10% of the company’s shareholding.

Any buyback undertaken on the NSE or BSE is chargeable to Securities Transaction Tax (STT).

## Taxation for American Depositary Shares (ADSs) Holders

ADS holders with underlying equity shares may, following withdrawal of those equity shares, participate in a buyback by selling the equity shares on Indian stock exchanges.

There is no assurance that equity shares offered by an ADS holder in a buyback will be accepted. Withdrawal of underlying equity shares is subject to the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 and related rules and regulations, the Income Tax Act, 1961 and related rules and regulations, the Depository Receipts Scheme, 2014, and other applicable approvals from the Reserve Bank of India.

ADS holders were advised to consult legal, financial, and tax advisors before participating in a buyback, including on regulatory approvals and tax issues.

### Income Tax Implications on Withdrawal and Re-Deposit of Equity Shares

- There are arguments that conversion of ADSs into shares should not be subject to capital gains tax, but the tax treatment is not free from doubt because the law is unclear.
- Shares not accepted by the company in a buyback may be reconverted into ADSs. Two views exist on whether reconversion is a taxable transfer that attracts capital gains tax or a non-taxable transfer. The view that reconversion is not a taxable transfer may be considered the better view.

### Income Tax Implications for Sale of Equity Shares Following ADS Conversion

Prior to October 1, 2024:

- Domestic companies were subject to buyback tax under section 115QA of the Income Tax Act, 1961 at 23.296%, including a 12% surcharge and 4% Health and Education cess.
- Shareholders were eligible for an income tax exemption under section 10(34A) of the Income Tax Act, 1961.
- A non-resident shareholder’s obligation to pay tax on gains, if any, in the country where the shareholder was tax resident was separate from the company’s buyback tax obligation. Shareholders were advised to consult their own tax advisors.
