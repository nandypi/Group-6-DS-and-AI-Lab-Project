## I am the Example for my Team

Most often, a manager is the first person contacted about a concern in the work environment. Managers have the following responsibilities:

- Be a role model of ethical behavior.
- Encourage team members to raise issues and speak up.
- Communicate a positive commitment to ethics and compliance.
- Promote Infosys values, the Code of Conduct, and compliance with policies and applicable law.
- Support ethics and compliance awareness and training programs.
- Maintain open avenues for communication.
- Listen and respond fairly to employee concerns.
- Find satisfactory and complete resolutions to ethical issues.
- Escalate concerns when additional assistance is needed.

## A. RESPECTING EACH OTHER

### An Equal Opportunity Workplace Free of Discrimination or Harassment

Infosys is committed to a work environment free of discrimination and harassment. It is an equal opportunity employer, and employment decisions are based on merit, business needs, and fair employment practices.

Infosys does not discriminate against or permit harassment based on race, color, religion, disability, gender, national origin, sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression, age, genetic information, military status, or any other legally protected status.

Infosys prohibits child labor, forced labor, and human trafficking in its supply chains, consistent with its Human Rights and Modern Slavery commitments. Employees in the United States may also refer to the U.S. Equal Opportunity Statement on the Policy Portal.

Decisions regarding hiring, promotion, termination, transfer, leave of absence, and compensation must be based only on relevant business factors.

Verbal or physical mistreatment, harassment, bullying, abusive or intimidating treatment, inappropriate language or gestures, disorderly conduct, violence, and other conduct that interferes with a co-worker’s ability to perform their job are prohibited.

Infosys’s Anti-Discrimination and Anti-Harassment Policy applies to all persons involved in Company operations. It prohibits harassment by Company employees toward other employees, outside vendors, and customers.

### A Safe Place to Work

All forms of substance abuse, and the use or distribution of drugs and alcohol while at work, are prohibited. Unless required as part of a role, possession or use of weapons, firearms, or ammunition while conducting Company business is prohibited.

Employees should report unsafe workplace situations through the Helpline and familiarize themselves with applicable emergency procedures and local safety manuals.

Infosys supports employee well-being and recognizes the importance of physical, mental, and emotional health. Confidential internal resources, including the HALE program, are available without fear of stigma or retaliation.

## B. ETHICS IN OUR BUSINESS ACTIVITIES

Infosys is subject to applicable regulations and requires compliance with those regulations in both letter and spirit.

### Preventing Corruption

As a global company, Infosys is subject to the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (India), the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA), as if it were a U.S.-incorporated company, and the U.K. Bribery Act 2010. These laws prohibit bribery of government officials and commercial partners.

### Government Officials

A government official includes an officer or employee of any agency, department, or instrument of a government, or anyone acting on its behalf. This includes officers or employees of political parties; military, civil service, or judicial systems; candidates for political or public office; members of ruling or royal families; employees of government-controlled businesses; and employees of public international organizations. Family members of government officials are also considered government officials.

### Prevention Corruption

Infosys must not offer, directly or indirectly, any gift, entertainment, or anything of value to a government official or commercial partner, including customers or their representatives, to:

- Obtain or retain business.
- Influence business decisions.
- Secure an unfair advantage.

This prohibition includes bribes, kickbacks, and facilitation payments.

### What is a bribe?

A bribe is anything of value that may be viewed as an attempt to influence an action or decision to obtain or retain business or acquire an improper advantage. Examples include money, gifts, favors, use of Company resources, entertainment, and other valuable items.

### What is a kickback?

A kickback is an arrangement in which two parties agree that a portion of sales or profits will be returned to the purchasing party in exchange for making a deal.

### What is a facilitation payment?

A facilitation payment is a payment to a government official to expedite or ensure a routine action, such as issuing a visa, work permit, or license.

Infosys does not make or permit such payments by third parties acting on its behalf, including vendors, agents, customers, consultants, alliance partners, suppliers, and contractors.

Infosys may be held responsible for the conduct of third parties that violate the law while working on its behalf. Employees must remain alert to bribery, kickbacks, improper payments, and other corrupting influences in third-party relationships.

### Anything of value? What does that mean?

“Anything of value” may include:

- Cash payments
- Gifts
- Entertainment
- Excessive business promotional activities
- Covering or reimbursing expenses
- Investment opportunities
- Shares and securities
- Loans or contractual rights
- Promises of future employment
- Payments under consulting agreements
- Subcontracts
- Stock options
- Similar items of value

### QUESTION

One of the Company’s vendors always sends me a large gift basket of fruit and chocolate during the holiday season. Can I accept this?

### RESPONSE

If a holiday gift basket is of low value and received infrequently, it is unlikely to create an obligation or influence. In that case, it may be accepted. Employees are encouraged to share such gift baskets with other employees in their department. For permissible value limits, refer to the Anti-Bribery and Anti-Corruption Policy.
