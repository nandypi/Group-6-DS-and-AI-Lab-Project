# Climate-Related Risks

## Climate-related market risks

Increasing awareness of climate change and related socioenvironmental issues has led clients to request climate action updates, including emissions performance disclosures such as the Carbon Disclosure Project (CDP) score.

Clients and potential clients have also asked about Infosys’ Net Zero commitment, commitment to Science Based Targets, and supply chain emissions reduction initiatives. Responses to these enquiries may influence clients’ overall decision-making. If performance is not effectively managed in these areas, Infosys’ ability to compete for and win or renew contracts may be adversely affected.

## Climate-related regulatory risks

Countries that agreed to the 2015 Paris Agreement on climate change have made promises or plans, known as Intended Nationally Determined Contributions, to reduce greenhouse gas emissions and address climate change. These commitments have resulted in emissions reduction goals, environmental targets, disclosures, and increased compliance requirements for businesses across different geographies.

Regulations requiring detailed disclosures on climate action, performance, risks, and opportunities already exist or are being developed in many regions. New and emerging regulations may increase compliance costs. Incorrect reporting of environmental data or metrics, including misinterpretation of evolving global standards, may result in penalties, reputational impact, and other consequences.

Potential violations or non-conformance with climate laws and regulations could affect Infosys’ reputation and profitability through penalties or limitations on its ability to operate in certain geographies. This could adversely affect business performance.
