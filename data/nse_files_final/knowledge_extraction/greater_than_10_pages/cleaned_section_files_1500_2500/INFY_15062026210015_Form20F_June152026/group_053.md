## Disclosure of Ownership Interest

Section 89 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013 requires holders of record who do not hold beneficial interests in shares of Indian companies to declare to the company the nature of their interest and details of the beneficial owner.

A person who fails to make the required declaration within 30 days may be liable for a fine of up to ₹50,000. For continuing failure, an additional fine of ₹200 for each day may apply, subject to a maximum of ₹500,000.

Beneficial interest in a share includes, directly or indirectly, through any contract, arrangement, or otherwise, the right or entitlement, alone or together with another person, to:

1. Exercise or cause to be exercised any or all rights attached to the share.
2. Receive or participate in any dividend or other distribution relating to the share.

Failure to comply with Section 89 does not affect the company’s obligation to pay dividends to the registered holder of the relevant shares.

It is unclear under Indian law whether Section 89 applies to holders of the company’s ADSs. Investors who exchange ADSs for the underlying equity shares will be subject to Section 89. ADS holders may also be required to comply with notification and disclosure obligations under the Deposit Agreement entered into by the holders, the company, and the Depositary.

## Declaration of Significant beneficial ownership in the Company

Section 90 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013 requires individual shareholders, acting alone or together or through one or more persons or trusts, including persons resident outside India, to declare beneficial interests of at least 10% in the shares of a company or the right to exercise, or actual exercise of, significant influence or control over the company.

The Companies (Significant Beneficial Ownership) Rules, 2018, as amended from time to time, prescribe the thresholds and rules for identifying a significant beneficial owner and require filings and disclosures when relevant details change.

Failure to submit the required disclosures is punishable by a minimum fine of ₹50,000, which may extend to ₹200,000. For continuing failure, an additional fine of up to ₹1,000 per day may apply after the first day.

Under the Companies (Management and Administration) Second Amendment Rules, 2023, a designated person within the company is responsible for providing the Registrar of Companies with information regarding beneficial interests in the company’s shares.

## Audit and Annual Report

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013, a company must file its financial statements with the Registrar of Companies within 30 days of the AGM.

Copies of the annual report and AGM notice must be submitted to the stock exchanges on which the company’s shares are listed and published on the company’s website no later than the date dispatch to shareholders begins.

At least 21 days before the AGM, a listed company must provide:

- Electronic copies of the full annual report to shareholders who have registered their email addresses.
- Physical copies to shareholders who have not registered their email addresses and to shareholders who request them.

The company must also file an annual return containing a list of shareholders and other company information within 60 days after the conclusion of the AGM.

## Reduction of Share Capital

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013, a company may reduce its share capital by special resolution and approval of the court or tribunal of the state in which its registered office is situated, in the manner prescribed by the Act.

The company’s articles of association must authorize the reduction. These provisions do not apply to a company share buyback.

## Company Acquisition of Equity Shares

A company may acquire its own equity shares without approval from the court or the National Company Law Tribunal if it complies with the prescribed requirements of the Indian Companies Act, 2013.

Public companies listed on recognized Indian stock exchanges must also comply with the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Buy-back of Securities) Regulations, 2018. As a public company listed on two recognized Indian stock exchanges, the company must comply with both the applicable provisions of the Indian Companies Act, 2013 and the Buy-back Regulations.

For ADS holders to participate in a company buyback, they must first withdraw the equity shares underlying their ADSs before the record date fixed for the buyback, so that they are holders of equity shares on that record date.

There is no assurance that shares offered by an ADS investor in a company buyback will be accepted. Conversion of ADSs into underlying equity shares and reconversion of those equity shares into ADSs is subject to the limits of permissible foreign shareholding. Indian law currently permits foreign investment of up to 100% of the company’s share capital.

ADS holder participation in a buyback is also subject to applicable requirements under:

- The Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 and related rules and regulations.
- The Income Tax Act and related rules and regulations.
- The Depository Receipts Scheme, 2014.
- The Circular on the framework for issue of Depositary Receipts dated October 10, 2019.
- SEBI circulars dated November 28, 2019, October 1, 2020, and December 18, 2020.
- The SEBI master circular for Depositories dated October 6, 2023, read with the SEBI master circular dated December 3, 2024.

The relevant SEBI circulars were subsequently rescinded and replaced by the applicable SEBI master circulars. Participation may also require approvals from relevant authorities, including the Reserve Bank of India under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999.

ADS investors should consult legal advisers before participating in a company buyback, including regarding regulatory approvals and tax matters.

## Liquidation Rights

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013 and the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, certain payments have priority over payments to equity shareholders. These include payments to:

- Employees.
- Tax authorities.
- Secured and unsecured lenders.
- Holders of shares whose terms provide preferential repayment over equity shares.

In a winding-up, holders of equity shares are entitled to repayment of the paid-up capital, or amounts credited as paid-up, after the company has made the priority payments described above. Subject to those payments, any surplus assets are distributed to equity shareholders in proportion to their shareholdings.

## Redemption of Equity Shares

Subject to the buyback provisions described in “Company Acquisition of Equity Shares,” equity shares are not redeemable under the Indian Companies Act, 2013.

## Discriminatory Provisions in Articles

The company’s Articles of Association contain no provisions discriminating against or favoring an existing or prospective holder of securities because that shareholder owns a substantial number of shares.

There are no restrictions on the company’s securities imposed by any other class of securities or contracts.

## Alteration of Shareholder Rights

Under the Indian Companies Act, 2013, and subject to the company’s memorandum or Articles of Association, the rights of a class of shareholders may be altered or varied by either:

1. Written consent from holders of at least three-fourths of the issued shares of that class.
2. A special resolution passed at a separate meeting of holders of the issued shares of that class.

If a variation by one class of shareholders affects the rights of another class, the consent of three-fourths of that other class is also required.

If the Articles of Association contain no applicable provision, an alteration or variation is permitted provided it is not prohibited by the terms of issue of the relevant class of shares.

## Limitations on the Rights to Own Securities

Limitations on the rights to own securities of Indian companies, including the rights of non-resident or foreign shareholders to hold securities, are discussed in the sections titled “Currency Exchange Controls” and “Risk Factors” in Items 10 and 3 of the Annual Report.

## Provisions on Changes in Capital

The company’s authorized capital may be altered by ordinary resolution of the shareholders in a general meeting.

The additional issue of shares is subject to shareholders’ pre-emptive rights. Subject to an ordinary resolution of shareholders in a general meeting, a company may also:

- Increase its share capital.
- Consolidate its share capital into shares with a larger face value.
- Subdivide its shares by reducing their par value.

## Takeover Code

Under the Securities and Exchange Board of India (Substantial Acquisition of Shares and Takeovers) Regulations, 2011 (the “Takeover Code”), an acquirer and persons acting in concert with the acquirer must disclose their aggregate shareholding and voting rights to the company and the relevant stock exchanges when their acquisition causes aggregate ownership to reach 5% or more of the company’s shares or voting rights.

The disclosure must be made within two working days of the acquisition of shares or voting rights or receipt of intimation of allotment of shares.

An acquirer and persons acting in concert who hold shares or voting rights representing 5% or more must disclose the number of shares or voting rights held and any change from the last disclosure when the change exceeds 2% of the company’s total shareholding or voting rights. This disclosure must be made within two working days of receipt of intimation of allotment, acquisition, or disposal of shares or voting rights.

For a sale, disclosure is required even if the sale causes the acquirer’s and persons acting in concert’s combined shareholding to fall below 5%.
