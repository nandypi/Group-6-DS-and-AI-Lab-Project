However, pursuant to the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024, Section 115QA of the Income Tax Act, 1961 will no longer apply to share buybacks undertaken on or after October 1, 2024. The corresponding shareholder exemption under Section 10(34A) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 will also cease to be available.

Consideration received by shareholders pursuant to share buybacks on or after October 1, 2024 will instead be treated as dividend income under Section 2(22)(f) and taxed as “Income from other sources.” The income will be subject to tax withholding at applicable rates prescribed under the Income Tax Act and applicable tax treaties with the respective countries, together with the Multilateral Instrument where applicable. Non-resident shareholders may need to provide tax documents, including a Tax Residency Certificate and electronic Form 10F.

For computing capital gains on shares bought back by the company, the consideration received will be deemed to be nil under Section 46A of the Income Tax Act, 1961, resulting in a capital loss for shareholders. The capital loss may be set off and carried forward under Section 74 of the Income Tax Act, 1961.

With effect from April 1, 2026, buyback proceeds will be taxed as capital gains.

### Stamp Duty and Transfer Tax

A transfer of ADSs is not subject to Indian stamp duty. A sale of equity shares in physical form by a non-resident holder is subject to Indian stamp duty at 0.015% of the market value of the equity shares on the trade date. Customarily, this tax is borne by the transferee. Shares must be traded in dematerialized form.

Under the Finance Act 2019, stamp duty is chargeable on the transfer of shares in dematerialized form. The rate is 0.015% of the total market value of the shares for delivery-based transfers and 0.003% for non-delivery-based transfers. This provision applies from July 1, 2020.

### Goods and Service Tax

Brokerage or commission paid to stockbrokers in connection with the sale or purchase of shares is subject to GST at 18%, effective July 1, 2017. The stockbroker is responsible for collecting GST from the shareholder and paying it to the relevant authority. Under the former service tax regime, the rate was 15%, inclusive of all cess.

## Material U.S. Federal Income and Estate Tax Consequences

The following is a summary of material U.S. federal income and estate tax consequences that may be relevant to the ownership and disposition of equity shares or ADSs, including through a company buyback. It is for general information only.

The summary addresses U.S. federal income and estate tax considerations for U.S. holders. U.S. holders are beneficial owners of equity shares or ADSs who are:

- Individuals who are citizens or residents of the United States.
- Corporations or other entities treated as corporations for U.S. federal income tax purposes that are created or organized under the laws of the United States, any U.S. state, or the District of Columbia.
- Estates whose income is subject to U.S. federal income taxation regardless of its source.
- Trusts for which a U.S. court exercises primary supervision and a U.S. person has authority to control all substantial decisions, or trusts with a valid election under applicable U.S. Treasury Regulations to be treated as U.S. persons.

The summary is limited to U.S. holders who hold equity shares or ADSs as capital assets for U.S. federal income tax purposes, generally for investment, and who are not resident in India for purposes of the Convention between the Government of the United States and the Government of the Republic of India for the Avoidance of Double Taxation and the Prevention of Fiscal Evasion with Respect to Taxes on Income (the “Treaty”).

If a partnership, including an entity treated as a partnership for U.S. federal income tax purposes, holds equity shares or ADSs, the tax treatment of a partner will generally depend on the partner’s status and the partnership’s activities. Partners in a partnership holding equity shares or ADSs should consult their own tax advisers regarding the tax treatment of the investment.

The summary does not address tax considerations applicable to holders subject to special tax rules, including:

- Banks, insurance companies, financial institutions, and dealers in securities or currencies.
- Tax-exempt entities.
- Persons holding equity shares or ADSs as part of a straddle, hedging transaction, or conversion transaction for tax purposes.
- Persons liable for alternative minimum tax.
- Persons holding ADSs or equity shares through partnerships or other pass-through entities.
- Persons whose functional currency is not the U.S. dollar.
- Holders of 10% or more, by voting power or value, of the company’s shares.
- Controlled foreign corporations, foreign-controlled foreign corporations, passive foreign investment companies, or corporations that accumulate earnings to avoid U.S. federal income tax.
- Persons subject to special tax accounting rules because an item of gross income from equity shares or ADSs is included in an applicable financial statement.
- Persons who hold or receive equity shares or ADSs through the exercise of an option or otherwise as compensation.
- Persons deemed to sell equity shares or ADSs under the constructive sale provisions of the Code.
- Certain U.S. expatriates and certain former citizens or long-term residents of the United States.

This summary is based on the Internal Revenue Code as in effect on the date of the Annual Report on Form 20-F; U.S. Treasury Regulations in effect or, in some cases, proposed, as of that date; and judicial and administrative interpretations available on or before that date. It is also based in part on the assumption that each obligation in the deposit agreement and any related agreement will be performed in accordance with its terms.

The foregoing authorities and interpretations are subject to change, including retroactive changes. The Internal Revenue Service or a court may interpret existing authorities differently, which could affect the tax consequences described in the summary. The summary does not address U.S. federal tax laws other than income or estate tax, or any U.S. state, local, or non-U.S. tax laws.
