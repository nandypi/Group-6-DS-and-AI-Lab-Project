## II.    Risks related to the investments we make for our growth

- Our business could suffer if we fail to anticipate and develop new services or enhance existing services in response to rapid changes in technology and the industries on which we focus.
- Investments in or use of artificial intelligence technologies may not be successful and may create business, financial, legal, and reputational risks.
- Acquisitions, strategic investments, strategic partnerships, alliances, and other ventures may not be successful.

## III.    Risks related to our cost structure

- Expenses are difficult to predict and may vary significantly between periods, causing fluctuations in profitability.
- An inability to manage growth could disrupt the business, reduce profitability, and adversely affect implementation of the growth strategy.
- Wage pressures and the hiring of employees and subcontractors in India or elsewhere may reduce profits and prevent the company from sustaining its competitive advantage.
- Substantial cash is invested in creating and maintaining physical and technological infrastructure. Profitability could decline if the business does not grow proportionately.
- Currency fluctuations and changes in interest rates may affect operating results and the yield on cash balances.

## IV.    Risks related to our employee workforce

- Success depends substantially on the ability to hire, attract, motivate, retain, and train highly skilled technology professionals.

## V.    Risks related to our contractual obligations

- Failure to complete fixed-price, fixed-timeframe, or transaction-based pricing contracts within budget and on time may reduce profitability.
- Client contracts are often conditional on performance. Unsatisfactory performance could result in lower-than-anticipated revenue.
- Work with governmental agencies may expose the company to additional risks.
- An inability to execute contracts or amendments with clients on a timely basis can affect revenue and profit, causing fluctuations in reported results.

## VI.    Risks related to our operations

- Maintaining a hybrid working model may expose the company to various risks.
- Unauthorized disclosure of confidential information and sensitive data could damage the company’s reputation and result in liability to clients or regulators.
- Cybersecurity incidents could damage the company’s reputation and result in liability to clients.
- Privacy breaches or incidents under the General Data Protection Regulation (“GDPR”) or other global data privacy regulations could result in financial liabilities and reputational damage. Failure to report such incidents to regulators and data subjects within stipulated timeframes, or to respond to data subject requests in a timely manner, could create additional risk. Client claims for losses caused by employee-related privacy breaches could also have financial and reputational consequences.
- Markets in which the company operates are exposed to earthquakes, floods, tsunamis, storms, pandemics, and other disasters.
- Untoward incidents beyond the company’s control could affect employee safety, assets, infrastructure, business continuity, and reputation.
- Terrorist attacks or war could adversely affect the business, operating results, and financial condition.
- Reputation, access to capital, and longer-term financial stability could be affected if the company does not meet its stated goals under its ESG 2030 vision.

## VII.     Risks related to legislation and regulatory compliance

- New or changing regulatory, corporate governance, and public disclosure requirements increase uncertainty and compliance costs.
- Indian intellectual property laws may not protect software and related intellectual property rights to the same extent as laws in the United States. The company may be unsuccessful in protecting its intellectual property and may face third-party infringement claims.

## VIII.    Risks related to the ADSs

- Fluctuations in the U.S. dollar-to-Indian rupee exchange rate may negatively affect ADS prices and the U.S. dollar value of declared dividends.
- ADS holders may be restricted in their ability to exercise voting rights.
- ADS holders may be restricted in their ability to participate in share buybacks.
- New taxes on profit distributions, changes to the application of such taxes, or changes to buyback regulations could adversely affect shareholder returns.

## I.       Risks related to the markets in which we and our clients operate

Spending on technology products and services by clients and prospective clients fluctuates based on technological advances, geopolitical changes, economic conditions, regulatory environments, tariffs, trade restrictions, and fiscal or monetary policies in the markets where they operate.

Technology and IT budgets may be affected by technology disruptions, geopolitical conflicts, economic slowdowns, tariffs, trade restrictions, and market uncertainty. Reductions in IT spending have adversely affected, and may in the future adversely affect, operating results.

Increased regulation, regulatory changes, government intervention, sanctions, tariffs, trade restrictions, inflation, geopolitical conflicts, and potential global economic slowdowns may adversely affect clients’ businesses and reduce demand for services. These factors may also increase costs related to processes and personnel, negatively affecting the business, operating results, and financial condition.

Clients may face stricter compliance requirements, including industry-specific compliance, privacy, and security standards for data handling. These requirements could affect how services are provided. Geopolitical conflicts, including conflicts in the Middle East, Eastern Europe, and India-Pakistan, may adversely affect clients’ operations or cause them to redirect spending toward business continuity, reducing IT spending. A slowdown in key client geographies or supply-chain locations may adversely affect revenue and profitability.

Regulators have imposed cloud-computing guidelines that require specific controls or regulatory approval before enterprises in certain sectors, including financial services, insurance, and aerospace, outsource certain functions. Regulators may also establish boundaries for the use of evolving technologies such as artificial intelligence. Evolving regulatory frameworks may affect the revenue and profitability of technology-sector clients investing in emerging technologies.

Reduced or delayed IT spending, including spending changes caused by tariffs and trade restrictions, may result in project cancellations, pricing discounts, extended payment terms, or consolidation of technology service providers. Such events have previously affected utilization rates, revenue per billed person-month, proposal competitiveness, gross margins, and cash flows.

Macroeconomic uncertainty, tariffs, trade restrictions, and geopolitical conflicts may increase consumer and wage inflation, energy prices, interest rates, and currency fluctuations, affecting client revenue and profitability. Some clients shut down operations in Russia because of the conflict in Eastern Europe. Clients may reduce discretionary spending or delay contract decisions. Clients seeking to optimize costs may prioritize cost-takeout projects, requiring changes to offerings, talent mobility, and marketing efforts.

Geopolitical conflicts, including conflicts involving India and Pakistan, South Asia, Eastern Europe, and the Middle East, may affect the ability to service global clients if operations cannot be moved from conflict zones promptly or if relocation increases operating costs. Conflicts may also affect clients with operations across the affected geographies and cause them to prioritize investments that decouple operations from conflict zones, reducing discretionary technology-services spending.

The sudden emergence or resurgence of a pandemic in geographies where the company, its clients, or a majority of its employees have significant exposure could affect revenue and profitability.

## An economic slowdown or other factors may affect the economic health of the United States, the United Kingdom, the European Union, Australia or various industries from where our revenues are derived.

Revenue is concentrated in certain geographies and client industries. In fiscal 2026, revenue was derived as follows:

| Geography | Percentage of revenue |
|---|---:|
| North America | 56.1% |
| Europe | 32.1% |
| Rest of the World, excluding India | 8.9% |

In fiscal 2026, 27.9% of revenue was derived from the financial services and insurance industry.

Instability and uneven global economic growth have adversely affected the IT industry in the past and may continue to affect the business and operating results. If the economies of the United States, United Kingdom, or European Union weaken, or if growth remains uneven, clients may significantly reduce or postpone technology spending, lowering demand, revenue, and profitability. Continued inflation and the effects of high interest rates in these geographies may weaken business sentiment and reduce client spending. Factors affecting clients may include supply-chain issues, availability of critical minerals or materials, labor shortages, inflation, political elections, and other macroeconomic conditions.

Global uncertainty arising from pandemics, tariffs, trade restrictions, geopolitical conflicts, or evolving technologies such as artificial intelligence may affect the financial services, retail, consumer goods, telecommunications, energy, and manufacturing industries, which account for a substantial portion of annual revenue. Clients may reduce, postpone, or consolidate IT spending; shorten contract durations; terminate contracts; defer projects; or delay purchases. These developments may reduce demand, utilization, and service prices, adversely affecting revenue and profitability.

The company also depends on energy-sector clients for revenue. Effects on the energy sector from oil-price volatility, including volatility arising from conflicts in Eastern Europe and the Middle East, may lead clients to suspend or close operations in affected geographies. This may create economic pressure for clients and affect revenue and profitability.
