## Our success depends in large part upon our management team and key personnel and our ability to attract and retain them.

Infosys is highly dependent on its Board of Directors, executive officers, and other senior executive leaders. Disruptions in the continued service of directors or senior executive leaders could affect future performance and client relationships.

Competition for senior management is intense. Infosys may be unable to retain existing senior management or attract and hire new senior management, and its leaders may be subject to targeted hiring. Failure to maintain adequate leadership bench strength or effectively manage succession planning could affect organizational stability and growth prospects.

Infosys does not maintain keyman life insurance for senior members of its management team or other key personnel. The loss or extended unavailability of senior management or other key personnel due to health issues, security threats, or other business issues could adversely affect its business, results of operations, and financial condition.

## V. Risks related to our contractual obligations

## Our failure to complete fixed-price and fixed-timeframe contracts, or transaction-based pricing contracts, within budget and on time, may negatively affect our profitability.

In response to client requirements and pressure on IT budgets, Infosys offers an increasing portion of its services on a fixed-price, fixed-timeframe basis rather than on a time-and-materials basis. In fiscal 2026 and 2025, revenues from fixed-price, fixed-timeframe projects accounted for 54% of total revenue, respectively.

Pressure on client IT budgets and AI disruptions have also led Infosys to deviate from standard pricing policies and offer varied pricing models, including output-based and outcome-based pricing in certain situations. Infosys also enters into transaction-based pricing contracts with certain clients to provide flexibility to pay as services are used.

If Infosys fails to estimate project pricing accurately, it may earn lower profits or incur losses because it cannot execute projects within the expected timeframe or with the expected amount of labor. Infosys uses software engineering methodologies, processes, and past project experience to reduce estimation, planning, and execution risks, but remains exposed to:

- Cost overruns
- Completion delays
- Wage inflation
- Inaccurate estimates of required resources and project timelines
- Lower-than-expected automation and productivity gains
- Complexity in executing large and multi-party programs
- Incorrect assumptions about future wage inflation or currency exchange rates

Failure to complete contractual obligations within the contracted timeframe could reduce profitability. Infosys expects to continue entering into fixed-price, fixed-timeframe, and transaction-based engagements, which may increase relative to time-and-materials engagements and increase associated business risks.

## Our client contracts can typically be terminated without cause, which could negatively impact our revenues and profitability.

Clients typically retain Infosys on a non-exclusive, project-by-project basis. Many client contracts, including fixed-price, fixed-timeframe contracts, may be terminated with or without cause upon the notice period specified in the contract.

Factors outside Infosys’s control that could lead to contract termination, project descoping, or client loss include:

- Client financial difficulties, including limited access to credit markets, higher debt-service costs, higher operating costs, insolvency or bankruptcy, macroeconomic effects, changes in tariffs or trade restrictions, and geopolitical conflicts
- Changes in strategic priorities resulting in reduced technology spending
- Client demands for price reductions or unwillingness to accept higher pricing due to higher wage or operating costs
- Shifts in outsourcing strategy toward clients’ internal technology departments or competitors
- Replacement of existing software with packaged software supported by licensors
- Mergers and acquisitions
- Consolidation of client technology spending
- Sudden project ramp-downs due to economic uncertainty, geopolitical conflicts, or a pandemic

Infosys cannot control clients’ decisions regarding contract termination. Although there have been no material project terminations due to economic uncertainty, changes in tariffs or trade restrictions, or geopolitical conflicts, prolonged uncertainty could increase the risk that clients invoke termination clauses to reduce expenditure, affecting anticipated growth and profitability.

## Our client contracts are often conditional upon our performance, which, if unsatisfactory due to any reason, could result in lower revenues than previously anticipated.

Some client contracts contain incentive-based or other pricing terms that condition part or all of Infosys’s fees on meeting defined performance goals or service levels. Certain client situations may also require Infosys to accept higher contractual liability exposure limits.

Failure to meet performance goals or client expectations, particularly where the contract does not clearly articulate dependencies on the client, may prevent Infosys from billing for effort already expended. This could result in less profitable or unprofitable engagements and may lead to penalties or fines.

Clients may seek more favorable contractual terms, particularly regarding limitations on Infosys’s liability for damages arising from unsatisfactory service performance. Damages resulting from such failures, especially when not recoverable from insurers, could adversely affect Infosys’s business, revenues, and operating margins.

Infosys’s ability to meet contractual commitments may also be affected by talent shortages or geopolitical conflicts, which could lead to client dissatisfaction and lost revenue.

As clients increasingly expect engagements to incorporate AI-enabled solutions, Infosys faces evolving contractual, operational, and legal risks relating to AI-based projects, including:

- Confidentiality
- Data privacy
- Intellectual property ownership
- Model training and reuse
- Accuracy of outputs
- Allocation of liability

## Some of our long-term client contracts contain benchmarking provisions which, if triggered, could result in lower future revenues and profitability under the contract.

As the size and duration of client engagements increase, clients may increasingly require benchmarking provisions. These provisions allow a client, in certain circumstances, to request a benchmark study prepared by an agreed third party. The study compares Infosys’s pricing, performance, and efficiency gains for delivered contract services with those of an agreed list of service providers offering comparable services in comparable geographies.

Depending on the benchmark results and the reasons for any unfavorable variance, Infosys may be required to reduce service prices or give clients the right to terminate services without paying a termination fee. This could adversely affect revenues and profitability.

Benchmarking provisions may have a greater effect during an economic slowdown because pricing pressure and declining rates may reduce the fees charged to clients whose engagements contain such provisions.

## Our work with governmental agencies may expose us to additional risks.

Infosys bids for work with governments and governmental agencies in key geographies in which it operates. Government projects may involve the following risks:

- Higher risk of scope reduction or termination due to changes in government, pending elections, reduced or inadequate funding, or disputes with other government departments or agencies
- More onerous contract terms, including higher liability exposure for direct or indirect damages, extensive audit rights, more punitive service-level penalties, and other restrictive covenants
- Delayed payments or changes in contract terms due to political or economic factors and delays in closing requirements
- Greater scrutiny and publicity, with negative publicity potentially affecting the business and reputation regardless of its accuracy
- Stricter regulatory requirements and higher compliance costs
- Multiple delivery parties and greater project-management requirements, with performance failures potentially affecting results

Infosys operates in jurisdictions where local business practices may be inconsistent with international regulatory requirements, including anti-corruption and anti-bribery regulations under the U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) and the U.K. Bribery Act 2010. These laws prohibit, among other things, giving or offering anything of value with the intent to influence the award of government contracts.

Infosys states that it has policies and enforcement mechanisms intended to support compliance with the FCPA, the U.K. Bribery Act 2010, and similar regulations. However, employees, subcontractors, agents, or partners could violate these requirements despite contractual provisions requiring compliance. Such violations could expose Infosys to criminal or civil enforcement actions, penalties, and suspension or disqualification from U.S. federal procurement contracting.
