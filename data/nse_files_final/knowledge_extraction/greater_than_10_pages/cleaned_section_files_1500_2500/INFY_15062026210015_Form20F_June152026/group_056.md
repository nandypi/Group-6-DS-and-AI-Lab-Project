## Foreign Direct Investment Issuances by the Company

The Government of India introduced the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, together with related rules, regulations, and notifications, to regulate foreign direct investment (FDI) in India.

FDI means investment through equity instruments by:

- A person residing outside India in an unlisted Indian company; or
- A person acquiring 10% or more of the post-issue paid-up equity capital, on a fully diluted basis, of a listed Indian company.

Regulatory approval is required for investment in certain sectors, including defense and other strategic industries. Prior approval is also required for:

- Foreign investments, including transfers of shares, exceeding applicable foreign investment limits.
- Proposals involving the transfer of control and/or ownership through the amalgamation, merger, or acquisition of an Indian company currently owned or controlled by resident Indian citizens or Indian companies controlled by resident Indian citizens to a non-resident entity, where the activity is not permitted under the automatic route under existing Indian foreign investment policy.

Subject to applicable conditions under the Consolidated FDI Policy effective October 15, 2020, FDI in most sectors does not require prior approval from the Government of India or the RBI when foreign ownership remains within specified industry-specific thresholds. Conditions include minimum pricing requirements, compliance with the Takeover Code, and ownership restrictions based on the nature of the foreign investor.

Purchases by foreign investors of ADSs are treated as direct foreign investment in the equity issued by Indian companies for such offerings. Indian law currently permits foreign investment of up to 100% of the company’s share capital.

Under the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Amendment Rules, 2020, issued on April 22, 2020, prior government approval is required for foreign investments, including subsequent transfers of ownership, made on or after April 22, 2020, from countries sharing a land border with India. The requirement also applies when the beneficial owner of the foreign investment, either at the time of investment or after a subsequent ownership transfer, is situated in or is a citizen of a country sharing a land border with India.

On March 10, 2026, the Government of India issued a press release regarding amendments to the Consolidated FDI Policy. The proposed amendments state that investments from countries sharing a land border with India resulting in non-controlling beneficial ownership of up to 10% by such investors would be permitted under the automatic route, subject to applicable sectoral caps, entry routes, conditions, and reporting of relevant information to the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade, Ministry of Commerce & Industry, Government of India. The formal amendment to the Consolidated FDI Policy was awaited as of the date of the source disclosure.

## Subsequent Transfers

Restrictions on subsequent transfers of shares of Indian companies between residents and non-residents were significantly relaxed in October 2004. Accordingly, a private transfer between a resident and a non-resident of securities of an Indian company in the IT sector, such as the company, does not require prior approval from the RBI or the Government of India if applicable pricing guidelines, the Takeover Code, and ownership restrictions are satisfied.

For a transfer of shares of a listed Indian company by a resident to a non-resident investor under the FDI route, the minimum price per share payable by the non-resident cannot be less than the higher of:

- The 90-trading-day volume-weighted average price of the relevant equity shares quoted on a recognized stock exchange before the relevant date; or
- The 10-trading-day volume-weighted average price of the relevant equity shares quoted on a recognized stock exchange before the relevant date.

This price is referred to as the Preferential Allotment Floor Price.

If the issuer’s Articles of Association specify a method of determination that results in a higher floor price, that higher price applies to the transfer.

For a sale of shares of a listed Indian company by a non-resident who invested under the FDI route to a resident, the transfer price cannot exceed the price determined under applicable SEBI guidelines, specifically the Preferential Allotment Floor Price.

A non-resident may transfer equity shares on the floor of Indian stock exchanges in accordance with applicable SEBI regulations.

A non-resident cannot acquire shares of a listed company on the floor of a stock exchange unless the non-resident is:

- Registered as a foreign portfolio investor (FPI) with SEBI;
- An NRI;
- An individual resident outside India registered as an Overseas Citizen of India cardholder under the Citizenship Act, 1955; or
- Investing through the FDI route, having already acquired control of the company in accordance with the Takeover Code, and continuing to hold that control.

In each case, the investment remains subject to the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019, as amended, and the Consolidated FDI Policy.

The conditions for investment by a non-resident on the floor of a stock exchange under the FDI route are:

1. The non-resident investor must have already acquired and continue to hold control in accordance with the Takeover Code.
2. Consideration for the transfer of shares to the non-resident following a stock exchange purchase may be paid through a method specified by the RBI or from dividends payable by the Indian investee company in which the non-resident has acquired and continues to hold control under the Takeover Code. The right to receive the dividend must be established, and the dividend must have been credited to a specially designated, non-interest-bearing rupee account for the acquisition of shares on the recognized stock exchange.
3. The original and resulting investments must comply with the prevailing FDI policy and regulations under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999, including requirements relating to sectoral caps, entry routes, reporting, and documentation.

Transfers by sale or gift of shares or fully and compulsorily convertible debentures of the company between two non-residents, other than NRIs, OCIs, or erstwhile OCBs, are not subject to RBI approval or pricing restrictions. However, where FDI requires prior government approval, government approval is required for transfers between two non-residents. FDI in the information technology sector does not require prior government approval.

## Investment by Non-Resident Indians and Overseas Citizens of India

NRIs and OCIs may purchase or sell equity instruments[^1] of a listed Indian company on a recognized Indian stock exchange on a repatriation basis through a branch of an authorized dealer designated for this purpose.

An individual NRI or OCI may acquire up to:

- 5% of the total paid-up equity capital of an Indian company on a fully diluted basis; or
- 5% of the paid-up value of each series of debentures, preference shares, or share warrants issued by an Indian company.

The combined holdings of all NRIs and OCIs may not exceed:

- 10% of the total paid-up equity capital on a fully diluted basis; or
- 10% of the paid-up value of each series of debentures, preference shares, or share warrants.

The 10% ceiling may be increased to 24% if the company’s shareholders pass a special resolution at a general meeting, subject to the applicable overall ceiling.

NRIs may also make FDI in Indian companies under the FDI route described above.

Overseas corporate bodies controlled by NRIs were previously permitted to invest on favorable terms under the Portfolio Investment Scheme. The RBI no longer recognizes OCBs as an eligible class of investment vehicle under the applicable foreign exchange regulations.

[^1]: Rule 2(k) of the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Rules, 2019, as amended, defines “equity instruments” to include equity shares, convertible debentures, preference shares, and share warrants issued by an Indian company.

Under the rule:

- Equity shares issued under the Companies Act, 2013, or other applicable law include partly paid equity shares.
- Convertible debentures are fully paid, fully and mandatorily convertible debentures.
- Preference shares are fully paid, fully and mandatorily convertible preference shares.
- Share warrants are issued in accordance with SEBI regulations, the Companies Act, 2013, or other applicable law.
- Equity instruments may contain an optionality clause, subject to a minimum lock-in period of one year or the period prescribed for the specific sector, whichever is higher, but may not provide an option or right to exit at an assured price.
- Partly paid shares issued to a person resident outside India must be fully called up within 12 months of issue or within a period specified by the RBI. At least 25% of the total consideration, including any share premium, must be received upfront.
- For share warrants, at least 25% of the consideration must be received upfront and the balance within 18 months of issuance.
