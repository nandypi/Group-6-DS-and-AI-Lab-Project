## Rishi Basu

Thank you, Haripriya. Next question is from Beena Parmar from *The Economic Times*.

## Beena Parmar

You mentioned that revenue was stronger. Some peers have reported revenue cannibalization. Is Infosys also witnessing this? Are productivity gains being passed on to customers, and are they affecting pricing and margins? What factors led to the margin decline in the first quarter?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys is seeing significant productivity benefits from AI, as well as from existing automation and lean initiatives. Typically, these benefits are shared between clients and Infosys. At a macro level, revenue productivity and pricing are improving.

The company is also doing work that creates greater client impact in addition to productivity improvements. This means some of the productivity benefits are shared with clients. On balance, management sees an improvement in revenue quality.

### Productivity benefits

Infosys is not disclosing the amounts passed on to clients. In AI programs involving clients with disparate systems, the company is seeing benefits of between 5% and 15%. Some internal initiatives have delivered slightly higher benefits. Finacle also benefits from having a relatively uniform code base, which supports higher productivity.

### Margin walk

Jayesh Sanghrajka stated that the 20-basis-point margin decline reflected the following factors:

| Factor | Impact |
|---|---:|
| Compensation-related factors, including the April salary increase and higher variable pay | 100 basis points headwind |
| Currency | 30 basis points headwind |
| Other factors | 20 basis points headwind |
| Pricing benefit from seasonality, productivity, and Project Maximus | 70 basis points benefit |
| Acquisition-related impairment recorded in the previous quarter, which was a one-off | 40 basis points benefit |
| Lower third-party costs | 20 basis points benefit |

Quarterly growth of 2.6% occurred despite third-party revenue and costs being 60 basis points lower.

## Beena Parmar

Have client conversations improved in terms of business demand? Has tariff-related uncertainty or caution ended?

## Salil Parekh

Clients remain focused on enterprise AI, particularly use cases that demonstrate what is working and where, including work involving agents.

The economic environment is affecting industries such as logistics, consumer packaged goods, and manufacturing. Clients are also focused on the cost and efficiency of their own operations, including clients not directly affected by economic changes.

In large deals during the quarter, Infosys benefited from clients making consolidation decisions.

## Rishi Basu

The next question is from Reshab Shaw from *Moneycontrol*.

## Reshab Shaw

Financial Services represented close to 28% two fiscal years ago and is now at 27%. Is this a new normal, a change in composition, or a loss of market share?

Active clients have declined over the last two fiscal years despite quarterly revenue growth. Is this related to pricing or other benefits? Is the addition of employees in three-digit numbers the beginning of a shift from a pyramid model toward IP-led businesses?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys is seeing strong traction in Financial Services. Among the company's 20 largest Financial Services clients, Infosys is the AI partner of choice for approximately half of them.

The company is benefiting from consolidation decisions and is at the forefront of regulatory change and major transformation programs involving technology and operations. Management considers Infosys well positioned in Financial Services, with broad geographic and capability coverage.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

There is a long tail of active clients that engage Infosys for small projects. Some later become larger projects, while others leave and may return after several months or quarters. Active client count is tracked but is not considered the most important metric. Management focuses more on the growth and expansion of clients generating $50 million and $100 million in revenue.

## Salil Parekh

Infosys has a clear growth focus. The company has a plan to recruit employees during the financial year from colleges and through lateral hiring, and is on track with that plan.

The insurance platform and Finacle, the company's Financial Services banking platform, are areas where Infosys is seeing increased IP. Some BPM work is not IP-led but is more outcome-driven. This differs from much of the rest of the company, where work is more headcount-based. Management does not see a major change in this mix.

## Rishi Basu

The next question is from Jas Bardia from *Mint*.

## Jas Bardia

Has AI caused revenue cannibalization or employee reshuffling? Given the lack of mega deals and the broader industry slowdown, has Infosys run out of momentum? Are macroeconomic uncertainty, client-specific issues, or AI contributing to this?

## Salil Parekh

Large deals are performing well. The $3.8 billion large-deal figure includes multiple deals, including one mega deal. These deals cover AI, transformation, and consolidation. Management believes the overall large-deal approach remains strong.

Some changes are related to the broader economic environment. Infosys is seeing traction in AI, digital transformation, cloud, and enterprise applications. These areas are expected to support future growth.

Enterprise AI is creating productivity opportunities as well as new work with clients, including deeper analytics, assessments, and business optimization focused on both productivity and growth. Management expects these activities to create new revenue streams and sees growth in the addressable market for these services.
