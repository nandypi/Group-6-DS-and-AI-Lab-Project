## Abhishek Kumar

My second question is on seasonality. Infosys expects H2 to be weaker than H1, whereas some other companies anticipate a second-half rebound. Is this view driven by seasonality, or does it reflect large deals in sectors such as Communication reaching a steady state and lower visibility from deal ramp-ups, given that large deals were weaker last year than the year before?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Seasonality is also influenced by H1 performance. If H1 is broadly in line with expectations, usual seasonality applies. If H1 experiences higher pressure, expectations for H2 improve.

Infosys considers Q1 performance strong relative to other market results and therefore expects usual seasonality in its model.

Thank you, and all the best.

## Moderator

Thank you. Next question is from Bryan from TD Cowen.

## Bryan Bergin

Europe was very strong, while North America was up slightly. Can you comment on North America and the visibility of an improvement in growth there?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

North America remains Infosys's largest geography and an important part of its business. The company sees opportunities in pockets, particularly in Financial Services. Manufacturing and Retail in some geographies remain challenging.

Of the large deals signed during the quarter, 20 came from North America, 6 from Europe, and 2 from the rest of the world. Infosys sees opportunities in North America from large deals, cost takeout, and consolidation.

## Bryan Bergin

How did smaller-deal activity progress during the quarter?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys does not regularly comment on small deals. The company previously highlighted a quarter with heightened small-deal activity because it considered the information relevant to investors.

The overall pipeline remains strong, including the large-deal pipeline. Infosys delivered $3.8 billion in large deals, representing a 44% sequential increase and 55% net-new value.

## Moderator

Thank you. Next question is from Jonathan Lee from Guggenheim Partners.

## Jonathan Lee

Does the midpoint of the outlook contemplate a slight deterioration in the demand environment?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys builds multiple models leading to different ends of the guidance range. These models are not designed to converge on the midpoint; the midpoint is an outcome of the lower and upper ends.

The lower end incorporates higher uncertainty from current levels. The upper end assumes a stable environment. Multiple models lead to the various points between the two ends.

## Jonathan Lee

How did client demand develop from April through June, and did those trends continue into July?

## Salil Parekh

Infosys saw substantial client interest in AI, including AI agents, large enterprise AI platforms, and small language models. This interest is translating into project work within larger programs.

The company also saw increased interest in vendor consolidation, cost, and efficiency. Other areas of interest included:

- Foundations of enterprise AI
- Cloud
- Data and analytics
- Newer SaaS data platforms
- Enterprise applications moving to new generations of SaaS platforms at enterprise scale

The economic environment created constraints in areas including logistics, consumer products, some aspects of manufacturing, and automotive.

## Jonathan Lee

Thanks for the color, Salil.

## Moderator

Thank you. Next question is from Surendra Goyal from Citi.

## Surendra Goyal

Was the lowering of the upper end of organic guidance caused by a more conservative view of the environment, or by business factors such as slower ramps, faster declines in discretionary spending, or weaker pickup?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

At the beginning of Q1, Infosys stated that the upper end of its guidance assumed a slightly improving environment. After one quarter and with greater visibility into Q2, the company does not see the environment changing significantly. This is reflected in the upper end of the guidance range.

The upper end now assumes a steady environment. H1 is stronger for Infosys than H2, so once the stronger part of the period has passed, the company's ability to raise guidance at the upper end is more constrained in an uncertain environment.

The company assesses client decision-making and discretionary spending across accounts through ground-up modeling.

## Surendra Goyal

Understood. Thank you.

## Moderator

Thank you. Next question is from Rishi Jhunjhunwala from IIFL.

## Rishi Jhunjhunwala

The overall wage-hike impact over the past two quarters appears to be almost 240 basis points. What was the rationale for the wage increases, and should there be any further action in FY26?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

The wage increase was phased in two parts. A large part of the organization, through middle-level employees, received a wage increase in January. The remaining employees received the increase effective April 1.

The 100-basis-point impact in the quarter comprised wage increases and higher variable pay. Infosys did not separately disclose the two components. The wage increases were relatively similar in percentage terms to those implemented in earlier years.

The company has not yet decided when the next wage increase will occur. Factors considered include the market scenario, inflation, and peer practices.

## Rishi Jhunjhunwala

Are the vendor consolidation and GCC deals different from earlier large deals in terms of required upfront investment on the profit-and-loss statement or balance sheet?

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys continues to expect free cash flow conversion to net profit of 100% or more. The company has had five strong quarters of cash generation and expects this to continue for the rest of the year.

Management does not expect these deals to materially affect the balance sheet or cash flow. They are expected to have the regular contours of Infosys's other deals and are not significantly different from that perspective.
