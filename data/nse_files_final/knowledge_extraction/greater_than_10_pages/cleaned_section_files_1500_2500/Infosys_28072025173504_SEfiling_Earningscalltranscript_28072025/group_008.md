## Ashwin Mehta

### Question

What drove depreciation and amortization down to almost 50 basis points, and what caused the approximately 90-basis-point increase in SG&A during the quarter?

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

The previous quarter included a one-off amortization expense for intangibles related to one acquisition, which affected the quarter-on-quarter comparison by 40 basis points. The absence of that expense this quarter accounts for the 40-basis-point difference. The remaining change was partly due to currency impact.

The SG&A increase reflected multiple factors:

- Compensation increases implemented in Q1.
- Variable pay.
- Hiring in sales and marketing to improve the growth trajectory, including a 20-basis-point sales investment in the margin walk.
- Brand-building investments and events held during the quarter.

## Abhishek Pathak

### Question

Is the 40-basis-point inorganic contribution entirely from acquisitions consolidated during the quarter? Including a residual impact from in-tech, would the full-year inorganic contribution be slightly higher?

There was also commentary that discretionary spending was being funded entirely by savings from AI. Could this become a structural trend in which clients expect discretionary initiatives to be self-funded through AI gains, or is there expected to be a broader demand recovery after the next 12 to 18 months requiring significant investment in data and technology modernization?

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

The 40-basis-point figure is sequential. The 2.6% inorganic contribution includes approximately 40 basis points from acquisitions made during the quarter: MRE and The Missing Link in Australia.

The full-year impact of in-tech is not significant because it was included for approximately 9 of the 12 months in the previous year. On a full-year basis, the impact is similar when considering 3.5 months of in-tech and 11 months of MRE and The Missing Link.

## Keith Bachman

### Question

Headcount, including software professionals, was relatively flat quarter-on-quarter. How should headcount trends be viewed through the year?

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

Utilization increased by 30 basis points during the quarter. Growth was also supported by pricing increases and business seasonality.

The company is operating at peak headcount. As volume growth continues, additional capacity will be required through subcontractors or company employees.

### Question

Could AI enable Infosys to grow headcount more slowly than effort? How might the delivery model change over the next year, particularly regarding full-time employee-based delivery versus success-based or fixed-price contracts?

### Jayesh Sanghrajka

The delivery model is not expected to change over the next couple of quarters. Over a longer period, AI may support the emergence of newer pricing models, including outcome-based, pod-based, and studio-based models. Any change is expected to occur gradually rather than transform the entire model within the next year.

## Salil Parekh

The quarter included strong growth, large deals, and a focus on enterprise AI consolidation, along with cloud and data work. Management also stated that it expects the remainder of financial year 2026 to be positive.
