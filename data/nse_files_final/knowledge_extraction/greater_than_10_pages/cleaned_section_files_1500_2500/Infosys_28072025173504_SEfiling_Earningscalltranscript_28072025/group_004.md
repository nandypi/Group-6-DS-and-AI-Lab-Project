## Salil Parekh

Infosys had a strong start to FY26:

- Revenue grew 2.6% sequentially and 3.8% year-on-year in constant currency terms.
- Growth was broad-based, with the five largest industry groups and largest geographies growing year-on-year in constant currency terms.
- Large deal total contract value was $3.8 billion.
- Operating margin was 20.8%.
- Free cash flow was $884 million.

Growth was driven primarily by enterprise AI capabilities and client consolidation activity. Infosys had built 300 AI agents across business operations and IT. Its horizontal and vertical agents were being used to support faster decision-making, customer experience, and operational efficiency.

Examples of enterprise AI projects included:

- An oil and gas major using AI agents to improve refinery production quality, orchestrate dynamic pricing in retail stores, and automate contract management for trading.
- A global manufacturing company using AI agents across its supply chain to improve productivity and reduce costs, and to resolve issues involving malfunctioning equipment.
- A logistics company using AI agents across customer care, operations and logistics, and finance and accounting.
- A North American retailer using physical AI, intelligent automation, and edge-based computing vision to improve in-store shopping, customer satisfaction, loyalty, and operational efficiency.
- A global financial services company using an Infosys Enterprise AI Solution with a fine-tuned large language model to translate code and automate documentation. The solution increased developer productivity by 25% and automated 50% of business requirement creation and support for the modernization plan.

Infosys stated that, following 19 leadership ratings received in FY25, it was also positioned as a leader in Gartner's first Generative AI Consulting and Implementation Services Quadrant.

Based on Q1 performance and the current outlook, FY26 revenue growth guidance was revised from 0%–3% to 1%–3% in constant currency terms. FY26 operating margin guidance remained unchanged at 20%–22%.

## Jayesh Sanghrajka

Infosys reported the following Q1 FY26 performance:

1. Revenue grew 2.6% sequentially, including 0.4% from acquisitions, and 3.8% year-on-year in constant currency terms.
2. Sequential growth was achieved despite a 60-basis-point reduction in third-party costs to 7.3% of revenue.
3. Sequential growth was driven by increased realization under Project Maximus. Volume growth was muted but positive.
4. Manufacturing grew in double digits, while Financial Services and EURS grew approximately 5% year-on-year in constant currency terms.
5. North America grew 2.9% sequentially in constant currency terms. Europe grew 12.3% year-on-year in constant currency terms.
6. Operating margin was 20.8%, down 20 basis points sequentially and 30 basis points year-on-year.
7. Margin performance reflected the impact of compensation increases, higher variable pay, and sales and marketing investments.
8. Utilization excluding trainees increased 30 basis points sequentially to 85.2%. Utilization including trainees increased 80 basis points to 82.7%.
9. EPS grew 8.6% year-on-year in rupee terms and 5.8% in dollar terms.
10. Free cash flow was $884 million, representing 109% of net profit. This was the fifth consecutive quarter in which free cash flow exceeded 100% of net profit.
11. Infosys expected FY26 free cash flow to exceed 100% of net profit.
12. Consolidated cash and cash equivalents were $5.27 billion at quarter-end after payment of the FY25 final dividend. Yield on the cash balance was 7.2% in Q1.
13. ROE improved by 140 basis points to 30.4%, driven by dividend payouts.
14. Large deal wins comprised 28 deals with total contract value of $3.8 billion, including 55% net new business.
15. Multiple vendor-consolidation deals had combined total contract value exceeding $1 billion, including a mega deal with one of the largest global banks.
16. Headcount at quarter-end was 323,788, and attrition increased marginally to 14.4%.

### Operating margin

Q1 operating margin was 20.8%, a sequential decline of 20 basis points.

| Category | Margin impact | Explanation |
|---|---:|---|
| Headwind | -100 basis points | Compensation increases and higher variable pay, partly offset by other salary-related items |
| Headwind | -30 basis points | Currency movement |
| Headwind | -20 basis points | Sales investments |
| Tailwind | -70 basis points | Increased realization from Project Maximus and seasonality |
| Tailwind | -40 basis points | Lower amortization cost on intangibles |
| Tailwind | -20 basis points | Lower third-party costs |

The effective tax rate for Q1 was 28.9%. FY26 effective tax rate was expected to be in the range of 29%–30%.

The business environment remained uncertain because of unresolved tariffs and geopolitical conditions. Clients remained cautious about discretionary spending, resulting in delayed decision-making. Near-term visibility remained good, with stronger first-half performance expected compared with the second half because of normal seasonality.

### Financial Services

Financial Services saw momentum in the United States, particularly in capital markets, commercial banking, and wealth management transformation opportunities. Agentic AI activity focused on areas including KYC, onboarding, and portfolio management.

Infosys stated that it was the preferred AI partner for 10 of the top 20 Financial Services clients, with several initiatives moving from proof of concept to production, particularly in Agentic AI. The company was also partnering with GCCs on both setup and growth-led deals.

The pipeline included vendor consolidation, cost optimization, and simplification opportunities. Clients remained cautious in decision-making because of the volatile environment.

### Manufacturing

Manufacturing continued to face challenges in automotive, industrial, and European markets, including decision-making delays and weak discretionary spending. Clients were re-evaluating supply chains because of tariff uncertainty.

Infosys was helping clients apply technology across the lifecycle from design through manufacturing and sales. The pipeline remained healthy, with a focus on cost-takeout opportunities. Infosys won a large Q1 deal to help a client establish a GCC.

In automotive, Infosys was helping clients rationalize their footprints. In industrial markets, it was helping clients with cost optimization.

### EURS

The Energy, Utilities, Resources and Services outlook remained mixed because of economic uncertainty. The pipeline for large and mega deals remained strong.

Investments in Industry Cloud, Energy Transition, and AI-driven operational efficiency were supporting growth and differentiation in large deals.

- In Energy, oil price volatility and high cost pressures were prompting clients to consolidate vendors for savings.
- In Utilities, renewable energy, smart grid technology, and sustainability regulations were reshaping the market.
- In Services, clients remained cautious about both capital expenditure and operating expenditure.

### Retail

Tariff uncertainty contributed to muted spending in major geographies, supply chain impacts, and procurement disruptions. Budgets remained tight and decision cycles became longer. Discretionary spending slowed, although the pipeline remained strong.

Clients were engaging Infosys for AI-first, outsourcing, and transformation deals across IT and BPM services. Increased interest in AI was leading to budget reallocation, with discretionary spending expected to be self-funded through AI-led productivity benefits. Sector deals continued to use the Topaz and AI Next platform capabilities.

### Communications

Communications faced growth challenges and increased operating expenditure pressures amid a volatile macroeconomic and political environment. Clients focused on cost takeouts and vendor consolidation.

There was strong interest in AI and customization to monetize 5G use cases, although return-on-investment concerns delayed newer investments. OEMs were seeking profitable growth through tighter IT budgets, reduced IT spending, AI, and automation. Infosys growth was being led by the ramp-up of previously won large deals.

### Hi-Tech

Hi-Tech clients remained cautious because of macroeconomic headwinds and geopolitical tensions. Cost pressures and budget cuts led to pauses in discretionary programs, partly because of significant investments in generative AI GPUs and AI.

## FY26 guidance

FY26 revenue growth guidance was revised to 1%–3% in constant currency terms. The guidance assumed a reduction in third-party revenues versus FY25 based on existing deals and the pipeline of new deals.

FY26 operating margin guidance remained 20%–22%. Infosys stated that it would continue monitoring the economic environment and its impact on client budgets and reassess guidance during the year.
