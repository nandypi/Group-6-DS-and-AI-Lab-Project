## A.G.S. Manikantha

The moderator moved to the next shareholder after the audio connection with Reddeppa Gundluru could not be established clearly.

## Aspi Bamanshaw Bhesania

The shareholder:

- Congratulated management on the results.
- Asked about the outlook for North American revenue in light of tariffs and “made in USA” requirements, including guidance for 2025–26 and 2026–27.
- Noted that fourth-quarter revenue was higher year over year but slightly lower quarter over quarter, while PBT declined both year over year and quarter over quarter.
- Noted that consolidated cash flow increased from ₹25,000 crore to ₹35,000 crore.
- Requested that the company retain the ₹5 face value and not issue a bonus.
- Expressed the view that the company should not undertake a buyback despite buybacks becoming taxable.
- Stated that ₹3.55 crore was transferred to the IEPF as unclaimed dividends, 51,242 shares were transferred to the IEPF, and ₹3.47 crore was transferred to the IEPF for shares held by the IEPF.
- Requested greater assistance for small shareholders with dematerialisation through KFintech and suggested that Infosys establish an internal share department or consider changing its registrar and transfer agent.

## Vasudha Dakwe

The shareholder had no questions and congratulated the company for the dividend.

## Krishan Lal Chadha

The shareholder raised the following points:

- Stated that 2025 revenue was ₹1,62,990 crore.
- Cited 1,869 clients and total employee strength of 323,578.
- Mentioned an employee-to-active-client ratio of 174 employees per client.
- Stated that revenue had grown by 60% from 2021 to 2025, but shareholders had not seen a comparable 60% increase.
- Questioned the absence of a bonus issue since 2019.
- Stated that ₹539 crore was allocated for CSR activities and ₹526 crore was spent.
- Said that CSR spending was concentrated in Karnataka, Bhubaneswar, and Odisha, and asked whether the remaining funds could also be spent in Delhi and other locations.
- Compared profitability figures of 0.62 in 2020 and 0.76 in 2025. The shareholder attributed the increase mainly to higher other income and lower expenses, rather than to an increase in core profitability, referring to information on page 58.
- Cited a previous-year dividend of ₹38, comprising an interim dividend of ₹18 and a final dividend of ₹20, and a current-year interim dividend of ₹21 and final dividend of ₹22.
- Questioned whether the dividend increase represented meaningful appreciation for shareholders given the ₹5 face value.
- Referred to approximately two lakh rural families described as beneficiaries on page 43 and asked for greater detail on how they benefited.
- Asked why other reserves and surplus declined during the financial year, referring to page 59.
- Referred to approximately 109 subsidiaries and raised concerns about the performance of two subsidiaries in Germany. The shareholder also acknowledged the difficult circumstances affecting the Israel subsidiaries and asked what initiatives were planned to address losses and prevent them from affecting the profitability of the main company.
- Described the board as effective and efficient.
