## Anil Mehta

The shareholder asked how much growth Infosys could expect in FY2025-26 given the trade war, the situation with a neighboring country and the prevailing repo rate. He stated that he supported all resolutions.

## Dilip Kumar Das

The shareholder asked whether Infosys has a recruitment policy or provision for hiring qualified employees from a shareholders’ quota. He also asked whether Infosys would introduce paternity leave for male employees in response to the Government of India’s policy.

He referred to Infosys’s annual report, dividend and CSR activities, and stated that the company had recruited 3,20,000 new employees from 15,000 colleges.

## Dinesh G. Bhatia

The shareholder cited the following figures and requested consideration of a share buyback and share split:

- BSE closing share price: ₹1,615.
- Equity share capital: ₹2,076 crore.
- Revenue: ₹34,136 crore.
- Total dividend: ₹43 per share, comprising ₹21 and ₹22.
- HCL Technologies’ ₹2 share price was cited as ₹1,716.
- Infosys’s ₹5 share was cited as trading at ₹1,615.

He suggested that reducing equity through a buyback could increase earnings per share. He also suggested splitting the ₹5 share into ₹1 shares, stating that this would increase the number of shares and potentially improve trading activity. He stated that Infosys had generated 400 AI projects.

## Tamal Kumar Majumder

The shareholder referred to a client figure declining from 40 to 39 during the current year; the transcript also contains the unclear phrase “US $100 million.”

He asked about the following matters:

- Whether the potential challenges from Enterprise AI adoption could include a substantial reduction in manpower in the near future.
- The risks associated with customers showing increased interest in Infosys’s AI models.
- The increase in attrition to 14.1% in Q4 FY2025, compared with TCS at 13.5%, and the steps taken to reduce attrition.
- Consolidated revenue growth of 1.6%—also rendered in the transcript as 1.06%—compared with FY2024, including the portions attributable to underlying revenue growth and exchange-rate effects.
- The expected growth in the current year and any potential negative revenue impact in FY2026 from global uncertainty.
- The reduction in margins cited on pages 103 and 103-related discussion:
  - Communications: 20.5% to 17.5%.
  - Life sciences: 25.2% to 22.5%.
  - Hi-tech: 25.4% to 24.6%.
- The difference between Infosys and TCS margins, including:
  - Manufacturing: TCS at 32.7% versus Infosys at 19.3%.
  - Life sciences: TCS at 28% versus Infosys at 22.5%.
  - Communications: TCS at 20.9% versus Infosys at 17.5%.
- The reasons for the company’s ₹4,317 crore investment in the equity of Infosys Singapore Private Limited and an additional ₹2,831 crore investment in redeemable preference shares of that company.
- Why the full accounts of Infosys Singapore Private Limited and other subsidiaries were not available on the company website.
- The total contribution to Infosys Foundation and Infosys Foundation USA during FY2025.
- Why the audited accounts of Infosys Foundation and Infosys Foundation USA were not disclosed to shareholders.
- The company’s focus on the Indian market, where Indian operations were stated to contribute 3% of total revenue in FY2025.
- The steps being taken to increase Infosys’s presence in India, compared with TCS’s cited Indian-segment revenue of ₹22,660 crore in FY2025.
