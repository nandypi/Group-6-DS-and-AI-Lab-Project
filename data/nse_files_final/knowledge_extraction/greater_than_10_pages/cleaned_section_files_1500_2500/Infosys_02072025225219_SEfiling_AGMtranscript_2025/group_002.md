Fiscal 2025 was a year of strong execution for Infosys despite significant global uncertainty. The company reported:

- Constant-currency revenue growth of 4.2%.
- Revenue of US$19.2 billion.
- Operating margin of 21.1%, an expansion of 50 basis points.
- Free cash flow of US$4.1 billion, an increase of 41.8% over fiscal 2024.
- Large deal total contract value of US$11.6 billion for FY25.

For fiscal 2025, the Board recommended a final dividend of ₹22 per share. Along with the interim dividend of ₹21 per share, already paid, the total dividend for fiscal 2025 was ₹43 per share.

Infosys expects to return approximately 85% of cumulative free cash flow over a five-year period through a combination of semi-annual dividends, share buybacks, and special dividends, subject to applicable laws and required approvals. Under this policy, the company expects to progressively increase its annual dividend per share, excluding any special dividend.

Salil Parekh, Chief Executive Officer and Managing Director, has a term extending until March 31, 2027. He was eligible to retire at the Annual General Meeting and offered himself for reappointment. Based on the performance evaluation and the recommendation of the Nomination and Remuneration Committee, the Board recommended his reappointment until March 31, 2027. There was no retirement or resignation of directors or key management personnel during the year.

## Business and Technology Environment

The company identified several business and technology trends affecting global enterprises:

- Global markets are becoming more fragmented, with companies needing to make strategic choices across regions.
- Businesses are diversifying supply chains and sourcing in response to tariffs and changing bilateral and regional trade rules.
- AI adoption is increasing the need for legacy-system modernization and data-architecture overhauls.
- Companies require AI foundries for innovation and AI factories for scaling.
- Regulatory differences across regions create implementation complexity.
- Energy transition is increasing the need for innovation in solar, wind, batteries, hydrogen, and nuclear technologies.
- Greater electrification requires infrastructure investment in transmission lines, charging stations, and transformers.
- Commodity prices may depend on the pace of these changes.

The company described industry-specific challenges including electric-vehicle transition for automakers, AI-enabled drug discovery for pharmaceutical companies, supply-chain reordering for logistics firms, tokenization in financial services, changing long-term energy demand, distributed utility operations, robotics and 3D printing in manufacturing, and competition between service companies and AI agents.

## Infosys Capabilities

Infosys’s portfolio spans multiple regions, industries, and technology domains. Its capabilities include solutions for established business needs and emerging market demands, with a focus on cost optimization, automation, and operational excellence.

The company described its capabilities in the convergence of AI, cloud, and data transformation:

- Infosys Topaz combines AI capabilities with cloud and data infrastructure.
- Its AI agent library supports functions including code generation, IT operations, bill-to-cash, and quote-to-order workflows.
- The company has developed specialized language models for specific industries, IT operations, and cybersecurity.
- Infosys offers model development as a complete service, supported by strategic industry alliances.
- Cobalt cloud capabilities connect with public and private cloud providers, SaaS vendors, and PaaS platforms.
- Data capabilities cover architecture, design, and structured and unstructured data management.
- These capabilities support the deployment of enterprise-grade AI solutions.

## Employees and Recognition

Infosys recruited over 15,000 college graduates during the year and ended fiscal 2025 with over 320,000 employees. Women employees represented 39% of the workforce.

During the year, Infosys was recognized as a Global Top Employer in 22 countries for its HR practices and processes. It was also recognized as one of the World’s Most Ethical Companies by Ethisphere for the fifth consecutive year.

## ESG and Sustainability

Infosys’s ESG 2030 commitments cover climate action, sustainable resource management, digital empowerment, workplace inclusivity, community development, employee wellness, and corporate governance.

The company aims to achieve climate-positive status in 2030 while maintaining carbon neutrality through 2029. Climate-positive status is defined as sequestering more carbon than the company emits. Infosys reported that it had been carbon neutral for six consecutive years, including FY25.

Through 11 lake rejuvenation projects across India, Infosys increased water-holding capacity by 4.3 billion liters.

The company’s human-capital and community goals include:

- Extending digital skills to over 18 million people.
- Enabling employment opportunities for more than 500,000 individuals.
- Empowering more than 1.25 million lives through Tech for Good programs in e-governance, healthcare, and education.
- Reaching 13.3 million people through digital-skilling initiatives.

Infosys became the first India-headquartered company to receive Binding Corporate Rules approval from EU data protection authorities. The company also stated its commitment to data-privacy standards and information-security practices.
