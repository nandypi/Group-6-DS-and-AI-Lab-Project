# The estimated useful life is around 1 year to 7 years

The excess of the purchase consideration paid over the fair value of assets acquired was attributed to goodwill. The primary items generating this goodwill were the value of the acquired assembled workforce and estimated synergies, neither of which qualified as an intangible asset.

Goodwill amounting to ₹79 crore was expected to be deductible for tax purposes.

The total purchase consideration of ₹811 crore included upfront cash consideration of ₹741 crore and contingent consideration with an estimated fair value of ₹70 crore as of the acquisition date.

At the acquisition date, the key inputs used to determine the fair value of contingent consideration were probabilities assigned to achieving financial targets and discount rates ranging from 2% to 3%. The undiscounted value of contingent consideration as of June 30, 2025 was approximately ₹73 crore.

The acquisitions also included retention bonuses and management incentives payable to employees of the acquiree over two to three years, subject to continuous employment with the Group and achievement of financial targets for the respective years. Retention bonuses and management incentives were recognized in employee benefit expenses in the Statement of Comprehensive Income over the period of service.

The fair value of trade receivables acquired was ₹194 crore as of the acquisition date. As of June 30, 2025, the amounts had been substantially collected.

Transaction costs incurred in connection with the business combination, including finder's fees, legal fees, due diligence fees, and other professional and consulting fees, were expensed as incurred. Acquisition-related transaction costs of ₹34 crore were included under administrative expenses in the Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income for the quarter ended June 30, 2025.

## 2.11 Employees' Stock Option Plans (ESOP)

## Accounting Policy

The Group recognized compensation expense relating to share-based payments in net profit based on the estimated fair values of the awards on the grant date. The estimated fair value of awards was recognized as an expense in net profit in the interim condensed consolidated statement of comprehensive income on a straight-line basis over the requisite service period for each separately vesting portion of the award, as if the award were, in substance, multiple awards, with a corresponding increase to share premium.

## Infosys Expanded Stock Ownership Program 2019 (the 2019 Plan):

On June 22, 2019, pursuant to shareholder approval at the Annual General Meeting, the Board was authorized to introduce, offer, issue, and provide share-based incentives to eligible employees of the Company and its subsidiaries under the 2019 Plan.

The maximum number of shares under the 2019 Plan shall not exceed 50,000,000 equity shares. To implement the 2019 Plan, up to 45,000,000 equity shares may be issued through secondary acquisition of shares by the Infosys Expanded Stock Ownership Trust.

Restricted Stock Units (RSUs) granted under the 2019 Plan shall vest based on defined annual performance parameters determined by the administrator, the Nomination and Remuneration Committee. The performance parameters are based on a combination of relative total shareholder return (TSR) against selected industry peers, certain broader domestic and global market indices, and the Company's operating performance metrics. Each performance parameter is distinct for calculating the number of shares to vest. These instruments generally vest between one and three years from the grant date.

## 2015 Stock Incentive Compensation Plan (the 2015 Plan):

On March 31, 2016, pursuant to shareholder approval through postal ballot, the Board was authorized to introduce, offer, issue, and allot share-based incentives to eligible employees of the Company and its subsidiaries under the 2015 Stock Incentive Compensation Plan.

The maximum number of shares under the 2015 Plan shall not exceed 24,038,883 equity shares, including 11,223,576 equity shares held by the trust toward the 2011 Plan as of March 31, 2016. These instruments generally vest over four years. The plan numbers were further adjusted for the September 2018 bonus issue.

Equity-settled and cash-settled RSUs and stock options generally vest over four years and are exercisable within the period approved by the Nomination and Remuneration Committee (NARC). The exercise price of RSUs is equal to the par value of the shares, while the exercise price of stock options is the market price on the grant date.

The controlled trust held 9,098,409 shares as of June 30, 2025 and 9,655,927 shares as of March 31, 2025 under the 2015 Plan. Of these shares, 200,000 equity shares were earmarked for employee welfare activities as of each date.

## Under the 2015 plan:

On April 17, 2025, based on the recommendations of the Nomination and Remuneration Committee, the Board approved the following grants for fiscal 2026. The grants became effective on May 2, 2025:

- 230,621 performance-based RSUs, an annual performance equity grant, with a fair value of ₹34.75 crore. These RSUs will vest in line with the employment agreement based on achievement of specified performance targets.
- 13,273 performance-based RSUs, an annual performance equity ESG grant, with a fair value of ₹2 crore. These RSUs will vest in line with the employment agreement based on achievement of environment, social, and governance milestones determined by the Board.
- 33,183 performance-based RSUs, an annual performance equity TSR grant, with a fair value of ₹5 crore. These RSUs will vest based on the Company's cumulative relative TSR performance over the relevant years, as determined by the Board.

Although the annual time-based grants and annual performance equity TSR grant for the remaining employment term ending March 31, 2027 had not been granted as of June 30, 2025, the Company recorded employment stock compensation expense in accordance with IFRS 2, *Share-based Payment*, because the service commencement date preceded the grant date. The grant date for this purpose under IFRS 2 was July 1, 2022.

The fair value of awards was estimated using the Black-Scholes model for time-based and non-market performance-based options. The Monte Carlo simulation model was used for TSR-based options.

The inputs to the models included the share price on the grant date, exercise price, expected volatility, expected dividends, expected term, and the risk-free interest rate. Expected volatility during the expected term of the options was based on historical volatility of the Company's publicly traded equity shares over a period equivalent to the expected term. Expected volatility for comparative companies was modeled based on historical movements in the market prices of their publicly traded equity shares over a period equivalent to the expected term. The correlation coefficient was calculated between each peer entity and the indices as a whole, or between each entity in the peer group.
