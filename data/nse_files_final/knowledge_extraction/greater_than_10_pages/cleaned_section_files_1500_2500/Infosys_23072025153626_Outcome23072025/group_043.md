## 2.9.2 Intangible assets

### Accounting Policy

Intangible assets are stated at cost less accumulated amortization and impairment. They are amortized over their respective estimated useful lives on a straight-line basis from the date they are available for use.

The estimated useful life of an identifiable intangible asset considers factors including obsolescence, demand, competition, industry stability, known technological advances, and the maintenance expenditures required to obtain expected future cash flows. Amortization methods and useful lives are reviewed periodically, including at each financial year end.

Research costs are expensed as incurred. Software product development costs are expensed as incurred unless:

- The project's technical and commercial feasibility has been demonstrated.
- Future economic benefits are probable.
- The Group intends and is able to complete and use or sell the software.
- The costs can be measured reliably.

Capitalized costs may include materials, direct labor, and overhead costs directly attributable to preparing the asset for its intended use.

### Impairment

Intangible assets are evaluated for recoverability whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. The recoverable amount, defined as the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use, is determined on an individual asset basis unless the asset does not generate cash flows largely independent of other assets. In such cases, recoverability is determined for the cash-generating unit (CGU) to which the asset belongs.

An impairment loss recognized in net profit in the statement of comprehensive income equals the amount by which the asset's carrying value exceeds its estimated recoverable amount.

An impairment loss is reversed in net profit in the statement of comprehensive income when there has been a change in the estimates used to determine the recoverable amount. The asset's carrying amount is increased to its revised recoverable amount, provided that it does not exceed the carrying amount that would have been determined, net of accumulated amortization, if no impairment loss had been recognized in prior years.

## 2.10 Business combinations

### Accounting policy

Business combinations are accounted for using the acquisition method under IFRS 3 (Revised), *Business Combinations*.

The purchase price is measured at the fair value of assets transferred, equity instruments issued, and liabilities incurred or assumed at the date control is transferred to the Group. It also includes the fair value of contingent consideration.

Identifiable assets acquired, liabilities assumed, and contingent liabilities assumed in a business combination are initially measured at fair value on the acquisition date. Contingent consideration is remeasured at fair value at each reporting date, with changes recognized in the interim condensed Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income.

Non-controlling interests are initially measured either at fair value or at the non-controlling interests' proportionate share of the acquiree's identifiable net assets. The measurement basis is selected separately for each acquisition. After acquisition, the carrying amount of non-controlling interests comprises the amount recognized initially plus the non-controlling interests' share of subsequent changes in the equity of subsidiaries.

Business combinations between entities under common control are outside the scope of IFRS 3 (Revised), *Business Combinations*, and are accounted for at the carrying value of assets acquired and liabilities assumed.

Payments related to options issued by the Group over non-controlling interests in subsidiaries are accounted for as financial liabilities and initially recognized at the estimated present value of gross obligations. These options are subsequently measured at fair value to reflect the amount payable when the option becomes exercisable. If an option expires unexercised, the liability is derecognized.

### Acquisition

During the three months ended June 30, 2025, the Group completed two business combinations by acquiring 100% partnership interests or voting interests in the following entities:

1. **MRE Consulting Ltd.**, a business and energy consulting services company headquartered in Texas, U.S., acquired on April 30, 2025. The acquisition is expected to bring newer capabilities in trading and risk management, especially in the energy sector.

2. **The Missing Link**, comprising The Missing Link Security Pty. Ltd., The Missing Link Security Limited, and The Missing Link Automation Pty. Ltd., a cybersecurity service provider headquartered in Australia, acquired on April 30, 2025. The acquisition is expected to strengthen the Group's cybersecurity capabilities and presence in the Australian market.
