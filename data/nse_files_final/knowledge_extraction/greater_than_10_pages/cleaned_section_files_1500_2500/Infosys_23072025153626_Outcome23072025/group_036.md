## 1.3 Basis of consolidation

Infosys consolidates entities that it owns or controls. The interim condensed consolidated financial statements comprise the financial statements of the Company, its controlled trusts, and its subsidiaries.

Control exists when the parent has power over an entity, is exposed or has rights to variable returns from its involvement with the entity, and can affect those returns by using its power over the entity. Power is demonstrated through existing rights that provide the ability to direct relevant activities that significantly affect the entity’s returns. Subsidiaries are consolidated from the date control commences until the date control ceases.

Group company financial statements are consolidated on a line-by-line basis. Intra-group balances and transactions, including unrealized gains or losses, are eliminated on consolidation. The financial statements are prepared using uniform accounting policies across the Group. Non-controlling interests, representing the portion of the net profit or loss and net assets of subsidiaries not directly or indirectly owned or controlled by the Company, are excluded.

## 1.4 Use of estimates and judgments

Preparation of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements in conformity with IFRS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies; the reported amounts of assets, liabilities, revenues, and expenses; and disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities.

Critical accounting estimates involving complex and subjective judgments are disclosed in Note 1.5. Estimates may change as circumstances change, and actual results may differ from estimates. Changes in estimates and judgments are recognized in the period in which they are made; material effects are disclosed in the notes to the interim condensed consolidated financial statements.

### a. Revenue recognition

The Group’s customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is considered for recognition and measurement when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to their respective obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to those obligations. Determining the deliverables, whether the customer can benefit independently from them, and the allocation of transaction price involves significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of customer benefit and the Group’s fulfillment costs are uneven because services are discrete rather than repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method. The method selected depends on the contractual promises and the nature of the deliverables.

The Group uses the percentage-of-completion method for other fixed-price contracts. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs, because inputs have a direct relationship with productivity. Estimates of total efforts or costs are assessed throughout the contract period and updated using the latest available information.

Certain integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as principal. The Group assesses whether it obtains control before transfer to the customer, considering responsibility for fulfillment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.

### b. Income taxes

The Group’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. The Company also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is required to determine income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing the realizability of deferred income tax assets, management considers whether some or all of those assets may not be realized. Realization depends on generating future taxable income during the periods in which temporary differences become deductible. Management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies.

Based on historical taxable income and projections for future taxable income during the relevant carry-forward periods, management believes the Group will realize the benefits of the deductible differences. The amount of deferred income tax assets considered realizable could be reduced if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. (Refer to Note 2.12.)

### c. Business combinations and intangible assets

Business combinations are accounted for under IFRS 3 (Revised), *Business Combinations*. Identifiable intangible assets and contingent consideration are fair-valued to determine the net fair value of the acquiree’s identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities.

External valuation experts conduct these valuations. Estimates are required to determine the value of contingent consideration, option arrangements, and intangible assets. Measurements are based on information available at the acquisition date and on assumptions considered reasonable by management. (Refer to Notes 2.10 and 2.9.2.)

### d. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Group’s asset base. Periodic depreciation depends on estimates of an asset’s useful life and residual value.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. (Refer to Note 2.7.)

### e. Impairment of Goodwill

Goodwill is tested for impairment annually and whenever there is an indication that the recoverable amount of a cash-generating unit (CGU) is less than its carrying amount. Goodwill is allocated to the CGU or group of CGUs that benefits from the acquisition synergies and represents the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes.

The recoverable amount of CGUs is the higher of value in use and fair value less costs to sell. Key assumptions in cash-flow projections include estimated long-term growth rates, the weighted average cost of capital, and estimated operating margins. (Refer to Note 2.9.1.)

### IFRS 18 - Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements

On April 9, 2025, the IASB issued IFRS 18, *Presentation and Disclosures in Financial Statements*, which will replace IAS 1, *Presentation of Financial Statements*, from its effective date.

IFRS 18 introduces new requirements for information presented in the primary financial statements and disclosed in the notes, with a focus on the statement of profit or loss. It introduces three categories for income and expenses—operating, investing, and financing—to improve the structure of the income statement.

IFRS 18 is effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2027. Early adoption is permitted. The Group is yet to evaluate the impact of the amendment.

### Amendments to IFRS 9 Financial Instruments and IFRS 7 Financial Instruments: Disclosures

On May 30, 2025, the IASB issued amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7. The amendments clarify the classification of financial assets with environmental, social, and governance (ESG) and similar features; address derecognition of financial liabilities settled through electronic payment systems; and introduce additional disclosure requirements concerning investments in equity instruments designated at fair value through other comprehensive income and financial instruments with contingent features.

The amendments are effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026. Early adoption is permitted. The Group is yet to evaluate their impact.

On December 18, 2024, the IASB issued amendments to IFRS 9 and IFRS 7 concerning factors considered in assessing own-use requirements for contracts to buy and take delivery of nature-dependent renewable electricity, hedge accounting treatment for such electricity, and related disclosures.

These amendments are effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2026. Early adoption is permitted. The Group has evaluated the amendments and determined that they have no impact on its consolidated financial statements.
