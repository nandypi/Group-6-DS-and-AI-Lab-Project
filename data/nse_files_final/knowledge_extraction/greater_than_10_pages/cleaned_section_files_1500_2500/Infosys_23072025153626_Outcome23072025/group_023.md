Depreciation methods, useful lives, and residual values are reviewed periodically, including at each financial year end. Useful lives are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology.

Advances paid toward the acquisition of property, plant and equipment outstanding at each balance sheet date, and the cost of assets not ready for use before that date, are disclosed as capital work-in-progress. Subsequent expenditures relating to property, plant and equipment are capitalized only when future economic benefits are probable and the cost can be measured reliably. The cost and related accumulated depreciation are removed from the financial statements upon sale or retirement of an asset.

## Impairment

Property, plant and equipment are evaluated for recoverability whenever events or changes in circumstances indicate that their carrying amounts may not be recoverable. The recoverable amount—the higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use—is determined for individual assets unless an asset does not generate cash flows that are largely independent of those of other assets. In such cases, recoverability is assessed for the cash-generating unit (CGU) to which the asset belongs.

An impairment loss recognized in net profit in the interim condensed consolidated statement of comprehensive income equals the excess of an asset’s carrying value over its estimated recoverable amount. An impairment loss is reversed in net profit if the estimates used to determine the recoverable amount change. The asset’s carrying amount is increased to its revised recoverable amount, subject to the amount that would have been determined, net of accumulated depreciation, if no impairment loss had been recognized in prior years.
