## 2.16 Revenue from Operations

## Accounting Policy

The Group derives revenues primarily from:

- IT services, including software development and related services, cloud and infrastructure services, maintenance, consulting, and package implementation.
- Licensing of software products and platforms across the Group's core and digital offerings, collectively referred to as software-related services.
- Business process management services.

Customer contracts may be entered into on a time-and-material, unit-of-work, fixed-price, or fixed-time-frame basis.

Revenue is recognized when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable. Revenue is recognized upon the transfer of control of promised products or services to customers, at an amount reflecting the consideration the Group expects to receive. When collectability is uncertain, revenue recognition is postponed until the uncertainty is resolved.

The Group identifies distinct performance obligations and allocates the transaction price to each obligation based on relative standalone selling prices. The regularly charged price for an item sold separately is the best evidence of its standalone selling price. If that evidence is unavailable, the Group estimates standalone selling price using expected cost plus an appropriate margin.

Contracts may include variable consideration such as rebates, volume discounts, and penalties. Variable consideration is included in the transaction price when it can be reasonably estimated and it is probable that recognizing it will not result in a significant reversal of cumulative revenue when the uncertainty is resolved.

Revenue recognition by contract type is as follows:

- Time-and-material and unit-of-work revenue is recognized as the related services are performed.
- Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services consist of an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. It is recognized using a percentage-of-completion method when the pattern of benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive.
- Revenue from other fixed-price, fixed-time-frame contracts satisfied over time is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method.
- Efforts or costs incurred are used to measure progress toward completion when there is a direct relationship between input and productivity.
- Estimates of transaction price and total costs or efforts are monitored throughout the contract. Changes in estimates are recognized in net profit in the period in which the estimates are revised.
- Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.

Customer billing schedules may include periodic performance-based billing or milestone-based progress billing. Revenue in excess of billing is classified as unbilled revenue, while billing in excess of revenue is classified as a contract liability, referred to as unearned revenue.

For software development and related services and maintenance arrangements, software development and related services generally qualify as distinct performance obligations. Revenue is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices, using expected cost plus margin when standalone selling prices cannot be determined. For software development and related services, performance obligations are satisfied as services are rendered because the customer generally obtains control of the work as it progresses.

Certain cloud and infrastructure services contracts include multiple elements subject to specific accounting guidance, including leasing guidance. Where hardware and services are distinct performance obligations, consideration is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices or, when unavailable, using expected cost plus margin. When the arrangement is treated as a single performance obligation, revenue is recognized over the contract period based on the progress of the promised services.

Revenue from software licenses is recognized:

- At the time the license is made available to the customer when the customer obtains a right to use the license.
- Over the access period when the customer obtains a right to access the license.

Software product arrangements generally include three elements: license, implementation, and Annual Technical Services (ATS). When the license and implementation are distinct performance obligations, the transaction price is allocated based on relative standalone selling prices. If the license is substantially customized as part of implementation, the license and implementation fee are treated as a single performance obligation, and revenue is recognized using the percentage-of-completion method as implementation is performed. Revenue from client training, support, and other services is recognized as the related performance obligations are satisfied. ATS revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis over the service period.

When contracts include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software, revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Group assesses control based on factors including responsibility for fulfilling the promise, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other relevant considerations.

A contract modification is a change in contract scope or price, or both, approved by the parties. A modification adding distinct performance obligations is accounted for as:

- A separate contract when the additional services are priced at their standalone selling price.
- A termination of the existing contract and creation of a new contract when the additional services are not priced at their standalone selling price.

A modification that does not add a distinct performance obligation is accounted for as part of the existing contract on a cumulative catch-up basis.

Incremental costs of obtaining a contract are recognized as an asset when the Group expects to recover them. Eligible nonrecurring costs, such as set-up, transition, or transformation costs, are recognized as an asset when they:

- Relate directly to the contract.
- Generate or enhance resources that will be used to satisfy future performance obligations.
- Are expected to be recovered.

Capitalized contract costs relating to upfront payments to customers are amortized to revenue. Other capitalized costs are amortized to cost of sales over the respective contract life on a systematic basis consistent with the transfer of the related goods or services. Capitalized costs are monitored for impairment, and impairment losses are recorded when the present value of projected remaining operating cash flows is insufficient to recover the carrying amount.

The Group presents revenue net of indirect taxes in its interim condensed Consolidated Statement of Comprehensive Income.
