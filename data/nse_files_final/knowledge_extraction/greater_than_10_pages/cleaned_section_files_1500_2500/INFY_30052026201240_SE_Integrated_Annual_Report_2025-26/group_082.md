## Overview and Notes to the Standalone Financial Statements

## 1. Overview

### 1.1 Company overview

Infosys Limited ("the Company" or "Infosys") provides AI-first business consulting and technology services to enable organizations to unlock AI value at scale. The Company has over four decades of experience managing the systems and workings of global enterprises.

Infosys is a public limited company incorporated and domiciled in India. Its registered office is at Electronics City, Hosur Road, Bengaluru 560100, Karnataka, India. The Company’s primary listings are on BSE Ltd. and the National Stock Exchange of India Limited. Its American Depositary Shares (ADSs), representing equity shares, are listed on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE).

The standalone financial statements were approved for issue by the Company’s Board of Directors on April 23, 2026.

### 1.2 Basis of preparation of financial statements

The standalone financial statements are prepared in accordance with the Companies Act, 2013, guidelines issued by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), and Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) under the historical cost convention on an accrual basis, except for certain financial instruments measured at fair value and defined benefit liabilities or assets recognized at the present value of defined benefit obligations less the fair value of plan assets.

Accounting policies have been applied consistently, except when a newly issued accounting standard is initially adopted or a revision to an existing accounting policy is required. Material accounting policy information used in preparing the audited standalone financial statements is discussed in the respective notes.

Year-end figures are taken from source data and rounded to the nearest digits. Consequently, figures reported for previous quarters may not always add up to the year-end figures reported in the statement.

### 1.3 Use of estimates and judgments

Preparing financial statements in conformity with Ind AS requires management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies, reported amounts of assets and liabilities, disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities, and reported revenues and expenses.

Critical accounting estimates and judgments involving complex or subjective assumptions are disclosed in Note 1.4. These estimates, judgments, and assumptions may change from period to period, and actual results could differ from the estimates. Changes in estimates are reflected in the period in which they are made; material effects are disclosed in the notes to the standalone financial statements.

### 1.4 Critical accounting estimates and judgments

#### a. Revenue recognition

Customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is recognized and measured when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their respective obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable.

The Company assesses the services promised in each contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Determining distinct performance obligations, assessing whether the customer can benefit independently from the deliverables, and allocating the transaction price to those obligations involve significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and the Company’s costs to fulfill the contract are uneven because the services are discrete and non-repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method. The method used depends on the contractual promises and the nature of the deliverables.

The Company uses the percentage-of-completion method for other fixed-price contracts. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs. Estimating total efforts or costs involves significant judgment and is reassessed throughout the contract period.

Some integrated services arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party products or services is recorded net of costs when the Company acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Company assesses control of the specified goods or services before transfer to the customer, considering responsibility for fulfillment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable, based on estimated efforts or costs to complete the contract.

#### b. Income taxes

The Company’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States, although it also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is required to determine income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

To assess the realizability of deferred income tax assets, management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies. The ultimate realization of deferred tax assets depends on generating future taxable income during the periods in which temporary differences become deductible. The realizable amount could be reduced if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. (Refer to Note 2.17.)

#### c. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Company’s asset base. Periodic depreciation depends on estimates of expected useful lives and residual values. Management determines these estimates when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end.

Useful lives are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. (Refer to Note 2.1.)

## 2. Notes to the Standalone Financial Statements

## 2.1 Property, plant and equipment

### Accounting Policy

Property, plant and equipment are stated at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment, if any. Costs directly attributable to acquisition are capitalized until the assets are ready for intended use. Property, plant and equipment are depreciated over their estimated useful lives using the straight-line method.

The estimated useful lives of assets are as follows:

| Asset | Estimated useful life |
|---|---|
| Building (1) | 22–25 years |
| Plant and machinery (1) | 5 years |
| Office equipment | 5 years |
| Computer equipment (1) | 3–5 years |
| Furniture and fixtures (1) | 5 years |
| Vehicles (1) | 5 years |
| Leasehold improvements | Lower of the asset’s useful life or the lease term |

(1) Based on technical evaluation, management believes these useful lives best represent the period over which the assets are expected to be used. Accordingly, the useful lives for these assets differ from those prescribed under Part C of Schedule II of the Companies Act, 2013.

Depreciation methods, useful lives, and residual values are reviewed periodically, including at each financial year-end.

Advances paid toward acquiring property, plant and equipment that remain outstanding at each balance sheet date are classified as capital advances under other non-current assets. The cost of assets not ready for use by that date is disclosed as capital work-in-progress.

Subsequent expenditure relating to property, plant and equipment is capitalized only when future economic benefits are probable and the cost can be measured reliably. The cost and related accumulated depreciation are removed from the financial statements upon sale or retirement of the asset.
