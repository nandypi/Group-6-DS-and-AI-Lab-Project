## Overview and notes to the Consolidated Financial Statements

## 1. Overview

## 1.1 Company overview

Infosys Limited ("the Company" or "Infosys") provides AI-first business consulting and technology services. The Company has over four decades of experience managing systems and operations for global enterprises.

Infosys together with its subsidiaries and controlled trusts is referred to as "the Group".

The Company is a public limited company incorporated and domiciled in India. Its registered office is at Electronics City, Hosur Road, Bengaluru 560100, Karnataka, India. The Company’s primary listings are on BSE Ltd. and the National Stock Exchange of India Limited. Its American Depositary Shares (ADS), representing equity shares, are listed on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE).

The Group’s consolidated financial statements were approved for issue by the Company’s Board of Directors on April 23, 2026.

## 1.2 Basis of preparation of financial statements

The consolidated financial statements are prepared in accordance with the Companies Act, 2013, guidelines issued by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), and Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS).

The financial statements are prepared under the historical cost convention on an accrual basis, except for certain financial instruments measured at fair value and defined benefit liabilities or assets recognized at the present value of the defined benefit obligation less the fair value of plan assets.

The Ind AS are prescribed under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013, read with Rule 3 of the Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015, and relevant amendment rules issued thereafter.

Accounting policies are applied consistently, except when a newly issued accounting standard is initially adopted or a revision to an existing accounting standard requires a change in accounting policy. Material accounting policy information used in preparing the audited consolidated financial statements is discussed in the respective notes.

Quarter and year-to-date figures are rounded to the nearest digits. As a result, quarter figures may not always add up to the year-to-date figures reported in the statement.

## 1.3 Basis of consolidation

Infosys consolidates entities that it owns or controls. The consolidated financial statements comprise the financial statements of the Company, its controlled trusts, and its subsidiaries.

Control exists when the parent has power over an entity, is exposed or has rights to variable returns from its involvement with the entity, and has the ability to affect those returns through its power. Power is demonstrated through existing rights that provide the ability to direct relevant activities that significantly affect the entity’s returns.

Subsidiaries are consolidated from the date control commences until the date control ceases. The financial statements of Group companies are consolidated on a line-by-line basis. Intra-group balances and transactions, including unrealized gains or losses, are eliminated upon consolidation.

The financial statements are prepared using uniform accounting policies across the Group. Non-controlling interests representing the portion of net profit or loss and net assets of subsidiaries not directly or indirectly owned or controlled by the Company are excluded.

Refer to Note 2.25 for the list of subsidiaries and controlled trusts of the Company.

## 1.4 Use of estimates and judgments

Preparing the consolidated financial statements in conformity with Ind AS requires Management to make estimates, judgments, and assumptions. These affect the application of accounting policies, reported amounts of assets and liabilities, disclosures of contingent assets and liabilities, and reported revenues and expenses.

The accounting policies involving critical accounting estimates and complex or subjective judgments are disclosed in Note 1.5. Estimates, judgments, and assumptions may change as circumstances change. Actual results could differ from these estimates. Changes are reflected in the consolidated financial statements in the period in which they are made, with material effects disclosed in the notes.

## 1.5 Critical accounting estimates and judgments

### a. Revenue recognition

The Group’s customer contracts may include promises to transfer multiple products and services. Revenue is considered for recognition and measurement when the contract has been approved in writing, the parties are committed to performing their obligations, and the contract is legally enforceable.

The Group assesses the services promised in a contract and identifies distinct performance obligations. Identifying distinct performance obligations, determining deliverables and the customer’s ability to benefit independently from them, and allocating the transaction price to those obligations involve significant judgment.

Fixed-price maintenance revenue is recognized ratably on a straight-line basis when services are performed through an indefinite number of repetitive acts over a specified period. When the pattern of benefits and fulfillment costs is uneven because services are discrete and non-repetitive, revenue is recognized ratably using a percentage-of-completion method. The method used depends on the contractual promises and nature of the deliverables.

The Group uses the percentage-of-completion method for other fixed-price contracts. Progress is measured using actual efforts or costs incurred to date as a proportion of estimated total efforts or costs. Estimating total efforts or costs involves significant judgment and is reassessed throughout the contract period.

Some integrated service arrangements include subcontractor services or third-party vendor equipment or software. Revenue from third-party vendor products or services is recorded net of costs when the Group acts as an agent and gross when it acts as the principal. The Group assesses control of the specified goods or services by considering responsibility for fulfillment, inventory risk, pricing discretion, and other factors.

Provisions for estimated losses on incomplete contracts are recorded when such losses become probable.

### b. Income taxes

The Group’s two major tax jurisdictions are India and the United States. The Company also files tax returns in other overseas jurisdictions.

Significant judgment is required to determine income tax provisions, including amounts expected to be paid or recovered for uncertain tax positions.

When assessing whether deferred income tax assets are realizable, Management considers scheduled reversals of deferred income tax liabilities, projected future taxable income, and tax-planning strategies. The realization of deferred tax assets depends on generating future taxable income during the periods in which temporary differences become deductible. The amount considered realizable could be reduced if estimates of future taxable income during the carry-forward period decline. Refer to Note 2.17.

### c. Business combinations and intangible assets

Business combinations are accounted for under Ind AS 103, Business Combinations. The standard requires identifiable intangible assets and contingent consideration to be fair valued to determine the net fair value of the acquiree’s identifiable assets, liabilities, and contingent liabilities.

External valuation experts conduct these valuations. Estimates are required to determine the value of contingent consideration, option arrangements, and intangible assets. Measurements are based on information available at the acquisition date and Management’s reasonable expectations and assumptions. Refer to Notes 2.1 and 2.4.2.

### d. Property, plant and equipment

Property, plant and equipment represent a significant proportion of the Group’s asset base. Periodic depreciation is determined using estimates of an asset’s expected useful life and residual value at the end of its useful life.

Management determines useful lives and residual values when assets are acquired and reviews them periodically, including at each financial year-end. The estimates are based on historical experience with similar assets and anticipated future events, including changes in technology. Refer to Note 2.2.

### e. Impairment of goodwill

Goodwill is tested for impairment annually and whenever there is an indication that the recoverable amount of a cash-generating unit (CGU) is less than its carrying amount.

For impairment testing, goodwill is allocated to the CGU or group of CGUs benefiting from acquisition synergies and representing the lowest level at which goodwill is monitored for internal management purposes.

The recoverable amount of CGUs is determined as the higher of value in use and fair value less costs to sell. Key assumptions in cash-flow projections include estimated long-term growth rates, weighted average cost of capital, and estimated operating margins. Refer to Note 2.4.1.
