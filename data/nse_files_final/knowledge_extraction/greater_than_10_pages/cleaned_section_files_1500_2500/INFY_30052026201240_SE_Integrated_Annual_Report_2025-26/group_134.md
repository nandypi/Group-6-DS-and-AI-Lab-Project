## Credit risk

Credit risk refers to the risk of default by a counterparty, resulting in a financial loss. The maximum exposure to credit risk at the reporting date is primarily from:

- Trade receivables of ` 35,234 crore at March 31, 2026 and ` 31,158 crore at March 31, 2025.
- Unbilled revenues of ` 17,221 crore at March 31, 2026 and ` 15,083 crore at March 31, 2025.

Trade receivables and unbilled revenues are typically unsecured and arise primarily from customers located in the United States of America and Europe.

The Group manages credit risk through credit approvals, credit limits, and continuous monitoring of customer creditworthiness. It uses the expected credit loss model to assess required allowances and a provision matrix to calculate expected credit loss allowances for trade receivables and unbilled revenues. The matrix considers available credit reports and related credit information.

Credit risk is influenced mainly by individual customer characteristics and concentration among the largest customers. Exposure is diversified, with no single customer contributing more than 10% of outstanding trade receivables and unbilled revenues.

The Group evaluates counterparty risk using criteria including Tier I Capital, Capital Adequacy Ratio, credit rating, profitability, non-performing asset levels, and deposit base of banks and financial institutions. These risks are monitored regularly under the Group’s risk management program.

## 2.12 Equity

## Ordinary shares

Ordinary shares are classified as equity share capital. Incremental costs directly attributable to issuing new ordinary shares, share options, and buybacks are deducted from equity, net of tax effects.

## Treasury shares

When an entity within the Group purchases the Company’s ordinary shares, the consideration paid, including directly attributable incremental costs, is presented as a deduction from total equity until the shares are cancelled, sold, or reissued. When treasury shares are subsequently sold or reissued, the amount received is recognized as an increase in equity, and the resulting surplus or deficit is transferred to or from securities premium.
