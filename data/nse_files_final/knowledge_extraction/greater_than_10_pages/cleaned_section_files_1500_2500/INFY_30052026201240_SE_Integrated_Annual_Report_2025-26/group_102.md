## Remaining performance obligation disclosure

The remaining performance obligation disclosure provides the aggregate transaction price yet to be recognized at the end of the reporting period and explains when the Company expects to recognize these amounts as revenue.

Applying the practical expedient under Ind AS 115, the Company has not disclosed remaining performance obligations for contracts where recognized revenue corresponds directly with the value to the customer of performance completed to date. These typically include time-and-material and unit-of-work-based contracts.

Remaining performance obligation estimates are affected by terminations, changes in contract scope, periodic revalidations, adjustments for revenue that has not materialized, and currency fluctuations.

The aggregate value of completely or partially unsatisfied performance obligations as at March 31, 2026, excluding contracts meeting the criteria above, was ` 1,13,675 crore. The Company expected to recognize approximately 50.1% within one year, approximately 21.6% between one and two years, and the remainder thereafter.

The aggregate value of completely or partially unsatisfied performance obligations as at March 31, 2025, was ` 90,815 crore. Contracts can generally be terminated by customers and typically include an enforceable termination penalty payable by them. Generally, customers have not terminated contracts without cause.

## 2.19 Other income, net

### 2.19.1 Other income

#### Accounting policy

Other income primarily comprises interest income, dividend income, gains or losses on investments, and exchange gains or losses on forward and options contracts and the translation of foreign-currency assets and liabilities.

Interest income is recognized using the effective interest method. Dividend income is recognized when the right to receive payment is established.

### 2.19.2 Foreign currency Accounting policy

#### Functional currency

The functional currency of the Company is the Indian rupee. The financial statements are presented in Indian rupees, rounded off to crore. One crore equals ten million.

#### Transactions and translations

Foreign-currency-denominated monetary assets and liabilities are translated into the relevant functional currency at exchange rates prevailing at the Balance Sheet date. Resulting gains or losses are recognized in the standalone Statement of Profit and Loss within exchange gains or losses on translation of assets and liabilities, net, except when deferred in Other Comprehensive Income as qualifying cash flow hedges.

Non-monetary foreign-currency assets and liabilities measured at fair value are translated at the exchange rate prevailing when fair value was determined. Those measured at historical cost are translated at the exchange rate prevailing on the transaction date. Related revenue and expenses are recognized using the same exchange rate.

Transaction gains or losses on settlement of foreign-currency transactions are included in net profit for the period in which settlement occurs. Foreign-currency-denominated revenue, expenses, and cash-flow items are translated using the exchange rate prevailing on the transaction date.

Other Comprehensive Income, net of taxes, includes translation differences on non-monetary financial assets measured at fair value at the reporting date, including equities classified as financial instruments measured at fair value through other comprehensive income (FVOCI).

#### Government grant

The Company recognizes government grants when there is reasonable assurance that the attached conditions will be complied with and that the grants will be received.

Government grants related to assets are treated as deferred income and recognized in net profit in the Statement of Profit and Loss systematically over the useful life of the asset. Grants related to revenue are recognized systematically over the periods necessary to match them with the related costs they are intended to compensate.

### 2.20.1 Impact of Labour Codes

On November 21, 2025, the Government of India notified provisions of the Code on Wages, 2019; the Industrial Relations Code, 2020; the Code on Social Security, 2020; and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020. These Labour Codes consolidate twenty-nine existing labour laws into a unified framework governing employee benefits during employment and post-employment.

The Labour Codes introduce changes including a uniform definition of wages and enhanced leave benefits. The Company assessed the financial implications, resulting in an increase of ` 1,146 crore in gratuity liability, defined benefit plan liability arising from past service cost relating to plan amendments, and compensated absences.

The Company presented this incremental, non-recurring amount as “Impact of Labour Codes” under “Exceptional Item” in the Standalone Statement of Profit and Loss for the year ended March 31, 2026. The Company continues to monitor developments relating to the Labour Codes and will evaluate any impact on the measurement of employee benefit liabilities.

## 2.21 Employee benefits

### 2.21.1 Gratuity and pensions

The Company provides gratuity through a defined benefit retirement plan covering eligible Indian employees of Infosys. The plan provides a lump-sum payment to vested employees upon retirement, death, incapacitation, or termination of employment, based on salary and tenure with the Company.

The Company contributes gratuity liabilities to the Infosys Limited Employees’ Gratuity Fund Trust. Trustees administer the contributions, which are invested in a scheme with the Life Insurance Corporation of India as permitted by Indian law.

The Company operates defined benefit pension plans in certain overseas jurisdictions in accordance with local laws. These plans are managed by third-party fund managers and provide periodic retirement payouts and/or lump-sum payments under the rules of each fund, including death and disability benefits. Contributions are based on a percentage of salary that varies according to the employee’s age.

Liabilities relating to these defined benefit plans are determined by an external actuary at each Balance Sheet date using the projected unit credit method. The plans expose the Company to actuarial risks including longevity risk, interest-rate risk, and market risk.

The Company recognizes the net obligation of a defined benefit plan as an asset or liability in its Balance Sheet. Re-measurement gains and losses on the net defined benefit liability or asset are recognized in Other Comprehensive Income and are not reclassified to profit or loss in subsequent periods. The actual return on plan assets in excess of the return calculated using the discount rate applied to the defined benefit obligation is recognized in Other Comprehensive Income. The effect of plan amendments is recognized in net profit in the Statement of Profit and Loss.
