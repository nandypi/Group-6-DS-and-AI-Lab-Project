# Defined Benefit Plans

For the domestic defined benefit plan in India, the yield on government bonds is used as the discount rate because a market for high-quality corporate bonds has not developed. For most overseas defined benefit plans, the government bond rate adjusted for corporate spreads is used for the same reason.

The average rate of increase in compensation levels is determined by the Company based on factors including past compensation revision trends, inflation in the respective markets, and management’s estimate of future salary increases.

The attrition rate is based on management’s estimate of the Company’s long-term employee turnover trend. Employee tenure reflects the long-term trend in employees’ average remaining service life and the average estimated term of the post-employment benefit obligation.

For the domestic defined benefit plan in India, future mortality assumptions are based on published statistics from the Life Insurance Corporation of India. For overseas plans, mortality assumptions consider the latest life-expectancy statistics, plan experience, and other relevant data.

The Company assesses these assumptions against its projected long-term growth plans and prevailing industry standards.

The Company contributes all ascertained gratuity liabilities to the Infosys Limited Employees’ Gratuity Fund Trust, which is administered by trustees. Overseas defined benefit plan assets are primarily invested in insurer-managed funds. Asset allocation is determined according to investment criteria prescribed under applicable pension-fund regulations and by insurer managers. The insurers’ investments are diversified and include guaranteed interest-rate arrangements.

These defined benefit plans expose the Company to the following actuarial risks:

- **Interest rate risk:** The present value of the defined benefit obligation is generally calculated using a discount rate based on government bond yields. In certain overseas jurisdictions, the rate is based on government bond yields adjusted for a corporate spread. If bond yields fall, the defined benefit obligation generally increases.
- **Life expectancy and investment risk:** The pension fund provides a choice between a lifelong pension and a cash lump sum at retirement. The fund has defined rates for converting the lump sum to a pension, creating a risk that members live longer than implied by those rates or that pension assets do not achieve the implied investment return.
- **Asset volatility:** A proportion of the pension fund is invested in equities, which are expected to outperform corporate bonds over the long term but create short-term volatility and risk. The insurer’s pension-fund board is responsible for the investment strategy and equity allocation.

Sensitivity for significant actuarial assumptions is computed by varying one actuarial assumption while keeping all other assumptions constant. In practice, this is not probable, and changes in some assumptions may be correlated.
