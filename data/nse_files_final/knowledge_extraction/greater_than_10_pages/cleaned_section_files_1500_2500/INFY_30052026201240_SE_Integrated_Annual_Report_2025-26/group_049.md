# Risk management report

Deepak Bhalla, EVP – Chief Risk Officer

The Company’s enterprise risk management processes address risks arising from geopolitical shifts, economic uncertainty, supply chain disruptions, evolving regulations, advances in artificial intelligence, technology changes, and cybersecurity threats.

Risk-related information in this section may not be exhaustive. Certain statements may be forward-looking, and actual results may differ materially because of known or unknown risks and uncertainties. Detailed risk factors and related disclosures are provided in the Company’s regulatory filings.

The Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) function supports the Company’s strategic objectives by identifying, analyzing, assessing, mitigating, monitoring, and governing risks and potential threats. The framework considers the interests of employees, customers, investors, regulators, partners, and the community.

Strategic decisions consider primary, secondary, consequential, and residual risks. The ERM function supports resource allocation through qualitative and quantitative assessments based on the Company’s risk appetite. Risk impact and likelihood are assessed using a four-point scale. Risks are categorized and mitigated in order of criticality: critical, high, medium, and low. The default risk appetite is low impact, supported by preventive, detective, and corrective controls and governance mechanisms intended to keep residual risk exposures within acceptable limits.

The ERM framework also identifies potential opportunities during risk assessments. It covers:

| Risk category | Description |
|---|---|
| Strategy and strategy execution | Risks arising from strategic choices and the successful execution of strategy, including industry risks, competitiveness, target markets, market offerings, the business model, and the talent base. |
| Operational | Risks affecting policies, procedures, people, and systems, including service delivery, operations, internal-process inefficiencies, human rights, fraud, business disruptions caused by natural calamities, climate change events, human conflicts, system failures, and cybersecurity attacks. |
| Legal and compliance | Risks arising from litigation, non-conformance with laws, regulatory or geopolitical developments, codes of conduct, and contractual compliance that could affect financial, organizational, or reputational standing. |

## Emerging risks

The ERM framework includes a process to identify, assess, plan for, and monitor risks emerging from the internal and external environment. This process includes horizon scanning and SWOT analysis covering political, economic, social, technological, legal, and environmental factors.

Emerging risks are monitored continuously and discussed periodically by risk councils and the Risk Management Committee (RMC) of the Board.

## Integrated Enterprise Risk Management Framework

Infosys has adopted an integrated ERM framework implemented across the organization by the Risk Management Office. The framework incorporates practices based on COSO and ISO 31000 and is tailored to the Company’s business requirements.

Risk management covers the first, second, and third lines of defense. It is embedded in core business processes, day-to-day decision-making, development processes, and project execution. Managing risks in support of strategic goals is the responsibility of management at all levels. Financial incentives are linked to the achievement of strategic goals.

The first line of defense evaluates risks and opportunities before key business decisions are made. It works with corporate ERM through risk councils and sub-risk councils across organizational units.

The second line of defense includes the Information Security Group (ISG), Data Privacy Office (DPO), Quality, Delivery Risk Management (DRM), Business Continuity, Legal, and Compliance functions. These functions work with ERM to identify, assess, report, and govern risks. Risks are primarily mitigated by the business and tracked and governed by the second line. Key risks are reported to executive leadership through councils including the Operational Risk Council (ORC) and the Legal & Compliance Risk Council (LCRC), and are presented to the RMC of the Board quarterly.

The third line of defense is internal audit. It periodically reviews risks and controls, assesses the effectiveness of the risk management process, and reports independently. ERM inputs on key risks are considered when preparing the audit plan.

The Company maintains a single hierarchical risk repository from L1 to L5, covering strategic to tactical risks. These risks are governed through the integrated ERM framework.

The Company periodically conducts an independent external assessment of ERM-process maturity. The maturity assessment was conducted in March 2024.

Risk management is supported by forums for risk discussions and decision-making at multiple organizational levels. Regular awareness and training programs cover risks applicable to employees and contractors. Lex, the Company’s in-house learning platform, provides risk-management courses accessible globally.

Periodic tabletop exercises are conducted in key areas to assess preparedness for high-impact, low-probability risks.
