## Tax Deduction at Source / Withholding of Taxes

For resident shareholders, tax shall be deducted at source under Section 194 of the Income Tax Act as follows:

| Members | Tax deduction rate |
|---|---:|
| Members having a valid Permanent Account Number (PAN) | 10%* or as notified by the Government of India |
| Members not having a PAN or having an invalid PAN | 20% or as notified by the Government of India |

\* Under Section 139AA of the Income Tax Act, individuals who have been allotted a PAN and are eligible to obtain Aadhaar must link their PAN with Aadhaar. If this is not done, the PAN may be deemed invalid or inoperative, and tax may be deducted at the higher rate under Section 206AA of the Income Tax Act, 1961, currently 20%.

No tax shall be deducted from resident individual shareholders if the aggregate of all dividends and buyback consideration received during financial year 2025–2026 does not exceed ₹10,000.

No tax shall be deducted where an individual shareholder provides Form 15G or Form 15H, subject to the conditions specified in the Income Tax Act. Form 15G applies to resident individual shareholders, while Form 15H applies to resident individual shareholders aged 60 years or more. If these forms become invalid during financial year 2025–2026, tax will be deducted on the total payments made during that financial year from the buyback consideration.

PAN is mandatory for shareholders submitting Form 15G or Form 15H. Resident shareholders may also submit a declaration or document specifying the relevant provisions of the Income Tax Act to claim a lower or nil tax deduction. Shareholders may provide a Lower Tax Deduction Certificate issued by the Income Tax Department under Section 197 or another applicable section, authorizing the company to deduct tax at a lower rate.

For non-resident shareholders, income tax is required to be withheld from buyback consideration in accordance with Sections 195, 196D, or other applicable provisions of the Income Tax Act, treating the consideration as dividend. The current withholding tax rate is 20%, plus applicable surcharge and cess, or such other rate as notified by the Government of India.

Non-resident shareholders may provide a Lower Tax Deduction Certificate issued by the Income Tax Department under Section 195 or another applicable section, authorizing the company to deduct withholding tax at a lower rate.

Under Section 90 of the Income Tax Act, non-resident shareholders may claim benefits under an applicable Double Tax Avoidance Agreement (DTAA), read with the Multilateral Instrument (MLI), if the applicable conditions are satisfied. This includes conditions relating to the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), the shareholder’s country of tax residence, and the requirements of the applicable tax treaty.

To claim DTAA benefits, non-resident shareholders must provide:

- A copy of the PAN card allotted by the Indian income tax authorities, duly attested by the shareholder; or the details prescribed under Rule 37BC of the Income-tax Rules, 1962, if the shareholder does not have a PAN card.
- A copy of the Tax Residency Certificate for financial year 2025–2026 obtained from the tax authorities of the country of tax residence, duly attested by the shareholder or authorized signatory.
- Electronic Form 10F, as required under Notification No. 03/2022 dated July 16, 2022, issued by the Central Board of Direct Taxes.
- A self-declaration confirming that the shareholder has no permanent establishment in India under the applicable tax treaty.
- A self-declaration confirming beneficial ownership of the equity shares.
- A self-declaration confirming that all conditions of the applicable tax treaty, read with the MLI, have been fulfilled.
- Any other documents prescribed under the Income Tax Act for lower withholding of taxes, if applicable, duly attested by the shareholder.

The availability of DTAA benefits depends on the documents submitted.

## Taxation for American Depositary Shares ('ADS') holders ('Holder')

The withdrawal of equity shares underlying ADSs, the re-deposit of equity shares not tendered or not accepted in the buyback against the creation of ADSs, and the sale in the buyback of withdrawn equity shares may have tax implications in India, the United States, and other jurisdictions. These implications may differ from, and may be more onerous than, those applicable to sales of ADSs on the NYSE.

The tax implications may vary depending on factors including the holding period, the holder’s residential status, the holder’s classification, and the nature of the income earned.

## a. India

### Withdrawal and Re-Deposit of Equity Shares by ADS Holders

Because there is limited precedent, it is unclear whether the re-deposit of equity shares against the creation of ADSs is subject to Indian taxation. Tax may be imposed, and the bank, broker, or other nominee in India with whom an ADS holder establishes an Indian Demat account (Subtype DR) and brokerage account may withhold taxes in connection with the re-deposit.

ADS holders should consult their legal, financial, and tax advisors, as well as the relevant bank, broker, or nominee in India, before requesting the Depositary to withdraw the equity shares underlying their ADSs.

ADS holders should also consider potential taxation under the laws of their country of residence and any other jurisdiction in which they may be subject to taxation arising from the withdrawal and re-deposit of equity shares.

## b. United States

### i. Withdrawal and Re-Deposit of Equity Shares by ADS Holders

For U.S. federal income tax purposes, ADS holders generally will be treated as the owners of the equity shares underlying their ADSs. Accordingly, withdrawing equity shares underlying ADSs and re-depositing equity shares that are not tendered, or that are tendered but not accepted in the buyback, generally will not be subject to U.S. federal income tax.

Further information on U.S. federal income tax consequences for U.S. holders of ADSs and equity shares is provided in the company’s Form 20-F.

### ii. Tendering Equity Shares in the Buyback

Information on the tax treatment of tendering equity shares in the buyback is provided in a Form 6-K furnished by the company with the SEC on or about the date of the Postal Ballot Notice, as well as in this Letter of Offer.

Each ADS holder should consult their own tax advisor regarding the U.S. federal, state, and local tax consequences of withdrawing and re-depositing equity shares and participating in the buyback.

U.S. holders should consult their own tax advisors regarding the treatment of dividends, eligibility for reduced tax rates in effect for the year of the buyback, and the availability of foreign tax credits for any Indian tax.

### Securities Transaction Tax

Because the buyback will be undertaken through the settlement mechanism on the Indian Stock Exchanges, the transaction is subject to Securities Transaction Tax (STT). STT is payable in India on the value of securities purchased or sold on recognized stock exchanges in India. The current STT rate applicable to the purchase or sale of shares on a stock exchange is 0.10% of the transaction value.

### Rate of Surcharge and Cess

In addition to the basic tax rate, surcharge, education cess, and secondary and higher education cess are leviable as applicable.
