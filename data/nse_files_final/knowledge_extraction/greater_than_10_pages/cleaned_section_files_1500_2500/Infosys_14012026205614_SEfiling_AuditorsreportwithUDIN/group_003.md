## Basis for Opinion

The audit of the standalone financial results was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing ("SAs") specified under Section 143(10) of the Companies Act. The auditors stated that they were independent of the Company in accordance with the Code of Ethics issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India ("ICAI") and other ethical requirements relevant to the audit of the standalone financial results for the quarter and nine months ended December 31, 2025.

The auditors stated that the audit evidence obtained was sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their audit opinion.

## Management's and Board of Directors' Responsibilities for the Statement

The Statement, including the standalone financial results, is the responsibility of the Company's Board of Directors and was approved by them for issuance. It was compiled from the related audited interim condensed standalone financial statements for the three months and nine months ended December 31, 2025.

The standalone financial results are intended to present a true and fair view of net profit, other comprehensive income, and other financial information in accordance with the recognition and measurement principles of Ind AS 34, prescribed under Section 133 of the Act, relevant rules, generally accepted accounting principles in India, and Regulation 33 of the LODR Regulations.

The Board of Directors is responsible for:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records in accordance with the Act.
- Safeguarding the Company's assets and preventing and detecting fraud and other irregularities.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls that operate effectively to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting records relevant to the preparation and presentation of the Statement.
- Assessing the Company's ability to continue as a going concern and disclosing relevant going-concern matters.
- Overseeing the Company's financial reporting process.

## Auditor's Responsibilities for audit of the Standalone Financial Results for the quarter and nine months ended December 31, 2025

The auditors' objectives are to obtain reasonable assurance that the Statement as a whole is free from material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error, and to issue an auditor's report containing their opinion.

Reasonable assurance is a high level of assurance but is not a guarantee that an audit conducted in accordance with SAs will always detect a material misstatement. Misstatements are considered material if, individually or in aggregate, they could reasonably be expected to influence users' economic decisions based on the standalone financial results.

As part of an audit conducted in accordance with SAs, the auditors exercise professional judgment and maintain professional skepticism. Their responsibilities include:

- Identifying and assessing the risks of material misstatement, whether due to fraud or error; designing and performing procedures responsive to those risks; and obtaining sufficient and appropriate audit evidence.
- Considering the higher risk of failing to detect fraud-related misstatements, as fraud may involve collusion, forgery, intentional omissions, misrepresentations, or management override of internal control.
- Obtaining an understanding of internal control relevant to the audit to design appropriate audit procedures, but not to express an opinion on the effectiveness of those controls.
- Evaluating the appropriateness of accounting policies and the reasonableness of accounting estimates made by the Board of Directors.
- Evaluating the appropriateness and reasonableness of disclosures made by the Board of Directors under Regulation 33 of the LODR Regulations.
- Concluding on the appropriateness of the Board of Directors' use of the going-concern basis of accounting and whether a material uncertainty exists that may cast significant doubt on the Company's ability to continue as a going concern.
- Drawing attention to related disclosures in the auditor's report when a material uncertainty exists, or modifying the opinion if those disclosures are inadequate.
- Evaluating the overall presentation, structure, and content of the Statement, including its disclosures, and whether it represents the underlying transactions and events fairly.

The auditor's conclusions are based on audit evidence obtained up to the date of the auditor's report. Future events or conditions may cause the Company to cease to continue as a going concern.

Materiality is the magnitude of misstatements that, individually or in aggregate, makes it probable that the economic decisions of a reasonably knowledgeable user may be influenced. The auditors consider quantitative materiality and qualitative factors when planning the audit, evaluating audit results, and assessing the effect of identified misstatements.

The auditors communicate with those charged with governance regarding deficiencies in internal control identified during the audit. They also provide a statement confirming compliance with relevant ethical requirements regarding independence and communicate relationships and other matters that may reasonably be considered relevant to their independence, including related safeguards where applicable.

## Opinion

The auditors audited the interim condensed consolidated financial statements of Infosys Limited and its subsidiaries, collectively referred to as the Group. These statements comprise:

- The condensed consolidated balance sheet as at December 31, 2025.
- The condensed consolidated statement of comprehensive income for the three months and nine months ended December 31, 2025.
- The condensed cash flows for the nine months ended December 31, 2025.
- Notes to the financial statements, including material accounting policies and other explanatory information.

In the auditors' opinion, the interim condensed consolidated financial statements give a true and fair view, in conformity with International Accounting Standard 34, "Interim Financial Reporting" ("IAS 34"), as issued by the International Accounting Standards Board ("IASB"), of:

- The consolidated state of affairs of the Group as at December 31, 2025.
- The consolidated profit and consolidated total comprehensive income for the three months and nine months ended December 31, 2025.
- The consolidated changes in equity and consolidated cash flows for the nine months ended December 31, 2025.

## Basis for Opinion

The audit of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements was conducted in accordance with the Standards on Auditing issued by the ICAI. The auditors stated that they were independent of the Group in accordance with the ICAI Code of Ethics and had fulfilled their other ethical responsibilities under that Code.

The auditors stated that the audit evidence obtained was sufficient and appropriate to provide a basis for their audit opinion on the interim condensed consolidated financial statements.

## Responsibilities of Management and Board of Directors for the Interim Condensed ConsolidatedFinancialStatements

The Company's Board of Directors is responsible for the preparation and presentation of the interim condensed consolidated financial statements so that they give a true and fair view of the Group's:

- Consolidated financial position.
- Consolidated financial performance.
- Consolidated total comprehensive income.
- Consolidated changes in equity.
- Consolidated cash flows.

The statements are prepared in accordance with IAS 34 as issued by the IASB.

The respective Boards of Directors of entities included in the Group are responsible for:

- Maintaining adequate accounting records to safeguard the Group's assets and prevent and detect fraud and other irregularities.
- Selecting and applying appropriate accounting policies.
- Making reasonable and prudent judgments and estimates.
- Designing, implementing, and maintaining adequate internal financial controls that operate effectively to ensure the accuracy and completeness of accounting records relevant to the preparation and presentation of the respective interim financial statements.
- Assessing the ability of the respective entities to continue as a going concern and disclosing relevant going-concern matters.
- Using the going-concern basis of accounting unless the respective Boards intend to liquidate the entities, cease operations, or have no realistic alternative but to do so.
- Overseeing the Group's financial reporting process.
